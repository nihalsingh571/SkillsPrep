# Placement Mental Maths Guide

## 1. Why Mental Maths is Crucial

In placement exams like TCS NQT, Infosys, and Capgemini, time is your biggest enemy. You often have less than 60 seconds per question. 
If you spend 40 seconds calculating $85 \times 85$ or $12.5\%$ of $6400$ on rough paper, you have already lost. This guide contains the most practical, high-frequency mental math tricks that will shave off those critical seconds.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Golden Rule:** Never reach for your pen for two-digit additions, basic percentage extraction, or squares under 30. Force your brain to do it.

---

## 2. Fast Multiplication Hacks

### Squaring numbers ending in 5
**Rule:** To find $(x5)^2$, multiply $x$ by $(x+1)$ and append 25 at the end.
*   $35^2 \implies 3 \times 4 = 12 \implies \mathbf{1225}$
*   $65^2 \implies 6 \times 7 = 42 \implies \mathbf{4225}$
*   $95^2 \implies 9 \times 10 = 90 \implies \mathbf{9025}$
*   $105^2 \implies 10 \times 11 = 110 \implies \mathbf{11025}$

### Multiplication by 11
**Rule:** To multiply a 2-digit number by 11, separate the digits and insert their sum in the middle.
*   $43 \times 11 \implies 4 \_ 3 \implies 4 (4+3) 3 \implies \mathbf{473}$
*   $72 \times 11 \implies 7 \_ 2 \implies 7 (7+2) 2 \implies \mathbf{792}$
*   *Carry over case:* $85 \times 11 \implies 8 (8+5) 5 \implies 8 (13) 5 \implies \mathbf{935}$

### Multiplication of numbers near 100
**Rule:** Find how much they are below 100. Cross-subtract, then multiply the deficits.
*   $98 \times 96$
    *   98 is $(-2)$
    *   96 is $(-4)$
    *   Cross subtract: $98 - 4 = 94$ (or $96 - 2 = 94$) $\implies$ First part: **94**
    *   Multiply deficits: $(-2) \times (-4) = 08$ $\implies$ Second part: **08**
    *   Result: **9408**
*   $93 \times 97 \implies (93-3) \text{ and } (7 \times 3) \implies \mathbf{9021}$

## 3. High-Speed Percentages

### The Fractional Equivalents (MEMORIZE THESE)
If you see $14.28\%$ in a question, do NOT write $14.28/100$. It is exactly $1/7$.

| Percentage | Fraction | Memory Trick / Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **50%** | $1/2$ | Half |
| **33.33%** | $1/3$ | Third |
| **25%** | $1/4$ | Quarter |
| **20%** | $1/5$ | Fifth |
| **16.66%** | $1/6$ | Very common in Profit & Loss |
| **14.28%** | $1/7$ | $14 \times 2 = 28$ |
| **12.5%** | $1/8$ | Half of $25\%$ |
| **11.11%** | $1/9$ | The "11" repeats $\implies /9$ |
| **9.09%** | $1/11$ | The "09" repeats $\implies /11$ |
| **8.33%** | $1/12$ | Very common in Time & Work |

**Example Application:**
"Find $12.5\%$ of 888."
*Traditional:* $(12.5 / 100) \times 888$. (Slow).
*Mental Math:* $(1/8) \times 888 = \mathbf{111}$. (Instant).

### The $a\% \text{ of } b = b\% \text{ of } a$ Trick
**Rule:** Percentages are reversible! Use this when the percentage number is ugly but the base is beautiful.
*   Find $18\%$ of $50$.
    *   Instead, calculate $50\%$ of $18$.
    *   Half of $18 = \mathbf{9}$.
*   Find $64\%$ of $25$.
    *   Instead, calculate $25\%$ of $64$.
    *   $1/4$ of $64 = \mathbf{16}$.

## 4. Rapid Ratio & Proportion

### Merging Ratios Mentally (The Reverse 'N')
To find $A : B : C$ given $A:B$ and $B:C$:
*   $A:B = 2:3$
*   $B:C = 4:5$
Write them stacked:
2   3
  4   5
Multiply vertically down the left (2x4), diagonally up (4x3), vertically down the right (3x5).
Result: $8 : 12 : 15$.

### The Constant Sum / Difference Hack
If ages are in ratio 3:4 and after 10 years they are 4:5.
Look at the ratio gap.
$A: 3 \rightarrow 4$ (+1 unit).
$B: 4 \rightarrow 5$ (+1 unit).
1 unit gap represents 10 actual years.
So, A's age = $3 \times 10 = \mathbf{30}$.
B's age = $4 \times 10 = \mathbf{40}$.
*Never use $x$ and cross multiply if the unit gaps are equal.*

## 5. Quick Divisibility Checks

When trying to simplify large fractions or factorize numbers, don't guess blindly. Use these checks instantly:

*   **Check for 3:** Sum the digits. Is the sum divisible by 3? (e.g., $1452 \implies 1+4+5+2=12$. Yes).
*   **Check for 4:** Look ONLY at the last two digits. (e.g., $87132 \implies 32 / 4 = 8$. Yes).
*   **Check for 8:** Look ONLY at the last three digits.
*   **Check for 9:** Sum the digits. Is it divisible by 9?
*   **Check for 11:** Alternate sum difference. (e.g., $1331 \implies (1+3) - (3+1) = 0$. Yes).

> [!WARNING]
> **The Prime Trap:** If a number isn't divisible by 2, 3, 5, or 11, don't waste 2 minutes checking 13, 17, 19 unless specifically required. It's often a prime number. Try to find another way around the calculation.

## 6. Daily Mental Drills (The 5-Minute Routine)

To get good at this, do the following drill mentally while commuting or waiting:
1.  **Squares:** Recite squares from 1 to 30. (Crucial for compound interest and number series).
2.  **Cubes:** Recite cubes from 1 to 15.
3.  **Fractions:** Convert $1/2$ down to $1/12$ into percentages.
4.  **Tables:** Recite tables from 12 to 20.

*If you master these 4 things, you will automatically solve aptitude questions 20% faster than the average candidate.*
