# Company-Wise Placement Revision Guide

## 1. Introduction

Every IT service and product-based company has a specific "flavor" to their quantitative aptitude tests. While the syllabus is generally the same, the **weightage** of topics and the **difficulty of calculation** vary drastically.

This guide provides a rapid 5-minute snapshot of what to expect from each major recruiter and which topics to revise on the night before the exam.

---

## 2. TCS NQT (National Qualifier Test)

**Exam Profile:** Heavy emphasis on logic over pure formula application. The Advanced Quants section is notoriously difficult and tricky.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Number System:** (Very High) Trailing zeros, Divisibility rules, Unit digit cyclicity.
*   **Time, Speed, Distance:** Trains crossing platforms, Relative speed of moving objects.
*   **Mensuration & Geometry:** Melting spheres/cylinders into other shapes, Inner/Outer path areas.
*   **Data Interpretation:** Usually a massive table requiring percentage difference calculations.

**TCS "Signature" Question:**
*The 'Contradiction' Probability question (A speaks truth x%, B speaks truth y%).*

**Strategy:** Accuracy over speed. Do not guess blindly in the advanced section as it carries higher weight for Prime/Digital profiles.

---

## 3. Infosys

**Exam Profile:** Known for mathematical puzzles, cryptarithmetic (A+B=C type logic), and heavy Permutation & Combination.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Permutations & Combinations:** (Very High) Arranging words with constraints, selecting committees.
*   **Probability:** Drawing balls without replacement, Dice sums.
*   **Percentages & Profit/Loss:** Successive discounts, false weights.
*   **Time & Work:** The "Leaving early" and Alternating days concepts.

**Infosys "Signature" Question:**
*Cryptarithmetic puzzles (SEND + MORE = MONEY).*

**Strategy:** Speed is crucial. If a P&C question seems to have too many cases, skip it and return later. Focus on the easy Profit/Loss questions first.

---

## 4. Cognizant (GenC & GenC Elevate)

**Exam Profile:** Moderate difficulty, very standard syllabus. Focuses heavily on basic arithmetic and ratios.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Ratio & Proportion:** (Very High) Coins ratio, mixture ratios, age problems.
*   **Averages & Mixtures:** Replacement of items, Allegation cross method.
*   **Simple & Compound Interest:** Effective rate for 2/3 years, Difference between SI and CI.
*   **Data Interpretation:** Usually simple Bar Graphs.

**Cognizant "Signature" Question:**
*The 'Coins' Value equation (A bag contains 1Re, 50p, 25p coins in ratio...).*

**Strategy:** Use mental math and options elimination extensively. Many questions can be solved just by looking at the divisibility of the options.

---

## 5. Wipro (NLTH)

**Exam Profile:** Straightforward but calculation-heavy. Expect decimals and fractions.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Time & Work / Pipes:** (Very High) LCM method, Monkey traps (alternating pipes).
*   **LCM & HCF:** Bells ringing together, finding the largest tile size.
*   **Percentages:** Population growth, Election votes.
*   **Boats & Streams:** Finding Boat speed from Up/Down times.

**Wipro "Signature" Question:**
*The 'Election' Percentage question (Valid/Invalid votes).*

**Strategy:** Do not panic at decimals. Convert decimals to fractions immediately (e.g., $12.5\% \rightarrow 1/8$, $16.66\% \rightarrow 1/6$) to speed up calculations.

---

## 6. Accenture

**Exam Profile:** Very balanced. Tests almost every topic with 1-2 questions each. Focuses heavily on logical reasoning mixed with Quants.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Profit & Loss:** (Very High) Discount percentages, CP to MP ratio.
*   **Time, Speed, Distance:** Average speed, Stoppages.
*   **Number System:** Finding the remainder, basic divisibility.
*   **Probability:** Basic coins and dice.

**Accenture "Signature" Question:**
*The 'Successive Percentage Change' question (Price increases by x%, decreases by y%).*

**Strategy:** Time management is key. Accenture rarely asks deeply twisted questions; if you know the formula, you will get the answer in 30 seconds.

---

## 7. Capgemini

**Exam Profile:** Often outsources to CoCubes. Focuses heavily on Geometry and high-level Algebra disguised as quants.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Geometry/Mensuration:** (Very High) Triangles, Circles, Cubes.
*   **Algebra:** Linear equations, Ages, basic quadratic factorization.
*   **Number System:** LCM/HCF difference tricks.
*   **Time & Work:** Men, Days, Hours (MDH) chain rule.

**Capgemini "Signature" Question:**
*The 'MDH Chain Rule' question (If 5 men working 6 hours...).*

**Strategy:** Memorize all mensuration formulas. If you forget the volume of a sphere or the surface area of a cylinder, you will automatically lose 2-3 marks.

---

## 8. IBM

**Exam Profile:** Often uses cognitive games and very specific quantitative assessment platforms (like IPAT).

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Number Series / Number System:** Identifying patterns, finding missing numbers.
*   **Percentages & Ratios:** Heavy focus on financial maths (Interest, Profit).
*   **Time, Speed, Distance:** Relative speed, meeting points.

**IBM "Signature" Question:**
*Complex Number Series that require double-difference logic.*

**Strategy:** Practice number series extensively. IBM tests your ability to spot patterns under pressure more than your ability to solve complex algebraic equations.

---

## 9. Big 4 (Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG)

**Exam Profile:** Heavy, heavy focus on Data Interpretation, Business Maths, and Data Sufficiency. They want to see if you can analyze financial data.

**High Priority Topics:**
*   **Data Interpretation:** (Extreme Priority) Complex tables, Pie charts, Line graphs.
*   **Data Sufficiency:** Given Statement I and II, are they sufficient to answer the question?
*   **Percentages & Averages:** Calculating growth rates over multiple years.
*   **Profit & Loss:** Markups, margins, successive discounts.

**Big 4 "Signature" Question:**
*Massive Tabular DI with 5 interconnected questions requiring heavy percentage calculations.*

**Strategy:** Do NOT calculate exact values for DI unless absolutely necessary. Use the "Base Approximation" trick and "Global Ratio" tricks to eliminate options quickly. Practice reading large datasets.
