## 1. Introduction to the Master Sheet

**Why this chapter exists:**
Placement exams (like TCS NQT, Infosys, Cognizant) often test your ability to recall a specific shortcut formula under immense time pressure. If you can't remember the exact relationship, you will waste 3 minutes deriving it algebraically. 

This chapter is the **"Last 1 Hour Revision"** guide. It compiles every single critical shortcut, formula, and "mental math hack" from all Quantitative Aptitude topics into one consolidated master sheet. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How to use this chapter:**

## 2. Percentages, Profit & Loss, Ratio

| Topic | Concept | The "Under 10 Seconds" Shortcut |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Percentages** | Successive % Change | $A + B + \frac{A \times B}{100}$ |
| **Percentages** | Price $\uparrow$ by $R\%$, Consumption $\downarrow$ | $\frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100 \%$ |
| **Percentages** | Population after $n$ years | $P(1 \pm \frac{R}{100})^n$ |
| **Percentages** | Passing Marks Equation | $\text{Score}_1 + \text{Fail Marks} = \text{Score}_2 - \text{Extra Marks}$ |
| **Profit & Loss** | CP from SP & Profit% | $\text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \frac{100}{100 + P\%}$ |
| **Profit & Loss** | False Weight Profit% | $\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}} \times 100$ |
| **Profit & Loss** | Sell 2 items at same SP (One at $+x\%$, one at $-x\%$) | Always a LOSS of $\frac{x^2}{100}\%$ |
| **Profit & Loss** | Buy $x$ items for Rs. $y$, Sell $a$ items for Rs. $b$ | Profit % = $\frac{xb - ya}{ya} \times 100$ (Cross Multiply) |
| **Discount** | Successive Discounts $d_1, d_2$ | $d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 \times d_2}{100}$ |
| **Discount** | Buy $x$ Get $y$ Free | Discount % = $\frac{y}{x + y} \times 100$ |
| **Discount** | CP to MP Ratio | $\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100 - D\%}{100 + P\%}$ |
| **Ratio** | Finding $A:B:C$ from $A:B$ and $B:C$ | The Reverse 'N' Multiplication |
| **Ratio** | Coins Value Equation | $\text{Total Value} = \text{NumCoins}_1 \times V_1 + \text{NumCoins}_2 \times V_2$ |
| **Ratio** | Adding mixture to constant component | $\text{Old\%} \times \text{Old Total} = \text{New\%} \times \text{New Total}$ |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Interest** | Core Concept | Calculate $RT\%$ first. Interest = $RT\% \text{ of } P$. |
| **Simple Interest** | Sum becomes $N$ times | $R = \frac{100(N-1)}{T}$ |
| **Simple Interest** | Extra Rate Shift | $\text{Extra Interest} = \text{Extra } (R \times T)\% \text{ of Principal}$ |
| **Simple Interest** | Split sum for Equal SI | $P_1 : P_2 = \frac{1}{R_1 T_1} : \frac{1}{R_2 T_2}$ |
| **Compound Interest** | Effective Rate (2 Years) | $2R + \frac{R^2}{100} \%$ |
| **Compound Interest** | Effective Rate (3 Years) | $3R + \frac{3R^2}{100} + \frac{R^3}{10000} \%$ |
| **Compound Interest** | Standard 10% Rates | 2yrs: 21%. 3yrs: 33.1%. 4yrs: 46.41%. |
| **Compound Interest** | Tree Method Ratios | 2yrs $\implies$ 2:1. 3yrs $\implies$ 3:3:1. 4yrs $\implies$ 4:6:4:1. |

| **Compound Interest** | Difference (CI - SI) for 3 Years | $D = P \times (\frac{R}{100})^2 \times (\frac{300+R}{100})$ |
| **Compound Interest** | Sum becomes $x$ times in $T$ yrs | It becomes $x^n$ times in $n \times T$ yrs. |

| **TSD** | Unit Conversion | km/hr $\times (5/18)$ = m/s. |

| **Trains** | Universal Formula | $T = \frac{L_1 + L_2}{S_1 \pm S_2}$ |
| **Trains** | Direction Rules | Opposite $\implies (S_1 + S_2)$. Same $\implies \|S_1 - S_2\|$. |

## 8. General Maths & Quick Arithmetic Hacks

**Divisibility Rules:**
*   **Div by 3:** Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
*   **Div by 4:** Last two digits are divisible by 4.
*   **Div by 8:** Last three digits are divisible by 8.
*   **Div by 9:** Sum of digits is divisible by 9.
*   **Div by 11:** (Sum of alternate digits) $-$ (Sum of other alternate digits) is 0 or multiple of 11.

**Squaring Numbers ending in 5:**
*   $45^2 = ?$
*   Multiply first digit (4) by next consecutive number (5) $\implies 4 \times 5 = 20$.
*   Append 25 $\implies 2025$.
*   $85^2 = (8 \times 9) \text{ and } 25 \implies 7225$.

**Multiplication by 11:**
*   $43 \times 11 = ?$
*   Separate 4 and 3: `4 _ 3`
*   Add them in the middle: `4 (4+3) 3` $\implies 473$.
*   $85 \times 11 = 8 \text{ (8+5) } 5 = 8 \text{ (13) } 5 \implies$ Carry over 1 $\implies 935$.

**Finding Last Two Digits:**
*   For $(...x)^n$, use Euler's Totient or binomial expansion shortcuts. (Covered deeply in Number System).

> [!WARNING]
> **Golden Rule of Placements:**
> If a calculation takes more than 60 seconds of brute-force multiplying, **YOU MISSED THE SHORTCUT**. Stop, look at the numbers, and see if it fits an algebraic identity like $(a+b)^2$, or if you can use the Unit Digit method to eliminate 3 out of 4 options.
