## 1. Introduction: The Need for Speed
In placement exams like TCS NQT or Infosys, you have limited time (e.g., 15 minutes for 15 RC questions). If your reading speed is average (200-250 words per minute), you will waste precious time just reading the passage, leaving little time for analysis. The goal is to reach **400-500 WPM** with 80%+ comprehension.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Common Mistake:** Trying to read faster by just moving your eyes quickly without processing the meaning. This leads to regression (rereading the same line multiple times) and wastes more time.

---

## 2. Eliminating Subvocalization
Subvocalization is the habit of pronouncing words in your head as you read them. 

``` rule
Rule of Subvocalization Limit:
Because your speaking speed is capped at about 250 WPM, subvocalizing limits your reading speed to your talking speed. To read faster, you must look at words and understand their meaning without "hearing" them.
```

### Techniques to Eliminate Subvocalization:
1. **The Mumble Technique:** Hum a tune or repeat "A-E-I-O-U" under your breath while reading. This occupies the motor mechanism of speech, forcing your brain to process text purely visually.
2. **Chewing Gum:** Chew gum while reading to keep your jaw and tongue occupied.
3. **Pointer Method:** Use a pen or your finger to guide your eyes faster than your inner voice can speak.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** Subvocalization = "Sub-vocal" (below vocal). To beat it, make your eyes the primary organ of reading, not your mouth/ears.

## 3. The Chunking Method
Chunking means reading groups of words rather than single words.

**Wrong Approach (Word-by-word):**
[The] [quick] [brown] [fox] [jumps] [over] [the] [lazy] [dog].

**Correct Approach (Chunking):**
[The quick brown fox] [jumps over] [the lazy dog].

### How to Practice Chunking:
- **Rule of 3:** Try to fixate your eyes only 3 times per line instead of 8-10 times.
- Focus your eyes on the *center* of a chunk of 3-4 words. Your brain will automatically process the surrounding words.

## 4. Expanding Peripheral Vision
Most readers only use their central (foveal) vision. By expanding your peripheral (parafoveal) vision, you can read the beginning and end of a line without directly looking at them.

``` rule
Rule of Peripheral Margin:
Do not look at the first word or the last word of a line. Start your eye movement one inch inside the left margin and stop one inch before the right margin.
```

**Exercise:**
Read the following by keeping your eyes ONLY in the center column. Let your peripheral vision catch the sides.
```text
          (look here)
It is       important       to remember
that your   peripheral      vision can
capture     more words      than you
currently   realize         right now.
```

## 5. RSVP Reading (Rapid Serial Visual Presentation)
RSVP is a digital reading method where words flash on the screen one by one at a set speed (e.g., 400 WPM).
- **Why it works:** It completely eliminates eye movement (saccades) and regression.
- **Application:** While you can't use RSVP software during the exam, training with it (using apps like Spritz or Spreeder) for 10 minutes a day trains your brain to process visual word recognition faster, reducing subvocalization.

## 6. Signpost Words (Transition Words)
Signposts tell you where the author's argument is going. They help you decide when to slow down and when to speed up.

| **Category** | **Signpost Words** | **Action to Take** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Continuations** | Furthermore, Moreover, Also, In addition | **Speed Up!** The author is adding similar information. You already know the point. |
| **Contrasts** | However, But, Yet, On the other hand, Conversely | **Slow Down!** The author is changing direction. The main point is often here. |
| **Conclusions** | Therefore, Thus, Hence, In conclusion | **Slow Down!** This summarizes the argument. High chance of a question from here. |
| **Examples** | For instance, Such as, To illustrate | **Skim!** You don't need to read every detail of an example if you understood the main point before it. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Strategy:** In TCS and Infosys RC, contrast words like *However* and *But* are goldmines. 80% of inference questions are based on the sentences immediately following these words.

---

## 7. Identifying and Skipping Non-Essential Content
You don't need to read 100% of the passage to score 100%.

**What to read carefully:**
- The first paragraph (Thesis)
- The first sentence of every body paragraph (Topic Sentence)
- The last paragraph (Conclusion)
- Sentences with contrast/conclusion signposts

**What to skim or skip:**
- Long lists of names, dates, or items (just note where they are so you can return if a question asks).
- Extended examples supporting a point you already understand.
- Tangential background information.

## 8. Practice Exercises for Speed Reading

### Exercise 1: The Skimming Challenge
**Passage:** (Read in 30 seconds)
The advent of artificial intelligence has drastically altered the landscape of modern employment. While many fear that automation will lead to widespread job losses, history suggests a different trajectory. During the Industrial Revolution, for instance, the introduction of machinery displaced manual laborers but simultaneously created entirely new industries. Similarly, AI is poised to automate repetitive tasks, thereby allowing human workers to focus on creative, strategic, and interpersonal endeavors. However, this transition requires a massive upskilling effort from both governments and corporations.

**Questions:**
1. What is the author's main tone regarding AI and jobs?
2. What historical event is used as a comparison?
3. What is the condition required for a successful transition?

*Answers:* 1. Optimistic/Balanced. 2. Industrial Revolution. 3. Massive upskilling effort.

### Exercise 2: Signpost Hunting
Identify the signposts in this paragraph and explain what they indicate:
"Solar energy is abundant and renewable; **therefore**, it is a key component of our future energy grid. **However**, the current cost of solar panels limits widespread adoption. **Moreover**, battery storage technology is not yet efficient enough to store excess energy for nighttime use."
- *Therefore* -> Conclusion based on the first premise.
- *However* -> Shift in tone to a problem/limitation.
- *Moreover* -> Addition of another problem/limitation.

---

## Key Takeaways
- **Subvocalization** slows you down; use the pointer method or mumble technique to stop it.
- **Chunking** allows you to read 3-4 words at a time.
- **Peripheral Vision** prevents you from wasting time on margins.
- **Signpost Words** act as traffic lights: Red (Slow down for 'However'), Green (Speed up for 'Moreover').
- **Skim** lists and examples; **Read** topic sentences and conclusions.

> [!TIP]
> Practice speed reading with newspaper editorials (The Hindu, Indian Express) daily for 15 minutes. Measure your WPM and try to increase it by 20 WPM every week.
