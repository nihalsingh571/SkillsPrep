## 2.1 Main Idea / Central Theme Questions

### What It Is
The main idea question asks you to identify the **central argument, primary focus, or overarching message** of the entire passage. It's not about any one paragraph — it's about what the passage as a WHOLE is communicating.

### Recognition — How to Spot This Question Type

Look for these exact phrasings:
- "The main idea of the passage is..."
- "The passage primarily discusses..."
- "Which of the following best summarizes the passage?"
- "The central theme of the passage is..."
- "The passage is mainly about..."
- "The author's primary purpose is to..."

### Solving Method: The TAPS Framework

```
T - Topic: What broad subject does the passage cover?
A - Angle: What specific aspect of that topic is discussed?
P - Position: What does the author argue/conclude about it?
S - Scope: Does my chosen answer cover the ENTIRE passage?
```

**Example Application:**
- Topic: Artificial Intelligence
- Angle: AI's impact on employment
- Position: AI creates new jobs while displacing routine-task workers
- Scope: The answer should cover both the creation AND displacement angle

### The 5 Wrong Answer Patterns for Main Idea Questions

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Too Narrow** | Only covers one detail/paragraph | "AI is useful in customer service" |
| **Too Broad** | Goes beyond what the passage covers | "Technology is changing the world" |
| **First-Sentence Trap** | Just repeats the introduction | "AI has disrupted corporate environments" |
| **Contradictory** | Opposite of author's actual argument | "AI has had minimal impact on jobs" |
| **Partial Truth** | True but incomplete — misses the full picture | "AI creates new jobs" (ignores displacement) |

> [!TIP]
> **The IDEAL main idea answer:**
> - Covers ALL major sections of the passage
> - Uses qualifying language (may, often, tends to, can)
> - Matches the author's tone (balanced, critical, optimistic, etc.)
> - Is NOT longer than the passage's own concluding sentence

### Practice Questions — Main Idea

---

**Q1. [Passage excerpt: The passage discusses how digital payments have grown exponentially in India, driven by UPI infrastructure, smartphone penetration, and government demonetization policies. It also addresses remaining challenges: financial literacy gaps, cybersecurity concerns, and exclusion of rural populations. The author concludes that digital payment growth is impressive but uneven, and calls for targeted intervention.]**

**Which of the following best represents the main idea?**

(A) UPI has made India a leader in global fintech innovation.
(B) Digital payment adoption in India, while impressive, faces critical challenges that require policy intervention.
(C) Demonetization was the primary driver of India's digital payment revolution.
(D) Cybersecurity concerns pose the greatest threat to India's financial digitization.

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Option (B) captures BOTH the positive trend (impressive growth) AND the challenges, AND includes the conclusion (policy intervention needed). It covers the full scope of the passage.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Too narrow — focuses only on UPI and global comparison. The passage covers much more.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Too narrow — demonetization is one factor mentioned, not the "primary driver" the passage establishes.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Too narrow AND introduces a ranking ("greatest threat") that the passage doesn't establish.

**📏 Rule:** Main idea answer = Topic + Angle + Author's Position, covering FULL passage scope.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find the word "however" or "but" in the passage — the main idea is usually the reconciliation of those two sides.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro

## 2.2 Title Questions

### What It Is
Title questions ask you to select the **most appropriate title** for the passage. This is essentially a main idea question in disguise — a good title captures the central topic and angle without being too specific or too vague.

### Recognition
- "Which of the following would be the best title for this passage?"
- "The passage can be best titled as..."
- "The most appropriate heading for this passage would be..."

### Solving Method

A good title has THREE properties:
1. **Captures the central topic** (not a sub-detail)
2. **Suggests the author's angle or stance** (not just the subject)
3. **Is specific enough to differentiate** from other possible passages on the same topic

```
GOOD TITLE FORMULA:
[Central Topic]: [Author's Angle/Position]
OR
[Central Topic] — [Key Tension or Insight]

Examples:
✅ "Digital Payments in India: Promise and Persistent Challenges"
✅ "AI and Employment: Navigating the Disruption Debate"
❌ "Technology Today" (Too vague)
❌ "Why UPI is Better Than Cash" (Too narrow, takes a side the passage may not)
```

### The 4 Wrong Title Patterns

| Pattern | Problem |
|---|---|
| Overly Broad | Could be the title of a textbook chapter |
| Too Specific | Only references one paragraph |
| Biased | Implies author's stance is stronger than it is |
| Misleading | Uses passage vocabulary but implies wrong focus |

## 2.3 Specific Detail / Fact Questions

### What It Is
Specific detail questions ask you to recall or locate **explicit information** stated in the passage. The answer is literally present in the passage text — no inference required.

### Recognition
- "According to the passage..."
- "The passage states that..."
- "Which of the following is mentioned in the passage?"
- "Based on the information given in the passage, [specific fact]..."
- "The author mentions that..."

### Solving Method: LOCATE → MATCH

```
Step 1: Identify the KEY TERM in the question
Step 2: Scan the passage for that key term
Step 3: Read 1-2 sentences around that term
Step 4: Match what the passage says to the options
Step 5: Choose the option that EXACTLY matches — not approximately
```

> [!WARNING]
> **The Specific Detail Trap:** Wrong options for detail questions often:
> 1. Use words FROM the passage but in a different context
> 2. State the OPPOSITE of what the passage says
> 3. Combine two true facts from the passage into a false compound statement
> 4. Add extra information that the passage doesn't state

### Common Mistake: The "Sounds Right" Trap

> Passage says: *"Solar energy adoption has increased by 40% in five years in urban areas."*
> Wrong option: *"Solar energy has doubled in the past decade."*
> Why wrong: The passage says 40% (not doubled) and says five years (not a decade) and says urban areas (not everywhere). Three subtle changes make this completely wrong.

### Practice — Specific Detail

---

**Q2. [Passage: "The gig economy, defined as a labor market characterized by short-term contracts and freelance work rather than permanent jobs, has grown by 33% in India between 2018 and 2023, according to NASSCOM. While platforms like Uber, Swiggy, and Urban Company have created flexible income opportunities, critics note that gig workers lack traditional employment benefits such as health insurance, paid leave, and job security."]**

**According to the passage, which of the following is a characteristic of the gig economy?**

(A) Workers receive comprehensive health benefits.
(B) The gig economy offers stable, long-term employment.
(C) Platforms like Uber have created flexible income opportunities.
(D) NASSCOM reported a 50% growth in gig economy workers.

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage explicitly states "platforms like Uber, Swiggy, and Urban Company have created flexible income opportunities." This is a direct match to option (C).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The passage says gig workers LACK health insurance. Direct contradiction.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** The passage defines gig economy as "short-term contracts" — opposite of "stable, long-term."
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The passage says 33% growth, NOT 50%. This is a deliberate number-swap trap.

**📏 Rule:** Specific detail answers must EXACTLY match passage content — verify every number, qualifier, and subject.
**⚡ Shortcut:** For detail questions, find the KEY TERM from the question in the passage first, then read 2 sentences around it.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Accenture, Capgemini

## 2.4 Inference Questions — The Most Important Type

### What It Is
An inference is a conclusion that **MUST be true** based on information given in the passage, even though it is not directly stated. It is the most commonly tested question type in competitive RC and the **most frequently wrong-answered**.

### The Golden Rule of Inference

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  INFERENCE = LOGICAL EXTENSION, NOT IMAGINATION       ║
║                                                       ║
║  The inference MUST:                                  ║
║  ✅ Be supported by evidence IN the passage           ║
║  ✅ Be a necessary logical consequence                ║
║  ✅ NOT require outside knowledge                     ║
║                                                       ║
║  The inference MUST NOT:                              ║
║  ❌ Go beyond what the passage logically supports     ║
║  ❌ Require assumptions not in the passage            ║
║  ❌ Use personal knowledge or common sense alone      ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### Recognition
- "Which of the following can be inferred from the passage?"
- "The passage implies that..."
- "The author suggests that..."
- "It can be concluded from the passage that..."
- "What does the author imply about..."

### The Inference Spectrum

```
EXPLICIT FACT ←————————————————→ PURE ASSUMPTION
  (Stated in passage)              (Not in passage)
         
   FACT → INFERENCE → ASSUMPTION → PREDICTION
   ↑           ↑            ↑           ↑
 Stated    Logical      Unstated    Speculative
 directly  extension    but needed  future claim
           of stated    for logic   
           facts        to hold     

TARGET ZONE FOR RC INFERENCE: Between FACT and INFERENCE
```

### The 3-Step Inference Test

1. **Evidence Test:** Can I find explicit text in the passage that supports this claim?
2. **Logic Test:** Does this conclusion NECESSARILY follow from that evidence?
3. **Scope Test:** Does this conclusion stay within the passage's boundaries?

If the answer is YES to all three → Valid inference.

### Strong vs Weak Inference

| Feature | Strong Inference | Weak Inference |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Directly tied to passage text | Loosely related |
| Logic | Necessarily follows | Possibly follows |
| Language | Qualified (may, tends to, often) | Absolute (always, never) |
| Scope | Within passage boundaries | Beyond passage |
| Uniqueness | Only this conclusion follows | Many conclusions possible |

### Practice — Inference

**Q3. [Passage: "Countries with higher levels of press freedom consistently demonstrate stronger economic institutions, lower corruption indices, and more robust democratic processes. A free press serves as a watchdog, exposing government inefficiency and corporate malfeasance, thereby creating accountability mechanisms that benefit citizens and investors alike."]**

**Which of the following can be most logically inferred from the passage?**

(A) Countries with low press freedom will always have corrupt governments.
(B) Economic development leads to greater press freedom.
(C) Press freedom may contribute to better governance and economic outcomes.
(D) A free press is more important than democracy for economic growth.

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage establishes a correlation between press freedom and positive outcomes (economic institutions, low corruption, democracy). It also explains the mechanism (watchdog role, accountability). A logical inference is that press freedom contributes to these outcomes. Note the qualified language in (C) — "may contribute" — which matches the correlational (not causal) nature of the passage's claims.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Always" is extreme. The passage says "consistently" — not always. One exception would disprove this.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** The passage implies press freedom → economic development, not the reverse. This reverses the causal direction.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The passage never compares press freedom and democracy in importance. This ranking is entirely out of scope.

**📏 Rule:** Inference = logical, qualified extension. Avoid extremes (always, never) and reversals.
**⚡ Shortcut:** The correct inference usually uses SOFTER language than the passage (may, could, might, tends to).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, IBM, Cognizant, Wipro

## 2.5 Tone Questions

### What It Is
Tone questions ask you to identify the **author's attitude** toward the subject — how the author "sounds" when writing. This is different from the subject matter itself.

### Recognition
- "The author's tone is best described as..."
- "The overall tone of the passage is..."
- "The author's attitude toward [X] can be described as..."
- "How does the author regard [topic]?"

### The 50-Word Tone Vocabulary Bank

#### Positive Tones
| Tone Word | Meaning | Used When Author... |
|---|---|---|
| Optimistic | Hopeful about outcomes | Expects positive results |
| Enthusiastic | Excited and eager | Shows strong positive energy |
| Laudatory | Full of praise | Praises someone/something |
| Celebratory | Marks with joy | Celebrates achievement |
| Appreciative | Grateful, recognizing value | Acknowledges contribution |
| Encouraging | Supportive, motivating | Urges positive action |
| Reverent | Deeply respectful | Shows high regard |
| Admiring | Looking up to something | Expresses admiration |
| Sympathetic | Understanding of difficulty | Shows compassion |
| Nostalgic | Fond of the past | Reflects on better times |

#### Negative Tones
| Tone Word | Meaning | Used When Author... |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Finding fault | Points out problems |
| Cynical | Distrustful, pessimistic | Doubts good motives |
| Skeptical | Doubtful, questioning | Questions claims/evidence |
| Indignant | Morally outraged | Shows righteous anger |
| Contemptuous | Showing disrespect | Looks down on something |
| Satirical | Using humor to criticize | Mocks through irony |
| Sarcastic | Bitterly ironic | Uses cutting irony |
| Pessimistic | Expects negative outcomes | Sees the worst |
| Dismissive | Treating as unimportant | Brushes off a view |
| Alarmist | Exaggerates danger | Raises panic about threats |

#### Neutral/Analytical Tones
| Tone Word | Meaning | Used When Author... |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Factual, unbiased | Presents facts without opinion |
| Analytical | Examining carefully | Breaks down components |
| Detached | Not emotionally involved | Observes without feeling |
| Didactic | Teaching-oriented | Intends to instruct |
| Informative | Providing information | Shares knowledge |
| Descriptive | Painting a picture | Describes in detail |
| Expository | Explaining clearly | Explains step by step |

#### Complex/Mixed Tones
| Tone Word | Meaning | Used When Author... |
|---|---|---|
| Ambivalent | Both positive and negative | Sees both sides equally |
| Ironic | Saying one thing, meaning another | Uses irony as a device |
| Polemical | Controversial, argumentative | Takes a strong, contested position |
| Pragmatic | Practical, realistic | Focuses on what works |
| Cautiously optimistic | Hopeful but careful | Shows hope with reservations |
| Measured | Careful, moderate | Avoids extremes |
| Nuanced | Acknowledges complexity | Shows shades of meaning |
| Resigned | Accepting the inevitable | Accepts with sadness |

### How to Identify Tone

**Step 1: Catalog Emotional Adjectives and Adverbs**
Read through the passage and list all adjectives and adverbs that suggest emotion or attitude.

**Step 2: Analyze Verb Choices**
- "He claims" vs "He demonstrates" — "claims" suggests skepticism; "demonstrates" suggests acceptance
- "The policy failed" vs "The policy struggled" — different degrees of negative evaluation

**Step 3: Assess the Conclusion's Direction**
- Ends with hope? → Optimistic
- Ends with warning? → Cautious or alarming
- Ends with mixed view? → Ambivalent or balanced

**Step 4: Match to Vocabulary Bank Above**

> [!TIP]
> **The Tone Triangle:** When stuck between two tone options, always choose the one that is:
> 1. More SPECIFIC (analytical > neutral)
> 2. Less EXTREME (cautiously optimistic > wildly enthusiastic)
> 3. Better supported by TEXTUAL EVIDENCE

**Q4. [Passage: "The government's ambitious renewable energy targets, while commendable in principle, appear to be aspirational rather than grounded in the infrastructure realities of the country's underdeveloped transmission networks and inadequate storage technology. Unless these fundamental challenges are addressed with the same urgency as target-setting, the transition to clean energy may remain little more than a well-meaning policy paper."]**

**The author's tone in this passage is best described as:**

(A) Enthusiastically supportive of renewable energy
(B) Skeptically analytical, questioning the feasibility of the targets
(C) Cynically dismissive of government renewable energy policy
(D) Alarmist about the environmental consequences of inaction

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The author acknowledges the targets are "commendable in principle" (not dismissive) but questions whether they are achievable given infrastructure realities. Words like "aspirational rather than grounded," "unless these challenges are addressed" indicate doubt about feasibility — classic skeptical analytical tone.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The author's praise is qualified ("commendable in principle") and immediately countered — not enthusiastically supportive.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Cynically dismissive means the author would ridicule the policy entirely. The author acknowledges the goal is good — just questions execution.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Alarmist would be extreme panic language. The author is measured and logical, not alarmist.

**📏 Rule:** Tone is not about what the author says but HOW they say it — look at qualifiers and word choices.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If the author praises something BUT then says "however/unless/but," the tone is SKEPTICAL or CAUTIOUSLY CRITICAL.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, TCS NQT, IBM

## 2.6 Vocabulary-in-Context Questions

### What It Is
These questions ask you to determine the meaning of a specific word or phrase **as it is used in the passage** — not its dictionary definition, but its CONTEXTUAL meaning.

### Recognition
- "The word '[X]' in paragraph 2 most nearly means..."
- "As used in line [N], '[word]' most closely means..."
- "The author uses the word '[X]' to mean..."

### The Critical Insight: Context > Dictionary

The word "bank" has 15+ dictionary definitions. In context, it's always clear. Vocabulary-in-context questions test whether you understand that **words have contextual meanings** that may differ from their most common definition.

### Solving Method: The Substitution Test

```
Step 1: Read the SENTENCE containing the word
Step 2: Read the sentence BEFORE and AFTER as well (3-sentence window)
Step 3: COVER the word and predict what synonym would work
Step 4: Read all 4 options
Step 5: SUBSTITUTE each option back into the sentence
Step 6: Choose the option that preserves the sentence's meaning
```

### Common Vocabulary Traps

| Trap | Example | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Primary definition trap | "Bank" = financial institution (when passage means riverbank) | Always use context, not dictionary |
| Similar-looking word trap | "Sanction" (approve) vs "sanction" (punish) — both meanings exist! | Context window solves this |
| Familiar but different | "Novel" = new (not the book) | Don't default to familiar meaning |
| Connotation mismatch | "Inexpensive" vs "cheap" — both mean low cost but differ in respect | Match register and tone |

**Q5. [Passage: "The startup's meteoric rise to a billion-dollar valuation was not without its casualties. Several co-founders departed under acrimonious circumstances, and the company's once-collegial culture had calcified into a rigid hierarchy that stifled innovation."]**

**The word "calcified" as used in the passage most nearly means:**

(A) Crumbled and fallen apart
(B) Become hard, rigid, and resistant to change
(C) Grown stronger and more effective
(D) Been dissolved and replaced

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Calcified" literally means "turned to calcium/bone-hard material." In context, the passage says the "collegial culture had calcified into a rigid hierarchy" — meaning the culture became rigid and stiff (like bone), resisting flexibility. The context clue is "rigid hierarchy that stifled innovation."

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Crumbled" would mean the culture fell apart, but the passage says it became more rigid — a hierarchy.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** The passage has a negative tone here ("stifled innovation") — the culture becoming "stronger and more effective" contradicts the negative context.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The culture wasn't dissolved — it still exists but in a rigid form.

**📏 Rule:** Vocabulary-in-context = Use the surrounding sentence as your definition source.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Substitute each option back into the sentence. The one that fits SMOOTHLY is correct.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys, GRE-style placements

## 2.7 Purpose Questions

### What It Is
Purpose questions ask WHY the author wrote the passage, or why the author included a SPECIFIC paragraph, example, or detail. Purpose is different from main idea — main idea is WHAT, purpose is WHY.

### Recognition
- "The author's purpose in writing this passage is to..."
- "Why does the author mention [specific example/detail]?"
- "The author refers to [X] primarily in order to..."
- "The purpose of paragraph 3 is to..."
- "The author includes [specific statistic] to..."

### Purpose Categories

| Purpose | Signal Words in Passage | Answer Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Inform** | "Research shows," "Studies indicate," "It is known that" | "To inform readers about X" |
| **Persuade** | "We must," "It is imperative," "Clearly," "Obviously" | "To argue that X is necessary" |
| **Describe** | Detailed descriptions, sensory details | "To describe the characteristics of X" |
| **Critique** | "However," "Despite," "fails to," "lacks" | "To criticize the existing approach to X" |
| **Compare** | "Unlike," "In contrast," "Similarly," "Both" | "To compare X and Y" |
| **Analyze** | "This suggests," "The reason is," "This can be attributed to" | "To analyze the causes of X" |
| **Entertain** | Humor, anecdotes, storytelling | "To entertain readers with the story of X" |

### Specific-Detail Purpose Questions (Why did the author mention X?)

This sub-type asks why a SPECIFIC detail was included. The answer relates to:
1. **Supporting the main argument** (most common)
2. **Providing a counterexample** to refute an opposing view
3. **Illustrating a broader principle** with a specific case
4. **Transitioning** from one idea to another
5. **Adding credibility** (statistics, expert citations)

**Q6. [Passage discusses corporate social responsibility. Paragraph 3 mentions that Patagonia, the outdoor clothing brand, donates 1% of all sales to environmental causes and was one of the first companies to embrace radical transparency in supply chain reporting.]**

**The author mentions Patagonia primarily in order to:**

(A) Advertise Patagonia's products to environmentally conscious readers.
(B) Provide a concrete example of a company that successfully implements CSR principles.
(C) Argue that all companies should imitate Patagonia's exact business model.
(D) Suggest that CSR is only viable for outdoor clothing brands.

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** In the context of a passage about CSR, a specific company example serves to ILLUSTRATE and SUPPORT the broader claim that CSR can be implemented successfully. Patagonia is an example, not the focus.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The passage is not an advertisement — it's an analysis. Advertising is completely out of scope.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "All companies should imitate the EXACT model" is too prescriptive. The passage uses Patagonia as an example, not a blueprint.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** This suggests a limitation the author doesn't state. The example is meant to inspire, not restrict.

**📏 Rule:** When an example appears, it is almost always there to SUPPORT the broader argument of the surrounding paragraph.
**⚡ Shortcut:** The purpose of any example = the conclusion sentence of the paragraph it appears in.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, Cognizant, IBM

## 2.8 Structure / Organization Questions

### What It Is
These questions ask about HOW the passage is organized — the logical structure and arrangement of ideas.

### Recognition
- "How is the passage organized?"
- "The structure of the passage can best be described as..."
- "The author develops the argument by..."
- "The relationship between paragraphs 2 and 3 is..."

### Common Passage Structures

| Structure | Description | Signal Words |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem → Solution** | States a problem, then offers solutions | "The challenge is...One approach..." |
| **Claim → Evidence → Conclusion** | States claim, supports it, concludes | "Research shows...This suggests...Therefore..." |
| **Compare and Contrast** | Presents two sides or viewpoints | "Unlike...Similarly...On the other hand..." |
| **Chronological** | Events in time order | "First...Then...Subsequently...Finally..." |
| **General → Specific** | Broad overview then detailed examples | "In general...Specifically...For example..." |
| **Cause → Effect** | What caused something and its results | "Because of...Resulted in...As a result..." |
| **Thesis → Antithesis → Synthesis** | Argument + counterargument + resolution | "While X...However Y...A balanced view..." |

### Building a Passage Map

Before answering structure questions, create a one-line summary per paragraph:
- P1: Introduction — defines [topic]
- P2: Argument FOR — [main supporting point]
- P3: Counterargument — [opposing view]
- P4: Response to counterargument — [author's rebuttal]
- P5: Conclusion — [author's final position]

## 2.9 Analogy Questions

### What It Is
Analogy questions ask you to find a situation or scenario that has the **same underlying relationship** as something described in the passage.

### Recognition
- "The relationship described in the passage between X and Y is most similar to..."
- "Which of the following is most analogous to the situation described in paragraph 2?"
- "The author's comparison of X to Y is most similar to..."

### Solving Method
1. **Identify the relationship** in the passage: X causes Y / X is an example of Y / X is contrasted with Y
2. **Express the relationship in abstract terms**: "[A] undermines [B] by introducing uncertainty"
3. **Find the option** where the same abstract relationship holds

## 2.10 True / False / Cannot Be Determined — The Placement Specialist

### What It Is
This question type is **extremely common in TCS NQT and Infosys** and is **rarely found in CAT or GRE**. It presents a statement and asks you to determine whether it is:
- **TRUE**: Explicitly or logically supported by the passage
- **FALSE**: Contradicted by the passage
- **CANNOT BE DETERMINED (CBD)**: The passage neither confirms nor denies it

### Recognition
- "Which of the following statements is TRUE according to the passage?"
- "Which statement is FALSE based on the passage?"
- "Which statement CANNOT BE DETERMINED from the passage?"
- Given a set of statements, "Which are true/false/cannot be determined?"

### The Critical Distinction: CBD vs False

This is the most important distinction students miss:

```
FALSE = The passage CONTRADICTS the statement
        (The passage says the OPPOSITE)

CANNOT BE DETERMINED = The passage is SILENT about it
        (The passage neither supports nor contradicts)
```

| Example Statement | Classification | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| "Passage says AI replaced 5 million jobs" — option says "AI replaced 3 million jobs" | FALSE | Contradicts the number |
| "Passage discusses AI in healthcare" — option says "AI improves education" | CBD | Passage doesn't discuss education at all |
| "Passage says X company grew by 40%" — option says "X company grew by 40%" | TRUE | Direct match |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The CBD Trap:** Students frequently mark TRUE for statements that "sound logical" or "make common sense" but are NOT stated in the passage. CBD means the PASSAGE doesn't address it — not that it's false in real life. Conversely, students mark CBD for statements that the passage actually DOES contradict — they should be marked FALSE.

### 4-Step True/False/CBD Method

```
Step 1: Read the statement carefully
Step 2: Search the passage for information related to the statement
Step 3: Found? → Compare: Does it MATCH (TRUE) or CONTRADICT (FALSE)?
Step 4: Not found? → Mark as CANNOT BE DETERMINED
```

### Practice — True/False/CBD

## 2.11 Data-Based RC

### What It Is
Some passages, especially in newer placement tests, include a passage with **a table, graph, or chart embedded in it**. Questions test both reading comprehension AND data interpretation skills.

### Recognition
- Passage contains a table, bar chart, pie chart, or line graph
- Questions reference specific data points
- Questions may ask you to combine textual and data information

### Solving Method

```
Step 1: Understand what the table/chart MEASURES
         (X-axis, Y-axis, column headers, row headers)
Step 2: Identify the UNITS (%, millions, years, etc.)
Step 3: Read passage text for CONTEXT about the data
Step 4: Answer questions by COMBINING text + data
```

### Types of Data Questions

1. **Direct reading:** "According to the table, in which year was revenue highest?"
2. **Comparison:** "Which company showed the fastest growth rate?"
3. **Text + Data combination:** "According to both the passage and the table..."
4. **Inference from data:** "What can be concluded about the trend?"

> [!WARNING]
> **Data RC Trap:** The text says something different from the data — which do you trust? Answer: BOTH. The question will specify "according to the table" or "according to the passage." Read the question carefully.

## 2.12 Completion Questions

### What It Is
Completion questions ask you to choose the **best word, phrase, or sentence** to complete a blank within the passage — or to identify what would logically come next.

### Recognition
- "Which sentence best completes the passage?"
- "Which of the following would logically follow the last sentence?"
- "Choose the best phrase to fill in the blank in paragraph 2."
- "The author would most logically conclude the passage with..."

### Solving Method

```
Step 1: Read what comes BEFORE the blank
Step 2: Identify the DIRECTION the passage is going
        (Adding more evidence? Contrasting? Concluding?)
Step 3: Identify the TONE that matches the passage so far
Step 4: Choose the option that:
        ✅ Maintains the same tone
        ✅ Continues the logical direction
        ✅ Uses appropriate scope (not too broad, not too narrow)
        ✅ Is consistent with all previously stated facts
```

### Signal Words for Direction

| What comes before blank | What should follow |
|---|---|
| "However,..." + blank | Contrast or qualified statement |
| "Therefore,..." + blank | Conclusion or consequence |
| "For example,..." + blank | Specific instance |
| "In addition,..." + blank | Supporting point, same side |
| "Although X..." + blank | Counter-claim or balance |

> The concept of a circular economy — in which materials are reused, refurbished, and recycled in closed loops rather than discarded — has gained significant traction among European corporations and policymakers.
