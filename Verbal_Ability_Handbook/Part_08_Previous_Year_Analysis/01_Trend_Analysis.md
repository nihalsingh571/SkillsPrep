## 2. Year-Wise Difficulty Trend (Are Exams Getting Harder?)

Yes. The transition from offline campus drives to AI-proctored online assessments has significantly altered the difficulty curve.

- **2019-2020 (The Baseline):** Direct grammar questions. Simple vocabulary. Shorter RCs (200 words).
- **2021-2022 (The Shift):** Introduction of adaptive testing. Questions scale in difficulty. RCs became inference-based rather than direct fact-finding.
- **2023-2024 (The Current Era):** Heavy focus on contextual grammar. Pure rule-cramming fails. Questions involve multiple grammar errors in one sentence.
- **Prediction for 2025-2026:** AI-generated question banks mean zero exact repetitions from previous years. Increased focus on "Critical Reasoning" and "Tone/Attitude" in RCs. Expect fill-in-the-blanks with phrasal verbs rather than simple prepositions.

## 3. Top 20 Most Repeated Question Patterns

### Pattern 1: The "As Well As" Trap
- **Rule tested:** When two subjects are connected by *as well as, along with, together with*, the verb agrees with the FIRST subject.
- **Example:** The manager, as well as his team members, **is** (not are) going to the conference.
- **Frequency:** Extremely High (TCS, Accenture)

### Pattern 2: The "Neither/Nor" Proximity Rule
- **Rule tested:** The verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
- **Example:** Neither the students nor the teacher **was** (not were) present.

### Pattern 3: The "Since/For" Tense Clash
- **Rule tested:** Sentences with 'since' or 'for' indicating duration up to the present require the Present Perfect or Present Perfect Continuous tense.
- **Example:** I **have been working** (not am working) here since 2020.

### Pattern 4: The Fake Plural (Scenery/Furniture)
- **Rule tested:** Uncountable nouns take singular verbs and do not have plural forms with 's'.
- **Example:** The **scenery** (not sceneries) of Kashmir is beautiful.

### Pattern 5: The "Hardly...When" Inversion
- **Rule tested:** Sentences starting with 'Hardly/Scarcely' use inverted structure (verb before subject) and pair with 'when'.
- **Example:** Hardly **had I** reached the station **when** the train arrived.

### Pattern 6: The "One of the..." Plural Noun / Singular Verb
- **Rule tested:** "One of the" is followed by a plural noun but a singular verb.
- **Example:** One of the **boys is** missing.

### Pattern 7: Conditional Type 3 (Unreal Past)
- **Rule tested:** If + Past Perfect, Subject + would have + V3.
- **Example:** If she **had studied**, she **would have passed**.

### Pattern 8: Parallelism in Lists
- **Rule tested:** Items in a list must be in the same grammatical form.
- **Example:** He likes swimming, dancing, and **reading** (not to read).

### Pattern 9: Redundancy / Superfluous Errors
- **Rule tested:** Avoid using two words with the same meaning.
- **Example:** He **returned** (not returned back) the book.

### Pattern 10: "Lest...Should" Pairing
- **Rule tested:** 'Lest' is followed by 'should' and never takes a negative word like 'not'.
- **Example:** Walk carefully lest you **should fall**.

### Pattern 11: The "Number of" vs "A Number of"
- **Rule tested:** "A number of" = Plural verb. "The number of" = Singular verb.
- **Example:** A number of students **are** here. The number of students **is** fifty.

### Pattern 12: Superlative + Present Perfect
- **Rule tested:** Superlative degree adjectives are followed by the present perfect tense.
- **Example:** This is the best movie I **have ever seen**.

### Pattern 13: "Although/Though...Yet"
- **Rule tested:** Although is paired with yet or a comma, NEVER with 'but'.
- **Example:** Although he is rich, **yet** he is humble.

### Pattern 14: Order of Pronouns (231 Rule)
- **Rule tested:** For positive sentences, the order is Second, Third, First person.
- **Example:** You, he, and I are going to the party.

### Pattern 15: "Prefer...to"
- **Rule tested:** Prefer is followed by 'to', not 'than'.
- **Example:** I prefer coffee **to** tea.

### Pattern 16: "It is high time..."
- **Rule tested:** Followed by V2 (simple past).
- **Example:** It is high time we **started** studying.

### Pattern 17: Bare Infinitive after Modals/Let/Make
- **Rule tested:** Do not use 'to' after let, make, bid, etc.
- **Example:** Let him **go** (not to go).

### Pattern 18: Use of "Whose" vs "Which"
- **Rule tested:** Whose is for living things, which is for non-living.
- **Example:** The car **which** (not whose) color is red.

### Pattern 19: The "No sooner...than" Rule
- **Rule tested:** No sooner is followed by inverted verb and pairs with 'than'.
- **Example:** No sooner **did I** arrive **than** it started raining.

### Pattern 20: Preposition after specific verbs (e.g., Congratulate on)
- **Rule tested:** Fixed prepositions must be memorized.
- **Example:** I congratulate you **on** (not for) your success.

## 4. Company-Wise Recent Changes (2023-2025 Pattern Updates)

### TCS NQT (National Qualifier Test)
- **Old Pattern:** 24 questions, heavy on direct grammar.
- **New Pattern:** Merged into cognitive sections. Passages are longer (400+ words). Vocabulary is tested *contextually* within the RC rather than standalone synonyms.
- **Trap:** TCS uses a lot of "Double Fillers" where option A fits blank 1 but fails blank 2.

### Infosys
- **Old Pattern:** Standard 40 questions.
- **New Pattern:** Extremely heavy on **Critical Reasoning** (Syllogisms, Fact/Inference/Judgment, Statement-Assumption). The grammar questions are now primarily Error Detection with 5 segments (A, B, C, D, No Error).

### Accenture
- **Old Pattern:** Balanced paper.
- **New Pattern:** Highly grammar-centric. 20 questions in 20 minutes demands speed. Massive focus on sentence improvement and phrasal verbs. Zero RC in many recent slots!

### Cognizant GenC
- **Old Pattern:** standard verbal.
- **New Pattern:** Para jumbles are notoriously tricky (often 5-6 sentences). They test advanced vocabulary.

## 5. Technology Impact: How Online Exams Changed The Game

> [!WARNING]
> **Adaptive Testing Algorithms:** Platforms like CoCubes, AMCAT, and HirePro use IRT (Item Response Theory). If you answer an easy question correctly, the next question is harder (and worth more weight). If you answer wrong, the next is easier (worth less).
> **Conclusion:** Accuracy on the FIRST 5 questions is critical to getting into the high-score bracket.
