**Q1. (Error Detection) Identify the grammatical error in the sentence below:**

(A) Neither of the two candidates / (B) who applied for the job / (C) have the required qualifications. / (D) No Error.

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "Neither of" is followed by a plural noun ("the two candidates") but it always takes a SINGULAR verb. The subject of the sentence is "Neither" (not the candidates), which means "not one nor the other". Therefore, the plural verb "have" is incorrect and must be replaced with the singular verb "has".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Neither of the two candidates" is grammatically sound.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** The relative clause correctly modifies candidates.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** There is a blatant subject-verb agreement error.  

**📏 Rule:** Neither of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Neither/Either/Each/Every = ALWAYS SINGULAR VERB.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Capgemini

**Q2. (Vocabulary) Choose the word that best expresses the meaning of the given word: OBFUSCATE**

(A) Clarify  
(B) Confuse  
(C) Celebrate  
(D) Condemn  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** 'Obfuscate' means to make something obscure, unclear, or unintelligible. It is often used when a person is intentionally trying to hide the truth by making the explanation overly complex. Thus, 'Confuse' is the exact synonym.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** 'Clarify' is the direct antonym of obfuscate.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** 'Celebrate' has no relation.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** 'Condemn' means to express complete disapproval of.  

**📏 Rule:** Vocabulary - Synonyms.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Obfuscate sounds like "obstacle". Putting an obstacle in the way of understanding = to confuse.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, IBM  
---

**Q3. (Sentence Completion) Despite the heavy rain, the football match _____ as planned.**

(A) went over  
(B) went ahead  
(C) went off  
(D) went through  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The phrasal verb "went ahead" means to proceed or continue as planned, especially in spite of difficulties or opposition. Since the match proceeded despite the rain, this is the perfect fit.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Went over" means to review or examine something.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Went off" can mean to explode (like a bomb) or to ring (like an alarm).  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Went through" means to endure a difficult experience.  

**📏 Rule:** Phrasal Verbs Contextual Usage.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** To "go ahead" means to give the green light.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Accenture
