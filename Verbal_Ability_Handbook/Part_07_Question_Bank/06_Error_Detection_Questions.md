## 1. Find the Error (Q1 - Q250)

---
**Q1. Read the sentence and find which part contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, mark (D).**

(A) One of the biggest challenge / (B) facing the IT industry today / (C) is the shortage of skilled workers. / (D) No Error.

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "One of the..." must ALWAYS be followed by a PLURAL noun, because you are selecting "one" out of a "many". Therefore, it should be "One of the biggest challenges", not "challenge". The verb following the entire phrase must be singular ("is"), which is correct in part (C), but the noun immediately after "One of the" must be plural. 

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Part B is grammatically correct. "Facing" acts as a participle modifying the industry.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Part C is correct. The verb "is" correctly agrees with the singular subject "One".  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** There is a clear error in part A.  

**📏 Rule:** "One of the" + Plural Noun + Singular Verb.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** One of the boy = WRONG. One of the boys = RIGHT.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Capgemini  
---

**Q2. Read the sentence and find which part contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, mark (D).**

(A) Despite of his repeated failures, / (B) he never gave up / (C) on his dream to build a startup. / (D) No Error.

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** The word "Despite" is never followed by the preposition "of". The correct usages are either "Despite his repeated failures" OR "In spite of his repeated failures". Mixing the two forms ("Despite of") is a very common trap in competitive exams.

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "He never gave up" is grammatically flawless.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "On his dream to build" is idiomatically correct (give up on [something]).  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** There is a glaring prepositional error in part A.  

**📏 Rule:** Despite vs In Spite Of.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Despite = In spite of. "Despite of" is illegal in English.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Wipro

## 2. Sentence Improvement (Q251 - Q500)

---
**Q251. Improve the underlined part of the sentence. If no improvement is needed, select (D).**

I have been living in this city <ins>since five years</ins>.

(A) for five years  
(B) from five years  
(C) since five years ago  
(D) No improvement  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** The preposition "since" is used for a specific point in time (e.g., since 2018, since Monday). The preposition "for" is used to indicate a duration or length of time (e.g., for five years, for two months). Because "five years" is a duration, "for" is the correct preposition. 

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "From" is usually used with "to" (from 2018 to 2023) or for starting points in the future, not duration.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** While "since five years ago" is technically grammatical in rare contexts, "for five years" is the direct, concise, and standard replacement.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The original sentence uses 'since' incorrectly.  

**📏 Rule:** Since vs For.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Since = Starting Point (Calendar/Clock). For = Duration (Stopwatch).  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, IBM
