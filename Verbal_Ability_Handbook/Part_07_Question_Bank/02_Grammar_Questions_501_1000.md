## 1. Prepositions (Q501 - Q550)

---
**Q501. The candidate is entirely absorbed _____ reading the annual report.**

(A) in  
(B) with  
(C) at  
(D) into  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** The word 'absorbed' takes the fixed preposition 'in'. It means to have one's attention fully engaged or occupied by something. In English grammar, certain verbs and adjectives must always be followed by specific prepositions, regardless of logical translation from a native language. 

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** 'With' is incorrect; one can be 'busy with' but not 'absorbed with'.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** 'At' is used for point of time or specific locations (e.g., 'good at').  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** 'Into' denotes inward movement, not mental engagement.  

**📏 Rule:** Fixed Prepositions (Absorbed in).  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize standard pairs: Absorbed IN, Good AT, Congratulate ON.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant GenC, Infosys

## 2. Conjunctions (Q551 - Q600)

---
**Q551. No sooner had he entered the examination hall _____ the bell rang.**

(A) when  
(B) then  
(C) than  
(D) and  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The correlative conjunction 'No sooner' is ALWAYS followed by 'than'. This is a highly tested rule in placement exams. 'No sooner' denotes that one action happened immediately after another. Also, note the inversion ('had he' instead of 'he had') which is mandatory after 'No sooner' at the start of a sentence.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** 'When' is used with 'Hardly' or 'Scarcely' (e.g., Hardly had he entered when...).  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** 'Then' is an adverb of time, not a conjunction for comparison.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** 'And' cannot be paired with a comparative phrase like 'No sooner'.  

**📏 Rule:** No Sooner... Than pairing.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Sooner ends in -er (comparative), so it takes 'than' just like 'better than' or 'faster than'.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Wipro

## 3. Parallelism (Q651 - Q700)

---
**Q651. The manager asked the team to increase productivity, reduce errors, and _____ customer satisfaction.**

(A) improving  
(B) to improve  
(C) improve  
(D) improved  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The rule of Parallelism states that items in a list must be in the same grammatical form. The sentence lists three actions the team needs to do: (1) "to increase productivity", (2) "[to] reduce errors", and (3) "[to] improve customer satisfaction". Since "increase" and "reduce" are base verbs (infinitive without 'to'), the third verb must also be the base verb "improve".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** 'Improving' is a gerund/participle and breaks the parallel structure of base verbs.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** While technically an infinitive, the 'to' is factored out at the beginning ("to increase, reduce, and..."), making an extra 'to' redundant and awkward.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** 'Improved' is past tense, breaking the infinitive structure.  

**📏 Rule:** Rule of Parallel Structure in Lists.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Match the word endings! increase (V1) -> reduce (V1) -> improve (V1).  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Accenture
