## 1. Introduction: What is Narration?

Narration is the act of reporting someone's words.
- **Direct Speech:** Quoting the exact words spoken, enclosed in quotation marks (" ").
  *Example:* The manager said, "I am busy today."
- **Indirect (Reported) Speech:** Conveying the essence of what was said without quoting exactly, usually removing quotation marks and changing tenses/pronouns.
  *Example:* The manager said that he was busy that day.

**Two crucial parts of Direct Speech:**
1. **Reporting Verb:** The verb outside the quotes (e.g., *said, asked, told*).
2. **Reported Speech:** The exact words inside the quotes ("I am busy").

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** Narration tests your ability to juggle multiple grammatical rules simultaneously: Tense shifts, Pronoun shifts, and Time/Place word shifts. **TCS** and **Capgemini** frequently test interrogative and imperative narration.

---

## 2. Core Rule: The Tense Shift (Backshifting)

**Rule:** If the Reporting Verb is in the PAST tense (e.g., *said, told*), the tense of the Reported Speech must shift ONE STEP BACK into the past.
*(Note: If the Reporting Verb is Present (says) or Future (will say), the tense inside the quotes DOES NOT CHANGE).*

### 2.1 Tense Conversion Table

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense | Example Shift |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present (V1)** | **Simple Past (V2)** | "I write code" -> *wrote code* |
| **Present Continuous** | **Past Continuous** | "I am writing" -> *was writing* |
| **Present Perfect** | **Past Perfect** | "I have written" -> *had written* |
| **Simple Past (V2)** | **Past Perfect (had+V3)** | "I wrote code" -> *had written code* |
| **Past Continuous** | **Past Perfect Cont.** | "I was writing" -> *had been writing* |
| **Past Perfect** | **No Change** | "I had written" -> *had written* |

### 2.2 Modal Conversion Table

| Direct Modal | Indirect Modal |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Will / Shall** | **Would / Should** |
| **Can** | **Could** |
| **May** | **Might** |
| **Must / Have to** | **Had to** |
| Could, Would, Should, Might | **No Change** |

> [!WARNING]
> **Universal Truth Exception:** If the reported speech is a universal truth, scientific fact, or habit, the tense DOES NOT change, even if the reporting verb is in the past!
> *Wrong:* The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun.
> *Right:* The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.

## 3. Pronoun Shifts (SON Rule)

Pronouns change depending on the speaker and the listener.
**The SON Formula:**
- **S**ubject: First Person pronouns (I, we, my) change according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **O**bject: Second Person pronouns (you, your) change according to the **Object** (listener) of the reporting verb.
- **N**o Change: Third Person pronouns (he, she, it, they) remain **unchanged**.

*Example (1st Person):* **He** said, "**I** am tired." -> He said that **he** was tired.
*Example (2nd Person):* He said to **me**, "**You** are smart." -> He told me that **I** was smart.

---

## 4. Time and Place Expression Shifts

Words indicating nearness in time or place change to words indicating distance.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| Now | Then |
| Today | That day |
| Tonight | That night |
| Tomorrow | The next day / The following day |
| Yesterday | The previous day / The day before |
| Last week | The previous week |
| Next month | The following month |
| Here | There |
| This | That |
| These | Those |
| Ago | Before |

## 5. Converting Different Sentence Types

### 5.1 Assertive Sentences (Statements)
- Conjunction used: **that**
- "Said to" changes to: **told** (only if there is an object/listener). "Said" remains "said".
*Direct:* She said to me, "I will finish this tomorrow."
*Indirect:* She **told** me **that** she **would** finish **that** **the next day**.

### 5.2 Interrogative Sentences (Questions)
**Crucial Rule:** The sentence structure changes from a question (Verb-Subject) to a statement (Subject-Verb). The question mark is removed!
- Conjunction used: **if / whether** (for Yes/No questions). For "Wh-" questions, use the "Wh-" word itself as the conjunction.
- "Said" changes to: **asked, inquired, questioned**.
*Direct (Yes/No):* He asked me, "Are you coming?"
*Indirect:* He asked me **if I was** coming. *(Note: NOT 'if was I coming')*
*Direct (Wh-):* The manager asked, "Where is the file?"
*Indirect:* The manager asked **where the file was**. *(Note: NOT 'where was the file')*

### 5.3 Imperative Sentences (Commands/Requests)
- Conjunction used: **to + V1** (Infinitive). For negative commands, use **not to + V1**.
- "Said" changes to: **ordered, requested, advised, commanded, suggested**.
*Direct:* The boss said to him, "Deploy the code."
*Indirect:* The boss **ordered** him **to deploy** the code.
*Direct:* He said, "Please don't delete the database."
*Indirect:* He **requested** me **not to delete** the database.

> [!TIP]
> **"Let" sentences in Imperative:**
> 1. Meaning suggestion (Let's): He said, "Let's go." -> He **suggested that they should** go.
> 2. Meaning permission (Let me): He said, "Let me go." -> He requested **to let him** go (or requested that he might be allowed to go).

### 5.4 Exclamatory Sentences
- Conjunction used: **that**
- "Said" changes to: **exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise/anger**.
- Remove words like Alas, Hurrah, Wow.
*Direct:* "Alas! I have failed the test," she said.
*Indirect:* She **exclaimed with sorrow that she had failed** the test.

### 5.5 Optative Sentences (Wishes/Prayers)
- Conjunction used: **that**
- "Said" changes to: **wished, prayed, blessed**.
*Direct:* He said to me, "May you get the job!"
*Indirect:* He **wished that I might** get the job.

## 6. Key Takeaways Table

| Type | Reporting Verb | Conjunction | Core Rule Shift |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assertive** | said / told | that | Tense backshifts |
| **Interrogative (Yes/No)**| asked | if / whether | S+V order, no '?' |
| **Interrogative (Wh-)** | asked | (the Wh- word) | S+V order, no '?' |
| **Imperative** | ordered/requested | to / not to | Keep base V1 verb |
| **Exclamatory** | exclaimed with... | that | Drop 'Alas/Wow' |

## 7. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Change to Indirect: The developer said, "I have committed the code yesterday."**
(A) The developer said that he has committed the code yesterday.
(B) The developer said that he had committed the code the previous day.
(C) The developer said that I had committed the code the day before.
(D) The developer told that he had committed the code the previous day.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "I" changes to "he". "have committed" (Present Perfect) changes to "had committed" (Past Perfect). "yesterday" changes to "the previous day".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Tense and time word not changed.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Pronoun "I" not changed.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Told" requires an object (e.g., told me).
**📏 Rule:** Present Perfect -> Past Perfect. Yesterday -> Previous Day.
**⚡ Shortcut:** have -> had. yesterday -> previous day.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q2. Change to Indirect: She said, "The sun rises in the east."**
(A) She said that the sun rose in the east.
(B) She said that the sun rises in the east.
(C) She said that the sun has risen in the east.
(D) She said the sun would rise in the east.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a universal truth. The tense does not change, even though the reporting verb ("said") is in the past.
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** They incorrectly shift the tense.
**📏 Rule:** Universal Truths/Facts do NOT backshift.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Fact = Keep it Present.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Change to Indirect: He said to me, "What are you doing?"**
(A) He asked me what I was doing.
(B) He asked me what was I doing.
(C) He told me what I am doing.
(D) He asked me that what I was doing.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Question becomes a statement. "are you doing" changes to "I was doing" (Subject before Verb). Conjunction is the wh- word "what".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Incorrect word order (was I).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "told" is not for questions. Tense didn't shift.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Never use "that" and a "Wh-" word together.
**📏 Rule:** Wh- word + Subject + Verb. No "that".
**⚡ Shortcut:** In indirect questions, the helping verb comes AFTER the subject.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q4. Change to Indirect: The manager said to the team, "Do not log out before 6 PM."**
(A) The manager ordered the team that they should not log out before 6 PM.
(B) The manager ordered the team not to log out before 6 PM.
(C) The manager advised the team to not log out before 6 PM.
(D) The manager ordered the team do not log out before 6 PM.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Negative commands use "not to + V1". "Said to" becomes "ordered".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Uses "that" clause instead of infinitive.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Split infinitive ("to not" instead of "not to").
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Grammatically incorrect structure.
**📏 Rule:** Do not + V1 -> Not to + V1.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Do not -> Not to.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q5. Change to Indirect: "I will call you tomorrow," she told him.**
(A) She told him that she will call him tomorrow.
(B) She told him that she would call him the next day.
(C) She told him she would call him tomorrow.
(D) She asked him if she would call him the next day.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "will" changes to "would". "tomorrow" changes to "the next day". "I" to "she", "you" to "him".
**📏 Rule:** will -> would. tomorrow -> the next day.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Just check the modal and the time word!
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q6. Change to Indirect: He said, "Let's automate this process."**
(A) He proposed that they should automate that process.
(B) He ordered to automate this process.
(C) He suggested that let us automate that process.
(D) He said that they must automate this process.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Let's" indicates a suggestion or proposal. It converts to "suggested/proposed that they should". "this" changes to "that".
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Double phrasing ("suggested that let us" is redundant).
**📏 Rule:** Let's + V1 -> proposed that + they + should + V1.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Let's -> should.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q7. Change to Indirect: She says, "I am learning Python."**
(A) She says that she was learning Python.
(B) She said that she is learning Python.
(C) She says that she is learning Python.
(D) She told that she is learning Python.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The reporting verb "says" is in the Present tense. Therefore, the tense inside the quotes DOES NOT CHANGE. We just change the pronoun "I" to "she".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Shifted to past tense.
**📏 Rule:** Reporting verb in Present/Future = No tense backshift.
**⚡ Shortcut:** says/will say -> No change in verbs inside.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q8. Change to Indirect: The intern said, "Did the client approve the mockup?"**
(A) The intern asked if the client approved the mockup.
(B) The intern asked that if the client had approved the mockup.
(C) The intern asked if the client had approved the mockup.
(D) The intern asked did the client approve the mockup.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Did approve" is Simple Past. It must change to Past Perfect "had approved". Because it's a Yes/No question, use "if".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Tense did not shift (remained simple past).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "that if" is grammatically wrong (never combine them).
**📏 Rule:** Did (V2) -> had + V3.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Did = had.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q9. Change to Indirect: "Alas! The server has crashed again," the admin said.**
(A) The admin exclaimed with sorrow that the server had crashed again.
(B) The admin exclaimed with joy that the server had crashed again.
(C) The admin sadly said that Alas the server had crashed again.
(D) The admin exclaimed that the server has crashed again.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Alas!" indicates sorrow. "said" -> "exclaimed with sorrow". "has crashed" (Pres Perf) -> "had crashed" (Past Perf).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Never retain exclamatory words like "Alas" in indirect speech.
**📏 Rule:** Alas -> exclaimed with sorrow.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Match the emotion word and backshift the tense.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q10. Change to Indirect: My friend said to me, "Can you help me debug this code?"**
(A) My friend asked me if I could help him debug that code.
(B) My friend asked me that can I help him debug that code.
(C) My friend requested me if I could help him debug this code.
(D) My friend asked me if I can help him debug that code.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Can" -> "could". "this" -> "that". Interrogative structure changes to statement ("could help" instead of "can you").
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Failed to shift 'can' to 'could'.
**📏 Rule:** Can -> Could. This -> That.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Change modals immediately.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q11. Change to Indirect: The teacher said to the students, "Work hard."**
(A) The teacher told the students that they should work hard.
(B) The teacher advised the students to work hard.
(C) The teacher said the students to work hard.
(D) The teacher ordered the students work hard.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Work hard" is an imperative advice. "said to" becomes "advised". The conjunction is "to" before the verb.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "said" cannot be followed by an object without "to", and it's weak for an imperative.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Missing "to" before "work".
**📏 Rule:** Imperative Advice -> advised + to + V1.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Advice -> "to V1".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q12. Change to Indirect: She said, "I had finished my assignment before the deadline."**
(A) She said that she finished her assignment before the deadline.
(B) She said that she has finished her assignment before the deadline.
(C) She said that she had finished her assignment before the deadline.
(D) She told that she had finished her assignment before the deadline.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "had finished" is Past Perfect. Past Perfect does not change tense in reported speech. Pronoun "I" changes to "she".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Changed to Simple Past.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "told" needs an object.
**📏 Rule:** Past Perfect -> No Tense Change.
**⚡ Shortcut:** had + V3 = stays had + V3.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q13. Change to Direct: He asked me where I was going.**
(A) He said to me, "Where are you going?"
(B) He said to me, "Where I was going?"
(C) He asked me, "Where are you going?"
(D) He said to me, "Where were you going?"
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Reversing the process. "asked" reverts to "said to". "I was going" (Past Continuous statement) reverts to "are you going" (Present Continuous question).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** That would mean the indirect was "where I had been going".
**📏 Rule:** Reverse the backshift and restore question word order.
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Was going" -> "Are going". Restore the '?'.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q14. Change to Indirect: The old man said, "May God bless you with success!"**
(A) The old man wished that God might bless me with success.
(B) The old man prayed that God may bless me with success.
(C) The old man said that God will bless me with success.
(D) The old man blessed that God might bless you with success.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Optative sentence. "May" changes to "might". The sentence structure becomes a statement ("God might bless").
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Failed to shift 'may' to 'might'.
**📏 Rule:** May -> Might. Optative -> Statement structure.
**⚡ Shortcut:** May God -> God might.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q15. Change to Indirect: She said, "I must submit the report today."**
(A) She said that she must submit the report today.
(B) She said that she had to submit the report that day.
(C) She said that she has to submit the report that day.
(D) She told she must submit the report that day.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "must" expressing obligation often changes to "had to" in the past. "today" changes to "that day".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Time word not changed. "must" is technically acceptable if the obligation is permanent, but "had to" is preferred for past actions.
**📏 Rule:** Must -> Had to. Today -> That day.
**⚡ Shortcut:** must -> had to.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q16. Change to Indirect: "Don't touch that wire!" he shouted.**
(A) He shouted not to touch that wire.
(B) He shouted at me not to touch this wire.
(C) He shouted to not touch that wire.
(D) He forbade me to not touch that wire.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Negative imperative uses "not to".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Forbade" is already negative. "Forbade me to not touch" creates a double negative meaning "told me to touch".
**📏 Rule:** Don't -> not to. (Or use 'forbade' + to).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Don't -> Not to.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q17. Change to Indirect: The CEO said, "We have been monitoring the metrics since yesterday."**
(A) The CEO said that they have been monitoring the metrics since the previous day.
(B) The CEO said that they had been monitoring the metrics since yesterday.
(C) The CEO said that they had been monitoring the metrics since the previous day.
(D) The CEO said that we had been monitoring the metrics since the day before.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "We" -> "they". "have been" -> "had been". "yesterday" -> "the previous day".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Tense not changed.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Time word not changed.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "We" should change to "they".
**📏 Rule:** Present Perf Cont. -> Past Perf Cont.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Change have->had, yesterday->previous day.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q18. Change to Indirect: "Do you know the way to the station?" she asked.**
(A) She asked did I know the way to the station.
(B) She asked if I knew the way to the station.
(C) She asked that if I knew the way to the station.
(D) She asked if I know the way to the station.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Yes/No question uses "if". "Do know" (Simple Present) changes to "knew" (Simple Past).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Never use "that if".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Did not backshift tense.
**📏 Rule:** Do + V1 (Question) -> V2 (Statement).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Do you know -> if I knew.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q19. Change to Indirect: He said to me, "You should apply for this role."**
(A) He advised me that I should apply for that role.
(B) He advised me to apply for that role.
(C) He suggested me to apply for this role.
(D) Both A and B are correct
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** You can report advice using a "that" clause (that I should apply) or an infinitive phrase (to apply). "this" changes to "that".
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Suggest" cannot be followed by an indirect object without "to" (suggested to me).
**📏 Rule:** Should + V1 -> can be reported with 'advised + to V1' or 'advised that + should V1'.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Should -> advised to.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q20. Change to Indirect: She said, "I might go to Paris next year."**
(A) She said that she might go to Paris the following year.
(B) She said that she may go to Paris the following year.
(C) She said that she would go to Paris the following year.
(D) She said that she might have gone to Paris the following year.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The modal "might" does not change in indirect speech. "next year" changes to "the following year".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Downgrades "might" to "may".
**📏 Rule:** Could/Would/Should/Might remain unchanged.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Might stays might.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q21. Identify the error: He asked me that why I was late for the meeting.**
(A) He asked me
(B) that why
(C) I was late
(D) for the meeting
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** In indirect questions starting with a Wh- word, we never use the conjunction "that". It should be "He asked me why I was late."
**📏 Rule:** No "that" before Wh- words.
**⚡ Shortcut:** that + why/where/what = WRONG.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q22. Change to Direct: She told me that she had been reading that book for two hours.**
(A) She said to me, "I have been reading this book for two hours."
(B) She said to me, "I was reading this book for two hours."
(C) She told me, "I had been reading that book for two hours."
(D) She said to me, "She has been reading this book for two hours."
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Reversing: "told me" -> "said to me". "had been reading" (Past Perf Cont) -> "have been reading" (Pres Perf Cont). "that" -> "this".
**📏 Rule:** Past Perf Cont reverts to Present Perf Cont.
**⚡ Shortcut:** had been -> have been. that -> this.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

(C) He said that he had been going to start his own business.
(D) He said that I was going to start my own business.
**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📊 Difficulty:** Easy

**Q24. Change to Indirect: "Where do you live?" the interviewer asked.**
(A) The interviewer asked where did I live.
(B) The interviewer asked where I lived.
(C) The interviewer asked where do I live.
(D) The interviewer asked that where I lived.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "do live" (Simple Present) -> "lived" (Simple Past). Word order becomes statement (S+V).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Retains question word order ("did I live").
**📏 Rule:** Where + do + you + V1 -> Where + I + V2.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Drop the "do/does/did" completely and use V2!
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q25. Change to Indirect: She said, "If I had the money, I would buy a car."**
(A) She said that if she had the money, she would buy a car.
(B) She said that if she had had the money, she would have bought a car.
(C) She said that if she has the money, she will buy a car.
(D) She told that if she had the money, she would buy a car.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Type 2 Conditionals (unreal present, using V2 and would) do NOT change tenses in reported speech. If you shifted to Past Perfect (Option B), you would change the meaning to a Type 3 Conditional (unreal past).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Changes the conditional type.
**📏 Rule:** Unreal conditionals (Type 2 and 3) do not backshift in tense.
**⚡ Shortcut:** "If + V2 + would" stays exactly the same.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT
