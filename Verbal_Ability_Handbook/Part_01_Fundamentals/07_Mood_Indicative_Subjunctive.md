## 1. Introduction: What is "Mood" in Grammar?

In English grammar, "Mood" refers to the attitude the speaker has toward the action of the verb. It indicates whether the statement is a fact, a command, or a hypothetical/unreal situation.

There are three primary moods in English:
1. **Indicative Mood:** States facts or asks questions.
2. **Imperative Mood:** Gives commands or makes requests.
3. **Subjunctive Mood:** Expresses hypothetical, unreal, wishful, or demanded situations.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** Companies like **IBM, Accenture, and TCS NQT** use Subjunctive Mood questions to filter out average candidates. The rules of the subjunctive mood violate normal Subject-Verb Agreement, making it a perfect trap for error-spotting!

---

## 2. The Indicative and Imperative Moods

### 2.1 Indicative Mood
This is the "normal" mood you use 95% of the time. It states a fact, an opinion, or asks a question. Normal SVA rules apply.
*Example:* The server **is** down.
*Example:* The manager **wants** the report tomorrow.

### 2.2 Imperative Mood
Used to give commands or instructions. The subject "You" is usually hidden (implied). The verb is always in its base (V1) form.
*Example:* **Restart** the server immediately.
*Example:* Please **send** the report tomorrow.

---

## 3. The Subjunctive Mood: The Ultimate Exam Trap

The subjunctive mood deals with the unreal: wishes, hypotheses, suggestions, and demands. Because these events haven't happened (or are contrary to reality), normal grammar rules are deliberately broken to signal that the sentence is hypothetical.

### 3.1 Type 1: The "Wish" / "Unreal Past" Subjunctive
Used when expressing a wish, a regret, or an unreal condition (If).
**Rule:** For the "to be" verb, ALWAYS use **were** for ALL subjects (I, he, she, it, they). Never use "was". For other verbs, use the V2 (Simple Past) form to talk about present wishes.

**Triggers:** If, I wish, If only, As if, As though, Would rather.

*Wrong:* I wish I **was** the CEO of the company.
*Right:* I wish I **were** the CEO of the company. (I am not the CEO).

*Wrong:* He acts as if he **knows** everything. (If he actually doesn't).
*Right:* He acts as if he **knew** everything. (Unreal present = Past tense).

*Wrong:* She commands me as though she **was** my boss.
*Right:* She commands me as though she **were** my boss.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** If it's a fantasy, it gets "were". (Beyoncé trick: "If I *were* a boy").

### 3.2 Type 2: The "Bossy" / "Mandative" Subjunctive
This is the most highly tested subjunctive type! It is used to express demands, suggestions, requests, or necessities.
**Rule:** After certain verbs/adjectives followed by a "that" clause, the verb in the "that" clause MUST be in its absolute BASE FORM (V1) without any 's', 'es', or 'should'.

**Verb Triggers:** suggest, demand, recommend, insist, request, order, propose, ask, require, advise.
**Adjective Triggers:** It is crucial, essential, imperative, important, necessary, vital + that.

*Wrong:* The manager demanded that the developer **works** on the weekend.
*Right:* The manager demanded that the developer **work** on the weekend. (No 's'!)

*Wrong:* It is essential that the system **is** updated immediately.
*Right:* It is essential that the system **be** updated immediately. (Base form of 'is' = 'be').

*Wrong:* I suggested that he **should apply** for the job. (Grammatically okay in UK English, but strictly WRONG in US/Standard Placement exams).
*Right:* I suggested that he **apply** for the job.

> [!WARNING]
> **Accenture Trap:** "The doctor recommended that he rests for a week." (Incorrect). It must be "...that he **rest** for a week." Because it's a recommendation, we drop the 's' to show subjunctive mood.

### 3.3 Subjunctive Negative
To make a mandative subjunctive clause negative, simply put **"not"** before the base verb. Do NOT use "does not" or "do not".
*Wrong:* The client insisted that we **do not release** the feature yet.
*Right:* The client insisted that we **not release** the feature yet.

## 4. Key Takeaways Table

| Mood / Trigger | Rule / Formula | Example | Exam Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wishes / If only** | Always use **were** (not was) | *I wish he were here.* | Using "was" for he/she/it/I |
| **As if / As though**| Use Past Tense for Present | *He acts as if he owned it.* | Using Present Tense |
| **Demand / Suggest** | **Base Verb** (No 's', no 'should') | *I demand that he resign.* | Using "resigns" or "should resign" |
| **It is crucial that** | **Base Verb** (Use 'be', not is/are) | *It's vital that she be told.* | Using "is" |
| **Negative Bossy** | **Not + Base Verb** | *I suggest he not go.* | Using "does not go" |

## 5. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Identify the error: "The lead architect recommended that the new API is deployed by Friday."**
(A) recommended that
(B) the new API
(C) is deployed
(D) by Friday
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Recommended that" triggers the mandative subjunctive. The verb following it must be in the base form. The base form of "is" is "be". It should be "be deployed".
**❌ Why A, B, D are wrong:** These parts are structurally sound.
**📏 Rule:** Recommend that + Subject + Base Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Demand/Suggest + that -> use "be" instead of is/are/was.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q2. "I wish I _____ more time to complete this assessment."**
(A) have
(B) had
(C) have had
(D) was having
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "I wish" indicates an unreal present situation. We use the Simple Past (V2) to express this subjunctive wish.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Present tense (Indicative).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Present Perfect.
**📏 Rule:** I wish + Subject + V2 (Past Tense).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Wish = Backshift tense by one step.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Fill in the blank: "It is imperative that the server logs _____ checked daily."**
(A) are
(B) is
(C) be
(D) should be
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "It is imperative that" triggers the subjunctive mood. The verb must be the absolute base form, which is "be".
**❌ Why A & B are wrong:** Indicative forms.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Using "should" is redundant because "imperative" already implies obligation. US Standard English strictly prefers "be".
**📏 Rule:** Adjective of urgency + that + Base Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Crucial/Vital/Imperative that -> BE.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q4. "He speaks to me as though he _____ the CEO."**
(A) is
(B) was
(C) were
(D) has been
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "As though" introduces a hypothetical comparison. For the verb "to be" in hypotheticals, we always use "were" regardless of the subject.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** He is not the CEO (Indicative).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Never use "was" in the subjunctive mood.
**📏 Rule:** As if / As though + Subject + Were.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Fantasy = Were.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q5. Identify the error: "The client requested that we do not change the UI design."**
(A) requested that
(B) do not change
(C) the UI
(D) design
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Requested that" triggers the mandative subjunctive. To make the subjunctive negative, we just use "not + base verb", skipping "do/does/did". It should be "that we **not change**".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Structurally correct.
**📏 Rule:** Subjunctive Negative = Not + V1.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Drop the "do/does". Just use "not".
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q6. Which sentence is grammatically correct?**
(A) The manager insists that everyone comes on time.
(B) The manager insists that everyone come on time.
(C) The manager insists that everyone should come on time.
(D) The manager insists that everyone is coming on time.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Insists that" requires the base verb (come), even though the subject "everyone" is singular and would normally take "comes".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Uses indicative 'comes' (fails SVA rule for subjunctive).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Should" is generally avoided in strict subjunctive testing.
**📏 Rule:** Demand verbs + that + Subject + Base Verb (no 's').
**⚡ Shortcut:** Erase the 's' from the verb after demand verbs.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q7. "If I _____ a bird, I would fly."**
(A) am
(B) was
(C) were
(D) have been
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Classic conditional subjunctive. Expressing a physical impossibility. Always "were".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q8. What mood is the following sentence in? "Please submit your code by Friday."**
(A) Indicative
(B) Imperative
(C) Subjunctive
(D) Interrogative
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** It is a request/command directed at an implied "You".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q9. "I suggest he _____ his approach to the algorithm."**
(A) modify
(B) modifies
(C) modified
(D) has modified
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Suggest" (even without 'that', though it is implied) triggers the subjunctive. The verb must be base form "modify", avoiding the singular 's' (modifies).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Indicative form for "he".
**📏 Rule:** Suggest (that) + Subject + Base Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Treat "he" like "they" (plural) when it's subjunctive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q10. Identify the error: "It is crucial that she attends the meeting."**
(A) It is crucial
(B) that
(C) she attends
(D) the meeting
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Crucial that" requires subjunctive base verb. "attends" should be "attend".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q11. "He acts as if he _____ the lottery."**
(A) wins
(B) has won
(C) had won
(D) is winning
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "As if" followed by a past hypothetical event uses the Past Perfect (had won). If it were a present hypothetical ("as if he were rich"), it would use Simple Past.
**📏 Rule:** As if + Past Unreal Event = Past Perfect (had + V3).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Unreal Past -> Past Perfect.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q12. "The terms of the contract require that the vendor _____ a penalty for late delivery."**
(A) pays
(B) pay
(C) will pay
(D) must pay
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Require that" triggers subjunctive. "Vendor" (singular) takes the base verb "pay".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Indicative or modal forms.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q13. Choose the correct sentence:**
(A) I would rather you go now.
(B) I would rather you went now.
(C) I would rather you to go now.
(D) I would rather you will go now.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Would rather" followed by a subject ("you") triggers the past subjunctive to express a present preference. "went" is correct.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Uses present tense.
**📏 Rule:** Subject 1 + would rather + Subject 2 + V2 (Past tense).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Would rather + Person + Past Tense Verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q14. "It is important that the data _____ backed up daily."**
(A) is
(B) was
(C) be
(D) are
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Important that" + Base verb "be".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q15. "Long _____ the King!"**
(A) live
(B) lives
(C) lived
(D) living
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is an archaic form of the subjunctive used for wishes/blessings. Despite "King" being singular, it takes the base verb "live", not "lives".
**📏 Rule:** Wishes/Blessings use the base form (God bless you, Long live the King).

(A) If the manager
(B) was here
(C) he would resolve

**📖 Explanation:** "If" introducing an unreal condition requires "were", never "was".

**Q17. "The judge ordered that the prisoner _____ released."**
(A) is
(B) was
(C) be
(D) should be

**📊 Difficulty:** Medium

**Q18. "If only I _____ studied harder for the placement test!"**
(A) have
(B) had
(C) would have
(D) did
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "If only" expressing a regret about the past uses the Past Perfect (had + V3).
**📏 Rule:** If only + Past Regret = Past Perfect.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Regretting the past? Use "had".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q19. "The team proposed that the deadline _____ extended."**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) be
(D) to be
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Proposed that" triggers subjunctive. Base form of is/are = "be".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q20. What is wrong here? "My supervisor advised that I took a few days off."**
(A) advised that
(B) I
(C) took
(D) off
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Advised that" triggers subjunctive. The verb must be in its base form, regardless of the past tense of the main verb ("advised"). It should be "take".
**📏 Rule:** Even if the mandative verb is in the past (demanded, suggested), the subjunctive clause still uses the base verb (V1).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Mandative word + that = Always V1, ignoring time.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q21. "I wish I _____ the answer to that coding question."**
(A) know
