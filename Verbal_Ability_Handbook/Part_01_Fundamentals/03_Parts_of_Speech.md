## 1. Nouns: The Naming Words

A noun is a word used to name a person, place, thing, or abstract idea. In placement exams, nouns are mostly tested on countability (countable vs. uncountable) and collective noun agreement.

### 1.1 Types of Nouns
1. **Proper Nouns:** Specific names, always capitalized (e.g., *Google, Mr. Sharma, Python*).
2. **Common Nouns:** General names (e.g., *company, manager, language*).
3. **Collective Nouns:** Names of groups (e.g., *team, jury, committee*).
4. **Abstract Nouns:** Ideas or concepts (e.g., *integrity, happiness, information*).
5. **Material Nouns:** Substances (e.g., *gold, iron, water*).

### 1.2 Placement Rules for Nouns

#### Rule 1: Uncountable Nouns
Uncountable nouns cannot be made plural by adding 's'. They take singular verbs.
```rule
Incorrect: I have many informations for you.
Correct: I have a lot of information for you.
```
**Important Uncountable Nouns for Exams:** Scenery, Poetry, Furniture, Advice, Information, Hair, Luggage, Baggage, Mischief, Knowledge, Equipment, Evidence, Machinery, Stationery.

> [!WARNING]
> **TCS Trap:** "The equipments in the lab are faulty." (Wrong! 'Equipment' has no plural form).
> Correct: "The equipment in the lab is faulty."

#### Rule 2: Nouns Plural in Form but Singular in Meaning
Some nouns end in 's' but are singular. They take singular verbs.
*Examples:* Mathematics, Physics, News, Innings, Measles, Billiards.
*Example:* The **news is** good today.

#### Rule 3: Collective Nouns Agreement
Collective nouns take a singular verb if acting as a unit, but a plural verb if members act individually (divided).
*Unit (Singular):* The jury **has** given its verdict.
*Divided (Plural):* The jury **are** arguing among themselves.

## 2. Pronouns: The Replacements

A pronoun replaces a noun to avoid repetition. The noun it replaces is called its **antecedent**.

### 2.1 Types of Pronouns
1. **Personal:** I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
2. **Possessive:** Mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.
3. **Reflexive:** Myself, yourself, themselves (reflects back to subject).
4. **Demonstrative:** This, that, these, those.
5. **Indefinite:** Someone, anybody, everything, none, all, few.
6. **Relative:** Who, whom, whose, which, that (links clauses).
7. **Interrogative:** Who, what, where, why.

### 2.2 Placement Rules for Pronouns

#### Rule 1: Pronoun Order (231 Rule)
When singular pronouns of different persons are used together for a positive sentence, the order is Second (2), Third (3), First (1).
```rule
Order: You (2), He (3), and I (1)
```
*Example:* **You, he, and I** will attend the meeting.
*(Note: If confessing a fault/guilt, the order is 123: I, you, and he made the mistake).*

#### Rule 2: Objective Case after Prepositions
Pronouns following a preposition or the word "let" must be in the objective case (me, him, her, us, them).
*Wrong:* Between you and I...
*Right:* Between **you and me**, the project is failing. (Very common in Infosys exams!)
*Right:* Let **him and me** do the work.

#### Rule 3: Who vs. Whom
- **Who:** Subject (Replace with He/She/They)
- **Whom:** Object (Replace with Him/Her/Them)
*Trick:* The manager hired the applicant (who/whom) I recommended. -> I recommended *him*. -> Use **whom**.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick for Indefinite Pronouns:** Words ending in -one, -body, -thing (everyone, anybody, something) are ALWAYS singular and take singular verbs. "Everyone **is** here."

## 4. Verbs: The Action/State Words

Verbs express action (run, write) or a state of being (is, seems). They are the engine of the sentence.

### 4.1 Types of Verbs
1. **Action Verbs (Transitive):** Need a direct object (e.g., She *delivered* the presentation).
2. **Action Verbs (Intransitive):** Do not need a direct object (e.g., The system *crashed*).
3. **Linking Verbs:** Connect subject to a complement (e.g., He *is* angry).
4. **Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs:** Be, do, have.
5. **Modal Verbs:** Can, could, shall, should, will, would, may, might, must.

### 4.2 Placement Rules for Verbs

#### Rule 1: Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA) Core
Singular subjects take singular verbs (often ending in -s); Plural subjects take plural verbs.
*Example:* The list of candidates **is** long. (Subject = list).

#### Rule 2: Either/Or & Neither/Nor (Proximity Rule)
When subjects are joined by either/or, neither/nor, not only/but also, the verb agrees with the subject **closest** to it.
*Example:* Neither the manager nor the developers **were** available. (Verb 'were' agrees with plural 'developers').
*Example:* Neither the developers nor the manager **was** available. (Verb 'was' agrees with singular 'manager').

#### Rule 3: Together with / As well as
When subjects are joined by "as well as", "along with", "together with", the verb agrees with the **FIRST** subject.
*Example:* The CEO, as well as the board members, **has** arrived. ('has' agrees with CEO).

## 5. Adjectives: The Describers

Adjectives describe or modify nouns and pronouns. They answer: Which one? What kind? How many?

### 5.1 Degrees of Comparison
1. **Positive:** Tall, Beautiful
2. **Comparative:** Taller, More beautiful (comparing two)
3. **Superlative:** Tallest, Most beautiful (comparing three or more)

### 5.2 Placement Rules for Adjectives

#### Rule 1: Double Comparatives
Never use "more" or "most" with words that already end in -er or -est.
*Wrong:* This algorithm is more faster.
*Right:* This algorithm is **faster**.

#### Rule 2: Comparatives Ending in -ior
Adjectives ending in -ior (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior) take the preposition **to**, NOT **than**.
*Wrong:* He is senior than me.
*Right:* He is senior **to me**. (Extremely common in Accenture tests).

#### Rule 3: Few vs. Little
- **Few/A few/The few:** Used with countable nouns (books, errors).
- **Little/A little/The little:** Used with uncountable nouns (water, time, knowledge).
*Wrong:* We have little computers left.
*Right:* We have **few** computers left.

## 6. Adverbs: The Modifiers of Modifiers

Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They often answer: How? When? Where? Why? To what extent? (Usually end in -ly).

### 6.1 Placement Rules for Adverbs

#### Rule 1: Adjective vs. Adverb
Use an adverb, not an adjective, to modify a verb.
*Wrong:* The code executed perfect.
*Right:* The code executed **perfectly**.

#### Rule 2: "Hard" vs. "Hardly"
- **Hard:** Adverb meaning 'with great effort' (e.g., He worked hard).
- **Hardly:** Adverb meaning 'barely' or 'almost not' (e.g., I hardly worked).
*Wrong:* To pass the exam, you must study hardly.
*Right:* To pass the exam, you must study **hard**.

#### Rule 3: Placement of 'Only'
'Only' must be placed immediately before the word it modifies to avoid ambiguity.
*Meaning 1:* **Only I** debugged the code. (No one else did).
*Meaning 2:* I **only debugged** the code. (I didn't write it, just debugged it).

## 7. Prepositions: The Connectors of Position/Time

Prepositions show the relationship of a noun/pronoun to another word in the sentence (in, on, at, by, for, from, of, to).

### 7.1 Key Preposition Rules for Placement

#### Rule 1: At, On, In (Time)
- **At:** Specific times (at 3 PM, at midnight).
- **On:** Days and dates (on Monday, on 5th July).
- **In:** Months, years, seasons, long periods (in 2023, in summer).

#### Rule 2: Beside vs. Besides
- **Beside:** Next to. (He sat *beside* me).
- **Besides:** In addition to. (*Besides* Python, he knows Java).

#### Rule 3: Between vs. Among
- **Between:** Used for exactly TWO items/people.
- **Among:** Used for THREE or more items/people.
*Example:* The prize was distributed **among** the five teammates.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Fixed Prepositions:** Certain words ALWAYS take specific prepositions.
> Prevent **from**, Accused **of**, Capable **of**, Good **at**, Comply **with**, Refer **to**, Abstain **from**.
> Memorizing these is crucial for TCS and Wipro fill-in-the-blanks!

## 8. Conjunctions: The Joiners

Conjunctions join words, phrases, or clauses.

### 8.1 Types of Conjunctions
1. **Coordinating (FANBOYS):** For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So (join equals).
2. **Subordinating:** Because, although, if, since, while (join dependent to independent).
3. **Correlative:** Paired conjunctions (Either/or, Neither/nor, Not only/but also, Both/and).

### 8.2 Correlative Conjunction Rules

#### Rule 1: Strict Pairing
Do not mix and match correlative pairs.
*Wrong:* Scarcely had I reached **than** it rained.
*Right:* Scarcely had I reached **when** it rained. (Scarcely/Hardly pairs with When).

**Must-Know Pairs for Exams:**
- Either... **or**
- Neither... **nor**
- Not only... **but also**
- Both... **and**
- Though/Although... **yet** (or comma)
- No sooner... **than**
- Hardly/Scarcely... **when**
- Lest... **should**

---

## 9. Interjections: The Emotion Words

Words that express strong emotion or sudden feeling. Usually followed by an exclamation mark. Rarely tested in error spotting, but used in Direct/Indirect speech conversions.
*Examples:* Alas!, Hurrah!, Wow!, Oh!

## 10. Key Takeaways Table

| Part of Speech | Function | Top Placement Exam Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Noun** | Names | Uncountable nouns don't take plural 's' (e.g., *information*). |
| **Pronoun** | Replaces Noun | Use objective case after prepositions (*between you and me*). |
| **Verb** | Action/State | Proximate subject agreement for either/or (*neither he nor I am*). |
| **Adjective** | Describes Noun | -ior endings take *to* instead of *than* (*senior to*). |
| **Adverb** | Modifies Modifiers | *Hard* means effort; *Hardly* means barely. |
| **Preposition** | Position/Time | *Besides* = in addition to; *Beside* = next to. |
| **Conjunction** | Joins | *No sooner* pairs with *than*; *Hardly* with *when*. |

## 11. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Identify the error in the sentence: "The panel of judges have given a unanimous decision."**
(A) The panel
(B) of judges
(C) have given
(D) unanimous decision
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Panel" is a collective noun acting as a single unit (indicated by "unanimous"). Therefore, it must take a singular verb "has given".
**❌ Why A, B, D are wrong:** The error lies strictly in subject-verb agreement.
**📏 Rule:** Collective nouns acting as one unit take singular verbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Unanimous/together = Singular verb. Divided/arguing = Plural verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q2. "She runs faster than ____."**
(A) me
(B) I
(C) my
(D) myself
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is a comparison between two subjects. Expanding it reveals: "She runs faster than I (run)." Therefore, the subjective pronoun "I" is required.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Me" is objective. You wouldn't say "me run".
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Possessive and reflexive are contextually absurd.
**📏 Rule:** After 'than' or 'as' in a comparison of subjects, use the subjective case.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Mentally add the verb at the end. (than I am/do).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, Wipro

**Q3. Fill in the blank: "He is senior _____ me by two years."**
(A) than
(B) to
(C) of
(D) from
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Adjectives ending in "-ior" (senior, junior, superior, prior) are followed by the preposition "to", never "than".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Standard comparative error.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Incorrect prepositions.
**📏 Rule:** -ior comparatives take "to".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize: senior, junior, prior, superior, inferior -> TO.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q4. "Scarcely had the deployment finished _____ the server crashed."**
(A) than
(B) then
(C) when
(D) while
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The conjunction "scarcely" is part of a correlative pair that must always be completed with "when".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Than" pairs with "No sooner".
**❌ Why (B) & (D) are wrong:** Incorrect pairs.
**📏 Rule:** Scarcely/Hardly pairs with When. No sooner pairs with Than.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Hardly/Scarcely = When.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q5. Identify the error: "He gave me a lot of advices regarding my career."**
(A) He gave me
(B) a lot of
(C) advices regarding
(D) my career
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Advice" is an uncountable noun. It cannot take an 's' to become plural. It should be "a lot of advice" or "many pieces of advice".
**❌ Why A, B, D are wrong:** Grammatically correct parts of the sentence.
**📏 Rule:** Uncountable nouns (advice, information, furniture) have no plural form.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for 's' attached to abstract/material nouns.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q6. "Not only the software but also the hardware ____ outdated."**
(A) are
(B) is
(C) were
(D) have been
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** According to the rule of proximity, when subjects are joined by "not only...but also", the verb agrees with the closest subject. The closest subject is "the hardware" (singular). Therefore, "is".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** These are plural verbs.
**📏 Rule:** Not only/but also uses proximity SVA.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ignore the first subject. Match the verb to the subject immediately before it.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q7. What part of speech is the word "fast" in the following sentence: "He is a fast runner."**
(A) Noun
(B) Verb
(C) Adjective
(D) Adverb
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Fast" modifies the noun "runner," describing what kind of runner he is. Words that modify nouns are adjectives.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** If the sentence were "He runs fast," then "fast" would be an adverb modifying the verb "runs."
**📏 Rule:** Adjectives modify nouns; adverbs modify verbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Placed right before a noun? Adjective.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q8. Fill in the blank: "The manager, along with his team members, _____ working on the weekend."**
(A) are
(B) is
(C) have been
(D) were
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Phrases like "along with", "together with", and "as well as" do not make the subject plural. The verb must agree with the primary (first) subject, which is "manager" (singular).
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** These are plural verbs.
**📏 Rule:** SVA for "along with" ignores the nouns in the commas.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Cross out the phrase between commas. "The manager [X] is working."
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q9. "Between you and ____, I don't think the project will succeed."**
(A) I
(B) me
(C) mine
(D) myself
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Between" is a preposition. Pronouns following prepositions must be in the objective case. "Me" is the objective case of "I".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "I" is the subjective case.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Possessive/reflexive forms are incorrect here.
**📏 Rule:** Prepositions + Objective Pronoun.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never say "Between you and I". Always "Between you and me".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q10. Identify the error: "He worked very hardly to pass the placement exam."**
(A) He worked
(B) very hardly
(C) to pass
(D) the placement exam
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Hardly" means 'barely' or 'almost not'. The intended meaning is 'with great effort', which uses the adverb "hard".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** No errors here.
**📏 Rule:** Hard = effort. Hardly = barely.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you mean sweating and crying, use "hard".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q11. Which sentence is grammatically correct?**
(A) The committee are debating the issue among themselves.
(B) The committee is debating the issue among themselves.
(C) The committee are debating the issue among itself.
(D) The committee is debating the issue among itself.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Because the members are debating *among themselves*, the collective noun "committee" acts as divided individuals. Thus, it requires the plural verb "are" and the plural pronoun "themselves".
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** They incorrectly mix singular verbs/pronouns with plural actions (debating among).
**📏 Rule:** Divided collective nouns take plural verbs and plural pronouns.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for clues like "divided", "arguing", "among".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q12. Fill in the blanks: "He is good _____ Mathematics but weak _____ Physics."**
(A) in, at
(B) at, in
(C) at, at
(D) in, in
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The fixed preposition for being skilled is "good at". For being deficient in a subject, we use "weak in".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Incorrect fixed preposition pairings.
**📏 Rule:** Fixed prepositions: Good at / Bad at / Weak in.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize "Good AT, Weak IN".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q13. Identify the error: "This machinery is more heavier than that one."**
(A) This machinery
(B) is more heavier
(C) than
(D) that one
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Heavier" is already a comparative adjective. Adding "more" creates a double comparative, which is grammatically incorrect. It should be just "is heavier".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Machinery is singular (correct), 'than' is used correctly for comparison.
**📏 Rule:** Avoid double comparatives (more + -er).
**⚡ Shortcut:** If it ends in -er, never use 'more'.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q14. "____ little knowledge of programming that he possessed helped him clear the interview."**
(A) A
(B) The
(C) Very
(D) (No article)
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "The little" means 'not much, but all there is'. The specific knowledge he possessed is being referred to, so the definite article is required.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "A little" just means 'some', without the specificity implied by "that he possessed".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Little" means 'almost zero', which contradicts clearing the interview.
**📏 Rule:** Use 'The little' when referring to a specific, small amount that acts as a whole.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for a relative clause like "that he had/possessed". It demands "The".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q15. "I, you, and he must work together to complete this assignment." Identify the error.**
(A) Pronoun order should be You, he, and I
(B) 'must work' should be 'must works'
(C) 'this' should be 'these'
(D) No error
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** For positive or neutral statements, the pronoun order must be 231 (Second person, Third person, First person): You, he, and I.
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Plural modal verb is 'work'. 'Assignment' is singular, so 'this' is correct.
**📏 Rule:** 231 Pronoun Order for positive contexts.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Put yourself (I) last when taking credit, first (I) when taking blame (123).
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q16. "Walk carefully lest you ____ fall."**
(A) should
(B) would
(C) may
(D) can
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The conjunction "lest" (meaning 'to avoid the risk of') is always followed by the modal verb "should".
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Incorrect pairings.
**📏 Rule:** Lest ALWAYS pairs with should.
**⚡ Shortcut:** See "lest"? Blindly pick "should".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q17. What part of speech is "since" in: "I have not seen him since Monday."**
(A) Adverb
(B) Preposition
(C) Conjunction
(D) Adjective
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Here, "since" establishes a time relationship between the action and the noun "Monday". It acts as a preposition.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** If it were a conjunction, it would join two clauses (e.g., Since he left, I have been sad).
**📏 Rule:** Words can change part of speech based on function. Noun follower = preposition. Clause follower = conjunction.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Since + Noun = Preposition. Since + S+V = Conjunction.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q18. "She has only read three chapters." / "She has read only three chapters." Are these two sentences meaning the same thing?**
(A) Yes
(B) No, the placement of 'only' changes the meaning.
(C) Both are grammatically incorrect.
(D) 'Only' cannot be used with numbers.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Placement of "only" restricts the word it immediately precedes. "Only read" implies she didn't write or memorize them. "Only three chapters" implies she didn't read four or five.
**📏 Rule:** Adverb placement changes meaning. Place adverbs as close as possible to the word they modify.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find what "only" touches. That's what it limits.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q19. "The CEO abstained ____ voting on the new merger."**
(A) from
(B) in
(C) against
(D) for
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The verb "abstain" is followed by the fixed preposition "from".
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Incorrect prepositions.
**📏 Rule:** Fixed prepositions. Abstain/Refrain + from.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize: Prevent, Prohibit, Abstain, Refrain all take "from".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q20. "Neither of the two candidates ____ selected for the role."**
(A) were
(B) was
(C) have been
(D) are

**Q22. Choose the correct sentence:**
(A) One must do his duty.
(B) One must do one's duty.
(C) One must do their duty.
(D) One must do her duty.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** When "one" is used as an indefinite pronoun acting as a subject, its possessive form must be "one's".
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Pronoun mismatch. "His" is for "he", "their" for "they".
**📏 Rule:** The pronoun "One" must be followed by "One's" or "Oneself".
**⚡ Shortcut:** One -> One's.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium

**Q23. "I am looking forward ____ meeting you."**
(A) to
(B) for
(C) towards
(D) at
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrasal verb is "look forward to". Note that "to" here is a preposition, which is why it is followed by a gerund ("meeting"), not a base verb ("meet").
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Incorrect preposition.
**📏 Rule:** Look forward to + V-ing.
**⚡ Shortcut:** This is a classic trick. Don't pick "to meet"!
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard

**Q24. Identify the conjunction in the sentence: "I will go to the party provided that you come with me."**
(A) provided that
(B) to
(C) with
(D) will go
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Provided that" is a subordinating conjunction meaning "on the condition that". It joins the independent and dependent clauses.
**❌ Why B & C are wrong:** Prepositions.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Verb phrase.
**📏 Rule:** Complex conjunctions like 'provided that', 'as long as' act as single subordinating units.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Substitutable with "if"? It's a conjunction.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q25. "Every boy and every girl ____ given a certificate."**
(A) was
(B) were
(C) have been
(D) are
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** When two singular nouns joined by "and" are preceded by "each" or "every", the verb must be singular.
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Plural verbs.
**📏 Rule:** Every + Noun + And + Every + Noun = Singular Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** The word "Every" makes everything singular, no matter how many times it's repeated.
