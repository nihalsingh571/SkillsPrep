## 1. Introduction: The Anatomy of a Sentence

A sentence is the largest independent unit of grammar. It begins with a capital letter and ends with a period, question mark, or exclamation point. In placement exams like **TCS NQT** and **Accenture**, sentence structure forms the foundation of all error detection questions. If you cannot identify the true subject of a sentence, you cannot verify Subject-Verb Agreement.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** In exams, sentences are deliberately made complex by adding multiple modifiers, relative clauses, and prepositional phrases between the subject and the verb to confuse you. Mastery of sentence structure helps you "strip away" the fluff and find the core: Subject + Verb.

---

## 2. The Subject

The subject is the "who" or "what" that performs the action of the verb or is being described.

### 2.1 Types of Subjects

#### 1. Simple Subject
The core noun or pronoun that performs the action, without any of its modifiers.
```rule
Simple Subject = Just the noun/pronoun (e.g., 'dog', 'manager', 'they')
```
*Example:* The highly experienced **manager** resolved the issue.

#### 2. Complete Subject
The simple subject plus all its modifiers (adjectives, prepositional phrases, relative clauses).
```rule
Complete Subject = Simple Subject + Modifiers
```
*Example:* **The highly experienced manager with ten years of tenure** resolved the issue.

#### 3. Compound Subject
Two or more subjects joined by a conjunction (and, or, nor) that share the same verb.
```rule
Compound Subject = Subject 1 + Conjunction + Subject 2
```
*Example:* **The manager and the team lead** resolved the issue.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** To find the subject, find the verb first and ask "Who?" or "What?" before it.
> *Sentence:* Under the bridge lived an old troll.
> *Verb:* lived. *Who lived?* An old troll. *Subject:* troll (simple), an old troll (complete).

### 2.2 Company-Specific Traps: Subject Identification

**TCS Trap:** Hiding the subject behind prepositional phrases.
*Incorrect approach:* The box of chocolates are on the table. (Seeing 'chocolates' and using 'are')
*Correct approach:* The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table. ('box' is the subject)

**Infosys Trap:** Using appositives to confuse the subject.
*Example:* Mr. Sharma, the CEO of the newly merged companies, **has** arrived. (Subject is Mr. Sharma, not companies).

---

## 3. The Predicate

The predicate tells us what the subject does or is. It contains the verb and everything that follows it (objects, complements, modifiers).

### 3.1 Types of Predicates

#### 1. Simple Predicate
Just the main verb and its helping verbs.
*Example:* The dog **has been barking** all night.

#### 2. Complete Predicate
The verb plus all its modifiers, objects, and complements.
*Example:* The dog **has been barking all night at the mailman**.

#### 3. Compound Predicate
Two or more verbs sharing the same subject.
*Example:* The candidate **completed the assessment and submitted it online**.

---

## 4. Objects: Direct and Indirect

Objects receive the action of a transitive verb.

### 4.1 Direct Object (DO)
Receives the action directly. Answers the question "What?" or "Whom?" after the verb.
```rule
Subject + Action Verb + What/Whom = Direct Object
```
*Example:* The company offered **a bonus**. (Offered what? a bonus)

### 4.2 Indirect Object (IO)
Indicates to whom/for whom or to what/for what the action is performed. It always sits between the verb and the direct object.
```rule
Subject + Action Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object
```
*Example:* The company offered **the employees** a bonus. (Offered to whom? the employees)

> [!WARNING]
> If a preposition (to/for) is used, it is NO LONGER an indirect object; it becomes a prepositional phrase!
> *Example:* The company offered a bonus *to the employees*. (Here, 'employees' is the object of the preposition, not an indirect object).

---

## 5. Complements

A complement is necessary to complete the meaning of a sentence. Unlike objects, complements follow **linking verbs** (is, am, are, was, were, seem, become, feel, look) or describe the object.

### 5.1 Subject Complement
Renames or describes the subject.
- **Predicate Nominative (Noun):** The manager is a **leader**.
- **Predicate Adjective (Adjective):** The manager is **efficient**.

### 5.2 Object Complement
Renames or describes the direct object. Follows the direct object.
- *Example:* The board appointed him **CEO**. (CEO renames 'him')
- *Example:* The news made the team **happy**. (happy describes 'team')

| Feature | Object | Complement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Follows...** | Action Verbs | Linking Verbs (Subject) or Direct Objects (Object) |
| **Function** | Receives action | Renames or describes |
| **Identity** | Different from subject | Same as subject (SC) or object (OC) |

---

## 6. Modifiers and Appositives

### 6.1 Modifiers
Words, phrases, or clauses that provide extra information about other words.
- **Adjectives:** Modify nouns/pronouns. (The *diligent* student)
- **Adverbs:** Modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. (Worked *very quickly*)

> [!CAUTION]
> **Dangling Modifiers (Accenture Favorite!):** When a modifier doesn't logically attach to any word in the sentence.
> *Wrong:* Running for the bus, my wallet fell. (Did the wallet run for the bus?)
> *Right:* While I was running for the bus, my wallet fell.

### 6.2 Appositives
A noun or noun phrase placed next to another noun to rename or explain it.
- *Example:* My brother, **a software engineer**, works at Wipro.

---

## 7. Natural vs. Inverted Word Order

### 7.1 Natural Order
Subject comes before the verb. (S + V)
*Example:* The server crashed.

### 7.2 Inverted Order
Verb comes before the subject. Often used in questions, sentences starting with 'There' or 'Here', or for dramatic effect (starting with negative adverbs).
- **Questions:** Are **you** ready? (Verb: Are, Subject: you)
- **Expletives:** There is a **bug** in the code. (Verb: is, Subject: bug)
- **Negative Adverbs:** Rarely do **we** see such errors. (Verb: do see, Subject: we)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Rule of Inversion (IBM & Capgemini focus):** When a sentence starts with negative or limiting adverbs (Hardly, Scarcely, Seldom, Never, Barely, No sooner), the auxiliary verb MUST come before the subject.
> *Wrong:* Hardly I had reached the office when the meeting started.
> *Right:* Hardly **had I** reached the office when the meeting started.

## 8. Sentence Diagramming

Sentence diagramming is a visual way to map out the grammar of a sentence. It helps in understanding complex structures.

### ASCII Flowchart of Basic Structure
```
       Sentence
      /        \
  Subject     Predicate
    |           /    \
  Noun      Verb    Object/Complement
```

**Complex Example Visualization:**
"The brilliant engineer quickly solved the complex problem."
1. **Core:** Engineer (S) -> solved (V) -> problem (O)
2. **Modifiers:** The, brilliant -> Engineer
3. **Modifiers:** quickly -> solved
4. **Modifiers:** the, complex -> problem

## 10. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Identify the simple subject in the following sentence: "The updated list of selected candidates for the interview process has been published."**
(A) The updated list
(B) list
(C) candidates
(D) interview process
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The core noun performing the action is "list". "The updated" are modifiers, and "of selected candidates for the interview process" are prepositional phrases acting as modifiers.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** This is the complete subject, not the simple subject.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** These are objects of prepositions.
**📏 Rule:** Simple subject is the single core noun without modifiers.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Eliminate prepositional phrases starting with "of", "for", etc.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q2. In the sentence "The HR manager offered the new recruit a generous compensation package", what is "the new recruit"?**
(A) Direct Object
(B) Indirect Object
(C) Subject Complement
(D) Object Complement
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The action is "offered". What was offered? "a generous compensation package" (Direct Object). To whom was it offered? "the new recruit" (Indirect Object).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The direct object is the package.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** No linking verb is present, and it doesn't rename the object.
**📏 Rule:** Indirect object answers 'to whom' or 'for whom' the direct object is given.
**⚡ Shortcut:** IO almost always comes immediately before the DO.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Which of the following sentences contains a Subject Complement?**
(A) The developer wrote the script.
(B) The manager seems extremely frustrated today.
(C) She gave him the report.
(D) They elected her president.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Seems" is a linking verb. "Frustrated" describes the subject "manager". Therefore, "frustrated" is a subject complement (predicate adjective).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Script" is a direct object.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Him" is IO, "report" is DO.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "President" is an object complement, renaming "her" (DO).
**📏 Rule:** Subject complements follow linking verbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you can replace the verb with "=" and it makes sense (Manager = frustrated), it has a subject complement.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q4. Choose the grammatically correct sentence involving inversion:**
(A) Seldom he has seen such a catastrophic system failure.
(B) Seldom has he seen such a catastrophic system failure.
(C) Seldom he have seen such a catastrophic system failure.
(D) Seldom does he has seen such a catastrophic system failure.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** When a sentence begins with a negative adverb (seldom), the auxiliary verb (has) must precede the subject (he).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** No inversion applied.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** 'he' takes 'has', not 'have', plus no inversion.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Double auxiliary error ('does' and 'has seen').
**📏 Rule:** Negative Adverb + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Treat negative adverb openers like questions (Has he seen...?).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Capgemini

**Q5. Identify the Object Complement in this sentence: "The committee painted the new office bright blue."**
(A) committee
(B) painted
(C) office
(D) bright blue
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** "bright blue" describes the direct object "office". It tells us what the office became as a result of the action.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Subject.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Verb.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Direct object.
**📏 Rule:** Object complement follows and modifies/renames the direct object.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Verb + DO + Adjective/Noun describing DO.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q6. What is the grammatical error in the following sentence? "Looking at the dashboard, the metrics showed a sharp decline."**
(A) Incorrect tense usage
(B) Subject-verb disagreement
(C) Dangling modifier
(D) Misplaced appositive
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Looking at the dashboard" is a participial phrase modifying the subject. The subject here is "metrics". Can metrics look at a dashboard? No. This is a dangling modifier.
**❌ Why (A), (B), (D) are wrong:** Tense and agreement are fine. No appositive exists.
**📏 Rule:** A modifier must logically attach to the noun immediately following the comma.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ask "Who is doing the -ing action?" If it's not the subject after the comma, it's dangling.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Accenture

**Q7. Identify the compound predicate in the following sentence: "The software engineer debugged the code and pushed it to the repository."**
(A) The software engineer
(B) debugged the code
(C) pushed it to the repository
(D) debugged the code and pushed it to the repository
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** A compound predicate consists of two or more verb phrases connected by a conjunction, sharing the same subject. "debugged..." and "pushed..." both relate to the engineer.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Subject.
**❌ Why (B) & (C) are wrong:** Only partial predicates.
**📏 Rule:** Compound predicates use coordinating conjunctions to join multiple verbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for one subject performing multiple actions separated by "and".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q8. Which sentence has a natural word order?**
(A) Down the street rolled the ball.
(B) Rarely do I drink coffee.
(C) The agile team delivered the sprint on time.
(D) Had I known, I would have prepared.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Natural order is Subject + Verb + Object. "The agile team" (S) + "delivered" (V) + "the sprint" (O).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Prepositional phrase fronting (Inverted).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Negative adverb inversion.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Conditional inversion.
**📏 Rule:** Standard declarative sentences without stylistic fronting use natural order.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for a standard noun starting the sentence.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q9. Find the appositive in the sentence: "Elon Musk, the founder of SpaceX, announced a new mission."**
(A) Elon Musk
(B) the founder of SpaceX
(C) announced
(D) a new mission
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right beside it. "the founder of SpaceX" renames "Elon Musk".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Simple subject.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Verb and Direct Object.
**📏 Rule:** Appositives are usually enclosed in commas immediately following a noun.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you remove the phrase between commas and the sentence still makes perfect sense, it's likely an appositive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q10. In the sentence, "There are multiple solutions to this architectural problem," what is the subject?**
(A) There
(B) are
(C) multiple solutions
(D) problem
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "There" is an expletive (a dummy subject). In "there is/are" sentences, the true subject comes after the verb. The subject is "solutions".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Expletive/Adverb.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Verb.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Object of preposition "to".
**📏 Rule:** In "There is/are" constructions, the subject follows the verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Reverse it: "Multiple solutions are there..."
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Accenture

**Q11. Identify the sentence structure error: "Not only he failed the exam, but he also lost his scholarship."**
(A) "Not only" should be "Only not"
(B) Missing inversion after "Not only"
(C) "but also" should be "and"
(D) No error
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** When a sentence starts with the negative correlative conjunction "Not only", inversion is required. It should be "Not only **did he fail** the exam..."
**❌ Why (A) & (C) are wrong:** "Not only... but also" is the correct pair.
**📏 Rule:** Negative adverbial openers require auxiliary inversion.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Not only + Auxiliary + Subject + Main Verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q12. What function does the underlined word serve? The silence in the room became **oppressive**.**
(A) Direct Object
(B) Indirect Object
(C) Subject Complement
(D) Object Complement
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Became" is a linking verb. "Oppressive" is an adjective describing the subject "silence". Thus, it is a subject complement.
**❌ Why (A) & (B) are wrong:** No action verb present.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** No direct object present.
**📏 Rule:** Adjectives following linking verbs are subject complements.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Linking verbs (become, seem, feel, look) take complements, not objects.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q13. In which sentence is "running" a simple subject?**
(A) The running water is cold.
(B) He is running a marathon.
(C) Running is excellent for cardiovascular health.
(D) The boy, running quickly, tripped.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Here, "Running" is a gerund (a verb acting as a noun) and serves as the simple subject of the verb "is".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Adjective modifying water.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Part of the continuous verb phrase.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Part of a participial phrase modifier.
**📏 Rule:** Gerunds function as nouns and can be subjects.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If an -ing word can be replaced by "It" (It is excellent...), it's a noun.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q14. Identify the Direct Object: "The API provides developers real-time data."**
(A) API
(B) provides
(C) developers
(D) real-time data
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** The verb is "provides". What does it provide? "real-time data" (DO). To whom? "developers" (IO).
**❌ Why (A) & (B) are wrong:** Subject and Verb.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Indirect Object.
**📏 Rule:** DO receives the action; IO receives the DO.
**⚡ Shortcut:** S + V + IO + DO.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q15. Correct the dangling modifier: "To master Java, consistent practice is required."**
(A) To master Java, one requires consistent practice.
(B) Consistent practice is required to master Java.
(C) To master Java, you must practice consistently.
(D) Both (A) and (C)
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** The infinitive phrase "To master Java" implies a person doing the mastering. "Consistent practice" cannot master Java. Both A and C provide logical subjects ("one" and "you") immediately after the comma.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** While grammatically okay as a passive construct, A and C perfectly fix the modifier issue by providing an active subject.
**📏 Rule:** The subject following an introductory modifying phrase must be the doer of the action in the phrase.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ensure the noun after the comma can actually "do" the intro phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q16. Find the complete subject: "The rapid development of artificial intelligence has sparked ethical debates."**
(A) The rapid development
(B) artificial intelligence
(C) The rapid development of artificial intelligence
(D) ethical debates
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The complete subject includes the simple subject "development" and all its modifiers, including the prepositional phrase "of artificial intelligence".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Misses the prepositional modifier.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Object of preposition.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Direct object.
**📏 Rule:** Complete subject = Simple subject + all its modifiers before the verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Take everything before the main verb ("has sparked").
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q17. Which of the following uses an Object Complement?**
(A) The judge found the defendant guilty.
(B) The judge found the missing evidence.
(C) The judge was very strict.
(D) The judge gave him a warning.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Found" is the verb, "defendant" is the DO. "Guilty" is an adjective describing the defendant, making it an object complement.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Only a DO ("evidence").
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Subject Complement ("strict").
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** IO ("him") and DO ("warning").
**📏 Rule:** Verbs like make, find, elect, call, paint often take object complements.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Does the last word describe the object before it? Yes -> OC.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q18. What is wrong with this sentence? "Under the desk is lying the missing documents."**
(A) "Under" should be "Beneath"
(B) "is" should be "are"
(C) "lying" should be "laying"
(D) Word order is totally illegal
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is an inverted sentence due to prepositional fronting. The true subject is "the missing documents" (plural). Therefore, the verb must be plural: "are lying".
**❌ Why (A) & (C) are wrong:** Word choice is acceptable; 'lying' is correct for intransitive rest.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Inversion for location is legal.
**📏 Rule:** In inverted sentences, locate the subject after the verb to check S-V agreement.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Un-invert it: "The missing documents are lying under the desk."
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q19. Identify the simple predicate: "The deployment script will be executed at midnight."**
(A) executed
(B) will be
(C) will be executed
(D) will be executed at midnight
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The simple predicate is the verb phrase alone, including auxiliary verbs.
**❌ Why (A) & (B) are wrong:** Incomplete verb phrases.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** This is the complete predicate.
**📏 Rule:** Simple predicate = main verb + helping verbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Strip away prepositional phrases and adverbs from the predicate.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q20. "Hardly had the server booted when it crashed again." What grammatical concept justifies the word order in the first clause?**
(A) Apposition
(B) Subject Complement
(C) Inversion
(D) Subjunctive Mood
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Beginning with a negative adverb ("Hardly") triggers subject-auxiliary inversion ("had the server" instead of "the server had").
**❌ Why (A), (B), (D) are wrong:** Irrelevant concepts.
**📏 Rule:** Negative adverbs at the start of a sentence require inversion.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Hardly/Scarcely/No sooner -> Inversion.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q21. Determine the structure: "He baked his mother a cake."**
(A) S + V + DO
(B) S + V + IO + DO
(C) S + V + DO + OC
(D) S + V + SC
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** S = He, V = baked, IO = his mother (for whom it was baked), DO = a cake (what was baked).
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Structure identifies dual objects.
**📏 Rule:** S + V + Whom + What.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Two nouns after an action verb usually mean IO + DO.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q22. Choose the correct sentence:**
(A) The new CEO, a former naval officer, have strong discipline.
(B) The new CEO a former naval officer has strong discipline.
(C) The new CEO, a former naval officer, has strong discipline.
(D) The new CEO; a former naval officer, has strong discipline.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The appositive "a former naval officer" must be set off by commas. The singular subject "CEO" correctly takes the singular verb "has".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Plural verb "have".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Missing commas.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Semicolon used incorrectly.
**📏 Rule:** Non-essential appositives are framed by commas.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Read the sentence without the part in commas to test verb agreement.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q23. Identify the grammatical function of the bolded part: "The code compiled **without any errors**."**
(A) Noun phrase acting as subject
(B) Prepositional phrase acting as an adverb modifier
(C) Prepositional phrase acting as an adjective modifier
(D) Direct Object
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Without any errors" modifies the verb "compiled" by telling us *how* it compiled. Therefore, it acts adverbially.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** It doesn't modify a noun.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Prepositional phrases are not direct objects.
**📏 Rule:** A prepositional phrase modifying a verb is adverbial.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Answers "How?", "When?", "Where?" -> Adverbial.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q24. In the sentence, "No sooner did the bell ring than the students rushed out," why is 'did' used?**
(A) Past tense of 'do' for emphasis
(B) To form a question
(C) Required for subject-verb inversion due to negative opener
(D) It is an error; it should be 'had'
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "No sooner" is a negative phrase at the beginning of a sentence. It requires inversion. Since the main verb is "ring" (base form), we use the auxiliary "did" (past tense) to create the inversion. (Note: "No sooner had the bell rung" is also correct).
**❌ Why (A) & (B) are wrong:** Not for emphasis or question.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** 'did' + V1 is perfectly valid alongside 'had' + V3.
**📏 Rule:** Inversion with simple past uses 'did' + subject + base verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** No sooner + did/had + Subject.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q25. Which is the complete predicate in "The exhausted developers staring at the screen finally deployed the application"?**
(A) finally deployed the application
(B) deployed the application
(C) staring at the screen finally deployed the application
(D) The exhausted developers staring at the screen
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The complete subject is "The exhausted developers staring at the screen". The complete predicate is everything else: the verb "deployed", its adverb "finally", and the object "the application".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Misses the adverb.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Includes a participial phrase modifying the subject.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** This is the complete subject.
**📏 Rule:** Split the sentence exactly before the main verb. Everything after is the complete predicate.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find main verb -> everything from there to the period.
