## 1. Introduction to Sentence Classification

Sentences can be classified in two ways:
1. **By Structure:** How many and what types of clauses they contain (Simple, Compound, Complex, Compound-Complex).
2. **By Function:** What the sentence is trying to do (Declarative, Interrogative, Imperative, Exclamatory).

In placement exams, particularly for **TCS** and **Cognizant**, you are often asked to transform a simple sentence into a complex one, or to identify the structural type of a given sentence.

---

## 2. Classification by Structure

Structure is determined by the number of **Independent Clauses (IC)** and **Dependent Clauses (DC)**.

### 2.1 Simple Sentence
Contains exactly ONE Independent Clause and NO Dependent Clauses. It may have compound subjects or compound verbs, but only one full clause structure.
```rule
Simple Sentence = 1 IC + 0 DC
```
*Example 1:* The developer deployed the code. (Basic)
*Example 2:* **The developer and the tester** deployed and reviewed the code. (Compound subject and verb, but still ONE clause).

### 2.2 Compound Sentence
Contains TWO or more Independent Clauses joined by a Coordinating Conjunction (FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So) or a semicolon.
```rule
Compound Sentence = IC + (FANBOYS / ;) + IC
```
*Example:* The server crashed, **but** the backup system immediately kicked in.
*(IC 1: The server crashed. IC 2: The backup system kicked in.)*

### 2.3 Complex Sentence
Contains ONE Independent Clause and ONE OR MORE Dependent Clauses. Joined by Subordinating Conjunctions (Because, Although, If, When, That, Who).
```rule
Complex Sentence = 1 IC + 1 (or more) DC
```
*Example:* **Although the server crashed (DC)**, the backup system immediately kicked in (IC).

> [!CAUTION]
> **Placement Trap:** Don't assume a long sentence is complex. "In the middle of the night, during the heavy storm, the exhausted developer finally fixed the legacy code." -> This is a **Simple Sentence**. It has many prepositional phrases but only one Subject-Verb pairing (developer - fixed).

### 2.4 Compound-Complex Sentence
Contains TWO or more Independent Clauses and AT LEAST ONE Dependent Clause.
```rule
Compound-Complex = 2+ IC + 1+ DC
```
*Example:* **Because the timeline was tight (DC)**, the team worked weekends (IC), **and** the client was highly impressed (IC).

## 3. Classification by Function

### 3.1 Declarative Sentence
Makes a statement or expresses an opinion. Ends with a period.
*Example:* Python is a highly versatile language.

### 3.2 Interrogative Sentence
Asks a direct question. Ends with a question mark. Often uses inverted word order.
*Example:* Did you commit the changes to the repository?

### 3.3 Imperative Sentence
Gives a command, request, or direction. The subject is usually an implied "You".
*Example:* (You) Please restart the server.

### 3.4 Exclamatory Sentence
Expresses sudden or strong emotion. Ends with an exclamation mark.
*Example:* What a catastrophic failure this is!

## 4. Sentence Transformations (Crucial for Exams)

Companies like **Wipro** and **Capgemini** frequently test your ability to convert sentences from one structural type to another without changing the meaning.

### 4.1 Simple to Compound
**Method:** Expand a word or phrase into an independent clause and use a FANBOYS conjunction.
- *Simple:* Seeing the error, he stopped the execution.
- *Compound:* He saw the error, **and** he stopped the execution.
- *Simple:* Besides being a developer, she is a designer.
- *Compound:* She is not only a developer, **but** she is **also** a designer.

### 4.2 Simple to Complex
**Method:** Expand a phrase into a dependent clause using a subordinator.
- *Simple:* On seeing the police, the thief ran away.
- *Complex:* **When** the thief saw the police, he ran away.
- *Simple:* Due to illness, he missed the meeting.
- *Complex:* **Because** he was ill, he missed the meeting.

### 4.3 Compound to Complex
**Method:** Change one of the independent clauses into a dependent clause.
- *Compound:* Do your best, **and** you will succeed.
- *Complex:* **If** you do your best, you will succeed.
- *Compound:* He is poor, **but** he is honest.
- *Complex:* **Although** he is poor, he is honest.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick for Conversions:**
> In spite of / Despite (Simple) -> But / Yet (Compound) -> Although / Though (Complex)
> Due to / Because of (Simple) -> So / Therefore (Compound) -> As / Because / Since (Complex)

## 6. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Identify the sentence structure: "Despite the heavy rain, the delivery executive completed all his orders on time."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Despite the heavy rain" is a prepositional phrase, not a clause (no verb). There is only one subject-verb pair ("executive completed"). Therefore, it is a simple sentence.
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** They require multiple clauses.
**📏 Rule:** 1 IC + 0 DC = Simple Sentence.
**⚡ Shortcut:** No subordinating or coordinating conjunctions joining verbs = Simple.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q2. Which conjunction makes this a compound sentence? "The system was outdated, ____ we decided to migrate to the cloud."**
(A) because
(B) so
(C) although
(D) since
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "So" is a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) that connects two independent clauses.
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** These are subordinating conjunctions, which would create a complex sentence.
**📏 Rule:** FANBOYS create compound sentences.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So).
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Transform into a complex sentence: "He worked hard to pass the exam."**
(A) He worked hard and he passed the exam.
(B) He worked hard so that he might pass the exam.
(C) Working hard, he passed the exam.
(D) He worked hard, therefore he passed the exam.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "so that he might pass the exam" is a dependent adverb clause. This makes the sentence complex.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Compound sentence (uses 'and').
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Simple sentence (uses participial phrase).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Compound sentence (uses 'therefore' / independent clauses).
**📏 Rule:** Complex sentences require a subordinating conjunction (so that, because, if).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Infinitive of purpose ("to pass") converts to "so that".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q4. Identify the sentence type: "Tell me where you have hidden the files."**
(A) Declarative
(B) Interrogative
(C) Imperative
(D) Exclamatory
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence gives a command. The implied subject is "You" (You tell me). Even though it contains a question word ("where"), it doesn't end in a question mark or ask a direct question.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** It is an indirect question acting as a command.
**📏 Rule:** Commands/requests with an implied "You" subject are imperative.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Verb at the very beginning of the sentence usually = Imperative.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q5. Choose the correct compound-complex sentence.**
(A) If you finish the code, you can go home.
(B) The code was finished, and the developer went home.
(C) After the developer finished the code, he went home, and he slept for ten hours.
(D) The developer went home after finishing the code and sleeping.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Contains one dependent clause ("After the developer finished the code") and two independent clauses ("he went home", "and he slept...").
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Complex (1 DC + 1 IC).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Compound (2 IC).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Simple (1 IC with prepositional phrases).
**📏 Rule:** Comp-Complex = 2 ICs + 1 DC.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for BOTH a FANBOYS and a subordinator (After + and).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q6. Transform to a simple sentence: "As he was ill, he didn't attend the office."**
(A) He was ill, so he didn't attend the office.
(B) Because of his illness, he didn't attend the office.
(C) He was ill and didn't attend the office.
(D) He didn't attend the office because he was ill.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Because of his illness" is a prepositional phrase, reducing the dependent clause into a phrase. This leaves only one independent clause.
**❌ Why A & C are wrong:** Compound sentences.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Complex sentence.
**📏 Rule:** Convert DC to a phrase using "due to", "because of", "on account of".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Subordinator -> Preposition.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q7. "What a brilliant piece of software this is!" - This sentence is:**
(A) Declarative
(B) Interrogative
(C) Imperative
(D) Exclamatory
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Expresses strong emotion and ends with an exclamation point.
**📏 Rule:** 'What' or 'How' followed by an adjective/noun and ending in '!' is exclamatory.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for the exclamation mark and emotion.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q8. Convert this compound sentence to complex: "Search his pockets and you will find the USB drive."**
(A) Search his pockets to find the USB drive.
(B) If you search his pockets, you will find the USB drive.
(C) Searching his pockets will reveal the USB drive.
(D) You will find the USB drive by searching his pockets.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "If" introduces a dependent clause, creating a complex sentence.
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** These are all simple sentences using phrases.
**📏 Rule:** Imperative + 'and' + outcome = If condition (Complex).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Command + And = If. (Eat well and you will be healthy -> If you eat well...).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q9. Identify the structure: "The manager, who is known for his strictness, approved my leave; however, he warned me not to extend it."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** "who is known for his strictness" (DC). "The manager approved my leave" (IC). "he warned me not to extend it" (IC). 2 ICs + 1 DC = Compound-Complex.
**❌ Why A, B, C are wrong:** Matches the Comp-Complex formula.
**📏 Rule:** Semicolons join ICs. Relative pronouns create DCs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Relative Clause (who) + Semicolon (;) = Comp-Complex.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q10. Transform into a compound sentence: "Besides being intelligent, she is hardworking."**
(A) She is intelligent and hardworking.
(B) She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.
(C) As she is intelligent, she is hardworking.
(D) Both (A) and (B)
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Both A and B use coordinating structures (and / not only...but also) to join two independent ideas. (Note: A is a simple sentence with a compound subject complement, but B is a full compound structure. However, in transformation MCQs, both are accepted as compound logic. Technically B is the purest compound transformation: She is not only intelligent, but she is also hardworking).
**✅ Standard Exam Answer:** (B) is the preferred transformation for "Besides".
*(Self-Correction: Let's assume the exam strictly wants (B). Let's mark B).*
**✅ Actual Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Not only... but also" joins two independent clauses (often with ellipted verbs).
**📏 Rule:** "Besides + V-ing" -> "Not only... but also".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Besides = Not only... but also.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q11. Identify the structure: "I will call you when I reach the station."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "I will call you" (IC). "when I reach the station" (DC). 1 IC + 1 DC = Complex.
**📏 Rule:** 'When' is a subordinating conjunction.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Time subordinators (when, before, after, until) always make complex sentences.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q12. Which of the following is a simple sentence?**
(A) The sky grew dark, and the wind began to howl.
(B) Feeling tired from the long journey, they decided to rest.
(C) I don't know why she left so early.
(D) The movie that we watched yesterday was boring.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Feeling tired from the long journey" is a participial phrase. There is only one subject-verb pair ("they decided").
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Compound (and).
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Complex (why, that).
**📏 Rule:** A sentence with verbals (participles, gerunds, infinitives) but only one true finite verb is a simple sentence.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for -ing openers with a comma. They are usually simple sentences.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q13. Transform to complex: "In spite of his wealth, he is unhappy."**
(A) He is wealthy, but he is unhappy.
(B) He is wealthy, yet he is unhappy.
(C) Though he is wealthy, he is unhappy.
(D) Being wealthy, he is unhappy.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Though" creates a dependent clause, making it a complex sentence.
**❌ Why A & B are wrong:** Compound (but, yet).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Simple (Being = participle).
**📏 Rule:** In spite of / Despite -> Though / Although.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Despite = Although.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q14. "Have you finished the report?" is an example of:**
(A) Declarative
(B) Interrogative
(C) Imperative
(D) Exclamatory
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Asks a question, features subject-auxiliary inversion (Have you), and ends with a question mark.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q15. Convert to simple: "I was surprised when I heard the news."**
(A) I was surprised to hear the news.
(B) Hearing the news, I was surprised.
(C) The news surprised me.
(D) All of the above
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** All three options reduce the dependent clause ("when I heard") into a phrase (infinitive, participial, or noun phrase) while keeping only one independent clause.
**📏 Rule:** Dependent clauses can be reduced to infinitive, participial, or prepositional phrases to form simple sentences.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Any option with only ONE main verb works.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q16. Identify the structure: "He must apologize, or he will be fired."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Two independent clauses joined by the coordinating conjunction "or".
**📏 Rule:** 'Or' is a FANBOYS conjunction.
**⚡ Shortcut:** IC + , or + IC = Compound.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q17. Transform to complex: "He must apologize, or he will be fired."**
(A) If he apologizes, he will not be fired.
(B) Unless he apologizes, he will be fired.
(C) He will be fired because he did not apologize.
(D) Both (A) and (B) are logically and structurally correct.
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Both A and B use conditional subordinators (If, Unless) to create a dependent clause, perfectly preserving the original meaning.
**📏 Rule:** "Do X, or Y happens" -> "If you don't do X / Unless you do X, Y happens."
**⚡ Shortcut:** 'Or' (Compound) -> 'Unless/If' (Complex).
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q18. "May you have a successful career!" - Sentence type?**
(A) Declarative
(B) Interrogative
(C) Imperative
(D) Optative (Exclamatory)
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Sentences expressing a wish, prayer, or curse are often called Optative sentences (a sub-category of Exclamatory).
**📏 Rule:** "May" + Subject + Verb + ! = Optative/Exclamatory.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Wishes = Exclamatory/Optative.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q19. Which subordinator is used to show contrast?**
(A) Because
(B) Therefore
(C) Although
(D) Since
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Although" shows concession or contrast. "Because" and "Since" show reason. "Therefore" is a conjunctive adverb, not a subordinator.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q20. Identify the structure: "Let me know if you need any help."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Let me know" is an imperative independent clause. "if you need any help" is a dependent adverb clause.
**📏 Rule:** Imperative clause + Subordinate clause = Complex.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Imperative + If = Complex.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q21. Transform to compound: "Seeing the tiger, he climbed a tree."**
(A) He saw the tiger and climbed a tree.
(B) When he saw the tiger, he climbed a tree.
(C) He climbed a tree because he saw the tiger.
(D) Having seen the tiger, he climbed a tree.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "and" connects the two independent actions logically.
**❌ Why B & C are wrong:** Complex.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Simple.
**📏 Rule:** Participle phrase -> IC + and + IC.
**⚡ Shortcut:** V-ing -> and.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q22. Choose the complex sentence.**
(A) The sun set, and the stars appeared.
(B) As the sun set, the stars appeared.
(C) The sun having set, the stars appeared.
(D) The setting of the sun brought out the stars.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "As" is a subordinating conjunction making the first clause dependent.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Compound.
**❌ Why C & D are wrong:** Simple.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q23. Identify the structure: "The code compiled, but the application crashed because there was a runtime error."**
(A) Simple
(B) Compound
(C) Complex
(D) Compound-Complex
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** "The code compiled" (IC), "but" (coordinator), "the application crashed" (IC), "because there was a runtime error" (DC). 2 ICs + 1 DC = Compound-Complex.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q24. What is the function of "therefore" in a sentence?**
(A) Coordinating Conjunction
(B) Subordinating Conjunction
(C) Conjunctive Adverb
(D) Preposition
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Words like therefore, however, moreover are conjunctive adverbs. They require a semicolon before them when joining two independent clauses (e.g., I was tired; therefore, I slept). They do NOT create complex sentences; they create compound sentences.
**📏 Rule:** IC ; conjunctive adverb , IC = Compound Sentence.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Semicolon + However/Therefore = Compound.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q25. Transform to simple: "He is so weak that he cannot walk."**
(A) He is very weak, so he cannot walk.
(B) Because he is weak, he cannot walk.
(C) He is too weak to walk.
(D) His weakness prevents his walking.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Too... to" is the standard transformation for "so... that... not" into a simple sentence. It reduces the dependent clause into an infinitive phrase.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Compound.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Complex.
**📏 Rule:** So + adj + that + neg clause -> Too + adj + to + V1.
**⚡ Shortcut:** So...that...not = Too...to.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT
