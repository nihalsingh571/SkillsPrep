## 1. Introduction: From Words to Sentences

In Chapter 3, we looked at individual words (Parts of Speech). Now, we zoom out to see how words group together.
- A **Phrase** is a group of words without a subject-verb pairing.
- A **Clause** is a group of words with both a subject and a verb.
Understanding the difference is critical for identifying sentence fragments, run-on sentences, and modifier errors—frequent traps in placement exams.

---

## 2. Phrases

A phrase functions as a single part of speech in a sentence. It does not contain a subject performing a verb.

### 2.1 Types of Phrases

#### 1. Noun Phrase
Consists of a noun and all its modifiers. It functions as a subject, object, or complement.
*Example:* **The highly skilled software developer** fixed the bug. (Subject)

#### 2. Verb Phrase
Consists of the main verb and any helping (auxiliary) verbs.
*Example:* The server **has been running** smoothly.

#### 3. Adjective Phrase
A group of words that describe a noun or pronoun.
*Example:* The manager is **extremely happy with the results**.

#### 4. Adverb Phrase
A group of words functioning as an adverb, answering how, when, where, or why.
*Example:* The code compiled **surprisingly quickly**.

#### 5. Prepositional Phrase
Begins with a preposition and ends with a noun or pronoun (the object of the preposition). It can function as an adjective or an adverb.
*Example (Adjective):* The book **on the table** is mine. (Which book? The one on the table).
*Example (Adverb):* The team met **in the conference room**. (Where did they meet? In the room).

> [!WARNING]
> **TCS Error Detection Trap:** Prepositional phrases placed between the subject and the verb often cause Subject-Verb Agreement errors.
> *Wrong:* The list of selected candidates are published.
> *Right:* The **list** [of selected candidates] **is** published. (Ignore the phrase!).

#### 6. Verbal Phrases (Participial, Gerund, Infinitive)
Verbal phrases look like verbs but function as other parts of speech.

**A. Participial Phrase (Acts as an Adjective)**
Begins with a present participle (-ing) or past participle (-ed/-en).
*Example:* **Exhausted from the hackathon**, the student slept for twelve hours. (Modifies "student").
> [!CAUTION]
> **Dangling Participles (Accenture Favorite!):** "Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful." (Did the trees walk? No. This is a dangling modifier). Correct: "Walking down the street, I saw the beautiful trees."

**B. Gerund Phrase (Acts as a Noun)**
Begins with an -ing word functioning as a noun.
*Example:* **Debugging legacy code** is very tedious. (Acts as the subject of "is").

**C. Infinitive Phrase (Acts as Noun, Adjective, or Adverb)**
Begins with "to + base verb".
*Example:* The team's goal is **to launch the app by Friday**. (Noun phrase, subject complement).

## 3. Clauses

A clause contains a subject and a predicate (a verb). It is the building block of complete sentences.

### 3.1 Independent Clause (Main Clause)
Can stand alone as a complete sentence. It expresses a complete thought.
*Example:* The company hired new freshers. (S = company, V = hired).

### 3.2 Dependent Clause (Subordinate Clause)
Contains a subject and a verb but does NOT express a complete thought. It begins with a subordinating word (because, if, when, who, that, etc.) and must be attached to an independent clause.
*Example:* **Because the company expanded**, they hired new freshers.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** If you walk up to a stranger and say a dependent clause ("Because it is raining"), they will stare at you waiting for the rest of the sentence. If you say an independent clause ("I brought an umbrella"), they will understand perfectly.

---

## 4. Types of Dependent Clauses

Dependent clauses function as single parts of speech.

### 4.1 Noun Clause
Functions as a noun (subject, object, or complement). Usually begins with that, how, what, who, or why.
*Example (Subject):* **What the client requested** is impossible. (Instead of "The request is impossible").
*Example (Object):* I know **that the server is down**.

### 4.2 Adjective Clause (Relative Clause)
Functions as an adjective, modifying a noun or pronoun. It always immediately follows the noun it modifies. Starts with relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that).
*Example:* The laptop **that I bought yesterday** is already broken.

#### 🌟 Essential Rule: That vs. Which (IBM & Wipro Focus)
This is one of the most highly tested concepts in placement exams!
- **That (Restrictive/Essential):** Use "that" when the clause is essential to the meaning of the sentence. Do NOT use commas.
  *Example:* The bugs **that are marked critical** must be fixed today. (Not all bugs, only the critical ones).
- **Which (Non-restrictive/Non-essential):** Use "which" when the clause provides extra, non-essential information. MUST use commas.
  *Example:* The login module, **which I wrote last week**, has a critical bug. (The fact that I wrote it is extra info).

### 4.3 Adverb Clause
Functions as an adverb, modifying a verb, adjective, or other adverb. Answers when, where, why, how, to what extent, or under what condition. Begins with subordinating conjunctions (because, although, if, when, while).
*Example:* The system will shut down **if the temperature exceeds the limit**.

## 5. Key Takeaways Table

| Concept | Structure | Function | Placement Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | Prep + Noun | Adjective or Adverb | S-V Agreement distraction |
| **Participial Phrase** | -ing / -ed verb | Adjective | Dangling modifiers |
| **Gerund Phrase** | -ing verb | Noun (Subj/Obj) | Confusing with continuous verbs |
| **Independent Clause**| S + V (Complete thought)| Sentence | Comma splices |
| **Adjective Clause** | Relative Pronoun + S + V | Adjective | 'That' vs 'Which' rule |

## 6. Practice Questions & Solutions

**Q1. Identify the bolded part: "The candidate, **having cleared all interview rounds**, was given the offer letter."**
(A) Gerund Phrase
(B) Infinitive Phrase
(C) Participial Phrase
(D) Noun Phrase
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Having cleared" is a perfect participle. The phrase acts as an adjective modifying the noun "candidate".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Gerunds act as nouns; this acts as a modifier.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** No "to + verb".
**📏 Rule:** Participial phrases begin with participles (-ing, -ed, having + V3) and modify nouns.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ask "Does this phrase describe the noun next to it?" Yes = Participial phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q2. Which of the following sentences correctly uses "that" or "which"?**
(A) The servers that are located in London need maintenance.
(B) The servers which are located in London need maintenance.
(C) My primary laptop that I bought in 2020 just crashed.
(D) The new iPhone, that was released yesterday, is expensive.
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause "that are located in London" restricts the meaning to specific servers. It is essential, so "that" without commas is correct.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Which" without commas is generally frowned upon in strict grammar testing (though common in UK English, placement exams prefer US strict rules).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "My primary laptop" is already specific. The clause is non-essential and should use ", which".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "That" can never be used in a comma-separated non-essential clause.
**📏 Rule:** Essential = that (no commas). Non-essential = which (commas).
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you can delete the clause and the core meaning remains, use ", which ,".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Wipro

**Q3. Identify the noun clause: "The manager announced that the project deadline has been extended."**
(A) The manager announced
(B) that the project deadline has been extended
(C) the project deadline
(D) has been extended
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause acts as the direct object of the verb "announced". What did the manager announce? [That the project deadline has been extended].
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Independent clause.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Only parts of the dependent clause.
**📏 Rule:** Noun clauses can be replaced by "it" or "something". (The manager announced *it*).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Substitute the clause with "IT". If the sentence makes sense, it's a noun clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q4. Find the dependent clause: "Unless the client approves the budget, we cannot start the development phase."**
(A) we cannot start the development phase
(B) Unless the client approves the budget
(C) the client approves
(D) start the development phase
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Unless" is a subordinating conjunction making the first clause dependent. It cannot stand alone as a complete thought.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** This is the independent main clause.
**❌ Why (C) & (D) are wrong:** Incomplete fragments.
**📏 Rule:** A dependent clause starts with a subordinator (unless, if, because, although).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for subordinating words. Whatever follows them until the comma is the dependent clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q5. Identify the error: "Running toward the finish line, the trophy was dropped by the athlete."**
(A) Running toward
(B) the finish line
(C) the trophy was dropped
(D) by the athlete
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a dangling modifier. The phrase "Running toward the finish line" modifies the noun immediately following the comma, which is "trophy". Did the trophy run? No. It should be "...the athlete dropped the trophy."
**❌ Why A, B, D are wrong:** The error is in the core structure (passive voice causing the modifier to dangle).
**📏 Rule:** Participial phrases at the beginning of a sentence must logically modify the grammatical subject of the main clause.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Make sure the person doing the -ing action is sitting right after the comma.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q6. Identify the prepositional phrase: "The file under the desk contains confidential data."**
(A) The file
(B) under the desk
(C) contains confidential data
(D) confidential data
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Under" is a preposition, and "the desk" is its object. It acts adjectivally to describe "file".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Noun phrase.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Verb phrase / Predicate.
**📏 Rule:** Preposition + Noun/Pronoun = Prepositional Phrase.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for position/time words (in, on, at, under, behind).
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q7. "To succeed in the placement drive requires intense preparation." What is the function of the bolded part?**
(A) Verb Phrase
(B) Infinitive Phrase acting as subject
(C) Prepositional Phrase acting as adverb
(D) Gerund Phrase
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "To succeed" is an infinitive. The whole phrase acts as a noun, serving as the subject of the verb "requires".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Not the main verb.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Not a preposition (to + verb = infinitive, to + noun = preposition).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Gerunds end in -ing.
**📏 Rule:** Infinitives (to + V1) can act as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If it's answering "What requires intense preparation?", it's a noun phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q8. Identify the clause type: "Wherever you go, I will follow you."**
(A) Adjective Clause
(B) Adverb Clause
(C) Noun Clause
(D) Independent Clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Wherever you go" modifies the verb "will follow" by answering the question "where?". Thus, it is an adverb clause.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Doesn't modify a noun.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Doesn't act as a subject/object.
**📏 Rule:** Adverb clauses answer where, when, why, how, and under what condition.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Clauses starting with wherever, whenever, because, if, although are adverbial.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q9. Select the sentence with an Adjective Clause.**
(A) She told me that she was leaving.
(B) The car that he bought is very fast.
(C) Because it rained, the match was cancelled.
(D) Walking in the park is my favorite hobby.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "that he bought" describes the noun "car". Therefore, it acts as an adjective.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Noun clause (Direct Object).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Adverb clause.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Gerund phrase.
**📏 Rule:** Adjective clauses begin with relative pronouns and immediately follow the noun they modify.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Noun + Who/Which/That clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q10. What is wrong with this sentence? "The company implemented the new policy, it caused much confusion."**
(A) Dangling modifier
(B) Run-on sentence (Comma Splice)
(C) Fragment
(D) Misplaced clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** This sentence contains two independent clauses joined only by a comma. This is a comma splice, a type of run-on sentence. It needs a conjunction (..., and it caused...) or a semicolon (..., policy; it caused...).
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** Not modifier issues, both are complete clauses.
**📏 Rule:** You cannot join two independent clauses with just a comma.
**⚡ Shortcut:** S+V , S+V = ILLEGAL. Need FANBOYS or a semicolon.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q11. Identify the phrase type: "The boy **in the blue shirt** is the team lead."**
(A) Adjective Phrase (Prepositional)
(B) Adverb Phrase
(C) Noun Phrase
(D) Verb Phrase
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "In the blue shirt" is a prepositional phrase. Since it describes the noun "boy" (answering "which boy?"), it functions as an adjective phrase.
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** It modifies a noun, so it cannot be adverbial, noun, or verb phrase.
**📏 Rule:** Prepositional phrases following a noun usually act as adjectives.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Answers "Which one?" -> Adjective Phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q12. Which sentence contains a restrictive (essential) clause?**
(A) Mr. Smith, who is our manager, approved the leave.
(B) The Python script that automates the backup failed tonight.
(C) Delhi, which is the capital of India, is highly populated.
(D) My father, who retired last year, loves gardening.
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "that automates the backup" specifies *which* Python script failed. It is essential to the sentence's meaning and lacks commas.
**❌ Why A, C, D are wrong:** All these use commas to provide non-essential (extra) information about already specific nouns.
**📏 Rule:** No commas + "that" = Restrictive Clause.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If removing the clause makes you ask "Wait, which one?", it's restrictive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q13. "Whoever finishes the test first will get a reward." The bolded part is a:**
(A) Adverb Clause
(B) Noun Clause
(C) Adjective Clause
(D) Independent Clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Whoever finishes the test first" acts as the subject of the verb "will get". A clause acting as a subject is a noun clause.
**❌ Why A & C are wrong:** Does not modify a verb or a noun.
**📏 Rule:** Clauses starting with 'whoever', 'whatever', 'that' acting as subjects are Noun Clauses.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Replace with "The person". If it works, it's a noun clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q14. Find the gerund phrase: "Avoiding syntax errors requires a lot of focus."**
(A) Avoiding syntax errors
(B) requires a lot
(C) of focus
(D) requires a lot of focus
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Avoiding syntax errors" begins with the gerund "Avoiding" and acts as the subject of the sentence.
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** These contain the verb and prepositional phrase.
**📏 Rule:** -ing phrase acting as a noun = Gerund Phrase.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you can swap it with the word "Coding", it's a gerund phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q15. Correct the sentence: "The recruiter interviewed the candidate with a smile."**
(A) The recruiter with a smile interviewed the candidate.
(B) Smiling, the recruiter interviewed the candidate.
(C) The recruiter interviewed the smiling candidate.
(D) Context is ambiguous; it depends on who is smiling.
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a misplaced modifier. Does "with a smile" modify the recruiter or the candidate? Because prepositional phrases usually modify the noun immediately preceding them, strictly speaking, the candidate has the smile. But it could mean the recruiter was smiling. Options B and C resolve the ambiguity depending on the intended meaning.
**📏 Rule:** Place modifying phrases as close as possible to the word they modify.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If it's funny/weird, the modifier is misplaced.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q16. What is the dependent clause in: "Although the server crashed, no data was lost."**
(A) Although the server crashed
(B) the server crashed
(C) no data was lost
(D) was lost
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause begins with the subordinating conjunction "Although", making it dependent.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** This is the independent main clause.
**📏 Rule:** Dependent clause = Subordinator + Subject + Verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Grab the joining word + everything up to the comma.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q17. Which is an independent clause?**
(A) Because she worked late
(B) After the meeting concluded
(C) The system updated successfully
(D) Which caused a massive outage
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Subject "The system" + Verb "updated". It expresses a complete thought and can stand alone.
**❌ Why A, B, D are wrong:** All begin with subordinating words (Because, After, Which) making them dependent.
**📏 Rule:** Complete thought = Independent.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Read it aloud. If it feels finished, it's independent.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

**Q18. Identify the clause: "She asked me **why I was late**."**
(A) Noun Clause
(B) Adjective Clause
(C) Adverb Clause
(D) Prepositional Phrase
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause acts as the direct object of the verb "asked" (She asked me *what*? -> why I was late). Thus, it is a noun clause.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Doesn't modify 'me'.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Doesn't answer adverbial questions about the verb.
**📏 Rule:** "wh-" clauses following verbs of asking/telling are usually noun clauses.
**⚡ Shortcut:** She asked me "IT". Fits perfectly = Noun Clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q19. "Fascinated by the presentation, the investors agreed to fund the startup." The bolded part is:**
(A) A dependent clause
(B) A participial phrase
(C) A gerund phrase
(D) An independent clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Fascinated" is a past participle. The phrase acts as an adjective describing "the investors". There is no subject in the phrase, so it is not a clause.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Lacks a subject.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Gerunds end in -ing and act as nouns.
**📏 Rule:** Verb-ed/-en phrase at start modifying subject = Participial Phrase.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Starts with an 'ed' word and followed by a comma? Participial phrase.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q20. Combine correctly: (1) The code was messy. (2) It compiled successfully.**
(A) The code was messy, it compiled successfully.
(B) The code was messy; but it compiled successfully.
(C) Although the code was messy, it compiled successfully.
(D) The code was messy because it compiled successfully.
**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** C correctly uses a subordinating conjunction to combine the clauses logically without creating a run-on.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Comma splice.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Do not use both a semicolon and "but" (unless separating items in a complex list). Semicolon replaces "but".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Illogical meaning.
**📏 Rule:** Join clauses with FANBOYS, semicolons, or subordinators.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for the most logical relationship. Contrast = Although.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q21. "The manager gave the employees **who met their targets** a bonus." The bolded clause is:**
(A) Non-restrictive adjective clause
(B) Restrictive adjective clause
(C) Noun clause
(D) Adverb clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause specifies *which* employees got the bonus (not all of them). Because it restricts the meaning and has no commas, it is a restrictive adjective clause.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Non-restrictive clauses have commas.
**📏 Rule:** Essential info = Restrictive.
**⚡ Shortcut:** No commas = Restrictive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

**Q22. Choose the correct option to fix the fragment: "Because the server crashed at midnight."**
(A) Because the server crashed at midnight, so we lost data.
(B) Because the server crashed at midnight. We lost data.
(C) The server crashed at midnight, because we lost data.
(D) Because the server crashed at midnight, we lost data.
**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** The original is a dependent clause fragment. It must be attached to an independent clause ("we lost data") with a comma.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Redundant conjunctions ("Because" + "so").
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Still leaves a fragment.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Illogical meaning.
**📏 Rule:** Dependent Clause + , + Independent Clause.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never use "Because" and "So" in the same sentence structure.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q23. "I am not sure **where the error originated**."**
(A) Noun Clause
(B) Adjective Clause
(C) Adverb Clause
(D) Infinitive Phrase
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The clause acts as the object of the adjective phrase "sure of/about". It replaces the noun object (I am not sure of *it*).
**❌ Why B & C are wrong:** Not modifying a noun or answering adverbial conditions.
**📏 Rule:** Clauses following "sure", "certain", "know", "wonder" are usually noun clauses.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Replace with "IT".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q24. Identify the error: "He is the candidate which I think should be hired."**
(A) which
(B) I think
(C) should be
(D) hired
**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Which" is used for things and animals. For people, use "who" or "whom". Here, "who" is correct because it acts as the subject of "should be hired".
**❌ Why B, C, D are wrong:** Grammatically correct context.
**📏 Rule:** Who/Whom = People. Which = Things. That = People or Things.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Person -> Who. Thing -> Which.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q25. "The company's primary objective, **to increase market share**, has been achieved." The bolded part is:**
(A) Noun clause
(B) Appositive phrase (Infinitive)
(C) Prepositional phrase
(D) Adjective clause
**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "to increase market share" is an infinitive phrase acting as a noun. It is placed immediately after "objective" to rename it, functioning as an appositive.
**❌ Why (A) & (D) are wrong:** Not a clause (no subject).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "To + verb" is an infinitive, not a preposition.
**📏 Rule:** Infinitive phrases can act as appositives renaming a noun.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Renames the word right before it? Appositive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** IBM
