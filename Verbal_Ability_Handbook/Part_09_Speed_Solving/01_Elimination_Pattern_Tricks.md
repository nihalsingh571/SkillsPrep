# PART 9: Speed Solving & Elimination Techniques

## Overview
Placement verbal sections are not about knowing perfect English; they are about **pattern recognition and speed**. When you have 20 minutes for 20 questions (Accenture) or 15 minutes for 15 questions (Infosys), you cannot read every word. You must scan, eliminate, and solve.

This section covers 30 expert-level speed-solving tricks to slash your time per question from 60 seconds to 15 seconds.

---

## 1. THE ELIMINATION METHOD
> **How to eliminate 2 wrong options in 5 seconds using grammar signals.**

**Concept:** 
In any multiple-choice grammar question, the test setter creates one correct answer, one "distractor" (looks correct but is slightly off), and two completely wrong answers. By finding a fatal grammar flaw, you can instantly eliminate options.

**The Strategy:**
Look for Subject-Verb Agreement first. If the subject is singular, eliminate all options with plural verbs instantly.

---
**Q1. The jury ___ divided in their opinions.**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) has
(D) have

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Jury" is a collective noun. When the group acts as a unit, it's singular. When the group is *divided*, it acts as individuals, taking a plural verb. Since "their" is used, we need plural. Eliminate (A) and (C). "Have divided" makes no sense contextually. Thus, (B).
**⚡ Shortcut:** "their" -> plural. (A) and (C) are singular. Cross them out in 1 second.
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Infosys

---

## 2. THE CONTEXT CLUE METHOD
> **Guess vocabulary from surrounding words.**

**Concept:** 
Never give up on a synonym/antonym question just because you don't know the main word. The surrounding sentence contains built-in clues.

**The Strategy:**
Look for contrast conjunctions (`although`, `but`, `however`) or supporting conjunctions (`and`, `moreover`, `because`).

---
**Q2. Although he is usually loquacious, today he is surprisingly ___.**
(A) talkative
(B) taciturn
(C) verbose
(D) eloquent

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Although" signals a contrast. "Surprisingly" signals the opposite of his usual behavior. Even if you don't know "loquacious" (talkative), you know the blank must be the opposite of his usual state. If he is "usually X, today he is surprisingly Y", X and Y are opposites. (A) and (C) mean talkative. (D) means well-spoken. (B) taciturn means quiet.
**⚡ Shortcut:** `Although` = Opposite.
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Cognizant

---

## 3. THE SUBSTITUTION TEST (MENTAL READ-ALOUD)
> **For fill in the blanks, substitute each option and read aloud mentally.**

**Concept:** 
Your brain has subconsciously internalized English grammar through years of reading. If something "sounds wrong," it usually is. 

**The Strategy:**
Don't just stare at the options. Whisper the sentence with each option inserted. Your ear will catch the missing preposition or tense clash.

---

## 4. THE FIRST-LAST SENTENCE METHOD (PARA JUMBLES)
> **Find the opener and closer first.**

**Concept:** 
You do not need to arrange all 5 sentences to find the answer. Finding just the first or last sentence often eliminates 3 out of 4 options.

**The Strategy:**
1. A starting sentence (A) usually introduces a noun. It cannot start with pronouns (He, She, It, They) unless the noun was mentioned.
2. It cannot start with transition words (However, Therefore, Thus).
3. Find the opener. Check the options.

---
**Q3. Arrange the sentences:**
P: However, this approach has its drawbacks.
Q: Artificial Intelligence is transforming the tech industry.
R: They are worried about job losses.
S: Many employees are anxious about this transformation.

(A) QSPR
(B) QSRP
(C) PQRS
(D) SQPR

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** 'Q' introduces the topic (AI). 'P' starts with 'However' (cannot be first). 'R' starts with 'They' (needs a prior plural noun). 'S' introduces 'Many employees'. So 'Q' is first. Now look at S and R: "Many employees" (S) is the noun for the pronoun "They" (R). So SR is a pair. 
**⚡ Shortcut:** Only Q can start. This leaves (A) and (B). S ends with "transformation" pointing back to Q. R starts with "They" pointing to "employees" in S. Thus, SR is a mandatory pair. Q-S-R-P.
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant GenC

---

## 5. THE SCOPE TEST
> **For RC, eliminate answers that are too broad or too narrow.**

**Concept:**
When asked for the "Main Idea" of a passage, trap options will either be too broad (covering things not in the passage) or too narrow (focusing on a minor detail in one paragraph).

**The Strategy:**
Apply the Goldilocks principle. The right answer is "just right" in scope.

---

## 6. THE NEGATION TEST (CRITICAL REASONING)
> **For assumption questions, negate the option. If the argument collapses, it is the correct assumption.**

**Concept:**
An assumption is an unstated premise that MUST be true for the conclusion to hold.

**The Strategy:**
To test an option, add "NOT" to it. If the passage's conclusion is destroyed by this negation, you've found the correct assumption.

---

## 7. THE EXTREME WORD DETECTOR
> **'Always', 'Never', 'All', 'None', 'Only' are usually wrong in RC.**

**Concept:**
In Reading Comprehension, authors rarely make absolute claims. They use qualifiers like 'often', 'many', 'sometimes', 'likely'. Options with extreme words are 95% of the time incorrect.

**The Strategy:**
When you see `only`, `all`, `always`, `never`, `must`, `completely`, cross out the option unless the passage explicitly used that exact extreme word.

---

## 8. THE POSITIVE BIAS TRICK
> **For RC, positive authors are more common; beware of negative inference.**

**Concept:**
Most placement RCs are sourced from professional editorials (The Hindu, NYT, Economist). The tone is usually objective, analytical, or cautiously optimistic. 

**The Strategy:**
Tone options like `Derogatory`, `Angry`, `Sarcastic`, or `Vindictive` are almost always wrong. Choose `Analytical`, `Informative`, `Objective`, or `Cautious`.

---

## 9. THE GRAMMAR DETECTIVE (5-STEP SCAN)
> **For Error Detection, scan in a specific order, not randomly.**

**The Strategy:**
1. Check Subject-Verb Agreement.
2. Check Tenses (Is the timeline logical?)
3. Check Prepositions (Are they fixed?)
4. Check Articles (A vs An vs The)
5. Check Pronouns (Do they match the noun?)

---

## 10. THE COLLOCATION TRICK
> **Identify natural word partnerships.**

**Concept:**
Certain words always go together. (e.g., we "commit a crime", we don't "do a crime").

**The Strategy:**
If you are stuck between two vocabulary words, think of which one naturally pairs with the noun/verb next to it.
*Example:* "Heavy rain" not "Strong rain". "Fast food" not "Quick food".

---

## 11. THE ARTICLE DIAGNOSTIC
> **Quick test to determine which article.**

**The Strategy:**
- Is the noun plural or uncountable? -> Cannot use A/An.
- Has the noun been mentioned before? -> Use The.
- Is it a specific, unique thing (Sun, Moon, CEO of the company)? -> Use The.

---

## 12. THE PRONOUN CASE TRICK (WHO vs WHOM)
> **HE/HIM substitution for WHO/WHOM.**

**The Strategy:**
- If you can substitute "HE", the answer is **WHO**.
- If you can substitute "HIM", the answer is **WHOM**.

*Example:* "The man (who/whom) I met yesterday." -> I met *him*. -> So, it's **whom**.
*Example:* "The man (who/whom) called me." -> *He* called me. -> So, it's **who**.

---

## 13. THE TENSE TIMELINE
> **Draw a quick timeline for tense questions.**

**The Strategy:**
When dealing with multiple past actions, visualize a timeline. The older action gets Past Perfect (had + V3), the newer action gets Simple Past (V2).

---

## 14. THE MODAL DEDUCTION
> **Which modal fits the meaning?**

**The Strategy:**
- Can/Could = Ability
- May/Might = Possibility
- Must = Necessity/Obligation
- Should = Advice

---

## 15. THE PARALLEL STRUCTURE CHECKER
> **Align bullet points to find parallel items.**

**The Strategy:**
When you see `and`, `or`, `but`, check the words on both sides. They must match in form (Noun and Noun, V-ing and V-ing, To-V and To-V).

---

## 16. THE SVA PARENTHETICAL BRACKET
> **Bracket out interrupting phrases to find true subject.**

**The Strategy:**
Mental cross out any phrase between commas or starting with prepositions (`of, along with, as well as, in addition to`).

*Example:* The manager [along with his 50 employees] **is** responsible.

---

## 17. THE ACTIVE-PASSIVE CHECKER
> **Identify the agent and patient quickly.**

**The Strategy:**
Ask: Is the subject performing the action (Active) or receiving the action (Passive)? If receiving, you MUST have `Be + V3` (is done, was done, has been done).

---

## 18. INFERENCE CERTAINTY SCALE
> **Rate the certainty of each inference option.**

**The Strategy:**
Inferences are educated guesses. If an option says "This PROVES that...", it's too certain. If it says "This SUGGESTS that...", it's likely correct.

---

## 19. THE VOCABULARY ROOT DECODER
> **Decode unknown words using prefixes/roots.**

**The Strategy:**
- Mal = Bad (Malicious, Malfunction)
- Ben/Bon = Good (Beneficial, Bonus)
- Chron = Time (Chronological, Chronic)
- Dict = Speak (Dictate, Contradict)
- Path = Feeling (Sympathy, Apathy, Pathology)

---

## 20. THE IDIOM CONTEXT TRICK
> **Idioms that don't fit the sentence meaning = wrong.**

**The Strategy:**
If you don't know the idiom, read the context of the sentence. If the sentence is about failing, the idiom must have a negative connotation (e.g., "bite the dust").

---

## 21. THE COMPARISON CHECKER
> **Both sides of comparison must be same grammatical category.**

**The Strategy:**
You cannot compare apples to oranges.
*Wrong:* The climate of Delhi is hotter than Mumbai. (Comparing climate to a city).
*Right:* The climate of Delhi is hotter than **that of** Mumbai.

---

## 22. THE CONJUNCTION LOGIC TEST
> **Does the conjunction match the logical relationship?**

**The Strategy:**
- Cause/Effect: because, since, therefore, thus.
- Contrast: but, yet, although, even though, however.
- Condition: if, unless, provided that.

---

## 23. REVERSE SOLVING
> **Start with the conclusion and work backward for CR questions.**

**The Strategy:**
Read the question prompt BEFORE reading the paragraph. Know what you are looking for (Assumption, Weakener, Strengthener) before you read the text.

---

## 24. THE TIME SPLIT STRATEGY (60-30-30 RULE)
> **How to manage Reading Comprehension.**

**The Strategy:**
- 60 seconds: Skim the passage for main idea and structure (do not read details).
- 30 seconds: Read the question and identify the keywords.
- 30 seconds: Scan the passage for the keyword, read the surrounding lines, and answer.

---

## 25. THE EXAM PSYCHOLOGY FRAMEWORK
> **How to handle unknown questions.**

**The Strategy:**
Do not ego-solve. If a question involves a vocabulary word you have never seen, and context clues fail, do not spend 2 minutes staring at it. Mark a guess and move on.

---

## 26. EDUCATED GUESSING (NON-NEGATIVE MARKING)
> **When to guess and how.**

**The Strategy:**
If the exam has no negative marking (like TCS, Cognizant), NEVER leave a blank. Use the Elimination method to remove 2 options, then guess between the remaining 2. Statistically, this increases your score significantly.

---

## 27. THE PATTERN RECOGNITION SYSTEM
> **Recognize 10 recurring question templates.**

**The Strategy:**
After doing 20+ mock tests, you will realize that "Hardly...when", "No sooner...than", and "Neither...nor" appear in almost every test. Memorize these specific pairs perfectly.

---

## 28. THE 30-SECOND RULE
> **If taking more than 30s, mark and move on.**

**The Strategy:**
A hard question carries the same marks as an easy question. Do not waste 3 minutes on a hard Para Jumble when you could solve 5 easy Fill in the Blanks in that same time.

---

## 29. THE REVIEW STRATEGY
> **How to use review time effectively.**

**The Strategy:**
If you have 2 minutes left, do not review questions you guessed blindly. Review questions where you were stuck between two options. A fresh look often reveals the trick.

---

## 30. THE CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION
> **How to manage exam anxiety.**

**The Strategy:**
If the paper seems extremely hard, remember the adaptive nature. It might be hard because you are doing well! Stay calm, apply the rules, and trust your preparation.
