## 1.3 Verbal Ability Section — Deep Breakdown

### Sub-Section A: Verbal Ability (10 Questions)

This sub-section tests **word knowledge, contextual usage, and sentence-level comprehension**. TCS groups several types under "Verbal Ability":

#### Type 1: Para Completion / Sentence Completion
- A paragraph is given with a blank at the end or in the middle
- You must choose the sentence that best completes it
- Tests: Logical flow, topic continuity, tone matching

```
📌 PARA COMPLETION APPROACH:
   Step 1: Read the full paragraph to understand the TOPIC
   Step 2: Identify the TONE (formal/informal, positive/negative)
   Step 3: Identify the DIRECTION (is it concluding? contrasting? continuing?)
   Step 4: Eliminate options that change the topic or tone
   Step 5: Among remaining, pick the one with best logical connection
```

#### Type 2: Fill in the Blanks (Vocabulary-based)
- Single or double blank sentences
- Tests word choice in context
- Often confusable pairs: affect/effect, principal/principle, accept/except

#### Type 3: Sentence Arrangement / Para Jumbles
- 4–5 sentences jumbled; arrange in logical order
- TCS frequently uses 4-sentence jumbles
- Look for: Opening sentence clues, pronoun references, time markers, contrast/continuation words

#### Type 4: Synonyms & Antonyms
- Usually tested in context (not just standalone definitions)
- TCS uses moderately advanced vocabulary
- Level: Norman Lewis Chapter 5–15 range

#### Type 5: Analogies (Rare but occasional)
- Word relationship testing
- Example: DOCTOR : STETHOSCOPE :: CARPENTER : ?

### Sub-Section B: Reading Comprehension (10 Questions)

**2 passages** — typically 400–500 words each — with **5 questions each**.

> **TCS LOVES Technology RC passages.** Passages about Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Data Science, Digital Transformation are extremely common in 2023–2025. Familiarize yourself with 10–15 technology articles before the exam. If you can read and understand a tech article, TCS RC becomes easy.

#### TCS RC Question Types

```
RC QUESTION TAXONOMY — TCS NQT
════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPE 1: Main Idea / Central Theme
  → "What is the primary purpose of the passage?"
  → "The passage mainly discusses..."
  Frequency: 2–3 per passage

TYPE 2: Direct Detail / Fact Retrieval
  → "According to the passage, which of the following is true?"
  → Can be answered by going back to the text
  Frequency: 1–2 per passage

TYPE 3: Inference
  → "The author implies that..."
  → "It can be inferred from the passage that..."
  → NOT directly stated — must LOGICALLY derive
  Frequency: 1–2 per passage

TYPE 4: Vocabulary in Context
  → "As used in paragraph 2, the word 'X' most nearly means..."
  → Substitution test in context
  Frequency: 1 per passage

TYPE 5: Author's Tone / Attitude
  → "The author's tone in the passage is best described as..."
  → Objective? Critical? Optimistic? Cautionary?
  Frequency: 1 per passage
════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## 1.6 Recent Pattern Changes (2023–2025)

### 2023 Changes
- Increased focus on **inference-based RC** questions (moved away from direct fact retrieval)
- More **technology and AI-themed** passages (reflecting industry trends)
- Para jumbles started appearing with **5 sentences** instead of 4

### 2024 Changes
- **Advanced Verbal section** added for high scorers (separate 20-question, 25-minute test)
- Grammar section standardized to exactly **4 questions** (was variable 3–6 earlier)
- Introduced **para completion** as the dominant verbal ability question type
- Vocabulary testing shifted toward **context-based** (meaning-in-sentence) rather than standalone definitions

### 2025 Changes (Current)
- **AI-proctored exam** with stricter monitoring
- Passage length increased: now **450–550 words** per RC passage
- Some batches report seeing **cloze test** (fill all blanks in a passage) replacing fill-in-the-blanks
- Advanced section now requires **18+ in Foundation** to unlock

> [!WARNING]
> **2025 TCS is harder than 2022 TCS.** Students who prepare using only 2020–2021 materials will be under-prepared. The RC passages are longer, the inference questions are more nuanced, and the Advanced Verbal section demands near-CAT-level reading skills. Calibrate your preparation accordingly.

## 1.7 TCS-Specific Traps & Tricks

### Trap 1: The "Almost Right" RC Option
TCS frequently places one answer option that is **almost correct** but contains one word that makes it wrong.

**Example Trap:**
> Passage states: "Digital transformation has *partially* disrupted traditional banking."
> 
> Wrong option: *"The author believes digital transformation has completely disrupted banking."*
> 
> Notice the shift from "partially" to "completely" — TCS tests if you read carefully.

**Defense:** Underline key qualifying words in the passage (partially, rarely, often, always, never). These words are the #1 source of wrong answers.

### Trap 2: Para Completion — Tone Mismatch
TCS will offer an option that **continues the topic** but changes the tone (positive → critical or vice versa).

```
MNEMONIC: TACT
T = Topic must match
A = All key words must link
C = Continuity of argument direction
T = Tone must be consistent
```

### Trap 3: Grammar — SVA with Intervening Phrases
```
WRONG: "The collection of books on the shelves ARE dusty."
RIGHT: "The collection of books on the shelves IS dusty."
```
The subject is "collection" (singular), not "books." TCS places intervening phrases to distract you.

### Trap 4: Vocabulary — Confusable Words
TCS loves these pairs:

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Difference |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Affect (verb) | Effect (noun) | Affect = to influence; Effect = result |
| Comprise | Compose | Whole comprises parts; Parts compose whole |
| Imply | Infer | Speaker implies; Listener infers |
| Complement | Compliment | Complement = complete; Compliment = praise |
| Discreet | Discrete | Discreet = careful/tactful; Discrete = separate |
| Precede | Proceed | Precede = come before; Proceed = continue |

### Trap 5: RC — "According to the author" vs "According to the passage"
- "According to the author" → Author's personal OPINION (often in hedging language: "seems," "appears," "may")
- "According to the passage" → Stated FACT in the text (can be directly quoted)

### The Skim-Scan-Answer Framework for TCS RC

```
STEP 1: SKIM (60 seconds)
└─ Read first and last sentence of each paragraph
└─ Identify the MAIN TOPIC and overall structure

STEP 2: READ QUESTIONS FIRST (30 seconds)
└─ Read all 5 questions before going back to passage
└─ Know what to look for while reading

STEP 3: SCAN for ANSWERS (2–3 minutes)
└─ Go back to passage with questions in mind
└─ Inference questions: synthesize, don't just scan

STEP 4: ANSWER & ELIMINATE (2 minutes)
└─ Eliminate extreme options first
└─ Among remaining, pick most "hedged" / balanced answer
```

## 1.9 — 15 SAMPLE QUESTIONS WITH FULL EXPLANATIONS

### 📘 SECTION A: Reading Comprehension

**Passage for Questions 1–5:**

*Artificial Intelligence (AI) has steadily transitioned from the realm of science fiction into an indispensable part of modern enterprise. What was once the exclusive domain of research laboratories has now permeated industries as diverse as healthcare, finance, retail, and manufacturing. The adoption of AI, however, has not been uniformly embraced. While technology enthusiasts celebrate the efficiency gains and predictive accuracy that machine learning algorithms provide, a parallel school of thought raises serious concerns about algorithmic bias, data privacy, and the displacement of human workers.*

*In the healthcare sector, AI-driven diagnostic tools have demonstrated the ability to detect certain cancers with an accuracy rate surpassing that of experienced radiologists. Proponents argue that such tools do not replace doctors but instead augment their capabilities, allowing physicians to focus on complex cases requiring human judgment and emotional intelligence. Critics, however, contend that the opacity of AI decision-making — often called the "black box" problem — poses an unacceptable risk in life-or-death medical contexts.*

*The question of regulatory oversight has therefore become central to discussions of AI governance. Policymakers across the globe are grappling with how to create frameworks that encourage innovation while protecting citizens from potential harm. The European Union's AI Act, passed in 2024, represents the most comprehensive legislative attempt to date, categorizing AI systems by risk level and imposing varying degrees of scrutiny accordingly.*

---

**Q1. What is the central theme of the passage?**

(A) AI has completely replaced human workers in all sectors  
(B) The growing adoption of AI presents both opportunities and challenges  
(C) Healthcare is the only sector benefiting from artificial intelligence  
(D) The European Union is leading global AI innovation  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage covers AI's growth across multiple sectors, the debate between proponents and critics, and the regulatory response. None of the other options capture this balanced, multi-perspective view. The passage explicitly acknowledges BOTH efficiency gains AND concerns.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The passage says AI "augments" human capabilities, not "completely replaces" — extreme language ("completely," "all sectors") is a classic TCS trap.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** The passage mentions healthcare, finance, retail, AND manufacturing — not ONLY healthcare.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The EU is mentioned as one example of regulation; the passage does not claim EU leads innovation.

**📏 Rule:** Main Idea = What the ENTIRE passage is about, not just one paragraph  
**⚡ Shortcut:** First and last paragraphs + topic sentences = main idea  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT (2023, 2024)

**Q2. According to the passage, proponents of AI in healthcare argue that:**

(A) AI tools are superior to doctors and should replace them  
(B) AI removes the need for human emotional intelligence  
(C) AI diagnostic tools enhance physician capabilities rather than supplant them  
(D) The "black box" problem is an acceptable trade-off in medicine  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** Paragraph 2 states: "Proponents argue that such tools do not replace doctors but instead augment their capabilities, allowing physicians to focus on complex cases requiring human judgment and emotional intelligence." This directly matches option (C).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Proponents specifically say AI does NOT replace doctors — opposite of what's stated.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** The passage says AI allows doctors to focus ON emotional intelligence, not that it removes it.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The "black box" problem and its acceptability are raised by CRITICS, not proponents.

**📏 Rule:** Direct Detail questions — return to the text; don't rely on memory  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Keywords in the question ("proponents") = find that exact word in the passage  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q3. The author's tone in the passage is best described as:**

(A) Enthusiastically pro-technology  
(B) Strongly critical of AI regulation  
(C) Balanced and informative, presenting multiple perspectives  
(D) Pessimistic about the future of AI  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The author presents both sides: proponents' views AND critics' concerns, and describes regulatory efforts neutrally. There is no personal stance taken — this is academic/journalistic balanced writing.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The author also presents criticisms of AI; not one-sided enthusiasm.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** The author describes regulation (EU AI Act) factually, not critically.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Pessimism would mean the author believes AI will fail — no such conclusion is drawn.

**📏 Rule:** Tone = Author's ATTITUDE, detected through word choice; balanced = objective/informative  
**⚡ Shortcut:** If both sides of a debate are presented equally → answer is usually "balanced" or "objective"

**Q4. It can be inferred from the passage that the "black box" problem refers to:**

(A) The high cost of AI diagnostic equipment  
(B) The difficulty in understanding how AI systems arrive at their decisions  
(C) The lack of internet connectivity in rural healthcare  
(D) The tendency of AI to discriminate based on gender  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage says "the opacity of AI decision-making — often called the 'black box' problem." Opacity means lack of transparency — you cannot see inside how the AI made its decision. Option (B) captures this inference perfectly.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Cost is never mentioned in connection with the black box problem.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Connectivity is not mentioned anywhere in the passage.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Gender discrimination is a bias concern but is not what "black box" refers to in this context.

**📏 Rule:** Inference questions — the answer is not stated directly; it is logically implied  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find the sentence containing the quoted phrase; read the full sentence for context

**Q5. As used in the passage, the word "permeated" most nearly means:**

(A) Overwhelmed  
(B) Spread throughout  
(C) Destroyed  
(D) Initiated  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "AI has now permeated industries as diverse as healthcare, finance, retail, and manufacturing." Permeate = to spread through every part of. In context, AI has spread into/through multiple industries.

**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Initiated" means started — but AI is described as already embedded, not just starting.

### 📘 SECTION B: Verbal Ability

**Q6. Arrange the following sentences (A–D) into a coherent paragraph:**

(A) This data is then used to train machine learning models that can predict future trends.  
(B) Companies today collect enormous amounts of data from customer interactions.  
(C) As a result, businesses can make more informed decisions and improve customer satisfaction.  
(D) The raw data, however, must first be cleaned and organized before it is usable.  

**(1) BDAC  (2) BDCA  (3) CABD  (4) ABDC**

**✅ Answer: (2) BDAC**

**📖 Explanation:**
- **B** introduces the TOPIC: companies collect data. *(Opening sentence — introduces the subject)*
- **D** gives a logical NEXT STEP: but data needs to be cleaned first. *(The word "however" creates contrast — must come after introducing data)*
- **A** continues the sequence: cleaned data is used to train ML models. *(Logical progression after cleaning)*
- **C** gives the RESULT/CONCLUSION: better decisions and satisfaction. *(Conclusion marker: "As a result")*

Order: B → D → A → C = **BDAC**

> Wait — let me re-check: BDAC means B-D-A-C. Option (1) is BDAC and option (2) is BDCA. Let me re-read.

Actually: Option (1) = BDAC, Option (2) = BDCA. The correct logical order is B-D-A-C = **Option (1)**.

**✅ Corrected Answer: (1) BDAC**

**📏 Rule:** Para Jumbles — find the sentence with no pronoun reference to another sentence = Opening. Look for conclusion markers (as a result, therefore, thus) = Closing sentence.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Connector words: "however" links to a previous positive statement; "as a result" = final sentence  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT (3–4 para jumbles expected)

**Q7. Choose the word that best fills the blank:**

*"The scientist's explanation was so __________ that even a child could understand the complex concept."*

(A) Abstruse  
(B) Lucid  
(C) Arcane  
(D) Convoluted  

**✅ Answer: (B) Lucid**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence says "even a child could understand" — this is a POSITIVE context indicating the explanation was CLEAR and simple. "Lucid" means clear, easy to understand.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Abstruse = difficult to understand — OPPOSITE of what's needed.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Arcane = known only to a few, mysterious — opposite meaning.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Convoluted = unnecessarily complex — again, opposite.

**📏 Rule:** Fill-in-the-Blank — identify POSITIVE or NEGATIVE context. "Even a child could understand" = POSITIVE/SIMPLE → need a word meaning clear/simple.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** LUCID = "LOOK, YOU CAN INSTANTLY DECIPHER" = Clear!  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Wipro

**Q8. Choose the sentence that best completes the paragraph:**

*"Renewable energy sources like solar and wind power have grown significantly over the past decade. Governments worldwide have introduced subsidies and incentives to accelerate adoption. Despite this progress, fossil fuels still account for more than 80% of global energy consumption. __________"*

(A) Therefore, renewable energy has already succeeded in replacing fossil fuels.  
(B) The transition to clean energy is complete in most developed nations.  
(C) Clearly, the shift to renewable energy, while promising, still has a long way to go.  
(D) Solar panels are the most effective form of renewable energy available today.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The paragraph has a **mixed tone** — progress in renewables BUT fossil fuels still dominate. The conclusion should acknowledge BOTH the progress AND the challenge. Option (C) does this perfectly: "promising" (acknowledges progress) + "still has a long way to go" (acknowledges the challenge).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Directly contradicts the fact that fossil fuels = 80% of consumption.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Passage says fossil fuels still dominate globally — "complete in most nations" is false.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Changes the topic to comparing solar vs. other renewables — irrelevant to the paragraph's conclusion.

**📏 Rule:** Para Completion — the concluding sentence must (1) be consistent with ALL facts in the paragraph, (2) match the tone, and (3) not introduce new unrelated topics.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** The "TACT" mnemonic: Topic ✓, All facts consistent ✓, Continuity ✓, Tone ✓  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT (para completion is their #1 verbal type)

**Q9. Select the synonym of "PRAGMATIC":**

(A) Idealistic

(C) Theoretical  
(D) Whimsical  

**✅ Answer: (B) Practical**

**📖 Explanation:** Pragmatic = dealing with things sensibly and realistically based on practical considerations. Synonym = Practical.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Idealistic = opposite of pragmatic (believing in ideals over reality).  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Theoretical = based on theory, not practice — also opposite.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Whimsical = playful/fanciful — completely different meaning.

**📏 Rule:** For synonyms, eliminate clear antonyms first; among remaining, pick the closest in both meaning AND usage.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** PRAGMATIC sounds like PRACTICAL — both start with PR and involve reality!  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini

**Q10. Choose the antonym of "VERBOSE":**

(A) Eloquent  
(B) Garrulous  
(C) Loquacious  
(D) Terse  

**✅ Answer: (D) Terse**

**📖 Explanation:** Verbose = using more words than necessary. Antonym = using fewer words = Terse (brief, concise).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Eloquent = well-spoken and persuasive — doesn't directly oppose verbose.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Garrulous = excessively talkative — SAME as verbose, not opposite.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Loquacious = tending to talk a lot — also same as verbose.

**📏 Rule:** Know the "talking" vocabulary cluster: Verbose = Garrulous = Loquacious = Talkative (ALL synonyms). Their antonyms: Terse = Laconic = Succinct = Concise = Brief.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "GARRULOUS VERBOSE LOQUACIOUS = BIG talkers. TERSE LACONIC = TIGHT-lipped"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys, ZS Associates

### 📘 SECTION C: Grammar / Error Detection

**Q11. Identify the error in the sentence:**

*"The team of players are (A) / preparing themselves (B) / for the championship game (C) / that begins tomorrow. (D)"*

(A) The team of players are  
(B) preparing themselves  
(C) for the championship game  
(D) that begins tomorrow  

**✅ Answer: (A) — "are" should be "is"**

**📖 Explanation:** "Team" is a collective noun. In formal/written American English (which Indian placement exams follow), collective nouns take SINGULAR verbs. The subject is "team," not "players" (players is part of an intervening prepositional phrase).

**Correct sentence:** "The team of players **is** preparing themselves for the championship game that begins tomorrow."

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement — collective nouns (team, committee, jury, committee, staff, faculty) take singular verbs in formal usage. Prepositional phrases ("of players") do NOT change the subject.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Cross out the prepositional phrase: "The team __ are preparing" → Is "team" singular? YES → Use "is."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Accenture, IBM

---

**Q12. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:**

(A) Neither the students nor the teacher are present today.  
(B) Neither the students nor the teacher is present today.  
(C) Neither the teacher nor the students is present today.  
(D) Neither the teacher nor the students was present today.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The rule for "Neither...nor" (and "Either...or") is **Proximity Rule** — the verb agrees with the subject CLOSEST to it (the subject nearest the verb).

- In (A): Closest subject = "teacher" (singular) → Verb must be "is" → WRONG (uses "are")
- In (B): Closest subject = "teacher" (singular) → Verb = "is" → CORRECT
- In (C): Closest subject = "students" (plural) → Verb must be "are" → WRONG (uses "is")
- In (D): Same as (C) — "was" is also wrong for plural "students"

**📏 Rule:** Neither/Nor + Either/Or → Proximity Rule (verb agrees with NEAREST subject)  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "NOR = NEAR" — the subject NEAR the verb determines agreement  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Accenture, Wipro (this specific rule appears in 60%+ of tests)

**Q13. Identify the incorrect sentence:**

(A) He has been working here since 2018.  
(B) She is living in this city for five years.  
(C) They have lived in this house since they were children.  
(D) I have been studying for the past three hours.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "For five years" specifies a DURATION. With a duration that extends to the present, use **Present Perfect Continuous** ("has/have been + V-ing") not Present Continuous ("is + V-ing").

- **Wrong:** "She is living in this city for five years." (Present Continuous)
- **Correct:** "She **has been living** in this city for five years."

(A) Correct: "since 2018" + has been working ✓  
(C) Correct: "since they were children" + have lived ✓  
(D) Correct: "for the past three hours" + have been studying ✓

**📏 Rule:** "For + duration" extending to present → Present Perfect or Present Perfect Continuous. NEVER use Simple Present or Present Continuous with "for" showing duration.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "FOR duration, SINCE point-in-time → both need PERFECT tense"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys, IBM

---

**Q14. Fill in the blank with the correct word:**

*"The committee will __________ the proposals submitted by the regional offices before making a final decision."*

(A) scrutinize  
(B) scrutinizing  
(C) has scrutinized  
(D) scrutinized  

**✅ Answer: (A) scrutinize**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence structure is "will + ___". The word "will" is a modal auxiliary verb. Modal verbs ALWAYS take the BASE FORM of the verb (infinitive without "to").

- Will + scrutinize ✓ (base form)
- Will + scrutinizing ✗ (progressive form — wrong with modal)
- Will + has scrutinized ✗ (perfect form — grammatically impossible with "will")
- Will + scrutinized ✗ (past form — wrong with modal)

**📏 Rule:** Modal + Base Form: will/would/can/could/shall/should/may/might/must → ALWAYS followed by base form of verb  
**⚡ Shortcut:** After any modal verb, use V1 (base form). No exceptions.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Capgemini

---

**Q15. Spot the error:**

*"Each of the managers have submitted their quarterly reports on time."*

(A) Each of the managers  
(B) have submitted  
(C) their quarterly reports  
(D) on time  

**✅ Answer: (B) — "have" should be "has"**

**📖 Explanation:** "Each" is an indefinite pronoun that is always SINGULAR. "Each of the managers" → subject = EACH → singular → verb must be "has."

**Correct:** "Each of the managers **has** submitted their quarterly reports on time."

Note: While "their" (plural) is used for the reports, this is modern singular-they usage which is acceptable. The verb error is the primary error.

**📏 Rule:** Indefinite Pronouns that are ALWAYS SINGULAR: each, every, either, neither, anyone, everyone, someone, no one, anybody, everybody, somebody, nobody → all take singular verbs.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "EACH is a SINGLE detective — it always hunts ALONE (singular verb)"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Accenture, Wipro, IBM
