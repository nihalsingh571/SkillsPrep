## 3.3 English Ability Section — Deep Breakdown

### Sub-Section A: Error Detection (6–8 Questions)

This is Accenture's **signature and dominant question type**. A sentence is divided into 4–5 parts (labeled A, B, C, D, E). One part has a grammatical error; you must identify it.

#### Error Detection — Grammar Domains Tested

```
ACCENTURE ERROR DETECTION — GRAMMAR PRIORITY
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIORITY 1: Subject-Verb Agreement (30% of errors)
   • Collective nouns, neither/nor, each/every
   • Inverted sentences, expletives (there is/are)

PRIORITY 2: Tense Consistency (25% of errors)
   • Temporal markers: since vs for
   • Sequence of tenses
   • Conditional errors (if...would in wrong clause)

PRIORITY 3: Article Errors (15% of errors)
   • a vs an vs the
   • Zero article situations (proper nouns, plural generals)

PRIORITY 4: Pronoun Agreement (10% of errors)
   • Singular they debates
   • Reflexive pronoun misuse (myself vs me)

PRIORITY 5: Preposition Errors (10% of errors)
   • Idiomatic prepositions (interested IN, good AT, capable OF)

PRIORITY 6: Parallelism (5%)
   • Lists, comparisons, correlatives

PRIORITY 7: Modifier/Adjective (5%)
   • Dangling participles, misplaced adverbs
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

#### The Error Detection Framework (ACCENTURE Method)

```
ACCENTURE Error Detection — GRAMMAR CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
When you see an error detection question, check in this order:

STEP 1: Check Subject-Verb Agreement
   → Identify the TRUE subject (cross out prepositional phrases)
   → Is the verb singular or plural? Does it match the subject?

STEP 2: Check Tense
   → Does the tense marker (since, for, already, yet, by the time)
     match the tense used?
   → Is tense consistent throughout the sentence?

STEP 3: Check Articles
   → Before consonant sound: "a"
   → Before vowel sound: "an" (a university, an hour)
   → Specific reference: "the"
   → First mention vs second mention

STEP 4: Check Pronouns
   → Does pronoun agree with its antecedent?
   → Is the pronoun case correct (I vs me vs myself)?

STEP 5: Check Prepositions
   → Is this an idiomatic preposition? (interested in, not interested on)

STEP 6: Check Parallelism
   → In a list or comparison, are all elements the same form?
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## 3.7 Accenture-Specific Traps & Tricks

### Trap 1: The "No Error" Trap
Accenture frequently includes "No Error" as option (E) in error detection. Many students force themselves to find an error even when the sentence is correct.

```
DEFENSE: After going through all 6 steps of your grammar checklist
and finding no error → confidently mark "No Error"
Don't be psychologically pressured to "find" errors that don't exist.
Approximately 15–20% of error detection questions have "No Error" as the answer.
```

### Trap 2: Article Error — "a" vs "an"
Accenture specifically tests the "sound rule" for articles (not the letter rule):

```
SOUND RULE:
"a" before CONSONANT SOUND: a book, a university (u = "yoo" sound), a European
"an" before VOWEL SOUND: an apple, an hour (h is silent!), an MBA (em-bee-ay)

TRAP EXAMPLES:
✗ "a honest person" → should be "an honest person" (h is silent → vowel sound)
✗ "an university" → should be "a university" (u sounds like "yoo" → consonant sound)
✗ "a MBA degree" → should be "an MBA degree" (M sounds like "em" → vowel sound)
```

### Trap 3: SVA — Expletive "There" Constructions
```
"There is/are ___" — the verb agrees with what COMES AFTER "there":

"There IS a book on the table."  (book = singular → is)
"There ARE three books on the table."  (books = plural → are)
"There IS milk and bread in the fridge."  (nearest = milk → singular → is)
```

Accenture uses "there is/are" constructions specifically to test this.

### Trap 4: Pronoun Case — Myself vs Me
Accenture loves testing "myself" misuse:

| Correct Use of "Myself" | Incorrect Use |
|------------------------|---------------|
| Reflexive: "I hurt **myself**." | ✗ "Contact **myself** for details." |
| Emphatic: "I **myself** was there." | ✗ "Send it to John and **myself**." |

**Rule:** "Myself" cannot be used when "me" is correct. It's only for reflexive/emphatic purposes.

### Trap 5: Preposition — Idiomatic Usage
Accenture tests specific idiomatic prepositions:

| Wrong Preposition | Correct Preposition |
|------------------|---------------------|
| interested **on** | interested **in** |
| good **in** something | good **at** something |
| capable **to** | capable **of** |
| different **than** (Indian context) | different **from** |
| comply **to** | comply **with** |
| superior **than** | superior **to** |
| accused **for** | accused **of** |

## 3.9 — 15 SAMPLE QUESTIONS WITH FULL EXPLANATIONS

### 📘 SECTION A: Error Detection

**Q1. Find the part with an error:**

*"The committee have (A) / decided to postpone (B) / the annual event (C) / until further notice. (D) / No Error (E)"*

**✅ Answer: (A) — "have" should be "has"**

**📖 Explanation:** "Committee" is a collective noun. In formal/standard English (which Accenture follows), collective nouns take **singular verbs** when the group acts as a unified whole. "The committee has decided" = the whole committee together made one decision = unity = singular.

**📏 Rule:** Collective Nouns (committee, team, jury, staff, government, board) + single unified action = SINGULAR verb  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "Committee = ONE UNIT deciding = HAS (singular)"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (most common error detection type, 2022–2025)

---

**Q2. Find the error:**

*"She has lived (A) / in Mumbai since (B) / the last five years (C) / and loves the city. (D) / No Error (E)"*

**✅ Answer: (C) — "since the last five years" should be "for the last five years"**

**📖 Explanation:** 
- "The last five years" = a **duration** (5 years long)
- **FOR** is used with duration
- **SINCE** is used with a specific point in time (since 2020, since Monday)
- "Since the last five years" is incorrect because "the last five years" is not a point in time.

**Correct:** "She has lived in Mumbai **for** the last five years."

**📏 Rule:** FOR = Duration period. SINCE = Starting point in time.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "FOR measures how MUCH time. SINCE pinpoints WHEN it started."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, TCS, Infosys, Wipro — Universal rule

---

**Q3. Find the error:**

*"An university degree (A) / is increasingly considered (B) / essential for corporate employment (C) / in the modern economy. (D) / No Error (E)"*

**✅ Answer: (A) — "An university" should be "A university"**

**📖 Explanation:** "University" begins with the LETTER 'U' but makes a CONSONANT SOUND: "yoo-niversity." The article choice depends on the **sound**, not the letter. Since "university" begins with a consonant sound ('y'), use **"a"** not "an."

**Correct:** "**A** university degree is increasingly considered..."

**📏 Rule:** Article Rule — "a" before consonant SOUND; "an" before vowel SOUND. University = "yoo" sound = consonant = "a university."  
**⚡ Memory Device:** Words beginning with 'u' that sound like 'yoo': university, unique, union, unit, user, usual → use "A." Words beginning with 'u' that sound like 'uh': umbrella, uncle, umpire → use "AN."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (article errors appear in nearly every test)

---

**Q4. Find the error:**

*"If he would have worked (A) / more carefully, (B) / he would not have made (C) / so many mistakes. (D) / No Error (E)"*

**✅ Answer: (A) — "would have worked" should be "had worked"**

**📖 Explanation:** Type 3 Conditional — past unreal situation:

```
CORRECT STRUCTURE:
IF + Past Perfect (HAD + V3) → WOULD HAVE + V3

WRONG: "If he WOULD HAVE worked"
RIGHT: "If he HAD worked"
```

"Would have" must NOT appear in the "if" clause. It belongs only in the main (result) clause.

**📏 Rule:** Type 3 Conditional: IF + Past Perfect (had V3) → Main Clause WOULD HAVE + V3.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "IF = HAD (past perfect). THEN = WOULD HAVE. Never WOULD in the IF!"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Infosys, IBM

---

**Q5. Find the error:**

*"Each of the engineers (A) / have submitted (B) / their project proposals (C) / before the deadline. (D) / No Error (E)"*

**✅ Answer: (B) — "have submitted" should be "has submitted"**

**📖 Explanation:** "Each" is an indefinite pronoun that is ALWAYS singular, regardless of what follows it. "Each of the engineers" → subject = EACH (singular) → verb must be **"has"** not "have."

Note: "their" in part (C) is acceptable as modern singular-they for indefinite "each."

**📏 Rule:** "Each" → always singular → takes "has/was/does" etc.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "Each ONE = ONLY ONE = HAS (not have)"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (appears in 70%+ of error detection sets)

---

**Q6. Identify the sentence that has NO ERROR:**

(A) He is one of the student who has submitted the assignment on time.  
(B) He is one of the students who have submitted the assignment on time.  
(C) He is one of the students who has submitted the assignment on time.  
(D) He is one of the student who have submitted the assignment on time.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The structure "one of the + plural noun + who/that + verb" requires a PLURAL verb after "who/that." Why? Because the relative clause "who have submitted" refers to the PLURAL noun "students," not to "one."

Analysis:
- (A): "student" (singular plural disagreement with "who has" — but "one of the student" is wrong; should be "students") ✗
- (B): "students" (plural) + "who have" (plural verb refers back to students) ✓
- (C): "students" ✓ but "who has" → singular verb → WRONG (the "who" refers to "students" plural)
- (D): "student" (should be "students") ✗

**📏 Rule:** "One of the + PLURAL NOUN + WHO/THAT + PLURAL VERB"  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "WHO points back to the PLURAL NOUN, not to 'one'"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (appears in many error detection tests)

### 📘 SECTION B: Sentence Correction

**Q7. Choose the correct version of the underlined part:**

*"The research findings, as well as the methodology, **was** presented to the board."*

(A) were presented  
(B) are presenting  
(C) have presented  
(D) No correction needed  

**✅ Answer: (A) were presented**

**📖 Explanation:** "As well as" is a COORDINATOR that does NOT change the grammatical subject. The subject = "The research findings" (plural). "As well as the methodology" is parenthetical.

→ "The research findings **were** presented" (plural subject → plural verb)

**📏 Rule:** "As well as," "along with," "together with," "in addition to" = parenthetical additions. They do NOT change the number of the subject. Verb agrees with the ORIGINAL subject.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Remove the "as well as ___" phrase: "The research findings ___ presented" → plural → "were"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, IBM, Wipro

---

**Q8. Choose the correct sentence:**

(A) The manager, along with his team members, are working on the project.  
(B) The manager, along with his team members, is working on the project.  
(C) The manager, along with his team members, work on the project.  
(D) The manager and his team members, is working on the project.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Along with" (same as "as well as") = parenthetical. Subject = "The manager" (singular) → Verb = **"is working"** (singular).

- (A): "are" — treats "along with" as AND (wrong)
- (C): "work" — bare form, grammatically impossible for singular/formal
- (D): Uses "and" not "along with" — if "and" is used, both subjects combine = plural → should be "are"

**📏 Rule:** Same as Q7 — "along with" doesn't change subject number. Subject alone determines verb.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (repeated pattern)

---

**Q9. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:**

(A) She is very good in playing chess and have won several tournaments.  
(B) She is very good at playing chess and has won several tournaments.  
(C) She is very good at playing chess and have won several tournaments.  
(D) She is very good in playing chess and has won several tournaments.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Two errors to fix:
1. **Preposition error**: "good IN" is wrong → idiomatic = "good **AT**"
2. **SVA error**: Subject = "She" (singular) → verb = "**has** won" not "have won"

Only (B) fixes BOTH errors.

**📏 Rule 1:** "Good at" = idiomatic preposition for skill/ability  
**📏 Rule 2:** She/He/It/Singular → has won (present perfect)  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (double-error SC — their signature)

### 📘 SECTION C: Fill in the Blanks

**Q10. Choose the correct word:**

*"The board of directors __________ to approve the annual budget at yesterday's meeting."*

(A) were voted  
(B) has voted  
(C) voted  
(D) were voting  

**✅ Answer: (C) voted**

**📖 Explanation:** 
- "Yesterday's meeting" = PAST TIME MARKER → Simple Past tense
- "Board of directors" = collective noun = singular in formal usage
- Simple Past = "voted" (no auxiliary needed)
- (A) "were voted" = past passive (wrong — the board IS VOTING, not being voted upon)
- (B) "has voted" = Present Perfect, contradicts "yesterday" (PP cannot be used with specific past time markers)
- (D) "were voting" = Past Continuous (less definitive than Simple Past for a completed decision)

**📏 Rule:** With specific past time markers (yesterday, last week, in 2022), use **Simple Past** tense. NEVER Present Perfect.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "Yesterday = DEAD and DONE = Simple Past. No present perfect with yesterday!"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, TCS, Wipro

---

**Q11. Choose the correct option to fill the blank:**

*"The auditors found several __________ in the company's financial records that required immediate investigation."*

(A) discrepancies  
(B) similarities  
(C) validations  
(D) endorsements  

**✅ Answer: (A) discrepancies**

**📖 Explanation:** "Financial records" + "required immediate investigation" = something was WRONG. "Discrepancies" = differences or inconsistencies that shouldn't be there — perfect for financial irregularities.

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Similarities = things that match — would NOT require investigation.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Validations = confirmations that things are correct — opposite of a problem.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Endorsements = approval/support — positive, not problematic.

**📏 Rule:** Context Direction — "found ___" + "required immediate investigation" → negative word needed  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** DISCREPANCY = "DISC + REPANCY" = something that DISC(ords) = doesn't match  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (business vocabulary)

---

**Q12. Fill in the blank:**

*"The safety regulations clearly state that employees must __________ protective gear at all times on the factory floor."*

(A) wear  
(B) wearing  
(C) to wear  
(D) wore  

**✅ Answer: (A) wear**

**📖 Explanation:** "Must" is a modal verb. **Modal + Base Form of verb** = always.
- must wear ✓ (base form)
- must wearing ✗ (progressive form with modal = wrong)
- must to wear ✗ (modal doesn't take "to" before verb)
- must wore ✗ (past form with modal = wrong)

**📏 Rule:** Modal verbs (must, can, will, shall, may, might, could, would, should) → ALWAYS followed by base form (V1) without "to."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, TCS, Capgemini

### 📘 SECTION D: Reading Comprehension

**Passage for Q13–Q15:**

*Remote work has transformed the modern workplace, offering employees unprecedented flexibility while presenting organizations with new challenges in collaboration and productivity management. Studies conducted in 2023 indicate that while 67% of remote workers report higher job satisfaction, their managers often struggle to assess performance accurately without traditional oversight mechanisms. The key tension, therefore, lies not in whether remote work is effective — evidence suggests it largely is — but in how organizations must evolve their management practices to fully harness its benefits.*

---

**Q13. What is the central argument of the passage?**

(A) Remote work reduces productivity significantly.  
(B) Most managers prefer remote work over traditional office setups.  
(C) While remote work brings benefits, organizations need to adapt management practices to fully capitalize on it.  
(D) The 2023 study proves that remote workers are more satisfied and more productive.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage acknowledges both benefits (flexibility, higher satisfaction) AND challenges (managers struggling with performance assessment) and concludes: "the key tension lies... in how organizations must evolve their management practices." = Organizations need to adapt = option (C).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Passage says evidence suggests remote work "largely IS effective" — opposite of reducing productivity.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Managers are described as STRUGGLING, not preferring remote work.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The study shows higher SATISFACTION not higher productivity specifically; "proves" is too strong.

**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (short-passage RC, current affairs topic)

---

**Q14. According to the passage, what challenge do managers face with remote work?**

(A) They cannot communicate with remote employees.  
(B) Remote employees are less satisfied with their work.  
(C) Assessing employee performance without traditional oversight mechanisms is difficult.  
(D) Remote work increases organizational costs significantly.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage directly states: "their managers often struggle to **assess performance accurately without traditional oversight mechanisms**." Option (C) is a near-verbatim paraphrase of this.

**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q15. The phrase "harness its benefits" in the passage most closely means:**

(A) Restrict the advantages of remote work  
(B) Fully utilize and maximize the advantages of remote work  
(C) Understand why remote work has become popular  
(D) Measure the cost-effectiveness of remote work  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Harness" = to control and use (something, especially something powerful) to good effect. "Harness its benefits" = make full use of the benefits. Context: organizations must evolve SO THAT they can harness = utilize effectively.

**📏 Rule:** Vocabulary in context — substitute each option: "to fully RESTRICT the advantages" doesn't make sense in context of organizations evolving to improve.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Accenture (vocabulary in context within RC)
