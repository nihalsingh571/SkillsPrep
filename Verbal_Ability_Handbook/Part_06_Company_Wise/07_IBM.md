### 7.1 IBM Test Pattern & Nuances

IBM's recruitment process often emphasizes cognitive ability alongside English comprehension.

#### Structure of the English Section
- **Sentence Correction & Error Spotting:** 8-10 questions. Highly focused on modifiers, parallelism, and idioms.
- **Vocabulary (Word Choice/Fillers):** 5-7 questions. You must choose the exact word that fits a corporate or technical context.
- **Reading Comprehension:** Usually 1 passage with 5 questions, leaning heavily on business and technology.

``` rule
IBM WORD CHOICE RULE:
If two options mean the exact same thing but one is formal and the other is colloquial, ALWAYS choose the formal one in the IBM test. 
Example: Choose "Acquire" instead of "Get".
```

### 7.2 Detailed Topic Analysis

#### 7.2.1 Sentence Correction
IBM loves testing "misplaced modifiers" and "parallel structure".
- *Misplaced Modifier Example:* "Running down the street, the car hit him." (Did the car run down the street? No.)
- *Parallelism Example:* "She likes swimming, running, and to bike." -> "She likes swimming, running, and biking."

#### 7.2.2 Word Choice & Phrasal Verbs
IBM tests phrasal verbs that are common in business, such as *carry out*, *bring up*, *fall through*, and *lay off*.

**Q1. Sentence Correction: Choose the best replacement for the underlined part.**
The committee *decided to postpone the meeting, due to* the fact that the CEO was ill.

(A) decided to postpone the meeting, because  
(B) decided to postpone the meeting due to  
(C) decided to postpone the meeting because  
(D) decided on postponing the meeting since  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Because" is more concise and direct than "due to the fact that". Also, no comma is needed before "because" when it introduces a dependent clause that follows the main clause. "Due to" should be used to modify nouns (e.g., The delay was due to rain), not verbs.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Unnecessary comma.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Due to" is incorrect here; it modifies verbs here incorrectly.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Wordy and awkward phrasing.

**📏 Rule:** Conciseness and Because vs. Due To  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Due to the fact that" is almost always wrong in formal English. Replace with "because".  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Deloitte  
---

**Q2. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word:**
The new security protocols will ______ the risk of data breaches significantly.

(A) exacerbate  
(B) mitigate  
(C) vindicate  
(D) instigate  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Protocols are designed to reduce risk. "Mitigate" means to make less severe, harmful, or painful.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Exacerbate means to make worse.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Vindicate means to clear of blame.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Instigate means to bring about or initiate (usually something bad).

**📏 Rule:** Contextual Vocabulary  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Risk -> Reduce. Mitigate = Reduce.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Capgemini  
---

**Q3. Identify the error in the sentence:**
Walking through the park, (A) / the beautiful flowers (B) / caught my attention (C) / immediately. (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** This is a classic dangling modifier. The phrase "Walking through the park" must modify the subject of the main clause. As written, it implies the "beautiful flowers" were walking through the park. It should be: "While I was walking through the park, the beautiful flowers caught my attention."

**❌ Why (A, C, D) are wrong:** The structural error lies in the connection between the modifier (A) and the subject (B). The error is triggered by the subject in B not matching the action in A.

**📏 Rule:** Dangling Modifiers  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ask "Who is walking?" If the noun immediately following the comma isn't the one walking, it's an error.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, ZS Associates  
---

**Q4. Choose the correct phrasal verb:**
The negotiations for the merger ______ at the last minute because of disagreements over the valuation.

(A) fell through  
(B) fell out  
(C) broke out  
(D) pulled through  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Fall through" means to fail to happen or be completed (often used for plans or arrangements).

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Fall out means to have an argument with someone.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Break out means to start suddenly (like a war or fire).  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Pull through means to survive a dangerous situation (like an illness).

**📏 Rule:** Phrasal Verbs  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Plans failing = falling through.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q5. Sentence Improvement:**
She not only spoke English fluently, *but also she was* proficient in French.

(A) but also was  
(B) but she also was  
(C) but also  
(D) No improvement  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** Correlative conjunctions like "Not only... but also" require parallel structure. "spoke" is a verb. Therefore, "but also" must be followed directly by a verb to match. "She not only [verb]... but also [verb]..." -> "She not only spoke... but also was..."

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Breaks the "but also" pair improperly in this context.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Missing the verb.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Breaks parallelism.

**📏 Rule:** Parallelism (Correlative Conjunctions)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** What comes after "not only" must structurally match what comes after "but also". Verb -> Verb.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Cognizant

*(Questions 6-8 are based on the following passage)*
**Passage:**
The concept of "agile methodology" has transcended its origins in software development and permeated various corporate departments, including HR and marketing. Unlike traditional "waterfall" models, which rely on rigid, sequential planning, agile frameworks emphasize iterative progress, flexibility, and cross-functional collaboration. Proponents argue that agile allows organizations to pivot quickly in response to market changes. However, critics warn that without proper training, "going agile" can lead to chaos, scope creep, and employee burnout, as the constant demand for rapid iteration can blur the lines between work and personal time.

**Q6. What is the primary difference between agile and waterfall models according to the passage?**

(A) Agile is used in HR, while waterfall is used in software.  
(B) Agile is rigid, while waterfall is iterative.  
(C) Agile emphasizes flexibility, while waterfall relies on sequential planning.  
(D) Agile leads to burnout, while waterfall leads to scope creep.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage explicitly states: "Unlike traditional 'waterfall' models, which rely on rigid, sequential planning, agile frameworks emphasize iterative progress, flexibility..."

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Both can be used in software.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Reversed definitions.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Burnout is a risk of agile, but not the primary structural difference.

**📏 Rule:** RC - Contrast and Comparison  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Scan for "Unlike" and read the two contrasting attributes.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q9. Choose the most appropriate word to complete the sentence:**
His speech was so ______ that most of the audience left before he finished.

(A) riveting  
(B) monotonous  
(C) eloquent  
(D) succinct  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** If the audience left, the speech must have been boring or bad. "Monotonous" means dull, tedious, and repetitious; lacking in variety and interest.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Riveting means extremely interesting.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Eloquent means fluent or persuasive.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Succinct means brief and clear.

**📏 Rule:** Vocabulary - Context Clues  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Audience left = Bad speech. Monotonous is the only negative word.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q10. Identify the error in the sentence:**
If he will finish (A) / the report by tonight, (B) / we can submit it (C) / tomorrow morning. (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** In a First Conditional sentence, the "if" clause takes the simple present tense, not the future tense. It should be "If he finishes".

**❌ Why (B, C, D) are wrong:** These parts are structurally correct ("can submit" represents the future outcome).

**📏 Rule:** First Conditional (If + Simple Present, Future)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never use "will" immediately after "if".  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Wipro  
---

**Q11. Sentence Correction:**
The board of directors *are completely opposed to* the hostile takeover.

(A) is completely opposed to  
(B) are entirely opposing against  
(C) is opposing to  
(D) No correction required  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Board" is a collective noun acting as a single unit here. Therefore, it takes a singular verb "is". 

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Are" is plural, "opposing against" is redundant.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "opposing to" is idiomatically incorrect.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "are" is plural.

**📏 Rule:** Collective Nouns and Subject-Verb Agreement  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Collective nouns (committee, jury, board, team) acting as one body take a singular verb.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** IBM  
---

**Q12. Fill in the blank:**
The manager demanded that the employee ______ immediately to his office.

(A) comes  
(B) come  
(C) will come  
(D) came  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** This sentence uses the **Subjunctive Mood**. After verbs expressing a demand, request, or proposal (demand, insist, suggest, recommend) followed by "that", the base form of the verb (without 's', 'es', or 'to') must be used, regardless of the subject. 

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Comes" is standard indicative, but subjunctive drops the 's'.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Future tense is incorrect here.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Past tense is incorrect.

**📏 Rule:** Subjunctive Mood (Bossy Verbs)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** demand/insist/suggest + that + Subject + BASE VERB (V1 without 's').  
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, ZS Associates  
---

**Q13. Choose the pair of words that best expresses a relationship similar to that of the given pair:**
**OBFUSCATE : CLARIFY**

(A) Expand : Widen  
(B) Conceal : Reveal  
(C) Demand : Request  
(D) Complicate : Entangle  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Obfuscate" (to make unclear) is the antonym of "Clarify". The relationship is Antonyms. "Conceal" is the antonym of "Reveal".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Synonyms.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Degrees of the same action.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Synonyms.

**📏 Rule:** Word Analogies  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Determine the relationship (Antonym). Find the antonym pair.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q14. Identify the correctly punctuated sentence:**

(A) The project is delayed; therefore, we need to request an extension.  
(B) The project is delayed therefore we need to request an extension.  
(C) The project is delayed, therefore, we need to request an extension.  
(D) The project is delayed; therefore we need, to request an extension.  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** When a conjunctive adverb (like *therefore*, *however*, *consequently*) connects two independent clauses, it must be preceded by a semicolon and followed by a comma.

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Missing punctuation (run-on sentence).  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** A comma before "therefore" creates a comma splice.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Incorrect comma placement.

**📏 Rule:** Punctuation (Semicolons and Conjunctive Adverbs)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Clause 1 + ; + therefore + , + Clause 2.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Capgemini

**Q15. Select the correct voice change:**
They are reviewing the policy document.

(A) The policy document is reviewed by them.  
(B) The policy document is being reviewed by them.  
(C) The policy document has been reviewed by them.  
(D) The policy document was being reviewed by them.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is in Present Continuous tense ("are reviewing"). In passive, the structure is "is/are being + past participle (V3)". Therefore: "is being reviewed".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Simple present.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Present perfect.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Past continuous.

**📏 Rule:** Active/Passive Voice (Continuous Tenses)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Continuous tense (-ing) in active ALWAYS requires "being" in passive.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** IBM
