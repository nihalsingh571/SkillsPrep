### 6.1 Wipro Test Pattern & Nuances

Wipro hires through the National Level Talent Hunt (NLTH) for the Elite profile, and conducts separate drives for the Turbo (higher package) profile.

#### Structure of the English Section
- **Grammar (8-10 Questions):** High focus on Subject-Verb Agreement, Parallelism, and Tenses. Error spotting and sentence correction are prominent.
- **Vocabulary (6-8 Questions):** Synonyms, Antonyms, and Fill in the blanks. Wipro loves asking the meaning of a word *as it is used in a specific sentence*.
- **Reading Comprehension (6-8 Questions):** Usually 2 short passages or 1 long passage. Topics range from technology trends to HR practices.

``` rule
THE WIPRO CONTEXT RULE:
Wipro rarely asks for standalone synonyms. They will highlight a word in a sentence. You MUST read the sentence because the word might be used in a secondary or metaphorical sense. 
Example: "He banked the aircraft sharply." (Banked means tilted, not a financial institution).
```

### 6.2 Detailed Topic Analysis

#### 6.2.1 Active & Passive Voice
Wipro frequently tests Active/Passive conversions, especially involving modals (can, could, should) and perfect tenses.
- *Rule:* The object becomes the subject. The tense NEVER changes.

#### 6.2.2 Direct & Indirect Speech
Another favorite. Watch out for pronoun changes and tense backshifting (Present -> Past, Past -> Past Perfect).

#### 6.2.3 Vocabulary in Context
You must identify if the word has a positive, negative, or neutral charge based on the sentence before looking at the options.

**Q1. Identify the grammatical error in the sentence:**
Neither the principal nor the teachers (A) / was present in the auditorium (B) / when the chief guest arrived. (C) / No error (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** According to the Rule of Proximity, when two subjects are joined by "Neither...nor", the verb must agree with the subject closest to it. The closest subject is "teachers" (plural). Therefore, the verb should be "were", not "was".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The structure "Neither X nor Y" is perfectly fine.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "when the chief guest arrived" is grammatically correct.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** There is an error.

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement (Rule of Proximity)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** In Either/Or and Neither/Nor, look at the word right before the verb. Teachers -> Plural -> Were.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, TCS

**Q2. Choose the correct passive form of the sentence:**
The company will launch the new software next week.

(A) The new software would be launched by the company next week.  
(B) The new software will be launched by the company next week.  
(C) The new software is going to launch by the company next week.  
(D) The new software will have been launched by the company next week.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is in Future Simple tense ("will launch"). The passive structure is "will be + V3 (past participle)". Therefore, "will be launched".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Changes "will" to "would". Tense/mood should not change in passive voice.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Grammatically incorrect structure.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Changes to Future Perfect tense.

**📏 Rule:** Active/Passive Voice (Future Simple)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** will + V1 -> will be + V3.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Infosys

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Obstinate means stubborn.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Bellicose means aggressive or willing to fight.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Apathetic means showing no interest or enthusiasm.

**📏 Rule:** Contextual Vocabulary

**📊 Difficulty:** Medium

**Q4. Choose the correct indirect speech:**
He said, "I have been working here for three years."

(A) He said that he has been working here for three years.  
(B) He said that he had been working there for three years.  
(C) He told that he had been working there for three years.  
(D) He said that he was working there for three years.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** 
1. "have been working" (Present Perfect Continuous) changes to "had been working" (Past Perfect Continuous).
2. "here" changes to "there".
3. "said" remains "said" (it only changes to "told" if there is a listener, e.g., "said to me").

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Tense and place ("here") didn't change.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "told" requires an object (e.g., told me).  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Changes to Past Continuous instead of Past Perfect Continuous.

**📏 Rule:** Direct to Indirect Speech  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Present Perfect -> Past Perfect. Here -> There.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Capgemini

**Q5. Identify the meaning of the highlighted word based on the context:**
Despite the mounting evidence against him, he remained entirely **resolute** in his claim of innocence.

(A) Wavering  
(B) Unwavering  
(C) Confused  
(D) Aggressive  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Resolute" means admirably purposeful, determined, and unwavering. The phrase "Despite mounting evidence... he remained" shows that he did not change his mind.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Wavering is an antonym.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Confused does not fit the context.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Aggressive is not implied by holding onto a claim.

**📏 Rule:** Contextual Vocabulary  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Resolute -> Resolution -> Firm decision. Unwavering.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Passage:**
Cloud computing has revolutionized how businesses manage their IT infrastructure. Instead of investing heavily in physical servers, companies can now rent computing power on demand. This shift shifts capital expenditure (CapEx) to operational expenditure (OpEx), freeing up cash flow. Furthermore, cloud providers offer robust disaster recovery solutions, ensuring that data is backed up across multiple geographic zones. However, relying on third-party vendors introduces significant data privacy and security compliance risks, particularly for industries handling sensitive health or financial records.

**Q6. According to the passage, how does cloud computing benefit a company's cash flow?**

(A) By eliminating operational expenditure entirely.  
(B) By converting capital expenditure into operational expenditure.  
(C) By increasing capital expenditure for better returns.  
(D) By providing free computing power on demand.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage states: "This shift shifts capital expenditure (CapEx) to operational expenditure (OpEx), freeing up cash flow."

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** It increases OpEx, it doesn't eliminate it.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** It decreases CapEx, it doesn't increase it.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** It says "rent", not "free".

**📏 Rule:** RC - Direct Fact Retrieval  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Match "cash flow" in the passage to the sentence before it.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy

**Q9. Fill in the blank with the correct conditional structure:**
If I had known about the server outage, I ______ the deployment.

(A) will delay  
(B) would delay  
(C) would have delayed  
(D) delayed  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** Third conditional. "If + Past Perfect (had known), Subject + would have + Past Participle (would have delayed)."

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** First conditional structure.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Second conditional structure.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Incorrect grammar.

**📏 Rule:** Third Conditional  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Had + V3 -> Would have + V3.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Cognizant

**Q11. Sentence Improvement:**
The software engineer *is working* on this project since last January.

(A) has been working  
(B) was working  
(C) had worked  
(D) No improvement  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** An action that started in the past (last January) and is still continuing into the present requires the Present Perfect Continuous tense. 

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Past continuous implies the action ended.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Past perfect requires another past action for context.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "is working" is present continuous, which cannot be used with "since".

**📏 Rule:** Tenses with Since/For  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Since/For + Time = Perfect or Perfect Continuous Tense.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, IBM

**Q12. Rearrange the following:**
P. Consequently, the team missed the deadline.
Q. The requirements provided by the client were highly ambiguous.
R. This led to multiple revisions in the code architecture.
S. The developers had to rewrite modules several times.

(A) QRSP  
(B) QSRP  
(C) RQSP  
(D) SQRP  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** 
1. Q introduces the root cause (ambiguous requirements).
2. R explains the immediate effect ("This led to... revisions").
3. S elaborates on the revisions (rewriting modules).
4. P provides the final conclusion ("Consequently... missed deadline").

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** S before R breaks the flow. R introduces the idea of revisions, S explains the severity.  
**❌ Why (C, D) are wrong:** Do not start with the root cause.

**📏 Rule:** Para Jumbles (Cause and Effect)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** P is clearly the conclusion. Q is the opening cause. Only A and B fit. R introduces revisions, S gives detail. General to specific: RS.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q13. Choose the appropriate prepositions:**
The CEO is not completely satisfied ______ the quarterly results and insists ______ a detailed review.

(A) by, on  
(B) with, on  
(C) of, for  
(D) with, for  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The adjective "satisfied" takes the preposition "with". The verb "insists" always takes the preposition "on".

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Satisfied by" is incorrect in this context.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Satisfied of" is incorrect.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Insists for" is a common Indian English mistake; it must be "insists on".

**📏 Rule:** Fixed Prepositions  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Satisfied WITH, Insists ON.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Infosys

**Q15. Find the error:**
One of the most significant (A) / challenges faced by the industry (B) / are the shortage of skilled labor. (C) / No error (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The subject of the sentence is "One", which is singular. The phrase "of the most significant challenges" is a prepositional modifier. Therefore, the verb must be singular ("is", not "are").

**❌ Why (A, B) are wrong:** Plural noun "challenges" after "One of" is correct.  

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement (One of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "One of..." always takes a singular verb.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, TCS
