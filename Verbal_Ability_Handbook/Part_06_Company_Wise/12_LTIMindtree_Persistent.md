**Q1. (LTIMindtree) Fill in the blanks with the most appropriate words:**
Despite the project’s ______ timeline, the team managed to deliver a product of ______ quality.

(A) generous, mediocre  
(B) grueling, exceptional  
(C) relaxed, inferior  
(D) vague, standard  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Despite" indicates a contrast. A difficult (grueling) timeline usually results in bad quality. Therefore, delivering "exceptional" quality creates the required contrast.

**📏 Rule:** Double Fillers / Tone Indicators  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Despite = Contrast. Negative condition (grueling) -> Positive result (exceptional).  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q2. (Persistent) Identify the error:**
The new version of the software (A) / are significantly faster (B) / than the previous (C) / iteration. (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The subject is "version" (singular). The phrase "of the software" is a prepositional modifier. Therefore, the verb must be singular ("is", not "are").

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Ignore "of the software". Version -> is.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Persistent, LTIMindtree  
---

**Q3. (LTIMindtree) Sentence Improvement:**
She *had completed* the report before the manager arrived.

(A) has completed  
(B) was completing  
(C) completed  
(D) No improvement  

**✅ Answer: (D)**

**📖 Explanation:** When two actions happen in the past, the action that happens *first* takes the Past Perfect tense (had + V3), and the action that happens *second* takes the Simple Past tense (V2). Completing the report happened first ("had completed"), and the manager arriving happened second ("arrived"). The sentence is perfectly correct.

**📏 Rule:** Past Perfect Tense (Timeline Rule)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** First action = Had + V3. Second action = V2.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree  
---

**Q4. (Persistent) Choose the synonym for: MITIGATE**

(A) Intensify

(C) Provoke

**Q8. (LTIMindtree) Fill in the blank:**
The CEO, along with the board of directors, ______ attending the summit tomorrow.

(A) are  
(B) is  
(C) have been  
(D) were  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Along with" does not make a subject plural. The verb agrees with the first subject ("CEO"). Therefore, singular "is" is correct.

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement (Accompaniment)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** CEO -> is. Ignore the phrase in between commas.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q10. (LTIMindtree) Rearrange to form a paragraph:**
A. Consequently, the company had to issue a public apology.
B. The software update contained a critical bug.
C. Customers were furious about the data loss.
D. This bug caused thousands of user accounts to be wiped out.

(A) BDCA  
(B) BACD  
(C) CBDA  
(D) DBAC  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** 
1. B introduces the root cause (the bug).
2. D explains what the bug did ("This bug caused...").
3. C explains the immediate reaction to D (Customers furious about the loss).
4. A provides the final conclusion/action ("Consequently... apology").

**📏 Rule:** Para Jumbles (Cause, Effect, Reaction, Conclusion)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "This bug" in D points to B. BD is a mandatory pair. Only option A has BD.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q12. (LTIMindtree) Active to Passive:**
The developers are testing the new API endpoints.

(A) The new API endpoints are tested by the developers.  
(B) The new API endpoints have been tested by the developers.  
(C) The new API endpoints are being tested by the developers.  
(D) The new API endpoints were being tested by the developers.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is Present Continuous ("are testing"). Passive structure: is/are being + V3. 

**📏 Rule:** Voice (Continuous Tense)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** -ing = being.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q13. (Persistent) Vocabulary (Analogy):**
**ENCRYPT : DECRYPT :: CONCEAL : ?**

(A) Hide  
(B) Reveal  
(C) Protect  
(D) Suppress  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Encrypt and Decrypt are antonyms (to encode vs decode). The antonym of Conceal (to hide) is Reveal.

**📏 Rule:** Word Analogies (Antonyms)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find the exact opposite action.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Persistent

**Q14. (LTIMindtree) Sentence Correction:**
If I *was* the CEO, I would increase the R&D budget.

(A) am  
(B) were  
(C) have been  
(D) No correction required  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** This is a Second Conditional sentence referring to an impossible or hypothetical present/future situation. In the subjunctive mood, "was" becomes "were" for all subjects (I, he, she, it).

**📏 Rule:** Subjunctive Mood / Second Conditional  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "If I were..." is always used for hypotheticals (If I were a bird, If I were you).  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree, Persistent

**Q16. (LTIMindtree) Identify the error:**
Hardly the presentation (A) / had started when (B) / the power went out. (C) / No error (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** When a sentence begins with a negative adverb like "Hardly", "Scarcely", or "No sooner", it requires inversion (Auxiliary Verb + Subject). It should be "Hardly had the presentation started".

**📏 Rule:** Inversion with Negative Adverbs  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Hardly + Had + Subject. Never Hardly + Subject + Had.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q18. (LTIMindtree) Which of the following is correct?**

(A) He is senior than me.  
(B) He is senior to me.  
(C) He is more senior than me.  
(D) He is senior then me.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Adjectives ending in "-ior" (senior, junior, superior, inferior) are followed by the preposition "to", not the conjunction "than".

**📏 Rule:** Comparative Adjectives ending in -ior  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Senior TO, Junior TO. Never "than".  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** LTIMindtree

**Q19. (Persistent) Direct to Indirect:**
He said, "I will deploy the code tomorrow."

(A) He said that he would deploy the code tomorrow.  
(B) He said that he will deploy the code the next day.  
(C) He said that he would deploy the code the next day.  
(D) He said that he deployed the code the next day.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Will" changes to "would". "Tomorrow" changes to "the next day" or "the following day".

**📏 Rule:** Indirect Speech (Modals and Time)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Will -> Would. Tomorrow -> The next day.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Persistent
