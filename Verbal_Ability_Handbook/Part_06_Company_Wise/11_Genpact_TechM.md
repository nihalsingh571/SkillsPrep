**Q1. (Genpact) Which is the most professional way to apologize for a delay in an email?**

(A) Sorry for the late reply, I was busy.  
(B) I apologize for the delay in my response.  
(C) My bad for replying late to you.  
(D) Sorry I couldn't get back to you sooner.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Option B uses formal vocabulary ("apologize", "delay", "response") and avoids personal excuses. It is the standard corporate apology.

**📏 Rule:** Email Etiquette  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Avoid slang ("My bad") and unnecessary personal details ("I was busy").  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact

**Q3. (Genpact) Identify the error in the sentence:**
I request you to kindly (A) / grant me leave (B) / for two days (C) / due to a family emergency. (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Request" and "kindly" (or "please") mean the same thing in this context. Using both is a redundancy common in Indian English. It should be "I request you to grant me" OR "Kindly grant me".

**📏 Rule:** Redundancy / Indianisms  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never use "request" and "kindly/please" together.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact

**Q5. (Genpact) Active to Passive Voice:**
The customer service representative resolved the issue within five minutes.

(A) The issue is resolved by the customer service representative within five minutes.  
(B) The issue has been resolved by the customer service representative within five minutes.  
(C) The issue was resolved by the customer service representative within five minutes.  
(D) The issue was being resolved by the customer service representative within five minutes.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is in Simple Past ("resolved"). The passive structure is "was/were + V3". Therefore, "was resolved".

**📏 Rule:** Active/Passive Voice (Simple Past)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** V2 -> Was/Were + V3.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact, Tech Mahindra

**Q10. (Tech Mahindra) Sentence Improvement:**
She *is knowing* the answer to this question.

(A) knows  
(B) has known  
(C) was knowing  
(D) No improvement  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** "Know" is a stative verb (a verb describing a state of being, not a physical action). Stative verbs are generally not used in the continuous (-ing) form. It must be "knows".

**📏 Rule:** Stative Verbs  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Verbs of perception/emotion/cognition (know, love, hate, believe) rarely take -ing.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Tech Mahindra

**Q11. (Genpact) Which phrase is grammatically correct to use in an email?**

(A) Please find attached the document.  
(B) Please find the attached document.  
(C) Both A and B  
(D) Please find document attached.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** While "Please find the attached document" is standard, "Please find attached the document" is widely accepted in business English (where "attached" acts as an adjective modifying the document).

**📏 Rule:** Email Etiquette / Syntax  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Both standard forms are correct. Avoid missing articles (Option D).  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact

**Q13. (Genpact) Identify the error:**
The team lead discussed about (A) / the new project timeline (B) / in the meeting (C) / yesterday morning. (D)

(B) B  
(C) C

**Q15. (Genpact) Direct to Indirect Speech:**
The manager said to me, "Please submit the report by EOD."

(A) The manager said to me to submit the report by EOD.  
(B) The manager requested me to submit the report by EOD.  
(C) The manager ordered me to submitted the report by EOD.  
(D) The manager asked me to please submit the report by EOD.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** 
1. "said to" changes based on the tone. "Please" indicates a request, so "requested" is appropriate.
2. The imperative sentence becomes an infinitive clause ("to submit").
3. We drop "please" in the indirect speech because "requested" already conveys politeness.

**📏 Rule:** Indirect Speech (Imperative Sentences)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Please" + Imperative = Requested + to + V1.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact

**Q17. (Genpact) Rearrange the sentences:**
P. Please let me know if you need any further information.
Q. Thank you for your inquiry regarding our services.
R. I have attached the pricing catalog for your reference.
S. Best regards, John.

(A) QRPS  
(B) RQPS  
(C) QPRS  
(D) PQRS  

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**📖 Explanation:** This is the structure of a standard email.
1. Q: Opening / Acknowledgment.
2. R: Core action (attaching catalog).
3. P: Closing offer to help.
4. S: Sign-off.

**📏 Rule:** Para Jumbles (Email Structure)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Q is the opener. S is the sign-off. Q...S. Option A and C remain. You provide the attachment (R) before asking if they need more info (P). QRPS.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Genpact

**Q18. (Tech Mahindra) SVA Error:**
Neither the manager nor the employees (A) / is willing to (B) / work on (C) / the weekend. (D)

(A) A  
(B) B  
(C) C  
(D) D  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** Rule of Proximity: In "Neither... nor", the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. The closest subject is "employees" (plural). Therefore, "is" should be "are".

**📏 Rule:** Subject-Verb Agreement (Proximity)  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look at the word before the verb. Employees -> plural -> are.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Tech Mahindra
