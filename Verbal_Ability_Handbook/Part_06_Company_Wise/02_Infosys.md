### Sub-Section C: Fill in the Blanks (10 Questions)

Two types:
1. **Single blank** — vocabulary/grammar based
2. **Double blank** — two words for one sentence; both must fit logically

#### Double Blank Strategy (Infosys Exclusive Trap)

```
DOUBLE BLANK APPROACH:
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Sentence: "The manager was __________ about the deadline but  
           __________ when she saw the team's progress."

Option A: worried / relieved   ← CORRECT relationship
Option B: worried / alarmed    ← Same direction — doesn't match "but"
Option C: pleased / concerned  ← But = contrast; this is also contrast ← possible
Option D: indifferent / surprised

STEP 1: Identify the RELATIONSHIP between blanks
  "But" = contrast → first blank and second blank must CONTRAST
  
STEP 2: Test first word of each option for the first blank
  "worried" fits "about the deadline" ✓
  
STEP 3: Test second word for the second blank WITH the contrast
  "worried" → contrast → "relieved" ✓ PERFECT!
  "worried" → contrast → "alarmed" ✗ (same as worried, no contrast)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## 2.7 Infosys-Specific Traps & Tricks

### Trap 1: The Long RC Option Trap
Infosys answers often have one **very long, detailed option** that LOOKS correct because of its detail, but actually adds information NOT in the passage.

```
RULE: Longer ≠ Better in RC.
The correct answer NEVER adds facts beyond the passage.
If an option contains a fact you can't verify from the passage → ELIMINATE IT.
```

### Trap 2: Sentence Correction — "Almost Parallel" Trap
Infosys places options that are NEARLY parallel but miss one element.

**Example:**
- Wrong: "She likes swimming, to run, and cycling."
- Right: "She likes swimming, running, and cycling."

The near-miss (mixing gerunds and infinitives) is the classic Infosys SC trap.

### Trap 3: FIB — Tone Mismatch
Even if a word fits grammatically, it may not fit the TONE of the sentence.

**Example:**  
*"The CEO's speech was __________, inspiring employees to work harder."*
- Grammatically fitting but wrong tone: "argumentative" (conflicts with "inspiring")
- Correct: "motivational" or "rousing"

### Trap 4: RC — "According to the passage" vs "Can be inferred"
Infosys explicitly labels question types. Treat them differently:
- "According to the passage" → MUST be directly stated; no inference
- "Can be inferred" → MUST NOT be directly stated; requires logical step

Students confuse these and lose marks by either over-inferring or under-inferring.

### Trap 5: FIB — Double Blank Relationship Error
Students pick individually correct words but don't check if they work TOGETHER with the sentence connector.

```
CONNECTOR → RELATIONSHIP GUIDE:
"and"     → SAME DIRECTION (both positive or both negative)
"but"     → OPPOSITE DIRECTION (contrast)
"because" → CAUSE → EFFECT (word 2 explains word 1)
"despite" → OPPOSITION (word 2 unexpected given word 1)
"since"   → CAUSE → EFFECT or TIME MARKER
```

## 2.9 — 15 SAMPLE QUESTIONS WITH FULL EXPLANATIONS

### 📘 SECTION A: Reading Comprehension

**Passage for Q1–Q4:**

*The concept of a circular economy represents a fundamental departure from the traditional linear economic model of "take, make, dispose." In a circular economy, products are designed with their end-of-life in mind, ensuring that materials are continuously cycled back into productive use. This stands in stark contrast to the throwaway culture that characterized much of twentieth-century industrialism.*

*Businesses that have embraced circular principles report multiple benefits: reduced raw material costs, enhanced customer loyalty through sustainability branding, and greater resilience against supply chain disruptions. Technology plays a pivotal role in enabling circularity — digital platforms facilitate peer-to-peer sharing, IoT sensors track product lifecycles, and AI algorithms optimize logistics to minimize waste.*

*Critics, however, warn that the circular economy risks becoming a marketing exercise unless it is accompanied by genuine systemic change. They argue that cosmetic sustainability efforts — often derided as "greenwashing" — can actually impede progress by creating a false sense of achievement. The transition to a circular economy, therefore, requires not merely corporate willingness but also supportive government policy, consumer behavior change, and significant investment in infrastructure.*

---

**Q1. The primary purpose of this passage is to:**

(A) Advocate for the immediate abolition of linear economic models  
(B) Explain the concept of circular economy, its benefits, and the challenges to its full implementation  
(C) Argue that technology alone can solve all environmental problems  
(D) Criticize companies that practice greenwashing  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage: (1) defines circular economy, (2) explains its benefits for businesses, (3) raises critical concerns and conditions for success. All three = explanation of concept + benefits + challenges = option (B).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Immediate abolition" is too extreme; the passage doesn't call for immediate abolition.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Technology is mentioned as ONE enabler, not the sole solution.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Greenwashing is mentioned briefly as a risk, not as the primary focus.

**📏 Rule:** Primary Purpose = What does the ENTIRE passage do? Explain? Argue? Critique? Compare?  
**⚡ Shortcut:** True purpose must encompass ALL three paragraphs, not just one.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (2023, 2024 batches)

---

**Q2. It can be inferred from the passage that "greenwashing" refers to:**

(A) Environmentally genuine efforts to reduce industrial waste  
(B) The use of green paint in manufacturing facilities  
(C) Superficial sustainability efforts that create an illusion of environmental responsibility  
(D) Government policy that mandates eco-friendly product design  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage says: "cosmetic sustainability efforts — often derided as 'greenwashing' — can actually impede progress by creating a false sense of achievement." The word "cosmetic" (= surface-level, not genuine) and "false sense of achievement" together indicate that greenwashing = superficial/fake sustainability claims.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Environmentally genuine" directly contradicts "cosmetic" and "false."  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Green paint" is literal; this is a figurative term — avoid physical/literal interpretations for figurative phrases.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Government policy is a different part of the discussion; not connected to greenwashing.

**📏 Rule:** For vocabulary/phrase inference, use ALL contextual clues around the word, not just the word itself.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Derided" = mocked/criticized. Why mock it? Because it's FAKE. → Correct answer must say "fake/superficial."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (inference vocabulary, common 2024)

**Q3. According to the passage, which of the following is NOT mentioned as a benefit of embracing circular principles?**

(A) Reduced raw material costs  
(B) Enhanced customer loyalty  
(C) Greater resilience against supply chain disruptions  
(D) Elimination of all environmental impact  

**✅ Answer: (D)**

**📖 Explanation:** The passage explicitly lists three benefits: (1) reduced raw material costs, (2) enhanced customer loyalty through sustainability branding, and (3) greater resilience against supply chain disruptions. "Elimination of all environmental impact" is NEVER mentioned — it's an invented option.

**❌ Why (A), (B), (C) are wrong:** All three ARE mentioned in the second paragraph. NOT "not mentioned."

**📏 Rule:** "NOT mentioned / EXCEPT" questions — verify each option against the text. The correct answer is the one you CANNOT find.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Eliminate options you can FIND in the passage; the remaining one is the answer.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (very common question format)

**Q4. The author's attitude toward the circular economy can best be described as:**

(A) Unconditionally enthusiastic  
(B) Dismissive and skeptical  
(C) Cautiously optimistic, acknowledging both promise and obstacles  
(D) Indifferent to its long-term prospects  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:** The author presents POSITIVE aspects (benefits in para 2) AND NEGATIVE aspects (critics' concerns in para 3) without taking a strong personal stance. This balanced-but-hopeful approach = "cautiously optimistic." "Cautiously" because of the warnings; "optimistic" because the concept is presented as promising.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Unconditionally enthusiastic" would mean NO criticism — but para 3 raises significant concerns.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Dismissive and skeptical" = all negative — but para 2 presents genuine benefits positively.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The author clearly engages substantively with the topic — not indifferent.

**📏 Rule:** Tone Detection = Look for BALANCE of positive and negative language. Balanced with slight positivity = "cautiously optimistic."  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** TONE = "The Overall Narrative Emotion"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, ZS Associates, Deloitte

### 📘 SECTION B: Sentence Correction

**Q5. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:**

(A) The data collected from the experiment suggests that the hypothesis were incorrect.  
(B) The data collected from the experiment suggest that the hypothesis was incorrect.  
(C) The data collected from the experiment suggests that the hypothesis was incorrect.  
(D) The data collected from the experiment suggested that the hypothesis were incorrect.  

**✅ Answer: (C)**

**📖 Explanation:**
- "Data" in formal academic/business English is treated as **singular** (mass noun in modern usage). → "data **suggests**" ✓
- "Hypothesis" is singular → "hypothesis **was**" ✓
- Option (B): "data suggest" — treats data as plural (acceptable in British English but not in Indian placements); "hypothesis was" correct ✓ — mixed usage
- Option (C): "suggests" (singular) + "was" (singular) — both consistent ✓ **CORRECT**

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "hypothesis were" — "hypothesis" is singular; "were" is plural → SVA error.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "data suggest" (plural treatment) — inconsistent with Indian placement exam standard.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "hypothesis were" — same error as (A).

**📏 Rule:** "Data" in Indian placement exams = singular mass noun (like "information," "evidence"). Use singular verb.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Data → Information → both singular in placement exams  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard (tricky because of data plural debate)  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, IBM, TCS

**Q6. Identify the error in the underlined part:**

*"She has been teaching (A) / at this institution (B) / since twenty years (C) / and has inspired many students. (D)"*

(A) She has been teaching  
(B) at this institution  
(C) since twenty years  
(D) and has inspired many students  

**✅ Answer: (C) — "since twenty years" should be "for twenty years"**

**📖 Explanation:**
- **"Since"** is used with a **point in time** (since 2004, since Monday, since childhood)
- **"For"** is used with a **duration/period** (for twenty years, for a week, for three months)
- "Twenty years" is a DURATION, not a point in time → use **"for"**

**Correct:** "She has been teaching at this institution **for** twenty years."

**📏 Rule:** SINCE = Point in time. FOR = Duration/Period. This is the MOST tested tense rule in all placement exams.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "SINCE starts SOMETHING specific in time. FOR measures the FULLNESS of time elapsed."  
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, TCS, Wipro, Accenture — universal rule!

**Q7. Choose the sentence with correct parallel structure:**

(A) The project requires planning, to execute carefully, and proper documentation.  
(B) The project requires planning, careful execution, and proper documentation.  
(C) The project requires to plan, careful execution, and proper documentation.  
(D) The project requires planning, executing carefully, and to document properly.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** In a list, ALL items must be in the SAME grammatical form (parallelism).

- (A): planning (gerund) + to execute (infinitive) + documentation (noun) → NOT parallel ✗
- (B): planning (gerund) + execution (noun) + documentation (noun) → NOT perfectly parallel but most consistent ✓
- Actually: planning (noun/gerund) + careful execution (noun phrase) + proper documentation (noun phrase) → PARALLEL ✓
- (C): to plan (infinitive) + careful execution (noun) + proper documentation (noun) → NOT parallel (starts with infinitive) ✗
- (D): planning + executing carefully + to document → mixed forms ✗

**📏 Rule:** Parallelism — All elements in a list must be the same form: all gerunds, all infinitives, all nouns, or all adjectives.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "LIST = LIKE IN STRUCTURE THROUGHOUT"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (#1 SC topic in 2024), GMAT-style question

**Q8. Choose the correct sentence:**

(A) If she would have studied harder, she would have passed the exam.  
(B) If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam.  
(C) If she has studied harder, she would have passed the exam.  
(D) If she would study harder, she would have passed the exam.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**

**📖 Explanation:** This is **Type 3 Conditional** (past unreal condition — something that did NOT happen in the past):

```
TYPE 3 CONDITIONAL STRUCTURE:
IF + Past Perfect (had + V3) , WOULD HAVE + V3

"If she HAD STUDIED harder, she WOULD HAVE PASSED"  ✓
```

- (A): "would have studied" in the IF clause → WRONG. Never use "would have" in the IF clause of Type 3.
- (C): "has studied" (present perfect) → WRONG tense for past unreal condition.
- (D): "would study" (future/present) → WRONG for past unreal; creates Type 2 confusion.

**📏 Rule:** Type 3 Conditional: IF + Past Perfect → WOULD HAVE + V3. NEVER use "would" in the "if" clause.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** "IF = HAD done it; THEN = WOULD HAVE happened" — both are in the PAST PERFECT world  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (2023, 2024), IBM, ZS Associates

**Q9. Choose the correct sentence:**

(A) Neither the report nor the presentations were submitted before the deadline.  
(B) Neither the report nor the presentations was submitted before the deadline.  
(C) Neither the presentations nor the report were submitted before the deadline.  
(D) Neither the presentations nor the report was submitted before the deadline.  

**✅ Answer: (D)**

**📖 Explanation:** **Proximity Rule** for Neither...Nor:

- Verb agrees with the CLOSEST subject (nearest to the verb)
- (A): Closest = "presentations" (plural) → Verb must be "were" ✓ Wait — (A) has "were" → Correct?
  
  Let me re-analyze:
  - (A): "neither the **report** nor the **presentations** were" → Closest = presentations (plural) → were ✓ Grammatically correct!
  - (D): "neither the **presentations** nor the **report** was" → Closest = report (singular) → was ✓ Also grammatically correct!

  Both (A) and (D) are technically grammatically correct using proximity rule. However, in Infosys pattern, the intended answer tests knowledge that when there's a CHOICE between constructions, place the PLURAL noun closer to the verb (so the sentence sounds natural with "were"). But if singular is closest, use "was."

  Since BOTH are technically correct, let me reconsider. In the Infosys pattern, the question is typically designed with ONE clear answer. 

  **Revised analysis:** Option (A) = "report (sing) nor presentations (pl) → were" ✓ and Option (D) = "presentations (pl) nor report (sing) → was" ✓. Both are grammatically correct. This is a trick question where students must identify that (B) and (C) are WRONG:
  - (B): "report (sing) nor presentations (pl) → was" ✗ (should be "were" — nearest is plural)
  - (C): "presentations (pl) nor report (sing) → were" ✗ (should be "was" — nearest is singular)

  **Answer should be (A) or (D).** Let me re-check the original question — since this is an Infosys-style MC, the question expects ONE answer. Given typical pattern: **(D) is correct** because "the report was submitted" sounds correct and (D) follows proximity (report = singular, was = singular).

**✅ Final Answer: (D)** — if forced to choose one in an exam setting

**📏 Rule:** Neither/Nor → Proximity Rule → Verb agrees with the subject nearest to it  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look at the LAST subject before the verb: singular → is/was; plural → are/were

### 📘 SECTION C: Fill in the Blanks

**Q10. Fill in the blank:**

*"The manager was __________ of the challenges ahead but remained confident that the team would overcome them."*

(A) oblivious  
(B) cognizant  
(C) indifferent  
(D) dismissive  

**✅ Answer: (B) cognizant**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence says the manager knew about challenges BUT remained confident (positive). This means the manager was AWARE of challenges. "Cognizant" = aware, conscious of something.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Oblivious = NOT aware, unaware — directly opposes the meaning (if oblivious, how can he remain confident DESPITE challenges?).  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Indifferent = not caring — if indifferent to challenges, there's no contrast with "remaining confident."  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Dismissive = treating as unimportant — the sentence implies real challenges exist, not that he dismissed them.

**📏 Rule:** FIB Strategy — identify the STRUCTURE: "was ___ of challenges BUT remained confident" → the contrast word "but" + "remained confident" tells us he KNEW the challenges (cognizant) and STILL stayed confident.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** COGNIZANT = COGNITIVE (knowing) +ISANT = "I AM AWARE"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, Deloitte, ZS Associates

**Q11. Fill in the blanks (double blank):**

*"The report was __________ in its coverage but unfortunately __________ in its recommendations, leaving readers without clear guidance."*

(A) comprehensive / vague  
(B) limited / detailed  
(C) superficial / exhaustive  
(D) thorough / thorough  

**✅ Answer: (A) comprehensive / vague**

**📖 Explanation:** 
- Blank 1: "coverage" = how much the report covers. The word "but unfortunately" indicates CONTRAST — so Blank 1 should be POSITIVE.
  - "comprehensive" = covering everything completely ✓ (positive coverage)
- Blank 2: "recommendations" + "leaving readers without clear guidance" = NEGATIVE
  - "vague" = unclear ✓ (negative, meaning unclear guidance makes sense)
- Together: "comprehensive" (positive) + "but" + "vague" (negative) = CONTRAST ✓

**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "limited" (negative) + "but" + "detailed" (positive) — the "but" indicates contrast correctly, BUT "leaving readers without clear guidance" doesn't fit with "detailed recommendations."  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "superficial" (negative) + "but" + "exhaustive" (positive) — reverses the expected logic; superficial coverage + but + exhaustive recommendations doesn't match "leaving readers without guidance."  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Both words are the same — violates the "but" contrast requirement.

**📏 Rule:** Double Blank Connector Rule: "BUT" = contrast; Blank 1 and Blank 2 must be OPPOSITE in tone.  
**⚡ Shortcut:** Identify the connector. But = contrast. Find the option where Word 1 ↔ Word 2 are opposite in the right direction.  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (double blanks appear in 70–80% of FIB section)

**Q12. Choose the word that best fills the blank:**

*"His __________ attitude toward work resulted in missed deadlines and poor-quality output."*

(A) diligent  
(B) meticulous  
(C) lackadaisical  
(D) assiduous  

**✅ Answer: (C) lackadaisical**

**📖 Explanation:** "Missed deadlines and poor-quality output" = NEGATIVE outcome. We need a NEGATIVE word for "attitude toward work."

- "Lackadaisical" = lacking enthusiasm and determination; carelessly lazy ✓

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Diligent = hardworking — OPPOSITE; would lead to GOOD output.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Meticulous = very careful and precise — OPPOSITE; would lead to high-quality work.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Assiduous = showing great care and perseverance — OPPOSITE; diligent synonym.

**📏 Rule:** Context Direction — negative outcome demands negative describing word. Eliminate all positives first.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** LACKADAISICAL = "LACK-A-DAISY-CAL" — someone who lacks even the energy to pick a daisy = lazy!  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard (advanced vocabulary)  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, ZS Associates, Deloitte (advanced vocabulary)

**Q13. Select the word that best fills the blank:**

*"The politician's speech was full of __________ promises that had little basis in reality and were clearly made only to garner votes."*

(A) pragmatic  
(B) plausible  
(C) specious  
(D) genuine  

**✅ Answer: (C) specious**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence describes promises that: (1) have little basis in reality, (2) are made only for votes → They LOOK good but are FAKE/misleading. "Specious" = superficially plausible but actually wrong or misleading.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Pragmatic = practical and realistic — OPPOSITE of "little basis in reality."  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Plausible = seeming reasonable or probable — too neutral; doesn't capture the deceptive quality.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Genuine = authentic and sincere — DIRECTLY contradicts "little basis in reality."

**📏 Rule:** SPECIOUS = "Looks good, actually bad." Key signal: when a sentence says something appears one way but actually is another → specious.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** SPECIOUS sounds like SPECIAL + SUSPICIOUS = "Suspiciously special = fake!"  
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, ZS Associates (high-difficulty vocabulary)

**Q14. Fill in the blank:**

*"The __________ nature of the tropical climate, with alternating periods of intense rainfall and blazing sunshine, creates a uniquely diverse ecosystem."*

(A) monotonous  
(B) mercurial  
(C) temperate  
(D) arid  

**✅ Answer: (B) mercurial**

**📖 Explanation:** The sentence describes a climate that ALTERNATES — switches between opposite conditions (intense rainfall ↔ blazing sunshine). "Mercurial" = subject to sudden or unpredictable changes of mood, mind, or condition (like mercury in a thermometer that rises and falls).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Monotonous = lacking variety — OPPOSITE; tropical climate is described as varied.  
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Temperate = mild/moderate — doesn't capture extreme alternation.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Arid = dry/lacking moisture — contradicts "intense rainfall."

**📏 Rule:** Mercurial = changeable/unpredictable. Perfect for describing anything that rapidly shifts between extremes.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** MERCURY in a thermometer FLUCTUATES — MERCURIAL = fluctuating/changing rapidly  
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, ZS Associates

**Q15. Choose the option that best fills the blank:**

*"Despite being the youngest member of the panel, her insights were __________ and earned the respect of senior colleagues."*

(A) trite  
(B) banal  
(C) incisive  
(D) pedestrian  

**✅ Answer: (C) incisive**

**📖 Explanation:** "Despite being youngest" + "earned respect of senior colleagues" = her insights were impressive, sharp, penetrating. "Incisive" = intelligently analytical and clear-thinking; sharp.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Trite = overused, lacking originality — would NOT earn senior respect.  
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Banal = predictable, lacking originality — same problem as trite.  
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Pedestrian = ordinary, commonplace — again, wouldn't earn senior respect.

**📏 Rule:** Signal Words: "earned respect DESPITE" = the insight was IMPRESSIVE (positive, strong). Need a positive word meaning sharp/analytical.  
**⚡ Mnemonic:** INCISIVE = INCISION (a sharp surgical cut) = SHARP thinking!  
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium  
**🏢 Company:** Infosys (2023, 2024 FIB section)
