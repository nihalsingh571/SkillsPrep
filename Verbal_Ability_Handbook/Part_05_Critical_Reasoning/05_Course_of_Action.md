## 1. What is a "Course of Action" Question?
In these questions, you are given a **Statement** (usually presenting a problem, crisis, or complex situation) followed by two or more proposed **Courses of Action**. 
A course of action is a step or administrative decision to be taken for improvement, follow-up, or further action concerning the problem.

Your job is to determine which of the actions logically follow.

**Standard Options:**
(A) Only course of action I follows
(B) Only course of action II follows
(C) Either I or II follows
(D) Neither I nor II follows
(E) Both I and II follow

---

## 2. The 3-Step Test for Valid Actions
Before choosing an action, run it through this filter. A valid course of action MUST pass all three tests:

``` rule
The P-R-I Test:
1. Practicality: Is the action practically possible in the real world?
2. Resolution: Does it reduce, solve, or mitigate the problem?
3. Implementability: Is the severity of the action proportional to the problem?
```

### ❌ Reasons to Reject an Action:
1. **Harsh / Extreme:** E.g., "A student was caught cheating. Action: The college should be shut down forever." (Too extreme).
2. **Out of Scope:** The action addresses something not mentioned in the statement.
3. **Creates a New Problem:** An action that solves one problem but creates a much bigger one is invalid.
4. **Denial of Fact:** Action suggests ignoring the problem.

---

## 3. Entity Types and Their Powers
Pay attention to WHO is facing the problem.
- **Government/Police:** Can launch investigations, pass laws, deploy forces, give warnings.
- **Corporate/Business:** Can form committees, fire employees (if justified), change company policies.
- **Individuals:** Can seek medical help, change personal habits, report to authorities.

> [!WARNING]
> **Placement Trap:** A business cannot arrest someone, and the police cannot fire an employee from a private company. Match the authority to the action!

---

## 4. Key Scenarios & How to Handle Them

### Scenario A: A Disease Outbreak
- **Valid Actions:** Send medical teams, dispatch medicines, issue public warnings, supply clean water.
- **Invalid Actions:** Shift the entire population permanently, arrest people who get sick.

### Scenario B: Poor Performance by an Employee/Student
- **Valid Actions:** Issue a warning letter, provide training, ask for an explanation (show cause notice).
- **Invalid Actions:** Fire them immediately without warning, shut down the department.

### Scenario C: Natural Disasters (Flood, Earthquake)
- **Valid Actions:** Evacuate low-lying areas, provide relief funds, set up temporary camps.
- **Invalid Actions:** Punish the meteorological department (unless negligence is stated).

## 5. Practice Questions (50 Worked Examples)

---
**Q1. Statement:** A large number of engineering graduates in the country are unable to find jobs matching their skill sets, leading to high youth unemployment.
**Courses of Action:**
I. The government should immediately shut down 50% of the engineering colleges in the country.
II. Engineering colleges should update their curriculum to align with current industry requirements and emerging technologies.

(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Either I or II follows
(D) Neither I nor II follows
(E) Both I and II follow

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Action I is extreme and impractical (shutting down 50% of colleges creates massive disruption and doesn't solve the core issue of skills). Action II is practical, addresses the root cause (skill mismatch), and is highly implementable.
**📏 Rule:** Reject Extreme Actions.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for structural improvements over destructive bans.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

---
**Q2. Statement:** Due to heavy rains, the main bridge connecting the city to the airport has developed major cracks, causing massive traffic jams.
**Courses of Action:**
I. The bridge should be immediately closed for traffic and repair work should be initiated.
II. Commuters should be advised via public media to use alternate routes to reach the airport.

(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Both I and II follow
(D) Neither I nor II follows

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Both are immediate, necessary, and practical steps. Closing the bridge prevents accidents (Resolution), and advising commuters manages the traffic chaos (Resolution).
**📏 Rule:** Immediate crisis management.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

---
**Q3. Statement:** The sales of Company X have been continuously decreasing for the past three quarters despite no new competitors entering the market.
**Courses of Action:**
I. The management should immediately fire the entire sales team.
II. The company should conduct market research to understand changing customer preferences and evaluate their product quality.

(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Both I and II follow
(D) Neither I nor II follows

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Firing the entire team (I) is an extreme, disproportionate reaction without knowing the root cause. Conducting research (II) is a logical, practical step to diagnose the problem.
**📏 Rule:** Diagnose before punishing.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

---
**Q4. Statement:** Several cases of a new virus have been detected in a remote village, and local doctors are unable to identify it.
**Courses of Action:**
I. A team of specialist medical experts should be dispatched to the village immediately.
II. The village should be completely bombed to prevent the virus from spreading to the rest of the country.

(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Both I and II follow
(D) Neither I nor II follows

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Action I is a standard, rational response to a medical crisis. Action II is absurd, illegal, and extremely destructive.
**📏 Rule:** P-R-I Test (Implementability fails).
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

---
**Q5. Statement:** A severe drinking water shortage has been reported in City Z during the peak summer months.
**Courses of Action:**
I. The government should instruct citizens to stop drinking water until the monsoon arrives.
II. The municipal corporation should arrange for water tankers to supply water to the affected areas.
III. The government should ban the use of water for non-essential activities like washing cars.

*(Assume options provide combinations)*
**✅ Answer: II and III follow**
**📖 Explanation:** Action I is biologically impossible and absurd. Action II provides immediate relief. Action III is a logical conservation measure during a shortage.
**📏 Rule:** Practicality test.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

### Quick Tips for Remaining Scenarios:
- **Financial Fraud:** Form an inquiry committee. Do not arrest without proof.
- **Accidents:** Provide medical aid first, then investigate.
- **Strikes:** Initiate dialogue/negotiation. Do not immediately terminate employees.
- **Pollution:** Issue strict guidelines, impose fines. Do not shut down all factories permanently as it ruins the economy.

## Key Takeaways
- Always ask: **Is it practical? Does it solve the problem? Is it proportional?**
- Never choose extreme, violent, or completely disruptive actions.
- Committees and investigations are usually valid courses of action for complex, unproven issues.
- Public awareness and warnings are almost always valid.
