## 1. Identifying Causal Relationships
A causal argument claims that one event (the Cause) directly makes another event (the Effect) happen. 

``` rule
The Causal Formula:
Event A (Cause) -> Event B (Effect)
```

### Signal Words for Causation:
- Caused by
- Leads to
- Results in
- Responsible for
- Due to
- Produces / Generates
- Therefore (when linking two events)

**Example:** "The implementation of the four-day workweek **resulted in** a 15% increase in employee productivity."
- *Cause:* Four-day workweek
- *Effect:* 15% increase in productivity

---

## 2. Correlation vs. Causation (The Post Hoc Fallacy)
The absolute biggest trap in Critical Reasoning is the assumption that because two things happen together (Correlation), one must cause the other (Causation).
*Post hoc ergo propter hoc* = "After this, therefore because of this."

> [!CAUTION]
> **The Golden Rule of Causation:** Just because Event B follows Event A, it does NOT mean Event A caused Event B.

**Example of the Trap:**
"Every time ice cream sales increase, the number of drowning incidents at the beach increases. Therefore, eating ice cream causes drowning."
- *Reality:* There is a third underlying factor (Summer/Hot weather) that causes BOTH ice cream sales to rise AND more people to swim (leading to more drownings).

---

## 3. How to Weaken a Causal Argument
When an author claims A causes B, you can destroy their argument in 3 ways:

1. **Find an Alternate Cause:** Show that C actually caused B.
   - *Argument:* "The new training module improved coding speed."
   - *Weakener:* "During the same period, the company upgraded all employee laptops to high-speed processors." (Alternate cause for speed).
2. **Reverse the Causation:** Show that B actually caused A.
   - *Argument:* "People who use productivity apps get promoted faster. Therefore, the apps lead to promotions."
   - *Weakener:* "Highly motivated and ambitious employees are the ones most likely to download productivity apps." (Ambition causes both).
3. **Show A without B, or B without A:** Break the correlation.
   - *Weakener:* "The marketing team took the same training module but their speed did not increase." (A happened, B didn't).

---

## 4. How to Strengthen a Causal Argument
To strengthen the claim that A causes B, you do the exact opposite of weakening:

1. **Rule out Alternate Causes:** 
   - *Strengthener:* "There were no other changes in hardware, management, or workload during the period the training module was used."
2. **Show that when A is absent, B is absent:**
   - *Strengthener:* "Employees who skipped the training module saw zero increase in coding speed."
3. **Show that A always leads to B:**
   - *Strengthener:* "In three other departments, using the training module also led to identical increases in speed."

> [!TIP]
> **Placement Strategy:** In "Weaken" questions for causal arguments, 80% of the correct answers introduce an **Alternate Cause**. Look for a new piece of information that explains the outcome better than the author's premise.

**Q1. The introduction of the new AI-powered code review tool in January was followed by a 30% drop in software bugs reported by clients in Q1. Therefore, the AI tool successfully eliminated bugs from our software.**

Which of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the argument?

(A) The AI tool is very expensive to maintain.
(B) In January, the company also hired a team of 50 senior Quality Assurance engineers.
(C) Software bugs are a major reason for client churn.
(D) The AI tool takes longer to review code than a human developer.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The argument claims A (AI tool) caused B (30% drop in bugs). Option B introduces an Alternate Cause (C): hiring 50 senior QA engineers. This alternative perfectly explains the drop in bugs, destroying the claim that the AI tool was solely responsible.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Cost is irrelevant to whether the tool reduced bugs.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Out of scope. We don't care about client churn.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Speed is irrelevant; we only care about the *number* of bugs.
**📏 Rule:** Weaken by finding an Alternate Cause.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Look for another major change that happened at the same time.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Infosys

**Q2. A study showed that individuals who drink three cups of green tea a day are less likely to develop heart disease. The researchers concluded that green tea prevents heart disease.**

Which of the following, if true, points out a logical flaw in the researchers' conclusion?

(A) Green tea contains high levels of antioxidants.
(B) People who drink green tea regularly also tend to exercise more and maintain healthier diets than those who do not.
(C) Some people who drink green tea still develop heart disease.
(D) Black tea has also been shown to have health benefits.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The argument claims A (Green tea) causes B (Less heart disease). Option B shows that a third factor (exercise/healthy diet) correlates with green tea drinking. This third factor is likely the real cause of lower heart disease, pointing out the correlation vs causation fallacy.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** This strengthens the argument by providing a biological mechanism.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** A cause doesn't have to be 100% foolproof to be a cause.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Irrelevant. Black tea doesn't invalidate green tea's effects.
**📏 Rule:** Alternate cause / Third variable.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Capgemini
---

---
**Q3. Cities with higher numbers of police officers per capita have higher crime rates. Therefore, hiring more police officers causes crime to increase.**

Which of the following best exposes the flaw in the argument above?

(A) It fails to consider that police officers are usually deployed to areas where crime is already high.
(B) It assumes that all crimes are reported to the police.
(C) It ignores the fact that different cities define "crime" differently.
(D) It does not account for the cost of hiring police officers.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The argument claims A (More police) causes B (More crime). Option A reverses the causation: B (More crime) actually causes A (Cities hire more police). This destroys the argument.
**❌ Why (B), (C), (D) are wrong:** None of these address the causal link between police presence and crime rates.
**📏 Rule:** Reverse Causation.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Does A cause B, or does B cause A?
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Cognizant
---

---
**Q4. A tech startup provided free gym memberships to its employees. Within six months, sick leaves decreased by 40%. The HR director concluded that the gym membership improved employee health.**

Which of the following, if true, most strongly supports the HR director's conclusion?

(A) Most employees used the gym membership at least three times a week.
(B) Another startup that provided free lunches saw a 20% decrease in sick leaves.
(C) Employees who did not use the gym membership had the same number of sick leaves as before.
(D) The gym membership cost the company a significant amount of money.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** To strengthen a causal claim (Gym -> Health), show that when the cause is absent, the effect is absent. Option C shows exactly this: No gym = No decrease in sick leave. This powerfully confirms the causal link.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Just because they used it doesn't prove it caused the drop in sick leave (maybe a flu season ended).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Free lunches at a different company are irrelevant.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Cost is out of scope.
**📏 Rule:** Strengthen by showing "Without A, no B".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Wipro

## Key Takeaways
- **Causation ≠ Correlation.** Just because things happen together doesn't mean one causes the other.
- **Weakeners:** Look for Alternate Causes or Reversed Causation.
- **Strengtheners:** Look for statements that block alternate causes or prove the "No Cause = No Effect" rule.
- Do not be distracted by options discussing "cost," "morality," or "time" unless the conclusion specifically mentions them. Focus on the core causal link.
