## 1. The Anatomy of Strengthen / Weaken
Every argument has a gap between the Premise and the Conclusion. This gap is the **Assumption**.
- **To Strengthen:** You must provide new information that proves the assumption is TRUE, or rules out other possibilities.
- **To Weaken:** You must provide new information that proves the assumption is FALSE, or shows a fatal flaw in the logic.

``` rule
The Golden Rule of S&W:
DO NOT attack the premise. The premise is a fact. 
You must attack (or support) the JUMP from the premise to the conclusion.
```

---

## 2. How to Strengthen an Argument

### Techniques:
1. **Confirm the Assumption:** State explicitly that the unstated assumption is true.
2. **Provide Additional Evidence:** Give a new fact/statistic that backs up the conclusion.
3. **Rule Out Alternate Causes:** If the argument claims A caused B, prove that C, D, and E did NOT cause B.
4. **Show a Control Group:** Show that when the cause is absent, the effect does not happen.

**Example Argument:**
*Premise:* The new marketing campaign features a popular celebrity.
*Conclusion:* Therefore, our sales will increase next month.
*(Assumption: The celebrity's presence influences people to buy the product).*

**Strengthener:** Market research shows that 80% of our target demographic makes purchasing decisions based on celebrity endorsements. (Confirms the assumption).

---

## 3. How to Weaken an Argument

### Techniques:
1. **Attack the Assumption:** Show that the unstated assumption is false.
2. **Provide Counter-Evidence:** Give a new fact that contradicts the conclusion.
3. **Show an Alternate Cause:** Prove that something else caused the effect.
4. **Highlight Unrepresentative Samples:** Show that the data used in the premise doesn't apply to the conclusion.

**Weakener (for the celebrity argument):** The celebrity recently faced a massive public scandal and is currently boycotted by the target demographic. (Attacks the assumption that the celebrity is a positive influence).

---

## 4. Scope Matching (The Elimination Technique)
Most wrong answers in S&W questions are "Out of Scope." 
- If the argument is about **profits**, an option about **employee happiness** is wrong (unless it explicitly links to profits).
- If the argument is about **percentage**, an option about **absolute numbers** might be a trap.
- If the argument is about **dogs**, an option about **cats** is irrelevant.

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** Use the **"So What?" Test**. Read the option and ask, "So what? Does this make the conclusion more or less likely?" If the answer is "It doesn't matter," eliminate it.

## 5. Practice Questions: Strengthen (50 Questions Core Concepts)

---
**Q1. Argument:** The city council has decided to plant 10,000 trees in the downtown area. The mayor claims that this will significantly reduce the city's air pollution levels.

Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the mayor's claim?
(A) The cost of planting 10,000 trees is within the city's budget.
(B) The specific species of trees being planted are scientifically proven to absorb 50% more toxins from the air than average trees.
(C) Several other cities have planted trees, though their pollution levels remained the same.
(D) Citizens are volunteering to help plant the trees.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The conclusion is that trees will reduce pollution. Option (B) provides direct new evidence that these specific trees are highly effective at absorbing toxins, directly supporting the conclusion.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Cost (budget) does not affect whether the trees will reduce pollution.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** This actually weakens the argument by showing a failed precedent.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Who plants them doesn't affect their ability to clean the air.
**📏 Rule:** Provide specific supporting evidence.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

---
**Q2. Argument:** To increase productivity, TechCorp is reducing its working hours from 9 hours to 7 hours a day. Management believes that rested employees will output more code.

Which of the following most strongly supports management's belief?
(A) Most employees at TechCorp currently spend 2 hours a day on non-work-related internet browsing due to fatigue.
(B) Employees have requested a pay raise.
(C) A competitor increased their working hours to 10 hours a day.
(D) The company provides free coffee in the break room.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** If employees waste 2 hours due to fatigue, reducing the day by 2 hours eliminates the waste, meaning actual working time remains the same but fatigue decreases. This strongly supports that they will be more productive.
**❌ Why (B), (C), (D) are wrong:** Out of scope.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

## 6. Practice Questions: Weaken (50 Questions Core Concepts)

---
**Q3. Argument:** Fast food chain 'BurgerKing' recently introduced a vegan burger. Since its introduction, overall store revenues have increased by 15%. The CEO claims that the vegan burger is a massive financial success that is driving new customers to the stores.

Which of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the CEO's claim?
(A) The vegan burger costs more to produce than a regular beef burger.
(B) During the same month the vegan burger was introduced, the chain launched a massive 50% off discount on all its classic beef burgers.
(C) Vegan diets are becoming increasingly popular nationwide.
(D) Some customers complained about the taste of the vegan burger.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The CEO assumes a causal link: Vegan Burger -> Increased Revenue. Option (B) provides an **Alternate Cause**: The 50% discount on classic burgers is likely the real reason revenues (or foot traffic) went up, totally undermining the CEO's claim about the vegan burger.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Production cost doesn't change the fact that revenue increased.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** This would strengthen the argument.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Some" customers complaining doesn't negate a 15% overall revenue increase.
**📏 Rule:** Find an Alternate Cause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Accenture

---
**Q4. Argument:** Studies show that teenagers who play violent video games are more likely to exhibit aggressive behavior at school. Therefore, to reduce school violence, parents must ban their teenagers from playing these games.

Which of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the argument?
(A) Many non-violent video games are also very popular among teenagers.
(B) Teenagers who are already prone to aggressive behavior are naturally drawn to playing violent video games.
(C) School violence has decreased slightly in the last decade.
(D) Parents find it difficult to monitor what video games their children play.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The argument assumes Causation: Games -> Aggression. Option (B) demonstrates **Reverse Causality**: Aggression -> Games. If teenagers are already aggressive and *therefore* choose these games, banning the games won't fix their underlying aggression, destroying the conclusion.
**❌ Why (A) and (C) are wrong:** Out of scope.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Practical difficulty doesn't mean the ban wouldn't work if implemented; it just means it's hard to implement.
**📏 Rule:** Reverse the Causal Direction.
**📊 Difficulty:** Expert
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

## Key Takeaways
- **Strengthen:** Close the gap, prove the assumption, provide a control group, block alternatives.
- **Weaken:** Widen the gap, destroy the assumption, provide alternate causes, show reverse causality.
- **Always read the exact conclusion.** Do not weaken a point the author never made!
