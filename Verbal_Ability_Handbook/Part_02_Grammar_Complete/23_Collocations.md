## 23.1 The "Natural Sound" of English

A collocation is a pair or group of words that are habitually juxtaposed. Native speakers use them instinctively. For example, we say **"heavy rain"**, not **"thick rain"**. We say **"strong coffee"**, not **"powerful coffee"**.

In placement exams, blanks often test your knowledge of these fixed pairings.

---

## 23.2 The Ultimate Trap: MAKE vs DO

Many languages use the same word for "make" and "do". English strictly separates them.
*   **DO** is used for actions, obligations, and repetitive tasks (focus is on the *process*).
*   **MAKE** is used for creating or producing something new (focus is on the *product*).

### Use "DO" with:
| Collocation | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Do business** | It's a pleasure to do business with you. |
| **Do a favor** | Can you do me a favor? |
| **Do your best** | Just do your best on the exam. |
| **Do research** | The team is doing research on AI. (NEVER 'make research') |
| **Do damage** | The storm did a lot of damage. |
| **Do the dishes/laundry** | I need to do the dishes. |

### Use "MAKE" with:
| Collocation | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Make a decision** | We need to make a decision by Friday. |
| **Make a mistake** | I made a mistake in the code. |
| **Make an effort** | You must make an effort to arrive on time. |
| **Make an excuse** | Stop making excuses. |
| **Make money** | The company made a lot of money this year. |
| **Make a promise** | He made a promise to finish the work. |

## 23.3 High-Frequency Adverb + Adjective Collocations

Modifiers must pair correctly with their adjectives.

*   **Deeply** offended / **Deeply** concerned / **Deeply** regret
*   **Highly** successful / **Highly** competitive / **Highly** likely
*   **Strongly** oppose / **Strongly** suggest / **Strongly** believe
*   **Heavily** guarded / **Heavily** armed / **Heavily** rely on
*   **Bitterly** disappointed / **Bitterly** cold / **Bitterly** opposed
*   **Strictly** forbidden / **Strictly** speaking
*   **Widely** known / **Widely** accepted / **Widely** available

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap:** You cannot say "highly disappointed" or "deeply successful". You must use the correct pairing: *bitterly disappointed* and *highly successful*.

## 23.5 Practice Questions

**Q1. Fill in the blank:**
Before deploying the new module, the QA team must ______ thorough research to ensure there are no security vulnerabilities.
(A) make
(B) do
(C) take
(D) create

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The noun "research" always collocates with the verb "do" (do research). "Make research" is grammatically incorrect in English.
**Rule:** Do vs Make.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Infosys

**Q2. Fill in the blank:**
The CEO was ______ disappointed by the massive drop in quarterly sales.
(A) deeply
(B) bitterly
(C) heavily
(D) strongly

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** While "deeply disappointed" is sometimes used, "bitterly disappointed" is the strongest and most grammatically formalized collocation in standard English testing.
**Rule:** Adverb + Adjective collocation.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** TCS

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
I asked the senior developer to ______ me a favor and review my pull request.
(A) make
(B) give
(C) do
(D) provide

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The fixed collocation is "do a favor".
**Rule:** Do vs Make.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Wipro

**Q4. Fill in the blank:**
It is ______ forbidden to enter the server room without proper authorization.
(A) strictly
(B) heavily
(C) deeply
(D) strongly

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The adjective "forbidden" pairs with the adverb "strictly". (Strictly forbidden).
**Rule:** Adverb + Adjective collocation.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Accenture

**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
The marketing team needs to ______ a decision quickly before the campaign budget expires.
(A) do
(B) make
(C) take
(D) have

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The noun "decision" always collocates with the verb "make" (make a decision). Note: "Take a decision" is used in British English sometimes, but "Make a decision" is the universally accepted standard for exams.
**Rule:** Do vs Make.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** IBM
