### 9.1 What is a Conditional?

Conditional sentences express a condition (If clause) and the result of that condition (Main clause).

> [!TIP]
> **Comma Rule:** 
> - If the "If clause" comes first, use a comma: *If it rains, I will stay home.*
> - If the "Main clause" comes first, NO comma: *I will stay home if it rains.*

There are 4 main types of conditionals (Zero, First, Second, Third) based on real/unreal situations.

---

### 9.2 Zero & First Conditionals (Real Situations)

#### Type 0: The Zero Conditional
Used for universal truths, scientific facts, and general habits. (100% chance of happening).
```rule
Formula: If + Simple Present , Simple Present
```
- *Example:* If you heat ice, it melts. (Fact)
- *Example:* If I am tired, I go to sleep. (Habit)

#### Type 1: The First Conditional
Used for real, possible situations in the future. (High chance of happening).
```rule
Formula: If + Simple Present , Simple Future (Will + V1)
```
- *Example:* If you study hard, you will pass the exam.

> [!WARNING]
> **Placement Trap:** NEVER use "will" in the "If clause".
> - ❌ *Incorrect:* If you **will study** hard, you will pass.
> - ✅ *Correct:* If you **study** hard, you will pass.

---

### 9.3 Second & Third Conditionals (Unreal/Hypothetical)

#### Type 2: The Second Conditional
Used for imaginary, hypothetical, or unlikely situations in the PRESENT or FUTURE.
```rule
Formula: If + Simple Past , Would + V1
```
- *Example:* If I won the lottery, I would buy an island. (I probably won't win).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The "Were" Rule:** In the Second Conditional, the verb 'to be' is always **were** for ALL subjects (I, he, she, it).
> - ❌ *Incorrect:* If I was a bird, I would fly.
> - ✅ *Correct:* If I **were** a bird, I would fly.
> - ✅ *Correct:* If she **were** the boss, things would be better.

#### Type 3: The Third Conditional (The Most Tested Rule)
Used for imaginary situations in the PAST. It expresses regret or missed opportunities. You cannot change the past.
```rule
Formula: If + Past Perfect (Had+V3) , Would have + V3
```
- *Example:* If you **had studied**, you **would have passed**. (But you didn't study, and you didn't pass).

---

### 9.4 Inverted Conditionals

In formal English (and tough placement exams), you can drop the word "If" and invert the subject and auxiliary verb.

**Type 1 Inversion (Should):**
- *Standard:* If you need help, call me.
- *Inverted:* **Should you need** help, call me.

**Type 2 Inversion (Were):**
- *Standard:* If I were rich, I would help you.
- *Inverted:* **Were I** rich, I would help you.

**Type 3 Inversion (Had) - VERY COMMON:**
- *Standard:* If he had known, he would have prepared.
- *Inverted:* **Had he known**, he would have prepared.

---

### 9.5 Other Conjunctions

- **Unless:** Means "if not". **NEVER** use a negative word (not, never) in the same clause as unless.
  - ❌ *Incorrect:* Unless you do not study, you will fail.
  - ✅ *Correct:* Unless you study, you will fail.
- **Provided that / As long as:** Emphasize the condition heavily.
  - I will go, provided that you pay for the tickets.
- **In case:** Done as a precaution.
  - Take an umbrella in case it rains. (Not 'in case it will rain').

---

### 9.6 Subjunctive Mood (Wish, If only, It's time)

These structures follow special tense rules because they deal with unreal desires.

1. **Present Wish:** Use Simple Past.
   - I wish I **had** a car. (I don't have one now).
   - I wish she **were** here. (She isn't here).
2. **Past Regret:** Use Past Perfect.
   - I wish I **had studied** harder for the exam. (I didn't study).
3. **Complaint/Desire for Change:** Use Would + V1.
   - I wish it **would stop** raining.

**It's high time / It's about time:**
Always followed by a subject + **Simple Past**.
- ❌ *Incorrect:* It's high time you **start** studying.
- ✅ *Correct:* It's high time you **started** studying.

### 9.7 Practice Questions

---
**Q1. If I ____ a millionaire, I would travel the world.**
(A) am
(B) was
(C) were
(D) have been

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a Type 2 Conditional (unreal present). For the verb 'to be' in the if-clause of a Type 2 conditional, we must use "were" for all subjects.
**📏 Rule:** Type 2 Subjunctive "Were".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Infosys
---
**Q2. ____ I known about the meeting, I would have attended.**
(A) Have
(B) If
(C) Had
(D) Should

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is an inverted Type 3 Conditional. "Had I known" is the exact same as "If I had known". 
**📏 Rule:** Inverted Third Conditional.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture
---
**Q3. Spot the error:**
**Unless you do not (A) / apologize to her, (B) / she will not (C) / forgive you. (D)**
(A) Unless you do not
(B) apologize to her,
(C) she will not
(D) forgive you.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Unless" intrinsically means "if not". Using "do not" directly after it creates a double negative. It should be "Unless you apologize".
**📏 Rule:** No negatives immediately following Unless.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant
---
**Q4. If you will heat water to 100 degrees Celsius, it boils.**
(A) If you will heat water
(B) to 100 degrees Celsius,
(C) it boils.
(D) No Error

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a Zero Conditional (scientific fact). The formula is If + Present, Present. Even for First Conditional, we NEVER use "will" in the "If" clause. It should be "If you heat water".
**📏 Rule:** No 'will' in the if-clause.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro
---
**Q5. It's high time we ____ for the airport.**
(A) leave
(B) left
(C) are leaving
(D) have left

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "It's high time" or "It's time" is always followed by a subject and a verb in the Simple Past tense, even though it refers to the present/future.
**📏 Rule:** It's high time + Simple Past.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM, TCS
---
*(Questions 6-25 explore mixed conditionals, "would rather", and further inversion scenarios. The mastery of Type 3 condition and "were" rule provides the highest ROI for placement exams.)*

### Key Takeaways
1. **Never put 'will' or 'would' in the 'If' clause.**
2. **Type 1:** If + Present, Will + V1.
3. **Type 2:** If + Past, Would + V1. (Always use **were**).
4. **Type 3:** If + Had+V3, Would have+V3. (Master this!).
5. **Inversion:** Drop the "If" and put Had/Were/Should at the front.
6. **It's time:** Always followed by Simple Past (It's time we **left**).
