### 6.1 Main Verbs (Transitive vs Intransitive)

The main verb represents the primary action or state of being in a sentence.

1. **Transitive Verbs:** Require a **direct object** to complete their meaning. They answer "who" or "what" to the verb.
   - *Example:* She **bought** a car. (Bought what? A car. 'Car' is the object).
   - *Placement importance:* Only sentences with transitive verbs can be changed into the Passive Voice.

2. **Intransitive Verbs:** Do NOT require an object. The action stops with the subject.
   - *Example:* The baby **slept**. (Slept what? No answer. No object needed).
   - *Common intransitive verbs:* arrive, die, laugh, sit, sleep, cry.

---

### 6.2 Linking & Causative Verbs

#### Linking Verbs
A linking verb connects the subject to a subject complement (an adjective or a noun that describes the subject). It does not show action.
*Examples:* is, am, are, was, were, look, seem, feel, smell, taste, become.

> [!WARNING]
> **Placement Trap:** Linking verbs take **Adjectives**, not Adverbs!
> - ❌ *Incorrect:* The flowers smell **sweetly**.
> - ✅ *Correct:* The flowers smell **sweet**.

#### Causative Verbs
Used when the subject causes someone else to do an action. (Make, Let, Have, Get).

```rule
1. Make / Let + Person + Base Verb (V1 without 'to')
2. Have + Person + Base Verb (V1)
3. Get + Person + 'To' + Base Verb (To + V1)
4. Have / Get + Object + Past Participle (V3)
```

**Examples:**
- The teacher **made** him **clean** the board. (Not 'to clean')
- I **had** the mechanic **fix** my car. (Not 'to fix')
- I **got** the mechanic **to fix** my car. (Requires 'to')
- I **had my car fixed** yesterday. (Passive meaning, uses V3)

### 6.3 Primary Helping Verbs (Be, Do, Have)

Helping (Auxiliary) verbs help the main verb show tense, voice, or mood.

1. **Forms of BE (is, am, are, was, were, been, being)**
   - Used in Continuous tenses: She **is** playing.
   - Used in Passive voice: The car **was** sold.

2. **Forms of DO (do, does, did)**
   - Used for negatives/questions in Simple Present/Past.
   - *Trap:* Always use V1 after do/does/did.
   - ❌ *Incorrect:* Did you **went** there?
   - ✅ *Correct:* Did you **go** there?

3. **Forms of HAVE (has, have, had)**
   - Used in Perfect tenses. Always followed by V3.
   - *Example:* I **have finished** the report.

### 6.4 Modal Verbs - Deep Dive

Modals express necessity, ability, permission, or possibility. 
**Golden Rule of Modals:** Modal + Base Verb (V1). Never add -s, -ed, or -ing to a verb immediately after a modal.

| Modal | Primary Usage | Example |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| **Can** | Present ability, informal permission | I **can** swim. / **Can** I go? |
| **Could** | Past ability, polite request | I **could** run fast when young. / **Could** you help me? |
| **May** | Formal permission, strong possibility | **May** I come in? / It **may** rain today. |
| **Might** | Weak possibility | It **might** rain, but the sky is clear. |
| **Will** | Future intent, certainty, promises | I **will** definitely pass the test. |
| **Would** | Past habit, polite offer, conditionals | I **would** like some tea. / If I won, I **would** travel. |
| **Shall** | Future (with I/We), suggestions | **Shall** we dance? |
| **Should** | Advice, logical deduction | You **should** see a doctor. |
| **Must** | Strong obligation, logical necessity | You **must** wear a helmet. / He **must** be rich. |
| **Ought to** | Moral duty (similar to should) | We **ought to** respect our elders. |

#### Marginal Modals (Semi-modals)
- **Need / Dare:** Act as main verbs normally, but as modals in negative/interrogative.
  - As Modal: He **need not go**. (No 's', followed by V1).
  - As Main Verb: He **needs to go**.
- **Used to:** Past habits no longer true.
  - I **used to play** football.

### 6.5 Perfect Modals (Modal + Have + V3)

This is a massive topic for Infosys and TCS error detection. Perfect modals refer to the **PAST**.

1. **Should have + V3**: You didn't do something, but it was a good idea. (Regret).
   - I **should have studied** harder. (But I didn't).

2. **Must have + V3**: A logical deduction about the past. (You are 99% sure).
   - The ground is wet. It **must have rained** last night.

3. **Could have + V3**: You had the ability/opportunity in the past, but didn't do it.
   - I **could have passed**, but I didn't write the exam.

4. **Might have + V3**: A possibility in the past.
   - He didn't answer the phone. He **might have been** sleeping.

5. **Would have + V3**: Used in 3rd Conditionals (Hypothetical past).
   - If I had money, I **would have bought** a car.

---

### 6.6 Modals in Passive Voice

When converting a sentence with a modal to passive voice, follow this simple formula:

```rule
Active: Modal + V1  --> Passive: Modal + be + V3
Active: Modal + have + V3 --> Passive: Modal + have been + V3
```

- Active: You **must finish** the work.
- Passive: The work **must be finished**.

- Active: You **should have sent** the email.
- Passive: The email **should have been sent**.

### 6.7 Practice Questions

---
**Q1. Identify the correct sentence:**
(A) He did not completed the assignment.
(B) He did not completes the assignment.
(C) He did not complete the assignment.
(D) He do not completed the assignment.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The helping verb "did" is already in the past tense. It must always be followed by the base form of the main verb (V1). Therefore, "did not complete" is correct.
**📏 Rule:** Do / Does / Did + V1.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS
---
**Q2. The manager made the team ____ late to finish the project.**
(A) to stay
(B) stayed
(C) staying
(D) stay

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Make" is a causative verb. The rule is: Make + Person + Base Verb (without 'to'). So it should be "made the team stay".
**📏 Rule:** Causative verb Make takes V1.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture
---
**Q3. He ____ have missed the train; he left home an hour late!**
(A) should
(B) must
(C) can
(D) would

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** We are making a logical deduction about the past. Since he left an hour late, we are very sure he missed the train. "Must have + V3" indicates strong logical deduction about the past.
**📏 Rule:** Perfect Modals: Must have + V3 for strong deduction.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys
---
**Q4. Spot the error:**
**You ought (A) / to not (B) / speak disrespectfully (C) / to your parents. (D)**
(A) You ought
(B) to not
(C) speak disrespectfully
(D) to your parents.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The negative form of "ought to" is "ought not to". The "not" must come before the "to". It should be: "You ought not to speak..."
**📏 Rule:** Position of 'not' with infinitive.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini
---
**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
**I had my house ____ last month.**
(A) paint
(B) painted
(C) to paint
(D) painting

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Causative verb "have" with a non-living object (house) takes the past participle (V3). Formula: Have/Get + Object + V3.
**📏 Rule:** Causative structure for passive meaning.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM
---
**Q6. She ____ speak five languages when she was a child.**
(A) can
(B) could
(C) should
(D) might

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence describes an ability in the past ("when she was a child"). "Could" is the past tense form of "can" used for past abilities.
**📏 Rule:** Modals of Ability.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant
---
**Q7. If you had warned me, I ____ have made such a huge mistake.**
(A) would not
(B) could not
(C) should not
(D) might not

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a Type 3 conditional sentence (Past perfect in 'if' clause -> would have + V3 in main clause). 
**📏 Rule:** Third conditional framework.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro
---
**Q8. The flowers in the garden smell ____.**
(A) wonderfully
(B) wonderfully well
(C) wonderful
(D) heavily

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Smell" is a linking verb. Linking verbs are followed by adjectives, not adverbs. "Wonderful" is the adjective.
**📏 Rule:** Linking verbs + Adjective.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS
---
*(Questions 9-25 test the nuances of Modal perfects, passive conversions, and marginal modals like dare/need. Ensure thorough practice on "must have" vs "should have" as it's the most highly tested logic puzzle).*

### Key Takeaways
1. **Did + V1**: Never say "Did you went?". Always "Did you go?".
2. **Causative Make**: Never use "to". (He made me cry. NOT: He made me to cry).
3. **Modal + V1**: Modals never take -s, -ed, or -ing.
4. **Perfect Modals**: Deal with the PAST. Should have (Regret), Must have (Strong deduction), Could have (Missed opportunity).
5. **Linking Verbs**: Take Adjectives! Feel good, taste bad, smell sweet.
