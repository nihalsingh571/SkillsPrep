## 16.1 The Three Degrees

Adjectives and adverbs change their form to show comparison.
1.  **Positive Degree:** The base form. Used when NO comparison is made, or when comparing equals (using *as...as*). (e.g., tall, intelligent).
2.  **Comparative Degree:** Used to compare exactly TWO things/people. Usually formed with `-er` or `more`. (e.g., taller, more intelligent).
3.  **Superlative Degree:** Used to compare THREE OR MORE things/people. Usually formed with `-est` or `most`. (e.g., tallest, most intelligent).

### Irregular Adjectives
You must memorize these, as standard `-er`/`-est` rules do not apply:
*   Good / Well -> Better -> Best
*   Bad / Ill -> Worse -> Worst
*   Little -> Less/Lesser -> Least
*   Much / Many -> More -> Most
*   Far -> Farther (physical distance) / Further (figurative distance/additional) -> Farthest / Furthest

## 16.2 Golden Rules for Placements

### Rule 1: The Article "The" with Superlatives
You MUST use the definite article "the" before a superlative adjective.
*   *Wrong:* He is tallest boy in the class.
*   *Correct:* He is **the** tallest boy in the class.
*   *Exception:* Do not use "the" if the superlative is preceded by a possessive pronoun (my, his, their). -> *He is my best friend.* (Not: my the best friend).

### Rule 2: The Article "The" with Comparatives (Two Cases)
Usually, we do not use "the" with comparatives. However, there are two strict exceptions heavily tested in exams:

**Case A: Parallel Increase/Decrease**
*   *Correct:* **The** higher you climb, **the** colder it gets.
*   *Correct:* **The** more you study, **the** better your chances are.

**Case B: "Of the two"**
If a sentence explicitly mentions "of the two", you must use "the" + comparative.
*   *Wrong:* Of the two brothers, John is taller.
*   *Correct:* Of the two brothers, John is **the taller**.
*   *Trap:* Of the two brothers, John is the tallest. (Wrong, because it's only two people).

### Rule 3: The Double Comparative / Superlative Trap
Never use two comparison markers together.
*   *Wrong:* He is **more smarter** than you.
*   *Wrong:* She is the **most prettiest** girl.
*   *Correct:* He is smarter than you. / She is the prettiest girl.

### Rule 4: Comparison of Equals (As...As)
When comparing two equal things, use `as + [Positive Degree] + as`.
*   *Correct:* He is as tall as I am.
*   *Negative form:* He is not **so** tall as I am. (or *not as tall as*).

### Rule 5: Using "Any Other"
When comparing one thing against the rest of its group using a comparative, you must use "any other" to exclude the thing itself from the group.
*   *Wrong:* Iron is more useful than any metal. (This implies iron is not a metal).
*   *Correct:* Iron is more useful than **any other** metal.

## 16.3 Interchanging Degrees (Sentence Transformation)

You can say the exact same thing using all three degrees. This is a common test in **Infosys and Capgemini**.

**Type 1: Superlative to Comparative to Positive**
*   **Superlative:** Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.
*   **Comparative:** Mount Everest is higher than any other peak in the world.
*   **Positive:** No other peak in the world is as high as Mount Everest.

**Type 2: "One of the..." (Superlative to Comparative to Positive)**
*   **Superlative:** Mumbai is one of the richest cities in India.
*   **Comparative:** Mumbai is richer than most other cities in India. (Note: *most other*, not *any other*).
*   **Positive:** Very few cities in India are as rich as Mumbai.

> [!TIP]
> **Transformation Cheat Sheet:**
> *   "The [Superlative]" <-> "Any other" <-> "No other"
> *   "One of the [Superlative]" <-> "Most other" <-> "Very few"

## 16.4 Practice Questions

**Q1. Identify the error in the sentence:**
Of all the candidates who applied for the software developer role, Amit is clearly the more experienced.
(A) Of all the candidates
(B) who applied for the software developer role,
(C) Amit is clearly the more experienced.
(D) No error.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "Of all the candidates" implies there are three or more people. When comparing three or more, we must use the superlative degree, not the comparative. It should be "the most experienced".
**❌ Why (A/B) is wrong:** Grammatically correct introductory phrase.
**📏 Rule:** Use superlative for three or more.
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Of all" = Superlative. "Of the two" = Comparative.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS

---

**Q2. Fill in the blank:**
The more RAM you install in your computer, ______ the system will run.
(A) faster
(B) the faster
(C) fastest
(D) the fastest

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** This sentence demonstrates a parallel increase. The structure must be "The + comparative ... the + comparative". "The more RAM..., the faster...".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Missing the definite article "the".
**❌ Why (C/D) is wrong:** Superlatives are not used in parallel increase structures.
**📏 Rule:** Parallel Increase requires "The + Comparative".
**⚡ Shortcut:** If the sentence starts with "The + -er/more", look for "the + -er/more" in the second half.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

**Q3. Transform the following sentence to the Positive degree without changing its meaning:**
"Gold is more precious than any other metal."
(A) Gold is as precious as any metal.
(B) All metals are as precious as gold.
(C) No other metal is as precious as gold.
(D) Very few metals are as precious as gold.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The original sentence states gold is at the very top. Option (C) ("No other metal is as precious") perfectly translates this into the positive degree.
**❌ Why (A/B) is wrong:** These change the meaning entirely, making gold equal to others.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Very few" is used to translate "one of the most precious", not "more precious than any other".
**📏 Rule:** Transformation Type 1 (Any other <-> No other).
**⚡ Shortcut:** "than any other" = "No other ... as ... as".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

---

**Q4. Fill in the blank:**
She decided to pursue ______ studies in Artificial Intelligence after completing her bachelor's degree.
(A) farther
(B) further
(C) furthest
(D) farthest

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Further" means additional or more advanced. "Farther" refers exclusively to physical distance. Since she is pursuing additional studies, "further" is correct.
**❌ Why (A/D) is wrong:** They refer to physical distance (e.g., walking farther down the road).
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Superlative is not needed; she is not comparing three or more things.
**📏 Rule:** Farther (physical) vs Further (figurative/additional).
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you can replace it with "additional", use "further".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q5. Identify the error:**
Very few programmers in our department are as talented than Sarah.
(A) Very few programmers
(B) in our department
(C) are as talented than
(D) Sarah.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The correlative pair for comparing equals is "as...as". "as talented than" is an illegal mix of positive and comparative structures. It must be "as talented as Sarah".
**❌ Why (A/B) is wrong:** Correct plural subject and prepositional phrase.
**📏 Rule:** The structure is `as + adjective + as`.
**⚡ Shortcut:** See an "as"? Look for the other "as". "Than" never pairs with "as".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q6. Error Detection:**
This algorithm is much more faster than the one we deployed last month.
(A) This algorithm is
(B) much more faster
(C) than the one
(D) we deployed last month.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "more faster" is a double comparative. The comparative of fast is faster. "much faster" is correct.
**Rule:** No double comparatives.
**Difficulty:** Easy

**Q7. Fill in the blank:**
Of the two marketing strategies proposed, the first one is definitely ______.
(A) best
(B) better
(C) the best
(D) the better

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** When comparing exactly two items and mentioning "Of the two", we must use "the + comparative".
**Rule:** "Of the two" rule.
**Difficulty:** Hard

**Q8. Sentence Transformation:**
"Chennai is one of the biggest cities in India." (Change to Comparative)
(A) Chennai is bigger than any other city in India.
(B) Chennai is bigger than most other cities in India.
(C) No other city in India is bigger than Chennai.
(D) Chennai is the biggest city in India.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "One of the" transforms to "bigger than most other". Option A would mean it is THE biggest.
**Rule:** Transformation Type 2.
**Difficulty:** Medium

**Q9. Error Detection:**
The climate of Bangalore is cooler than Chennai.

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Comparing climate to a city. It must be "cooler than that of Chennai".
**Rule:** Compare like with like.
**Difficulty:** Medium

**Q10. Fill in the blank:**
He is the ______ of the three brothers.

(B) the taller

**Q11. Sentence Improvement:**
She is smarter than **any student** in her class.
(A) any other student
(B) all student
(C) any students

**📖 Explanation:** To exclude herself from the rest of the class, she must be smarter than "any other student".
**Rule:** Use of "any other".
**Difficulty:** Medium

The manager's condition is ______ today than it was yesterday.

My oldest brother is a software engineer in San Francisco.

Please do not ask for ______ details regarding the confidential merger.

(B) further

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Additional details = further.
**Rule:** Farther vs Further.

**Q15. Error Detection:**
This is the most unique piece of code I have ever seen.
(A) This is
(B) the most unique
(C) piece of code
(D) I have ever seen.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Unique" means one of a kind. It is an absolute adjective. You cannot be "more unique" or "most unique". It should just be "a unique piece".
**Rule:** Absolute Adjectives.
**Difficulty:** Hard

**Q16. Sentence Transformation:**
"No other boy in the team is as fast as Rahul." (Change to Superlative)
(A) Rahul is faster than any other boy.
(B) Rahul is the fastest boy in the team.
(C) Rahul is one of the fastest boys.
(D) Very few boys are as fast as Rahul.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "No other... as fast as" translates to the absolute superlative: "the fastest".
**Rule:** Transformation Type 1.
**Difficulty:** Easy

**Q17. Fill in the blank:**
I have ______ money than he does, but I am happier.
(A) lesser
(B) less
(C) fewer
(D) fewest

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Money is uncountable. We use "less". "Fewer" is used for countable nouns (fewer coins). "Lesser" usually means of lower quality/importance.
**Rule:** Less vs Fewer vs Lesser.
**Difficulty:** Medium

**Q18. Error Detection:**
He works as hard, if not harder, than his colleagues.
(A) He works
(B) as hard,
(C) if not harder, than
(D) his colleagues.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The "as...as" structure is incomplete. It must be "as hard as, if not harder than".
**Rule:** Incomplete comparisons.
**Difficulty:** Expert

**Q19. Fill in the blank:**
The ______ you practice coding, the ______ bugs you will write.
(A) more, less
(B) more, fewer
(C) most, fewest
(D) most, least

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Parallel increase/decrease structure. Bugs are countable, so we use "fewer" not "less". "The more..., the fewer...".
**Rule:** Parallel structure + Countable nouns.
**Difficulty:** Hard

**Q20. Error Detection:**
It is becoming more and more colder as winter approaches.
(A) It is becoming
(B) more and more colder
(C) as winter
(D) approaches.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Double comparative. It should be "colder and colder". You don't use "more colder".
**Rule:** Double comparative.
**Difficulty:** Easy
