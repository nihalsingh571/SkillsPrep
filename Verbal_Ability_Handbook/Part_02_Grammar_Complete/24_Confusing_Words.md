## 24.1 The "Big Three" Grammatical Confusions

These three pairs appear in almost every single placement paper.

### 1. Affect vs. Effect
*   **Affect** (Verb): To influence or change something.
    *   *Example:* The lack of sleep will **affect** your performance.
*   **Effect** (Noun): The result of an influence.
    *   *Example:* The new policy had a positive **effect** on sales.
> [!TIP]
> **Mnemonic: RAVEN**
> **R**emember: **A**ffect = **V**erb, **E**ffect = **N**oun.

### 2. Then vs. Than
*   **Then** (Adverb): Refers to time or sequence (next, at that time).
    *   *Example:* We wrote the code, **then** we tested it.
*   **Than** (Conjunction): Used exclusively for comparison.
    *   *Example:* Python is slower **than** C++.

### 3. Its vs. It's
*   **Its** (Pronoun): Possessive form of 'it' (belonging to it).
    *   *Example:* The company increased **its** revenue.
*   **It's** (Contraction): Short for "it is" or "it has".
    *   *Example:* **It's** going to rain.

## 24.2 Logical & Meaning-Based Confusions

### 4. Imply vs. Infer
*   **Imply:** To hint or suggest something without saying it directly. (Done by the SPEAKER/WRITER).
    *   *Example:* Are you **implying** that my code is bad?
*   **Infer:** To deduce or conclude from evidence. (Done by the LISTENER/READER).
    *   *Example:* From the error logs, I **inferred** that the server was down.

### 5. Disinterested vs. Uninterested
*   **Disinterested:** Impartial, unbiased, having no personal financial stake (This is a GOOD thing for a judge).
    *   *Example:* We need a **disinterested** third party to resolve this dispute.
*   **Uninterested:** Bored, not caring (This is a BAD thing for a student).
    *   *Example:* He was completely **uninterested** in the lecture.

### 6. Farther vs. Further
*   **Farther:** Physical, measurable distance. (Think "far").
    *   *Example:* I can run **farther** than you.
*   **Further:** Figurative distance, additional time/degree.
    *   *Example:* We need to discuss this matter **further**.

### 7. Fewer vs. Less
*   **Fewer:** Use for countable nouns (things you can pluralize: cars, coins, people, errors).
    *   *Example:* We have **fewer** errors in this build.
*   **Less:** Use for uncountable nouns (water, money, time, patience).
    *   *Example:* I have **less** patience today.

### 8. Lie vs. Lay (The Hardest Pair)
*   **Lie** (Intransitive: requires NO object): To recline/rest.
    *   *Forms:* Lie (present), lay (past), lain (past participle).
    *   *Example:* I will **lie** down on the bed.
*   **Lay** (Transitive: REQUIRES an object): To put or place something down.
    *   *Forms:* Lay (present), laid (past), laid (past participle).
    *   *Example:* Please **lay** the laptop on the desk.

## 24.3 Spelling-Based Confusions

### 9. Principal vs. Principle
*   **Principal:** (Noun) Head of a school, main sum of money. (Adjective) Most important.
    *   *Example:* The **principal** reason for failure was bad planning.
*   **Principle:** (Noun) A moral rule or fundamental truth.
    *   *Example:* He is a man of strong **principles**. (Rule of thumb: Princi**ple** ends in **ple**, just like ru**le**).

### 10. Eminent vs. Imminent
*   **Eminent:** Famous, respected, outstanding in a profession.
    *   *Example:* An **eminent** scientist will give the keynote.
*   **Imminent:** About to happen very soon (usually something bad).
    *   *Example:* The storm is **imminent**.

### 11. Compliment vs. Complement
*   **Compliment:** Praise or flattering remark. (I with an 'i').
    *   *Example:* She gave me a **compliment** on my tie.
*   **Complement:** Something that completes or goes well with something else. (With an 'e', like 'complete').
    *   *Example:* This wine is a perfect **complement** to the cheese.

### 12. Stationary vs. Stationery
*   **Stationary:** Not moving (ends in **ary** - parked like a c**ar**).
    *   *Example:* The car remained **stationary**.
*   **Stationery:** Writing materials/paper (ends in **ery** - like pap**er**).
    *   *Example:* We need to order more office **stationery**.

## 24.4 Practice Questions

**Q1. Fill in the blank:**
The new tax laws will significantly ______ small businesses in the manufacturing sector.
(A) affect
(B) effect
(C) affects
(D) effects

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence requires a verb following the auxiliary "will". "Affect" is the verb meaning to influence. "Effect" is the noun.
**Rule:** Affect (Verb) vs Effect (Noun).
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** TCS, Wipro

**Q2. Choose the correct word:**
Based on the tone of the manager's email, I could ______ that the project deadline had been moved up.
(A) imply
(B) infer
(C) imply to
(D) inferred

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The manager's email might *imply* something (send the hint), but the reader *infers* it (receives the hint and deduces the meaning).
**Rule:** Imply (sender) vs Infer (receiver).
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
Because the judge owned shares in the company, he was removed from the case for not being a ______ party.
(A) uninterested
(B) disinterested
(C) non-interested
(D) uninteresting

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Disinterested" means unbiased and having no financial/personal stake in the outcome. A judge must be disinterested. "Uninterested" means bored.
**Rule:** Disinterested vs Uninterested.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Accenture

**Q4. Fill in the blank:**
The ______ objective of this software update is to patch the security vulnerability.
(A) principle
(B) principal
(C) principally
(D) principled

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** As an adjective meaning "main" or "most important", the correct word is "principal". "Principle" is a noun meaning a rule.
**Rule:** Principal vs Principle.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Cognizant

**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
If you feel dizzy, you should go and ______ down for a few minutes.
(A) lay
(B) lie
(C) laid
(D) lain

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Lie" means to recline or rest yourself. It does not take an object. "Lay" means to put an object down (e.g., lay the book down).
**Rule:** Lie vs Lay.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** IBM
