## 12.1 Parallelism: The Art of Balance

Parallelism (or parallel structure) dictates that elements in a sentence that are equal in function or importance should be equal in grammatical form. Think of it as a mathematical equation: the left side must balance the right side.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** Companies like **TCS, Infosys, and IBM** frequently test parallelism in Error Detection and Sentence Correction questions. A sentence might look logically sound but grammatically unbalanced.

### Rule 1: Items in a List or Series
When listing items, all items must share the same grammatical form (all nouns, all gerunds, all infinitives, or all clauses).

```rule
Noun + Noun + Noun
Gerund + Gerund + Gerund
Infinitive + Infinitive + Infinitive
Clause + Clause + Clause
```

*   **Wrong:** The manager asked us to write the report, analyzing the data, and to present the findings. (Infinitive, Gerund, Infinitive)
*   **Correct:** The manager asked us to write the report, analyze the data, and present the findings. (Base verb, Base verb, Base verb)

> [!TIP]
> **Mnemonic: The "Scale of Justice"** ⚖️
> Imagine a weighing scale. If you put an `-ing` word on one side, you must put an `-ing` word on the other. 

### Rule 2: Correlative Conjunctions
Correlative conjunctions come in pairs. The grammatical structure immediately following the first part of the pair MUST exactly match the structure following the second part.

**Common Pairs:**
*   Either ... Or
*   Neither ... Nor
*   Not only ... But also
*   Both ... And
*   Whether ... Or

```rule
Not only + [Structure A] + But also + [Structure A]
```

*   **Wrong:** She is not only *a brilliant programmer* but also *writes beautifully*. (Noun Phrase vs. Verb Phrase)
*   **Correct:** She is not only *a brilliant programmer* but also *a beautiful writer*. (Noun Phrase vs. Noun Phrase)
*   **Correct:** She not only *programs brilliantly* but also *writes beautifully*. (Verb Phrase vs. Verb Phrase)

> [!WARNING]
> **The Placement Trap:** The exam will often misplace the first half of the conjunction. 
> *Trap:* He not only wants to visit Paris but also London. (Wrong: 'wants' vs 'London')
> *Solution:* He wants to visit not only Paris but also London. (Noun vs Noun)

### Rule 3: Comparisons and Parallelism
When comparing two items using "than" or "as...as", the items must be structurally parallel.

*   **Wrong:** Swimming in the ocean is much tougher than a pool. (Comparing an action to a place)
*   **Correct:** Swimming in the ocean is much tougher than swimming in a pool. (Gerund vs Gerund)

## 12.2 The Rules of Comparison

### Rule 4: Comparing Like with Like (Logical Comparison)
You cannot compare a person to a city, or a population to a country. You must compare similar entities.

```rule
Entity A's [Characteristic] must be compared to Entity B's [Characteristic]
NOT
Entity A's [Characteristic] to Entity B
```

*   **Wrong:** The population of Mumbai is greater than Delhi. (Comparing population to a city)
*   **Correct:** The population of Mumbai is greater than the population of Delhi.
*   **Better (and common in exams):** The population of Mumbai is greater than **that of** Delhi. (Use "that of" for singular, "those of" for plural).

*   **Wrong:** The salaries of software engineers are higher than teachers.
*   **Correct:** The salaries of software engineers are higher than **those of** teachers.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Accenture & Cognizant Favorite:** Look for plural vs. singular mismatches here. If the subject is plural (salaries), you must use "those of". If singular (population), use "that of".

### Rule 5: Subjective vs Objective Case in Comparison
When using "than" or "as", are you comparing subjects or objects? In placement exams, it is almost always subjects.

```rule
Subject + Verb + Comparative/As + Subjective Pronoun (+ auxiliary verb)
```

*   **Wrong:** He is taller than me. (Colloquial, but grammatically incorrect for formal exams).
*   **Correct:** He is taller than I. (Implied: He is taller than I am).
*   **Wrong:** She types as fast as him.
*   **Correct:** She types as fast as he (does).

*Exception:* If comparing objects, use the objective case. 
*   **Example:** The manager praised you more than me. (Implied: praised you more than he praised me).

### Rule 6: The Double Comparative Trap
Never use two comparatives or two superlatives together.

*   **Wrong:** He is more smarter than his brother. (more + -er)
*   **Correct:** He is smarter than his brother.
*   **Wrong:** This is the most best solution.
*   **Correct:** This is the best solution.

## 12.3 Key Takeaways

| Rule | Incorrect Example | Correct Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **List Parallelism** | I like hiking, swimming, and to bike. | I like hiking, swimming, and biking. |
| **Correlative Pairs** | He both is smart and hardworking. | He is both smart and hardworking. |
| **Like with Like** | His car is faster than John. | His car is faster than John's. (or that of John) |
| **Pronouns in Comparison** | She is older than me. | She is older than I. |
| **Double Comparative** | It is more hotter today. | It is hotter today. |

## 12.4 Practice Questions

**Q1. Identify the erroneous segment in the sentence:**
The new policy will require employees to arrive on time, limiting their break durations, and to submit weekly reports.

(A) The new policy will require employees
(B) to arrive on time,
(C) limiting their break durations,
(D) and to submit weekly reports.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence lists three requirements. The first is an infinitive ("to arrive"), and the third is an infinitive ("to submit"). To maintain parallel structure, the second item must also be an infinitive. "limiting" (a gerund/participle) breaks this parallelism. It should be "to limit their break durations".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Grammatically sound subject and verb.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Correctly uses the infinitive form.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Correctly uses the infinitive form, matching (B).
**📏 Rule:** Items in a list must be in parallel grammatical form.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Scan lists separated by commas and "and". Ensure the verbs all match (e.g., to V1, to V1, to V1).
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

**Q2. Choose the correct sentence:**
(A) The climate of Ooty is much more pleasant than Chennai.
(B) The climate of Ooty is much pleasant than Chennai.
(C) The climate of Ooty is much more pleasant than that of Chennai.
(D) The climate of Ooty is much more pleasant than those of Chennai.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** We are comparing "the climate" (singular) of Ooty with "the climate" of Chennai. Option A incorrectly compares a climate to a city. Option C correctly uses "that of" to represent the singular noun "climate". Option D incorrectly uses "those of" (plural).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Compares a climate to a city (Logical Comparison error).
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "much pleasant" is grammatically incorrect; "more pleasant" is the comparative form. Also compares climate to city.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "those of" is plural, but "climate" is singular.
**📏 Rule:** Compare like with like using "that of" for singular nouns and "those of" for plural nouns.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If you see "climate of X", look for "that of Y" in the options.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, Wipro

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
The recruitment team found that Rohan was as capable ______ for the job.
(A) as me
(B) as I
(C) than me
(D) than I

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The structure "as...as" is used for comparing equals. When comparing the subject of a clause ("Rohan was"), we must use the subjective pronoun after the second "as". "as I" implies "as I am".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "me" is an objective pronoun. We are comparing subjects.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "than" is not used with "as" to show equal comparison.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Incorrect conjunction "than" used instead of "as".
**📏 Rule:** Subjective vs Objective Case in comparisons. Use subjective pronouns (I, he, she, they) when comparing subjects.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Mentally add the verb at the end. "as capable as me am" sounds wrong. "as capable as I am" sounds right.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q4. Identify the error in the following sentence:**
Not only the CEO promised higher bonuses but also flexible working hours for all the developers.

(A) Not only the CEO promised
(B) higher bonuses but also
(C) flexible working hours
(D) for all the developers.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The correlative conjunction is "not only... but also". What follows "but also" is "flexible working hours" (a noun phrase). Therefore, what follows "not only" must also be a noun phrase. In the original sentence, "not only" is followed by "the CEO promised" (a clause/subject+verb). The correct placement is: The CEO promised not only higher bonuses but also flexible working hours.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Correct continuation of the parallel structure.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Correct noun phrase.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Correct prepositional phrase.
**📏 Rule:** Correlative conjunctions must be followed by identical grammatical structures.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Find "but also", see what follows it (noun phrase). Go back to "not only" and move it so a noun phrase immediately follows it.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Cognizant

**Q5. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:**
(A) To learn a new programming language is like learning a new spoken language.
(B) Learning a new programming language is like to learn a new spoken language.
(C) Learning a new programming language is like learning a new spoken language.
(D) To learn a new programming language is learning a new spoken language.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Comparisons connected by words like "is like" must be parallel. Option C compares a gerund phrase ("Learning...") with a gerund phrase ("learning..."). This is perfectly parallel. Option A compares an infinitive with a gerund.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Infinitive vs Gerund mismatch.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Gerund vs Infinitive mismatch.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** The phrasing changes the meaning slightly and loses the parallel comparative structure of "is like". However, even "To learn... is to learn..." would be better, but C is the most natural and parallel.
**📏 Rule:** Both sides of a comparative equation must be parallel.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Match the -ing with -ing, or the 'to' with 'to'.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

**Q15. Error Detection:**
Of the two candidates interviewed today, Ramesh is definitely the most qualified for the database administrator role.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
