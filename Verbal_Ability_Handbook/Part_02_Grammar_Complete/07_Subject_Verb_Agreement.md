### 7.1 The Fundamental Principle

The most important rule in English grammar is this:
**A singular subject takes a singular verb. A plural subject takes a plural verb.**

> [!WARNING]
> **The Spelling Trap:**
> - To make a **NOUN** plural, you add **-s / -es**. (Boy -> Boys)
> - To make a **VERB** singular, you add **-s / -es**. (Play -> Plays)
> Therefore, *The boy plays* (Singular + Singular). *The boys play* (Plural + Plural).

---

### 7.2 Compound Subjects (And, Or, Nor)

#### Rule 1: Subjects joined by "AND"
Generally, two subjects joined by "and" are plural and take a plural verb.
- *Example:* Ram **and** Shyam **are** studying.

**Exceptions (The "One Idea" Rule):**
If the two nouns joined by "and" refer to the same person/thing, or represent a single idea, the verb is singular.
- *Example:* Bread and butter **is** my favorite breakfast. (Considered a single dish).
- *Example:* The project manager and team lead **is** arriving. (Same person holds both roles. Notice there is only one "The").
- *Example:* The project manager and **the** team lead **are** arriving. (Two "The"s = two different people).

#### Rule 2: Either/Or, Neither/Nor, Not Only/But Also
```rule
The Proximity Rule: When subjects are joined by "or", "nor", "either...or", "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the subject CLOSEST to it.
```
- ❌ *Incorrect:* Neither the teacher nor the students **is** in the classroom.
- ✅ *Correct:* Neither the teacher nor the students **are** in the classroom. (Verb 'are' agrees with 'students').
- ✅ *Correct:* Neither the students nor the teacher **is** in the classroom. (Verb 'is' agrees with 'teacher').

### 7.3 Intervening Phrases (The Parenthetical Trap)

This is Accenture's favorite question type.

```rule
Words between the subject and verb do NOT change the number of the subject. 
Phrases like:
- as well as
- along with
- together with
- accompanied by
- in addition to
- besides
Do NOT make a singular subject plural.
```

- ❌ *Incorrect:* The CEO, along with the managers, **are** attending the meeting.
- ✅ *Correct:* The CEO, along with the managers, **is** attending the meeting.
*(The subject is "CEO". The phrase "along with the managers" is extra information. Hide it, and read: The CEO is attending).*

### 7.4 Indefinite Pronouns

#### Always Singular
These pronouns ALWAYS take a singular verb, no matter what prepositional phrase follows them.
*Each, every, everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything, nobody, nothing, either, neither.*

- ❌ *Incorrect:* Each of the students **have** a laptop. (Trap! You see 'students' and think plural).
- ✅ *Correct:* Each of the students **has** a laptop. (Subject is 'Each').
- ✅ *Correct:* Neither of the plans **was** successful.

#### Always Plural
*Several, few, both, many.*
- ✅ *Correct:* Both of the books **are** interesting.

#### Singular or Plural (SANAM Rule)
*Some, Any, None, All, Most.*
These depend on the noun that follows them.
- *Uncountable Noun -> Singular Verb:* All of the **water is** spilled.
- *Countable Noun -> Plural Verb:* All of the **apples are** eaten.

---

### 7.5 Collective Nouns & Fractions

#### Collective Nouns
Group words like *team, jury, committee, family, audience, government.*
- Takes a **Singular** verb if the group acts as one single unit.
  - *Example:* The jury **has** reached its verdict.
- Takes a **Plural** verb if the members act individually (divided).
  - *Example:* The jury **are** divided in their opinions.

#### Fractions and Percentages
Just like the SANAM rule, look at the noun after the "of".
- Half of the **cake is** eaten. (Cake = uncountable).
- Half of the **cakes are** eaten. (Cakes = countable).
- Fifty percent of the **time is** wasted.
- Fifty percent of the **employees are** absent.

---

### 7.6 Special Rules and Tricky Plurals

#### Rule: Sums of Money, Periods of Time, Distances
When considered as a single unit, they take a singular verb.
- ✅ *Correct:* Ten kilometers **is** a long distance to walk.
- ✅ *Correct:* Five hundred dollars **is** too much for this shirt.

#### Rule: A number of vs The number of
- **A number of** + plural noun + **Plural Verb**
  - *A number of students **are** absent.* (Means 'many' students).
- **The number of** + plural noun + **Singular Verb**
  - *The number of students in the class **is** 50.* (Refers to the specific statistic).

#### Rule: Plural-looking Singular Nouns
Nouns ending in 's' but representing a single entity take a singular verb.
- *Subjects:* Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Politics.
- *Diseases:* Measles, Mumps, Rickets.
- *Games:* Billiards, Darts.
- *News:* The news **is** bad.

### 7.7 Practice Questions

---
**Q1. The captain, along with his teammates, ____ arriving tonight.**
(A) are
(B) is
(C) have
(D) were

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "along with his teammates" does not affect the verb. The true subject is "The captain" (singular). Therefore, we need the singular verb "is".
**📏 Rule:** Intervening phrases do not change subject number.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, TCS
---
**Q2. Neither the manager nor the employees ____ happy with the new policy.**
(A) is
(B) was
(C) are
(D) has been

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** In "Neither...nor" constructions, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. The closest subject is "employees" (plural). So, the plural verb "are" is correct.
**📏 Rule:** Proximity Rule for Or/Nor.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys
---
**Q3. Each of the participants ____ received a certificate.**
(A) have
(B) has
(C) are
(D) were

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Each" is an indefinite pronoun that is ALWAYS singular. The prepositional phrase "of the participants" is meant to distract you. Subject = Each. Verb = has.
**📏 Rule:** 'Each' takes a singular verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant
---
**Q4. Spot the error:**
**Bread and butter (A) / are essentially (B) / the only food (C) / he eats. (D)**
(A) Bread and butter
(B) are essentially
(C) the only food
(D) he eats.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Bread and butter" is considered a single dish or single idea. Thus, it acts as a singular subject and requires a singular verb. It should be "is essentially".
**📏 Rule:** Compound subjects representing one idea.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM
---
**Q5. A number of students ____ applying for the scholarship.**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) has
(D) was

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "A number of" acts as a quantifier meaning "several" or "many", and it takes a plural verb. Do not confuse it with "The number of" which takes a singular verb.
**📏 Rule:** A number of (Plural) vs The number of (Singular).
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro
---
**Q6. Three-quarters of the pizza ____ already eaten.**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) were
(D) have been

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** For fractions, look at the noun following the preposition "of". "Pizza" is an uncountable unit in this context. Therefore, it takes a singular verb "is".
**📏 Rule:** Fractions follow the object of the preposition.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini
---
**Q7. The committee ____ divided on the issue of budget cuts.**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) has
(D) was

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Because the word "divided" indicates that the committee members are not acting as a unified whole, the collective noun acts as a plural subject. Thus, "are" is correct.
**📏 Rule:** Collective nouns acting individually take plural verbs.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT
---
**Q8. Identify the correct sentence:**
(A) The news on TV are always depressing.
(B) Physics are my favorite subject.
(C) Mathematics is a tough subject for many.
(D) Measles are a dangerous disease.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Words like News, Physics, Mathematics, and Measles are plural in form (they end in -s) but singular in meaning. They must take singular verbs.
**📏 Rule:** Plural-looking singular nouns.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

### Key Takeaways
1. **Hide the Garbage**: In a sentence like "The box of chocolates is...", "of chocolates" is garbage. The subject is "box".
2. **Along with / As well as**: Ignore everything between commas. The first noun is the boss.
3. **Either/Or, Neither/Nor**: The noun closest to the verb wins.
4. **Each/Every/Neither/Either (without or/nor)**: ALWAYS singular.
5. **A number of = Plural**. **The number of = Singular**.
