## 13.1 Non-Finite Verbs: The Overview

In English, verbs are finite or non-finite. 
*   **Finite Verbs** change based on tense or subject (I *eat*, he *eats*, I *ate*).
*   **Non-Finite Verbs** do NOT change based on tense or subject. They act as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs. There are three types: Gerunds, Infinitives, and Participles.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** Error detection sections in **Accenture and IBM** rely heavily on distinguishing between gerunds and infinitives. If you know the list of verbs that take ONLY gerunds, you will answer these questions in 5 seconds.

---

## 13.2 The Gerund (Verb + -ing acting as a Noun)

A gerund looks like a continuous verb, but it functions entirely as a noun. It can be a subject, an object, or an object of a preposition.

*   *Subject:* **Swimming** is good for health.
*   *Object:* I enjoy **swimming**.
*   *Object of Prep:* She is afraid of **swimming**.

### Rule 1: Verbs Followed ONLY by Gerunds
Certain verbs absolutely refuse to be followed by an infinitive (to + verb). They must be followed by a gerund.

```rule
Verb + Gerund (V-ing)
```
**Key Verbs:** Enjoy, Avoid, Finish, Deny, Consider, Mind, Suggest, Risk, Miss, Appreciate, Delay, Postpone, Admit, Practice.

*   **Wrong:** He avoided *to answer* the question.
*   **Correct:** He avoided **answering** the question.
*   **Wrong:** Have you finished *to eat*?
*   **Correct:** Have you finished **eating**?

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick (Mnemonic): MEGA FEAP**
> **M**ind, **E**njoy, **G**ive up, **A**void / Admit
> **F**inish, **E**scape, **A**ppreciate, **P**ostpone / Practice
> *All MEGA FEAP verbs take a Gerund.*

### Rule 2: Prepositions are followed by Gerunds
If a verb comes immediately after a preposition (in, on, at, for, about, of, from, to), it MUST be a gerund.

*   **Wrong:** He is interested in *to learn* Python.
*   **Correct:** He is interested in **learning** Python.

**The "TO" Exception:** Usually, "to" is part of an infinitive. But in certain fixed phrases, "to" acts as a preposition, and MUST be followed by a gerund.
*   *Look forward to + V-ing* (I look forward to **meeting** you.)
*   *Accustomed to / Used to + V-ing* (He is used to **waking** up early.)
*   *With a view to + V-ing* (He bought land with a view to **building** a house.)

## 13.3 The Infinitive (To + Base Verb)

An infinitive acts as a noun, adjective, or adverb. 

### Rule 3: Verbs Followed ONLY by Infinitives
```rule
Verb + Infinitive (To + V1)
```
**Key Verbs:** Want, Hope, Decide, Refuse, Promise, Plan, Seem, Appear, Afford, Agree, Learn, Manage.

*   **Wrong:** She decided *resigning* from the job.
*   **Correct:** She decided **to resign** from the job.
*   **Wrong:** I cannot afford *buying* this car.
*   **Correct:** I cannot afford **to buy** this car.

### Rule 4: The Bare Infinitive (Infinitive without 'To')
Certain verbs take an infinitive without the word 'to'. 
**Key Verbs:** Let, Make, Bid, See, Hear, Feel, Watch. (Also all modal verbs: can, must, should, etc.)

*   **Wrong:** The manager made him *to work* late.
*   **Correct:** The manager made him **work** late.
*   **Wrong:** Let me *to go*.
*   **Correct:** Let me **go**.

## 13.4 Verbs Taking Both (With a Change in Meaning)

This is the hardest concept in this topic and highly favored by **TCS NQT and Cognizant**.

**1. STOP**
*   *Stop + Gerund:* Quit an action completely. (He stopped smoking = He quit the habit).
*   *Stop + Infinitive:* Pause one action to do another. (He stopped to smoke = He halted his car in order to smoke).

**2. REMEMBER / FORGET**
*   *Remember + Gerund:* Recalling a past memory. (I remember locking the door = I have a memory of doing it in the past).
*   *Remember + Infinitive:* A task you must perform in the future/present. (Please remember to lock the door = Don't forget to do it).

**3. TRY**
*   *Try + Gerund:* Experiment to see if something works. (Try restarting the computer = see if this fixes the problem).
*   *Try + Infinitive:* Make an effort to achieve something difficult. (I tried to lift the heavy box = I made a physical effort).

**4. REGRET**
*   *Regret + Gerund:* Sorry for a past action. (I regret leaving my job).
*   *Regret + Infinitive:* Formal announcement of bad news. (We regret to inform you that you failed).

## 13.5 Participles and Dangling Modifiers

Participles act as adjectives.
*   *Present Participle:* V-ing (The **crying** baby)
*   *Past Participle:* V3 (The **broken** vase)

### Rule 5: Dangling Modifiers (The Ultimate Trap)
A participial phrase at the beginning of a sentence MUST logically modify the subject of the main clause. If it doesn't, it "dangles."

```rule
[Participial Phrase], [Subject] + [Verb]
*The Subject must be the one performing the action in the Participial Phrase.*
```

*   **Wrong:** Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful. 
    *(Grammatical meaning: The trees were walking down the street. Logical fail.)*
*   **Correct:** Walking down the street, I saw the beautiful trees.

*   **Wrong:** Having finished the assignment, the TV was turned on.
    *(Grammatical meaning: The TV finished the assignment.)*
*   **Correct:** Having finished the assignment, he turned on the TV.

> [!WARNING]
> **Placement Strategy:** In Error Detection, whenever a sentence starts with an `-ing` or `-ed` word followed by a comma, IMMEDIATELY check the noun right after the comma. Does it logically make sense? If not, that's the error.

## 13.6 Key Takeaways

| Concept | The Rule | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MEGA FEAP Verbs** | Always followed by Gerund | I avoid **driving** at night. |
| **Prepositions** | Always followed by Gerund | He is good at **coding**. |
| **Special 'To'** | look forward to, used to + Gerund | I look forward to **seeing** you. |
| **Bare Infinitive** | Make, Let, Help + Base Verb | She made me **cry**. |
| **Dangling Modifiers** | Match the opening phrase to the subject | *Exhausted*, **John** fell asleep. |

## 13.7 Practice Questions

**Q1. Fill in the blank:**
The suspect denied ______ any involvement in the cyber attack on the company's servers.
(A) to have
(B) having
(C) had
(D) to having

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The verb "deny" is a MEGA FEAP verb. It must always be followed by a gerund (V-ing). 
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Deny does not take an infinitive.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Grammatically incoherent structure.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Incorrect mix of infinitive and gerund.
**📏 Rule:** Verbs followed only by gerunds (Deny + V-ing).
**⚡ Shortcut:** Memorize "Deny + -ing".
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Infosys

**Q2. Identify the error in the sentence:**
Looking out the window, a beautiful rainbow caught my eye.
(A) Looking out the window,
(B) a beautiful rainbow
(C) caught
(D) my eye.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a classic dangling modifier. The phrase "Looking out the window" is a participial phrase modifying the subject immediately after the comma. Grammatically, this sentence says "a beautiful rainbow" was looking out the window. It should be "Looking out the window, I saw a beautiful rainbow."
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** The phrase itself is grammatically fine as an introductory modifier.
**❌ Why (C/D) is wrong:** The verb and object are fine, the error lies in the subject mismatch.
**📏 Rule:** Dangling Modifiers. The subject of the main clause must logically align with the introductory participial phrase.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If sentence starts with V-ing, check the noun right after the comma. Can it perform that V-ing action?
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Wipro

---

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
I completely forgot ______ the server logs before I left the office yesterday, so the data was lost.
(A) saving
(B) to save
(C) save
(D) to saving

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Forget" changes meaning based on what follows. "Forget + gerund" means forgetting a memory (I forgot singing that song = I did it, but don't remember). "Forget + infinitive" means forgetting to perform a required task. Here, the person failed to perform the task of saving.
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Forgot saving" would mean they did save the logs, but lost the memory of doing it. But the sentence says "data was lost", implying the action never happened.
**❌ Why (C/D) is wrong:** Incorrect grammatical forms.
**📏 Rule:** Forget + Infinitive = failing to perform a future/required task.
**⚡ Shortcut:** If the action DID NOT happen, use "to + verb". If the action DID happen, use "verb-ing".
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant

**Q4. Choose the correct sentence:**
(A) I am looking forward to meet the new project manager.
(B) I am looking forward to meeting the new project manager.
(C) I am looking forward meeting the new project manager.
(D) I am looking forward to met the new project manager.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "look forward to" contains "to" as a preposition, not as part of an infinitive. Prepositions must be followed by gerunds (nouns).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Treats "to" as an infinitive marker, which is a very common trap.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Missing the preposition "to".
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Grammatically incoherent.
**📏 Rule:** Exceptions to the Infinitive Rule. "Look forward to", "used to", "accustomed to" take V-ing.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Drill this into your brain: "Look forward to -ING". It appears in 90% of placement exams.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** All (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture)

---

**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
To resolve the compile error, try ______ the cache and restarting your IDE.
(A) clear
(B) to clear
(C) clearing
(D) cleared

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Try" + Gerund is used when suggesting an experiment to solve a problem (e.g., Try restarting it). "Try" + Infinitive is used for a physical/mental exertion (e.g., Try to lift this). Since this is troubleshooting/experimenting, we use the gerund.
**❌ Why (A/D) is wrong:** Grammatically incorrect following "try".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Indicates making a difficult effort rather than an experimental solution.
**📏 Rule:** Try + Gerund = Experimenting a solution.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Troubleshooting tech issues? Always use Try + -ing.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q6. Error Detection:**
The professor didn't object to us leaving the lecture early because of the severe storm warning.
(A) The professor didn't object
(B) to us leaving
(C) the lecture early
(D) because of the severe storm warning.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** A gerund ("leaving") must be preceded by a possessive pronoun, not an objective pronoun. It should be "object to OUR leaving". (Whose leaving? Our leaving).
**Rule:** Possessive case before a gerund.
**Difficulty:** Expert
