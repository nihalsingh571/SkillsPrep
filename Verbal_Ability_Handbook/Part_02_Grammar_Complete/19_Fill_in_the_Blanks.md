## 19.1 The Three Types of Fill in the Blanks

1.  **Grammar-Based:** Tests SVA, prepositions, tenses, conjunctions. (Solved via strict grammar rules).
2.  **Vocabulary-Based:** Tests your knowledge of exact word meanings. (Solved via context and tone).
3.  **Collocation-Based:** Tests words that naturally go together (e.g., "commit a crime", not "do a crime").

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Relevance:** **TCS NQT and Cognizant** love vocabulary double-blanks. **Infosys** favors grammar-based single blanks. **Wipro** almost always includes a 5-question Cloze Test (a paragraph with 5 blanks).

---

## 19.2 The "A.C.T." Strategy for Vocabulary Blanks

When you face a vocabulary blank and don't know the exact word, use **A.C.T.**:

*   **A - Analyze the Grammar:** If the blank has "an" before it, eliminate any options starting with a consonant. If it is after a preposition, you need a noun or gerund.
*   **C - Context Clues (Conjunctions):** Look for structural road signs.
    *   *Continuing signs (and, moreover, because, similarly):* The blank will MATCH the tone of the rest of the sentence. 
    *   *Contrasting signs (but, although, despite, however):* The blank will be the OPPOSITE of the tone in the rest of the sentence.
*   **T - Tone (Positive/Negative):** Is the sentence praising someone or criticizing them? Eliminate options that don't match the required + or - tone.

### Example Application of A.C.T.
*Although the manager was known for his strictness, his response to the intern's mistake was surprisingly ________.*
1.  **Grammar:** Adjective needed.
2.  **Context Clue:** "Although" (Contrast). The first part is negative ("strictness"). So the blank must be POSITIVE.
3.  **Options:** (A) severe (-) (B) harsh (-) (C) lenient (+) (D) apathetic (-)
4.  **Answer:** (C) lenient.

## 19.3 Tackling the Cloze Test

A Cloze Test is a paragraph with multiple missing words.
**Golden Rule:** NEVER fill in blank #1 immediately. Read the ENTIRE paragraph first. The clue for blank #1 is often hidden in the last sentence. Understanding the overarching theme (e.g., technology is good, or technology is dangerous) will help you pick the right shade of meaning for every blank.

## 19.4 Practice Question Bank

### Type 1: Single Blank (Grammar & Context)

**Q1. The Board of Directors decided to ________ the meeting until next week due to the CEO's sudden illness.**
(A) postpone
(B) cancel
(C) pre-pone
(D) resume

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Until next week" indicates pushing the date forward. "Cancel" means it won't happen. "Postpone" means delaying it.
**Rule:** Contextual vocabulary.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** TCS

**Q2. He is so ______ that he immediately believed the scammer's email and clicked the link.**
(A) credible
(B) credulous
(C) incredulous
(D) creditworthy

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Credulous" means gullible or easily fooled. "Credible" means believable (a credible story). "Incredulous" means disbelieving.
**Rule:** Confusing word pairs.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Infosys

**Q3. The suspect was completely ______ of all charges after the DNA evidence was presented in court.**
(A) convicted
(B) exonerated
(C) indicted
(D) reprimanded

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The DNA evidence (usually proves innocence if the person is cleared) resulted in being cleared of charges. "Exonerated" means to be freed from blame. "Convicted/Indicted" mean found guilty or formally charged.
**Rule:** Legal vocabulary.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Accenture

**Q4. The new software update is not compatible ______ older operating systems.**
(A) to
(B) for
(C) with
(D) by

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The adjective "compatible" is always followed by the fixed preposition "with".
**Rule:** Fixed Prepositions.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Capgemini

**Q5. ______ the heavy traffic, we managed to reach the airport just in time for boarding.**
(A) Although
(B) Despite
(C) However
(D) In spite

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "the heavy traffic" is a noun phrase. "Despite" is a preposition that takes a noun phrase. "Although" is a conjunction and requires a full clause (Although the traffic was heavy...). Option D is missing "of".
**Rule:** Preposition vs Conjunction.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** IBM

### Type 2: Double Blanks (High Level)

**Q6. The author's latest novel is a ______ of genres, blending sci-fi, romance, and historical fiction into a truly ______ narrative.**
(A) separation ... boring
(B) mishmash ... incoherent
(C) synthesis ... compelling
(D) division ... engaging

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "blending" implies bringing things together (synthesis). Since it's described neutrally/positively as a new novel, "compelling" (fascinating) fits. "mishmash" is too informal/negative unless the second word is also negative, but "incoherent" means unclear. "Synthesis" fits best for "blending".
**Rule:** Tone matching.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Cognizant

**Q7. Because the student was ______ in his studies, his grades were ______ at the end of the semester.**
(A) diligent ... abysmal
(B) negligent ... stellar
(C) meticulous ... outstanding
(D) apathetic ... excellent

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Because" is a continuing conjunction. The cause must match the effect. If he studied well (+), grades are good (+). If he studied poorly (-), grades are bad (-).
(A) + / - (Mismatch)
(B) - / + (Mismatch)
(C) + / + (Match: meticulous = careful; outstanding = great)
(D) - / + (Mismatch)
**Rule:** Cause and Effect polarity.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** TCS

### Type 3: Grammar Focus

**Q8. Had I known the codebase was so legacy, I ______ the job offer.**
(A) will not accept
(B) would not have accepted
(C) would not accept
(D) had not accepted

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Type 3 Conditional (Inverted). "Had I known" = "If I had known" (Past Perfect). The main clause must take "would have + V3".
**Rule:** Conditionals.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Infosys

**Q9. She is one of those programmers who ______ always willing to help juniors.**
(A) is
(B) are
(C) was
(D) has

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Relative Pronoun Rule. "who" refers to the noun immediately preceding it, which is "programmers" (plural). Therefore, the verb must be plural ("are"). 
**Rule:** Relative Pronouns and SVA.
**Difficulty:** Expert
**Company:** Wipro

**Q10. I am not accustomed ______ up this early in the morning.**
(A) to wake
(B) to waking
(C) for waking
(D) waking

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Accustomed to" is a fixed phrase where "to" is a preposition, not an infinitive marker. Prepositions must be followed by gerunds (V-ing).
**Rule:** Exceptions to Infinitives.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Accenture
