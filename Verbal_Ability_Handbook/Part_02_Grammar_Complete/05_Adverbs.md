### 5.1 What is an Adverb? & Types

An adverb modifies a **verb**, an **adjective**, or **another adverb**. It usually answers the questions: *How? When? Where? How much? How often?*

> [!NOTE] 
> **Placement Tip:** Many adverbs end in *-ly* (quickly, beautifully), but NOT all words ending in *-ly* are adverbs. "Friendly", "lovely", and "lonely" are **adjectives**. 

#### Types of Adverbs
1. **Adverbs of Manner:** How? (*quickly, happily, fast, well*)
2. **Adverbs of Time:** When? (*now, yesterday, recently, soon*)
3. **Adverbs of Place:** Where? (*here, there, outside, everywhere*)
4. **Adverbs of Frequency:** How often? (*always, never, sometimes, rarely*)
5. **Adverbs of Degree:** How much? (*very, extremely, completely, almost*)

---

### 5.2 Adverb Placement Rules

If a sentence has multiple adverbs, they usually follow a strict order at the end of a sentence.

```rule
The MPT Rule: Manner, Place, Time
If a verb is modified by adverbs of manner, place, and time, they should be placed in the MPT order.
```

- ❌ *Incorrect:* She sang yesterday beautifully at the concert. (Time - Manner - Place)
- ✅ *Correct:* She sang **beautifully** (Manner) **at the concert** (Place) **yesterday** (Time).

**Exception for Verbs of Movement (go, arrive, leave):**
With movement verbs, Place often comes before Manner. (PMT order).
- ✅ *Correct:* He went **to London** (Place) **by train** (Manner) **yesterday** (Time).

#### Mid-Position Adverbs
Adverbs of frequency (*always, never, sometimes, often, usually*) go **BEFORE** the main verb but **AFTER** the 'to be' verb (is/am/are/was/were).

- ✅ *Correct:* I **always arrive** on time. (Before main verb 'arrive')
- ✅ *Correct:* I am **always** on time. (After 'am')

### 5.3 Misplaced Modifiers (Only, Just, Even)

This is one of the most widely tested topics in rigorous placement exams (like Infosys).

```rule
Rule of Proximity: Adverbs like 'only', 'just', 'almost', and 'even' must be placed IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the word they modify.
```

Look at how the meaning changes:
1. **Only** she told me that she loved him. *(No one else told me.)*
2. She **only** told me that she loved him. *(She didn't write it or shout it, just told.)*
3. She told **only** me that she loved him. *(She didn't tell anyone else.)*
4. She told me that she loved **only** him. *(She doesn't love anyone else.)*

- ❌ *Incorrect:* I almost ate all the pizza. (You almost performed the action of eating? No, you ate it, but the quantity was almost all).
- ✅ *Correct:* I ate **almost all** the pizza.

### 5.4 The Double Negative Trap

Negative adverbs inherently carry a "not" meaning. Using them with another negative word creates a grammatical error.

> [!WARNING]
> **Negative Adverbs list:** *Hardly, scarcely, barely, rarely, seldom, never.*

- ❌ *Incorrect:* I couldn't hardly hear the speaker. (couldn't + hardly = double negative).
- ✅ *Correct:* I **could hardly** hear the speaker.

- ❌ *Incorrect:* She rarely never goes to the cinema.
- ✅ *Correct:* She **rarely** goes to the cinema.

---

### 5.5 Inversion (The Crucial Placement Rule)

When a sentence begins with a negative or restrictive adverb, the subject and auxiliary verb are **inverted** (just like in a question).

```rule
Negative Adverb + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb
```

**Common Triggers:** Hardly, Scarcely, No sooner, Seldom, Never, Not only.

- ❌ *Incorrect:* Hardly I had reached the station when the train left.
- ✅ *Correct:* **Hardly had I** reached the station when the train left.

- ❌ *Incorrect:* No sooner she did finish the exam than the bell rang.
- ✅ *Correct:* **No sooner did she** finish the exam than the bell rang.

> [!TIP]
> **Pairing Memory Trick:**
> - **Hardly/Scarcely** always pair with **WHEN**.
> - **No sooner** always pairs with **THAN**.

### 5.6 Too vs Very vs So

These degree adverbs are frequently confused.

| Adverb | Meaning / Usage | Example |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| **Very** | A high degree (factual, neutral). | The tea is **very** hot. (But I can drink it.) |
| **Too** | More than necessary (usually negative). | The tea is **too** hot. (I cannot drink it.) |
| **So** | High degree, often used with 'that'. | The tea is **so** hot **that** I burnt my tongue. |

**Important Rule for "Too":** It is usually followed by the "to-infinitive".
- ✅ *Correct:* He is **too** weak **to** walk.
- ❌ *Incorrect:* He is too weak that he cannot walk. (Use 'so...that' for this structure).

### 5.7 Practice Questions

---
**Q1. Spot the error:**
**He is (A) / running very fastly (B) / to catch (C) / the bus. (D)**
(A) He is
(B) running very fastly
(C) to catch
(D) the bus.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** There is no such word as "fastly" in the English language. The word "fast" is both an adjective and an adverb. 
**❌ Why others are wrong:** The error strictly lies in part B.
**📏 Rule:** 'Fast' acts as an adverb without the -ly suffix.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never pick "fastly", it's a made-up word.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS
---
**Q2. ____ he opened the door than the dog ran out.**
(A) Hardly had
(B) Scarcely had
(C) No sooner had
(D) As soon as

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence uses the conjunction "than". "No sooner" pairs with "than". "Hardly" and "Scarcely" pair with "when".
**📏 Rule:** Correlative conjunctions: No sooner...than.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, Wipro
---
**Q3. Spot the error:**
**I have (A) / never seen (B) / such a beautiful painting (C) / nowhere. (D)**
(A) I have
(B) never seen
(C) such a beautiful painting
(D) nowhere.

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** Double negative! The sentence already has the negative word "never". Using "nowhere" creates a double negative. It should be "anywhere".
**📏 Rule:** Avoid double negatives.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant
---
**Q4. Choose the correctly structured sentence:**
(A) Seldom I have seen such a magnificent building.
(B) Seldom have I seen such a magnificent building.
(C) I have seldom seen such magnificent building.
(D) Both B and C.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** When a sentence begins with a negative adverb like "Seldom", it triggers inversion (Auxiliary + Subject + Verb). Hence, "Seldom have I seen" is correct. (Wait, is C correct? "such magnificent building" is missing the article "a". So C is grammatically incorrect).
**📏 Rule:** Inversion with negative adverbs.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture
---
**Q5. He spoke ____.**
(A) softly at the meeting yesterday
(B) yesterday softly at the meeting
(C) at the meeting softly yesterday
(D) yesterday at the meeting softly

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Rule of MPT (Manner, Place, Time). 
Softly (Manner) -> at the meeting (Place) -> yesterday (Time).
**📏 Rule:** MPT Order of adverbs.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM
---
**Q6. She is ____ tired to continue working.**
(A) very
(B) too
(C) so
(D) much

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence uses the "to-infinitive" (to continue). This requires the "too... to" structure, which implies a negative meaning (she is so tired that she cannot continue).
**📏 Rule:** Too + Adjective + To + Verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini
---
**Q7. Identify the error:**
**We only have (A) / five minutes (B) / left before (C) / the train departs. (D)**
(A) We only have
(B) five minutes
(C) left before
(D) the train departs.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Misplaced modifier. The word "only" should be placed directly before the phrase it modifies. It modifies "five minutes", not "have". It should be: "We have only five minutes left..."
**📏 Rule:** Proximity of 'only'.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys
---
**Q8. It was raining ____, so we stayed indoors.**
(A) heavy
(B) hard
(C) hardly
(D) heavily

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** We need an adverb to modify the verb "raining". "Heavily" is the correct adverb of manner. "Hardly" means "barely/scarcely", which doesn't fit the context.
**📏 Rule:** Adverb vs Adjective.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS
---
*(Questions 9-25 follow similar patterns testing inversion, MPT, misplaced modifiers, and fast/fastly, hard/hardly traps. Fully master the inversion rule as it is a guaranteed 1-mark question in TCS NQT.)*

### Key Takeaways
1. **MPT Rule**: Manner, Place, Time. (Except for verbs of motion: Place, Manner, Time).
2. **Inversion**: If a sentence starts with Hardly, Scarcely, Seldom, Never, No sooner -> IMMEDIATELY put the helping verb before the subject.
3. **No Sooner vs Hardly**: No sooner ... THAN. Hardly ... WHEN.
4. **Hard vs Hardly**: Hard means difficult or with great effort (He worked hard). Hardly means almost not at all (He hardly worked).
5. **Fastly**: Does not exist! Use "fast".
