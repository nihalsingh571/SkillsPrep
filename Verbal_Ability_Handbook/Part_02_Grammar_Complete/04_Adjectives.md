### 4.1 What is an Adjective? & Types

An adjective is a word that modifies, describes, or gives more information about a **noun** or **pronoun**.
*Why it exists:* Without adjectives, our sentences would be bland. Instead of saying "A boy walked," we can say "A **tall**, **intelligent** boy walked."

> [!NOTE] 
> **Placement Tip:** In error detection questions, adjectives often mistakenly modify verbs. Remember: Adjectives modify NOUNS. Adverbs modify VERBS.

#### Types of Adjectives
1. **Descriptive Adjectives**: Describe the quality. (*beautiful, tall, blue*)
2. **Demonstrative Adjectives**: Point out specific nouns. (*this, that, these, those*)
3. **Possessive Adjectives**: Show ownership. (*my, your, his, her, its, our, their*)
4. **Interrogative Adjectives**: Ask questions. (*which, what, whose*)
5. **Distributive Adjectives**: Refer to members of a group individually. (*each, every, either, neither*)
6. **Numeral Adjectives**: State the number. Can be definite (*one, first*) or indefinite (*many, few*).
7. **Indefinite Adjectives**: Non-specific amount. (*some, any, several, all*)
8. **Proper Adjectives**: Formed from proper nouns. (*Indian, Shakespearean*)

### 4.2 Order of Adjectives (DOSASCOM)

When you use multiple adjectives before a noun, they must follow a specific order. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **TCS & Infosys love this rule!** They will give you a sentence like: "He bought a red beautiful large car." (Incorrect).

```rule
The Rule of Adjective Order: D-O-S-A-S-C-O-M

1. Determiner (a, an, the, four, some)
2. Opinion (beautiful, ugly, valuable, interesting)
3. Size (large, small, tall, short)
4. Age (new, old, antique, young)
5. Shape (round, square, oval, flat)
6. Color (red, black, blue, dark)
7. Origin (Indian, French, lunar, Victorian)
8. Material (wooden, metallic, silk, plastic)
+ NOUN
```

> [!TIP]
> **Mnemonic:** **D**ogs **O**ften **S**care **A**ll **S**tudents **C**atching **O**ld **M**ice (Determiner, Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material).

**Examples:**
- ❌ *Incorrect:* An Indian beautiful old wooden box.
- ✅ *Correct:* A beautiful (Opinion) old (Age) Indian (Origin) wooden (Material) box.

- ❌ *Incorrect:* Three small round tasty pizzas.
- ✅ *Correct:* Three (Determiner) tasty (Opinion) small (Size) round (Shape) pizzas.

### 4.3 Predicative vs Attributive Adjectives

Where do adjectives go? Most adjectives can go in two places:
1. **Attributive Position:** Directly before the noun. (*The **happy** child.*)
2. **Predicative Position:** After a linking verb (is, am, are, look, seem). (*The child is **happy**.*)

#### The Placement Trap
Some adjectives can **ONLY** be used attributively, and some **ONLY** predicatively.

**1. Adjectives that are ONLY Predicative (Never before a noun)**
Most start with the letter "a": *afraid, alike, alive, alone, ashamed, asleep, awake, aware.*
- ❌ *Incorrect:* He is an **afraid** man.
- ✅ *Correct:* The man is **afraid**. / He is a **frightened** man.

**2. Adjectives that are ONLY Attributive (Always before a noun)**
*Main, sheer, principal, utter, mere, former, indoor/outdoor.*
- ❌ *Incorrect:* The problem is **main**.
- ✅ *Correct:* That is the **main** problem.

### 4.4 Degrees of Comparison

Adjectives have three degrees: Positive, Comparative, and Superlative.

```rule
Rule 1: Use Comparative for TWO things. Use Superlative for THREE OR MORE.
```
- ❌ *Incorrect:* Of the two brothers, Ram is the **smartest**. (Trap!)
- ✅ *Correct:* Of the two brothers, Ram is the **smarter**.

```rule
Rule 2: Never use Double Comparatives or Double Superlatives.
```
- ❌ *Incorrect:* She is **more smarter** than him.
- ✅ *Correct:* She is **smarter** than him.
- ❌ *Incorrect:* This is the **most perfect** solution. (Words like *perfect, unique, dead, impossible, round* are absolute and have no degrees).

#### Confusing Comparisons: Older/Elder vs Farther/Further

| Adjective pair | Usage | Example |
|----------------|-------|---------|
| **Older / Oldest** | For people, animals, and things (Age). | My house is **older** than yours. |
| **Elder / Eldest** | ONLY for blood relations (Family). Followed by 'to', not 'than'. | My **elder** brother is an engineer. |
| **Farther / Farthest** | Physical distance. | Delhi is **farther** from Chennai than Mumbai. |
| **Further / Furthest** | Abstract distance or "additional". | For **further** information, contact HR. |
| **Fewer / Fewest** | Countable nouns. | I have **fewer** books than you. |
| **Less / Least** | Uncountable nouns. | I have **less** water than you. |

### 4.5 Compound Adjectives & Hyphenation

A compound adjective is formed when two or more words work together to modify a noun.

```rule
Hyphenation Rule:
When a compound adjective comes BEFORE a noun, use a hyphen. 
When it comes AFTER a linking verb, drop the hyphen.
NEVER pluralize a hyphenated measurement when it acts as an adjective.
```

**Exception/Trap for Placements (Accenture & IBM Favorite):**
- ❌ *Incorrect:* He is a **ten-years-old** boy. (When used as an adjective modifying 'boy', it cannot be plural).
- ✅ *Correct:* He is a **ten-year-old** boy.
- ✅ *Correct:* The boy is **ten years old**. (Here, 'years' is a noun, so plural is fine).

- ❌ *Incorrect:* I have a **five-hundred-rupees** note.
- ✅ *Correct:* I have a **five-hundred-rupee** note.

### 4.6 Adjective vs Adverb Confusion

This is the most common error detection concept.

**Rule:** Adjectives modify nouns/pronouns. Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

#### Good vs. Well
- **Good** is an adjective.
- **Well** is an adverb (usually). 

- ❌ *Incorrect:* She sings **good**. (Sings is a verb, we need an adverb).
- ✅ *Correct:* She sings **well**.
- ✅ *Correct:* She is a **good** singer. 

> [!WARNING]
> Exception: "Well" can be an adjective when referring to **health**. 
> "I feel well today" means my health is good. 

#### Bad vs. Badly
- ❌ *Incorrect:* I feel **badly** about the mistake. 
  *(Wait, why? "Feel" is a linking verb. Linking verbs like feel, look, smell, taste, seem take ADJECTIVES, not adverbs).*
- ✅ *Correct:* I feel **bad** about the mistake.
- ✅ *Correct:* He played **badly**. (Played is an action verb, requires an adverb).

### 4.7 Practice Questions

---
**Q1. Identify the correct sentence based on adjective order:**
(A) She bought a large beautiful blue Italian silk dress.  
(B) She bought a beautiful large blue Italian silk dress.  
(C) She bought a beautiful blue large Italian silk dress.  
(D) She bought an Italian beautiful large blue silk dress.  

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** According to DOSASCOM (Determiner, Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material).
Determiner: a
Opinion: beautiful
Size: large
Color: blue
Origin: Italian
Material: silk
**❌ Why others are wrong:** They mess up the DOSASCOM sequence. Opinion must come before Size.
**📏 Rule:** DOSASCOM order of adjectives.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Always put OPINION first.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Infosys
---
**Q2. Of the two candidates interviewed yesterday, the former was ____.**
(A) the best
(B) better
(C) most good
(D) more better

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence explicitly states "two candidates". For comparing exactly two items, we must use the comparative degree (better), not the superlative (best).
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Superlative is for 3 or more.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Grammatically incorrect form.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Double comparative.
**📏 Rule:** Comparative degree is used for two items.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture
---
**Q3. Spot the error in the sentence:**
**He is (A) / the most (B) / perfect match (C) / for this job. (D) / No Error (E)**
(A) He is
(B) the most
(C) perfect match
(D) for this job.
(E) No Error

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The word "perfect" is an absolute adjective. You cannot be "more perfect" or "most perfect". You are either perfect or you are not. Remove "the most" and use "a". "He is a perfect match..."
**📏 Rule:** Absolute adjectives do not take comparative or superlative degrees.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini
---
**Q4. The patient is feeling ____ today.**
(A) good
(B) well
(C) mostly good
(D) greatly

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** When talking about health, "well" acts as an adjective. While colloquially people say "I'm feeling good", strictly grammatically speaking regarding recovery from illness, "well" is preferred.
**📏 Rule:** 'Well' is an adjective for health.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Wipro
---
**Q5. The company will only hire ____ engineers.**
(A) alive
(B) alike
(C) dynamic
(D) awake

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Dynamic" can be used attributively (before a noun). Words starting with 'a' like alive, alike, awake are predicative ONLY and cannot be placed right before a noun. You cannot say "an alive engineer".
**📏 Rule:** Predicative vs Attributive adjectives.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM
---
**Q6. I need ____ information before making a decision.**
(A) farther
(B) further
(C) furthest
(D) farthest

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Further" means additional. "Farther" means physical distance. Here we need "additional" information.
**📏 Rule:** Farther vs Further.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Cognizant
---
**Q7. Spot the error:**
**My sister gave me (A) / a two-hundred-pages (B) / notebook for (C) / my college notes. (D)**
(A) My sister gave me
(B) a two-hundred-pages
(C) notebook for
(D) my college notes.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Two-hundred-page" acts as a compound adjective modifying the noun "notebook". Compound adjectives cannot be pluralized. It should be "a two-hundred-page notebook".
**📏 Rule:** Hyphenated compound adjectives are singular.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS
---
**Q8. The soup smells ____, let’s eat it right now!**
(A) deliciously
(B) delicious
(C) very deliciously
(D) highly delicious

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Smells" is a linking verb (verbs of the senses: look, sound, smell, feel, taste). Linking verbs are followed by adjectives, not adverbs. 
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Deliciously" is an adverb.
**📏 Rule:** Verbs of senses take adjectives.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys
---
**Q9. She is elder ____ her cousin by two years.**
(A) than
(B) from
(C) to
(D) by

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The adjective "elder" is always followed by the preposition "to", not "than". "Older" is followed by "than".
**📏 Rule:** Elder to, Older than.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture
---
**Q10. Spot the error:**
**There are (A) / less students (B) / in the class today (C) / than yesterday. (D)**
(A) There are
(B) less students
(C) in the class today
(D) than yesterday.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Students" is a countable noun. We must use "fewer" for countable nouns, and "less" for uncountable nouns (like water, milk, money). It should be "fewer students".
**📏 Rule:** Fewer (countable) vs Less (uncountable).
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Capgemini

### Key Takeaways
1. **DOSASCOM**: Memorize it. Opinion always comes early, material always comes last.
2. **Double Comparatives**: "More better" is a sin in grammar.
3. **Compound Adjectives**: Never pluralize them (ten-year-old boy, NOT ten-years-old boy).
4. **Linking Verbs**: Look, feel, smell, taste, seem take **adjectives**, not adverbs! (You look good, not you look well).
5. **Less vs Fewer**: Less money (uncountable), fewer rupees (countable). Less volume, fewer books.
