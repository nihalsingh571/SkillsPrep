## 22.1 What is an Idiom?

An idiom is a phrase whose figurative meaning is completely different from its literal meaning. You cannot translate them word-for-word. (e.g., "Kick the bucket" means to die, not to physically kick a pail).

> [!TIP]
> **Placement Strategy:** **TCS and Wipro** frequently test idioms by placing them in a sentence and asking for the meaning. Do not choose the literal option. If the idiom involves "cats and dogs," the answer has nothing to do with animals.

## 22.2 High-Frequency Idioms (Categorized)

### 🦁 Animal Idioms
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Let the cat out of the bag** | Reveal a secret | He let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party. |
| **A dark horse** | An unknown person who succeeds | The new startup is a dark horse in the tech industry. |
| **Barking up the wrong tree** | Accusing the wrong person/pursuing a false lead | If you think I stole the code, you're barking up the wrong tree. |
| **Hold your horses** | Wait, be patient | Hold your horses! We can't deploy until testing is done. |
| **A bull in a china shop** | Extremely clumsy in a delicate situation | When dealing with HR, he is like a bull in a china shop. |
| **Straight from the horse's mouth** | Hearing from the original source | I heard the CEO is resigning; I got it straight from the horse's mouth. |

### 🧠 Body Part Idioms
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost an arm and a leg** | Very expensive | That new Macbook costs an arm and a leg. |
| **Cold feet** | Loss of nerve/confidence | He got cold feet right before the presentation. |
| **By the skin of your teeth** | Just barely | We passed the audit by the skin of our teeth. |
| **Keep an eye on** | Monitor carefully | Keep an eye on the server logs tonight. |
| **Bite the bullet** | Face a difficult situation bravely | We have to bite the bullet and rewrite the legacy code. |
| **Rule of thumb** | A general, unwritten rule | As a rule of thumb, always comment your code. |

### 🎨 Color Idioms
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Out of the blue** | Unexpectedly | The layoff announcement came out of the blue. |
| **Catch someone red-handed** | Catch someone in the act of doing wrong | The firewall caught the hacker red-handed. |
| **Once in a blue moon** | Very rarely | I eat junk food once in a blue moon. |
| **Red tape** | Excessive bureaucracy/rules | There is too much red tape to get a new laptop approved. |
| **Green with envy** | Very jealous | My colleagues were green with envy over my promotion. |

### 💼 Business & Work Idioms (Crucial for Placements)
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut corners** | Do something poorly to save time/money | Never cut corners when writing security protocols. |
| **Get down to brass tacks** | Focus on the essential facts | Let's stop chatting and get down to brass tacks. |
| **On the back burner** | Postponed, low priority | We put the UI redesign on the back burner. |
| **Call it a day** | Stop working for the rest of the day | We've been coding for 10 hours; let's call it a day. |
| **Go the extra mile** | Make more effort than is expected | She always goes the extra mile for her clients. |
| **Think outside the box** | Think creatively/unconventionally | To solve this bug, we need to think outside the box. |

## 22.3 High-Frequency Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a Verb + Preposition/Adverb that creates a totally new meaning. 

### 📞 The "CALL" Family
*   **Call off:** Cancel (They called off the meeting).
*   **Call for:** Demand / Require (This situation calls for immediate action).
*   **Call on:** Visit someone briefly / Ask someone to speak (The professor called on me).

### 💔 The "BREAK" Family
*   **Break down:** Stop working (machine) / Lose emotional control. (The server broke down).
*   **Break into:** Enter forcefully. (Hackers broke into the database).
*   **Break out:** Start suddenly (fire, war, disease). (A fire broke out in the server room).
*   **Break up:** End a relationship / Disperse. (The meeting broke up at 5 PM).

### ⬇️ The "PUT" Family
*   **Put off:** Postpone. (Never put off until tomorrow what you can do today).
*   **Put up with:** Tolerate. (I cannot put up with this slow internet).
*   **Put out:** Extinguish. (Put out the fire).
*   **Put on:** Wear / Gain (weight).

### 👀 The "LOOK" Family
*   **Look after:** Take care of. (Look after the new interns).
*   **Look into:** Investigate. (We need to look into this bug).
*   **Look forward to:** Anticipate with pleasure. (I look forward to joining the team).
*   **Look down upon:** Despise / Think someone is inferior. (Never look down upon juniors).

## 22.4 Practice Questions

**Q1. Fill in the blank with the correct idiom:**
The project was supposed to be a secret, but John ____________.
(A) let the cat out of the bag
(B) barked up the wrong tree
(C) beat around the bush
(D) bit the bullet

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** To let the cat out of the bag means to reveal a secret, usually accidentally. 
**Rule:** Idiom meaning.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** TCS

**Q2. What is the meaning of the underlined phrase?**
When the manager asked about the missing funds, the accountant tried to **beat around the bush**.
(A) To search everywhere
(B) To avoid talking about what is important
(C) To act aggressively
(D) To confess immediately

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** To beat around the bush means to avoid answering a question directly or to avoid the main topic.
**Rule:** Idiom meaning.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Infosys

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
Due to the sudden heavy rain, the management had to ______ the outdoor corporate event.
(A) call out
(B) call on
(C) call off
(D) call for

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Call off" means to cancel an event.
**Rule:** Phrasal Verbs (Call).
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Wipro

**Q4. What is the meaning of the idiom: "A blessing in disguise"?**
(A) A gift given anonymously
(B) A good thing that seemed bad at first
(C) A religious ceremony
(D) A person who pretends to be a friend but is an enemy

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Something that seems unlucky/bad initially, but results in a good outcome. (e.g., Getting fired was a blessing in disguise because I found a much better job).
**Rule:** Idiom meaning.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Accenture

**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
I cannot ______ his arrogant behavior any longer; I am resigning.
(A) put off
(B) put out
(C) put up with
(D) put down

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Put up with" means to tolerate or endure something unpleasant.
**Rule:** Phrasal Verbs (Put).
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** IBM
