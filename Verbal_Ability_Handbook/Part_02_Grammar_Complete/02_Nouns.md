## 2.1 Types of Nouns — Complete Classification

### The Big Picture: Noun Family Tree

```
NOUNS
├── Proper Nouns (specific names)
├── Common Nouns
│   ├── Concrete Nouns
│   │   ├── Countable
│   │   │   ├── Singular
│   │   │   └── Plural
│   │   └── Uncountable (Material/Mass)
│   └── Abstract Nouns
│       ├── Countable abstract (idea, plan)
│       └── Uncountable abstract (love, peace)
└── Collective Nouns
```

---

### 2.1.1 Proper Nouns

**Definition:** Names of specific, unique people, places, organizations, or things. Always capitalized.

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| People | Ravi, Sundar Pichai, Mahatma Gandhi |
| Places | Mumbai, India, the Ganges |
| Organizations | TCS, Google, IIT Delhi |
| Events | Diwali, World Cup, Independence Day |
| Titles (with names) | Dr. Sharma, President Modi |

**Key Grammatical Rules:**
- Proper nouns usually do NOT take articles (except special cases — see Chapter 1)
- Proper nouns do NOT have plural forms (usually)
- When a proper noun is used as a common noun (metaphorically), it can take articles: "He thinks he's a Napoleon." "She became a Gandhi of our times."

---

### 2.1.2 Common Nouns

**Definition:** General names for people, places, things, or ideas — NOT specific.

- dog, city, book, teacher, river, software

Common nouns are further classified into:

#### Concrete Nouns
Nouns that can be perceived by the five senses (touch, see, hear, smell, taste).
- Countable concrete: book, table, student, car
- Uncountable concrete (material/mass): water, rice, sand, iron, gold

#### Abstract Nouns
Nouns that represent ideas, qualities, emotions, conditions — cannot be perceived by senses.

| Countable Abstract | Uncountable Abstract |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| idea (ideas) | love |
| plan (plans) | peace |
| suggestion | freedom |
| decision | happiness |
| thought | knowledge |

> [!TIP]
> **Forming Abstract Nouns:** Many abstract nouns are formed from adjectives, verbs, or other nouns:
> - Adjective → Abstract: happy → **happiness**, brave → **bravery**, kind → **kindness**
> - Verb → Abstract: decide → **decision**, achieve → **achievement**, arrive → **arrival**
> - Noun → Abstract: friend → **friendship**, child → **childhood**, king → **kingdom**

---

### 2.1.3 Collective Nouns

**Definition:** A noun that refers to a **group** of individuals or things as a single unit.

#### Collective Nouns for People

| Group | Collective Noun |
|-------|----------------|
| Students | a class of students |
| Soldiers | an army of soldiers |
| Singers | a choir of singers |
| Criminals | a gang of criminals |
| Directors | a board of directors |
| Ministers | a cabinet of ministers |
| Jurors | a jury of jurors |
| Workers | a staff/crew of workers |

#### Collective Nouns for Animals

| Animal | Collective Noun |
|--------|----------------|
| Wolves | a pack of wolves |
| Fish | a school/shoal of fish |
| Bees | a swarm of bees |
| Birds | a flock of birds |
| Lions | a pride of lions |
| Elephants | a herd of elephants |
| Whales | a pod of whales |
| Crows | a murder of crows |
| Geese | a gaggle of geese |

> [!NOTE]
> **British vs American English on Collective Nouns:**
> - British: "The team **are** playing well." (collective noun = plural)
> - American: "The team **is** playing well." (collective noun = singular)
> - In Indian placement exams: generally treat collective nouns as **singular** unless the context clearly shows members acting individually.

---

### 2.1.4 Material/Mass Nouns (Uncountable)

**Definition:** Nouns that represent substances, materials, or concepts that cannot be counted individually.

**Categories:**
1. **Liquids:** water, milk, blood, oil, juice
2. **Gases:** air, oxygen, steam, smoke
3. **Solids (powders/grains):** rice, sand, flour, sugar, salt
4. **Metals/Minerals:** gold, silver, iron, coal, copper
5. **Natural phenomena:** rain, thunder, lightning, sunshine
6. **Abstract qualities:** beauty, honesty, courage, wisdom

> [!WARNING]
> **Common Mistake:** Treating uncountable nouns as countable:
> - ❌ "She gave me informations." → ✅ "She gave me **information**." (no plural)
> - ❌ "He needs a furniture." → ✅ "He needs **furniture**."
> - ❌ "They have many luggages." → ✅ "They have **much luggage**."

**Making Uncountable Nouns Countable (Partitives):**

| Uncountable Noun | Partitive Expression |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| water | a **glass of** water, a **bottle of** water |
| bread | a **loaf of** bread, a **slice of** bread |
| rice | a **bowl of** rice, a **bag of** rice |
| advice | a **piece of** advice |
| information | a **piece of** information |
| furniture | a **piece of** furniture |
| luggage | a **piece of** luggage |
| news | a **piece of** news |
| paper | a **sheet of** paper |

---

### 2.1.5 Compound Nouns

**Definition:** Nouns formed by combining two or more words.

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| Noun + Noun | toothbrush, football, classroom, software |
| Adjective + Noun | blackboard, greenhouse, hardware |
| Verb + Noun | breakwater, pickpocket |
| Noun + Verb | sunrise, rainfall, snowfall |
| Verb + Preposition | output, income, outbreak |
| Preposition + Noun | income, underground |

**Plural of Compound Nouns:**

```
rule
For CLOSED compound nouns (one word): Add -s/-es to the END
For HYPHENATED compound nouns: Add -s to the MAIN/HEAD noun
For OPEN compound nouns (two words): Add -s to the MAIN noun
```

| Compound Noun | Plural Form | Rule |
|---------------|-------------|------|
| toothbrush | toothbrush**es** | End of word |
| mother-in-law | mother**s**-in-law | Main noun 'mother' |
| passer-by | passer**s**-by | Main noun 'passer' |
| attorney general | attorney**s** general | Main noun 'attorney' |
| runner-up | runner**s**-up | Main noun 'runner' |
| forget-me-not | forget-me-not**s** | Entire phrase treated as unit |

## 2.2 Plural Formation Rules — Complete System

### Rule 1: Regular Plurals — Add -s

**Most nouns:** Simply add **-s**

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| book | books |
| student | students |
| computer | computers |
| table | tables |

---

### Rule 2: Nouns Ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -z — Add -es

**Mnemonic:** **S-S-S-C-X-Z** → add -ES

| Ending | Singular | Plural |
|--------|----------|--------|
| -s | bus | bus**es** |
| -ss | class | class**es** |
| -sh | bush | bush**es** |
| -ch | church | church**es** |
| -x | box | box**es** |
| -z | quiz | quiz**zes** |

> [!NOTE]
> **Exception for -ch:** Words where -ch sounds like /k/ add only -s:
> - stomach → stomachs (not stomaches)
> - monarch → monarchs

---

### Rule 3: Nouns Ending in Consonant + -y — Change y to i, Add -es

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| city | cit**ies** |
| country | countr**ies** |
| baby | bab**ies** |
| quality | qualit**ies** |
| category | categor**ies** |
| lady | lad**ies** |

> [!WARNING]
> **Exception:** If -y is preceded by a VOWEL, simply add -s (NO change to y):
> - key → key**s** (not keies)
> - boy → boy**s** (not boies)
> - day → day**s**
> - monkey → monkey**s**

---

### Rule 4: Nouns Ending in -f or -fe — Change to -ves

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| leaf | lea**ves** |
| knife | kni**ves** |
| life | li**ves** |
| wife | wi**ves** |
| wolf | wol**ves** |
| half | hal**ves** |
| shelf | shel**ves** |
| loaf | loa**ves** |
| self | sel**ves** |
| thief | thie**ves** |

**Exceptions (just add -s):**

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| roof | roof**s** |
| chief | chief**s** |
| cliff | cliff**s** |
| belief | belief**s** |
| reef | reef**s** |
| proof | proof**s** |

> [!TIP]
> **Mnemonic for -f to -ves:** SHELF LEAVES HALF WOLVES KNIFE LIVES — these transform. ROOF CHIEF CLIFF — these don't.

---

### Rule 5: Nouns Ending in -o

Some add -s, some add -es — must be memorized.

**Add -es (the "POTATO-TOMATO" group):**

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| potato | potato**es** |
| tomato | tomato**es** |
| hero | hero**es** |
| volcano | volcano**es** |
| echo | echo**es** |
| mosquito | mosquito**es** |
| torpedo | torpedo**es** |

**Add -s (foreign/musical/abbreviated words):**

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| photo | photo**s** |
| piano | piano**s** |
| radio | radio**s** |
| video | video**s** |
| logo | logo**s** |
| memo | memo**s** |
| studio | studio**s** |
| portfolio | portfolio**s** |

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick:** Native English -o words with LIFE: potato, tomato, hero → add -ES (they're alive/food/heroes). Modern/borrowed/musical words → add just -S.

| woman | **women** |

### Rule 7: Same Form Singular and Plural (Zero-Plural Nouns)

```
rule
These nouns have IDENTICAL singular and plural forms.
The verb tells you which form is being used.
```

| Noun | Singular Example | Plural Example |
|------|-----------------|---------------|
| sheep | One sheep is grazing. | Five sheep are grazing. |
| fish | A fish is swimming. | Many fish are swimming. |
| deer | A deer ran past. | Twenty deer ran past. |
| aircraft | The aircraft is landing. | Three aircraft are landing. |
| series | This series is good. | Two series were made. |
| species | This species is rare. | Many species are endangered. |
| offspring | One offspring survived. | Many offspring survived. |
| means | This means is effective. | All means are exhausted. |
| crossroads | The crossroads is near. | Many crossroads were blocked. |

> [!WARNING]
> **"Fish" vs "Fishes":** 
> - "Fish" (plural) = multiple fish of the same species: "There are five fish in the tank."
> - "Fishes" = multiple different species: "The river has many fishes" (meaning different types).
> In placement exams, "fish" as plural is the standard answer.

## 2.3 Latin and Greek Plurals — The Placement Trap Masters

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Latin and Greek plural forms are **extremely frequently tested** in TCS, Infosys, IBM, and Accenture exams. Using "criterias", "datas", or "phenomenons" is a classic error in error-detection questions.

### The Critical Latin/Greek Plural Table

| Singular | Plural | Pattern | Domain |
|----------|--------|---------|--------|
| criterion | **criteria** | -on → -a | Logic, standards |
| datum | **data** | -um → -a | Computer science |
| medium | **media** | -um → -a | Communication |
| curriculum | **curricula** | -um → -a | Education |
| memorandum | **memoranda** | -um → -a | Business |
| symposium | **symposia** | -um → -a | Academic |
| stratum | **strata** | -um → -a | Geology |
| bacterium | **bacteria** | -um → -a | Biology |
| erratum | **errata** | -um → -a | Publishing |
| phenomenon | **phenomena** | -on → -a | Science |
| criterion | **criteria** | -on → -a | Logic |
| analysis | **analyses** | -is → -es | Science |
| thesis | **theses** | -is → -es | Academic |
| hypothesis | **hypotheses** | -is → -es | Science |
| crisis | **crises** | -is → -es | General |
| basis | **bases** | -is → -es | General |
| diagnosis | **diagnoses** | -is → -es | Medical |
| axis | **axes** | -is → -es | Math |
| parenthesis | **parentheses** | -is → -es | Grammar |
| oasis | **oases** | -is → -es | Geography |
| alumnus | **alumni** | -us → -i | Education |
| cactus | **cacti** (or cactuses) | -us → -i | Biology |
| nucleus | **nuclei** | -us → -i | Physics |
| syllabus | **syllabi** (or syllabuses) | -us → -i | Education |
| stimulus | **stimuli** | -us → -i | Psychology |
| radius | **radii** | -us → -i | Math |
| fungus | **fungi** | -us → -i | Biology |
| focus | **foci** (or focuses) | -us → -i | Optics |
| appendix | **appendices** | -ix/-ex → -ices | Medical |
| index | **indices** (or indexes) | -ix/-ex → -ices | Math/Library |
| matrix | **matrices** | -ix/-ex → -ices | Math |
| vertex | **vertices** | -ix/-ex → -ices | Math |

### Critical Rules for These Plurals:

```
rule
NEVER say:
- "criterias" → should be "criteria"
- "datas" → should be "data"
- "phenomenons" → should be "phenomena"
- "alumnuses" → should be "alumni"
- "analysises" → should be "analyses"
```

> [!WARNING]
> **"Data" debate:** Technically, "data" is plural (datum = singular). In academic/scientific writing: "The data **are** accurate." But in common/tech usage, "data" is treated as singular: "The data **is** ready." In placement exams, when given the choice, use "data ARE" in formal/academic contexts.

### Quick Memory Patterns:

| Pattern | Singular Ending | Plural Ending | Example |
|---------|----------------|---------------|---------|
| Pattern A | -um | -a | datum → data |
| Pattern B | -us | -i | alumnus → alumni |
| Pattern C | -is | -es | crisis → crises |
| Pattern D | -ix/-ex | -ices | matrix → matrices |
| Pattern E | -on | -a | criterion → criteria |

## 2.4 Nouns That Are Always Plural (Pluralia Tantum)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These nouns ALWAYS take plural verbs. Using a singular verb is a guaranteed error in placement tests.

### Group 1: Paired/Two-Part Objects

These nouns refer to things that have two matching parts:

| Noun | Example |
|------|---------|
| scissors | The scissors **are** on the desk. |
| trousers | Your trousers **are** dirty. |
| jeans | These jeans **are** expensive. |
| pants | The pants **are** too long. |
| shorts | His shorts **are** wet. |
| spectacles/glasses | My glasses **are** broken. |
| binoculars | The binoculars **are** useful. |
| tongs | The tongs **are** in the drawer. |
| pliers | The pliers **are** old. |
| shears | The garden shears **are** sharp. |
| tweezers | The tweezers **are** small. |

> [!TIP]
> **Memory Trick for Two-Part Objects:** If the tool/garment has TWO matching parts that would be useless separately, it's always plural. Scissors = two blades. Trousers = two legs.

### Group 2: Always Plural Nouns (Miscellaneous)

| Noun | Example |
|------|---------|
| thanks | **Thanks are** due to the committee. |
| tidings | Good tidings **were** received. |
| riches | His riches **were** well-known. |
| surroundings | The surroundings **are** beautiful. |
| belongings | Her belongings **were** scattered. |
| earnings | His earnings **have** increased. |
| savings | My savings **are** in the bank. |
| premises | The premises **are** locked. |
| outskirts | The outskirts **are** crowded. |
| environs | The environs **are** polluted. |
| clothes | Her clothes **are** fashionable. |
| goods | The goods **have** arrived. |
| remains | The remains **were** identified. |
| lodgings | The lodgings **are** expensive. |
| contents | The contents **of** the box were checked. |
| archives | The archives **are** well-maintained. |
| annals | The annals **of** history record this. |

**To count them, use "a pair of":**
- ❌ "A scissor is on the table."
- ✅ "**A pair of scissors** is on the table." (pair = singular → singular verb)
- ✅ "Two pairs of scissors are on the table." (pairs = plural → plural verb)

## 2.5 Nouns That Look Plural but Are Grammatically Singular

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These nouns END in -s but take SINGULAR verbs. This is the #1 SVA trap in placement exams.

### Group 1: Academic Subjects and Fields

| Noun | Example |
|------|---------|
| mathematics | Mathematics **is** a difficult subject. |
| physics | Physics **was** his favorite. |
| economics | Economics **deals** with resources. |
| statistics | Statistics **is** a branch of math. |
| civics | Civics **is** taught in schools. |
| politics | Politics **is** a dirty game. |
| ethics | Ethics **requires** honesty. |
| linguistics | Linguistics **studies** language. |
| acoustics | Acoustics **is** the study of sound. |
| aesthetics | Aesthetics **involves** beauty. |
| gymnastics | Gymnastics **is** an Olympic sport. |
| athletics | Athletics **requires** training. |
| aerobics | Aerobics **is** good for health. |

> [!WARNING]
> Exception: When these words are used in a descriptive/plural sense:
> - "His statistics **are** impressive." (his personal statistical figures — plural use)

## 2.6 Collective Nouns — Singular or Plural? The Placement Dilemma

```
rule
A collective noun takes a SINGULAR verb when the group acts as ONE UNIT.
A collective noun takes a PLURAL verb when members act INDIVIDUALLY/SEPARATELY.
```

| Sentence | Verb | Reason |
|----------|------|--------|
| The committee **has** decided. | Singular | Acting as one body |
| The committee **are** divided in opinion. | Plural | Members individually divided |
| The team **is** winning. | Singular | Acting as a unit |
| The team **are** arguing among themselves. | Plural | Acting individually |
| The audience **was** silent. | Singular | As a whole group |
| The audience **were** leaving their seats. | Plural | Individual actions |
| The jury **has** reached a verdict. | Singular | One collective decision |
| The jury **are** still deliberating. | Plural | Members individually thinking |

> [!TIP]
> **Quick Test:** Can you add "all" before the collective noun and it still makes sense?
> "The team **all** are arguing" → YES → plural verb.
> "The team **all** has decided" → NO → singular verb.

### Important Collective Nouns for Placement Exams:

| Collective Noun | Usually Singular | Usually Plural |
|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| class | The class is studying. | — |
| team | The team is ready. | The team are quarreling. |
| army | The army is advancing. | — |
| committee | The committee has voted. | The committee are split. |
| government | The government has decided. | — |
| family | The family is united. | The family are all tall. |
| police | The police **are** on patrol. | (police = always plural) |
| staff | The staff is competent. | The staff are all experts. |
| clergy | The clergy are respected. | (tends toward plural) |

> [!WARNING]
> **POLICE is special:** "Police" is ALWAYS plural — it's a collective noun that behaves as a plural noun by convention. "The police **are** investigating."

## 2.7 Gerunds as Nouns

**Definition:** A gerund is the **-ing form of a verb** used as a **noun**. It can perform all noun functions.

### Gerund Functions in Sentences:

| Function | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Subject | **Swimming** is good exercise. |
| Object of verb | She enjoys **reading**. |
| Object of preposition | He is good at **coding**. |
| Complement | Her hobby is **painting**. |
| Appositive | His habit, **smoking**, is harmful. |

### Gerund vs Present Participle:

| | Gerund | Present Participle |
|-|--------|-------------------|
| Function | Noun | Adjective or part of verb phrase |
| Example | **Swimming** is fun. (subject) | She is **swimming**. (verb form) |
| Example | I love **reading**. (object) | The **reading** child is quiet. (adjective) |

### Verbs Followed by Gerunds (not infinitives):

```
rule
FINISH, AVOID, ENJOY, MIND, SUGGEST, CONSIDER, PRACTICE, DENY,
KEEP, POSTPONE, RECALL, RESIST, RISK, DELAY, DISCUSS, IMAGINE → + GERUND
```

- She **finished** reading the report. ✅
- He **avoided** making mistakes. ✅
- They **enjoyed** working together. ✅
- She **denied** stealing the documents. ✅

### Possessive with Gerunds:

```
rule
When a noun or pronoun precedes a gerund, use the POSSESSIVE form.
```

- ✅ I appreciate **his** helping me. (NOT: him helping)
- ✅ **Ravi's** coming late annoyed everyone. (NOT: Ravi coming)
- ✅ Do you mind **my** asking a question? (NOT: me asking)

## 2.8 Possessive Noun Formation — Complete Rules

### Rule 1: Singular Nouns → Add 's

| Noun | Possessive |
|------|-----------|
| dog | dog**'s** collar |
| Ravi | Ravi**'s** book |
| company | company**'s** policy |
| child | child**'s** toy |

---

### Rule 2: Plural Nouns Ending in -s → Add Only '

| Noun | Possessive |
|------|-----------|
| students | students**'** results |
| companies | companies**'** policies |
| teachers | teachers**'** books |
| boys | boys**'** hostel |

---

### Rule 3: Plural Nouns NOT Ending in -s → Add 's

| Noun | Possessive |
|------|-----------|
| children | children**'s** park |
| men | men**'s** section |
| women | women**'s** rights |
| people | people**'s** court |
| mice | mice**'s** (rare, unusual) |

---

### Rule 4: Singular Nouns Ending in -s → Add 's or Just '

Both forms are acceptable; context and style guide:

| Noun | Option 1 | Option 2 |
|------|----------|----------|
| James | James**'s** book | James**'** book |
| boss | boss**'s** office | boss**'** office |
| class | class**'s** performance | class**'** performance |
| bus | bus**'s** route | bus**'** route |

> [!NOTE]
> For **ancient names** ending in -s (classical usage), use only the apostrophe:
> - Achilles' heel (not Achilles's)
> - Jesus' teachings (not Jesus's)
> - Socrates' philosophy (not Socrates's)

---

### Rule 5: Compound Nouns → Possessive on the Last Word

| Compound Noun | Possessive |
|---------------|-----------|
| mother-in-law | mother-in-law**'s** advice |
| attorney general | attorney general**'s** statement |
| commander in chief | commander in chief**'s** order |

---

### Rule 6: Joint Possession vs Separate Possession

```
rule
JOINT POSSESSION: Apostrophe only on the LAST name.
SEPARATE POSSESSION: Apostrophe on EACH name.
```

| Type | Example | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| Joint | Ravi and Priya**'s** house | One house they both own |
| Separate | Ravi**'s** and Priya**'s** houses | Different houses |
| Joint | Ram and Shyam**'s** project | One project together |
| Separate | Ram**'s** and Shyam**'s** projects | Different projects |

---

### Rule 7: 'of' Phrase vs Apostrophe — When to Use Which

```
rule
ANIMATE/LIVING beings → Apostrophe ('s) is preferred
INANIMATE objects → 'of' phrase is preferred
```

| Animate (use 's) | Inanimate (use 'of') |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| the **dog's** paw | the leg **of the table** |
| **Ravi's** decision | the rules **of the game** |
| the **committee's** report | the cover **of the book** |
| **India's** growth | the population **of India** (both acceptable) |

> [!NOTE]
> Exceptions: Some inanimate nouns naturally use 's in fixed expressions:
> - a day**'s** work
> - a stone**'s** throw
> - the world**'s** best
> - a week**'s** time
> - the car**'s** engine (increasingly acceptable)
> - for heaven**'s** sake

**Q2. Which sentence is grammatically CORRECT?**

(B) A scissors are on the table.
(C) The scissors are on the table.
(D) A pair of scissors are on the table.

**✅ Answer: (C) The scissors are on the table.**

**📖 Explanation:** "Scissors" is always plural (pluralia tantum). It takes a plural verb "are." Note: "A pair of scissors IS" (pair = singular verb), but "scissors ARE" (scissors = plural verb).

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Scissors is" — scissors is always plural, takes plural verb.

**📏 Rule:** Always-plural nouns (scissors, trousers, etc.) take plural verbs.

**Q14. Which sentence contains an error?**

**📏 Rule:** Chief → chiefs (exception to -f→-ves rule). Also: roof, proof, cliff, belief → add -s.
