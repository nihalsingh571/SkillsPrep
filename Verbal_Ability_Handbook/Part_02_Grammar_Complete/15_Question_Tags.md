## 15.1 What is a Question Tag?

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement to ask for confirmation or agreement. They are extremely common in spoken English and heavily tested in **TCS NQT and Infosys**.

*   *Statement:* It is raining,
*   *Tag:* isn't it?

### Rule 1: The Golden Rule of Polarity (+/-)
*   If the main statement is **POSITIVE**, the tag must be **NEGATIVE**.
*   If the main statement is **NEGATIVE**, the tag must be **POSITIVE**.

> *   He **is** working hard, **isn't** he? (+ to -)
> *   She **cannot** swim, **can** she? (- to +)

### Rule 2: The Pronoun Rule
The subject of the tag MUST ALWAYS be a pronoun that matches the subject of the statement. Never use a proper noun in the tag.

> *   *Wrong:* John is late, isn't John?
> *   *Correct:* John is late, isn't **he**?

### Rule 3: The Auxiliary Verb Match
The verb in the tag must match the auxiliary (helping) verb in the statement. If there is no auxiliary verb, use **do / does / did**.

*   **Has auxiliary:** They **have** finished, **haven't** they?
*   **Has auxiliary:** She **will** come, **won't** she?
*   **No auxiliary (Present):** He **plays** cricket, **doesn't** he? *(plays -> does)*
*   **No auxiliary (Present):** They **play** cricket, **don't** they? *(play -> do)*
*   **No auxiliary (Past):** She **played** cricket, **didn't** she? *(played -> did)*

## 15.2 The 10 Special Cases & Traps (High Frequency)

This is where placement exams set their traps. Memorize these.

**1. "I am" Trap**
*   The negative tag for "I am" is NOT "amn't I". It is **"aren't I"**.
*   *Correct:* I am selected for the role, **aren't I?**
*   *(Note: The positive tag remains normal: I am not late, **am I?**)*

**2. "Let's" (Let us)**
*   Whenever a sentence starts with "Let's", the tag is ALWAYS **"shall we?"**
*   *Correct:* Let's start the project, **shall we?**

**3. Imperatives (Commands/Requests)**
*   For positive commands, the tag is usually **"will you?"** or **"won't you?"**
*   For negative commands (Don't...), the tag is ALWAYS **"will you?"**
*   *Correct:* Open the door, **will you?**
*   *Correct:* Don't touch that wire, **will you?**

**4. Semi-Negative Words (Hard Trap)**
*   Words like *hardly, scarcely, barely, rarely, seldom, never, few, little* carry a NEGATIVE meaning. Therefore, the tag must be POSITIVE.
*   *Wrong:* He rarely visits us, doesn't he?
*   *Correct:* He **rarely** visits us, **does** he? (rarely is negative)
*   *Correct:* Barking dogs **seldom** bite, **do** they?

**5. Indefinite Pronouns (Everyone, Nobody, Someone)**
*   Words referring to people (Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Nobody, None) take the plural pronoun **"they"** in the tag.
*   Because the pronoun is "they", the verb must change to plural!
*   *Trap:* Everyone **has** arrived, hasn't he? (Wrong)
*   *Trap:* Everyone **has** arrived, hasn't they? (Wrong verb agreement)
*   *Correct:* Everyone **has** arrived, **haven't they?** (Everyone is positive -> negative tag. Pronoun becomes 'they'. Verb 'has' becomes 'have' to match 'they').
*   *Correct:* Nobody **is** perfect, **are they?** (Nobody is negative -> positive tag. 'is' becomes 'are').

**6. Indefinite Pronouns (Everything, Nothing, Something)**
*   Words referring to things take the singular pronoun **"it"** in the tag.
*   *Correct:* Everything is fine, **isn't it?**
*   *Correct:* Nothing matters now, **does it?** (Nothing is negative).

**7. Have as Possession vs Have as Action**
*   *Possession:* I have a car, **don't I?** (or *haven't I?* - British). "Don't I" is preferred in modern exams.
*   *Action:* I had lunch at 2 PM, **didn't I?** (Had here means ate/experienced. No auxiliary, so use did).

**8. Used to**
*   The tag for "used to" is **"didn't"**.
*   *Correct:* He used to smoke, **didn't he?**

**9. This/That vs These/Those**
*   Subject "This/That" -> Tag pronoun **"it"**.
*   Subject "These/Those" -> Tag pronoun **"they"**.
*   *Correct:* That is your laptop, **isn't it?**
*   *Correct:* Those are your keys, **aren't they?**

**10. Had better / Would rather**
*   *He had better go* -> auxiliary is 'had' -> tag: **hadn't he?**
*   *He would rather stay* -> auxiliary is 'would' -> tag: **wouldn't he?**

## 15.4 Practice Questions

**Q1. Fill in the blank:**
The new interns hardly ever submit their reports on time, ______?
(A) do they
(B) don't they
(C) are they
(D) aren't they

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Hardly" is a semi-negative word, making the statement negative. Therefore, the tag must be positive. The main verb is "submit" (present tense, plural), so the auxiliary is "do".
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Negative tag for a negative statement.
**❌ Why (C/D) is wrong:** Uses "are" instead of "do". The verb is an action verb (submit), not a 'to be' verb.
**📏 Rule:** Negative words (hardly, rarely, seldom) take positive tags.
**⚡ Shortcut:** See "hardly/scarcely/barely/seldom/never" = Positive Tag.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Infosys

---

**Q2. Choose the correct tag:**
I am responsible for deploying the final code to production, ______?
(A) am not I
(B) amn't I
(C) aren't I
(D) don't I

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence is positive ("I am"). The negative tag for "I am" is an irregular exception in English grammar. It is always "aren't I?".
**❌ Why (A/B) is wrong:** "amn't I" doesn't exist in formal English.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Incorrect auxiliary verb.
**📏 Rule:** Exception for "I am".
**⚡ Shortcut:** I am = aren't I. I am not = am I.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT

---

**Q3. Fill in the blank:**
Let's take a 10-minute break before we start the next module, ______?
(A) will we
(B) don't we
(C) shall we
(D) can't we

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Sentences beginning with "Let's" (Let us) always take the tag "shall we?".
**❌ Why (A/B/D) is wrong:** They violate the fixed structure for "Let's".
**📏 Rule:** Let's = shall we?
**⚡ Shortcut:** See "Let's", pick "shall we". No thinking required.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro

---

**Q4. Choose the correct tag:**
Everyone in the office has completed the mandatory security training, ______?
(A) hasn't he
(B) haven't they
(C) hasn't they
(D) have they

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Everyone" is positive, so the tag is negative. The pronoun for "Everyone" in a tag is always "they". The verb "has" (singular) must change to "have" (plural) to agree with the pronoun "they".
**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Uses "he". "They" is the required pronoun for indefinite pronouns of people.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "hasn't they" is grammatically incorrect (subject-verb disagreement).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** Positive tag for a positive statement.
**📏 Rule:** Indefinite pronouns (Everyone/Everybody/Someone/Nobody) = 'They' + Plural verb.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Everyone has = haven't they. Nobody has = have they.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, Cognizant

---

**Q5. Fill in the blank:**
You'd better finish the assignment before the manager arrives, ______?
(A) didn't you
(B) hadn't you
(C) wouldn't you
(D) shouldn't you

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "You'd better" is a contraction of "You had better". The auxiliary verb is "had". Therefore, the tag is "hadn't you?".
**❌ Why (A/D) is wrong:** Incorrect auxiliary.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** Confuses "had better" with "would rather". (If it was "You'd rather", the tag would be "wouldn't you?").
**📏 Rule:** 'd better = had better -> hadn't. 'd rather = would rather -> wouldn't.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Expand the contraction mentally.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** IBM

**Q13. Question Tag:**
You and I have to submit the project today, ______?
(A) haven't we
(B) haven't you
(C) haven't they
(D) don't we

**✅ Answer: (D)**
**📖 Explanation:** "have to" means "must" (action). In modern English, "don't we" is preferred over "haven't we". Also, "You and I" = "we".
**Rule:** Have as action/obligation.
**Difficulty:** Hard

**Q14. Question Tag:**

(D) did they

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Someone" is positive -> negative tag. Pronoun for Someone is "they". Verb is knocked (past) -> did. "didn't they?".
**Rule:** Indefinite pronoun matching.
**Difficulty:** Medium
