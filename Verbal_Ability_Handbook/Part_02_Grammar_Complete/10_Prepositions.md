## 10.1 What Is a Preposition?

A **preposition** is a word (or group of words) placed **before** a noun or pronoun to show its **relationship** to another word in the sentence.

```
DEFINITION: A preposition links a noun/pronoun to the rest of the sentence
showing relationships of TIME, PLACE, DIRECTION, MANNER, CAUSE, or POSSESSION.
```

**The Word "Preposition" itself tells you:**
- **PRE** = before
- **POSITION** = position
- So a preposition is always **positioned before** its object.

### The Prepositional Phrase

```
Structure: PREPOSITION + (ARTICLE/ADJECTIVE) + NOUN/PRONOUN
Example:   in           the                  garden
Example:   at           my                   desk
Example:   during       the                  meeting
```

### Types of Prepositions

| Type | Examples | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | in, on, at, by, to, for, of, from, with | Basic relations |
| **Compound** | into, onto, upon, within, without, beside | Extended relations |
| **Phrasal** | because of, in front of, on behalf of, instead of | Multi-word |
| **Participial** | considering, regarding, concerning, pending | Derived from verbs |
| **Double** | from behind, from under, from within | Two prepositions |

> [!TIP]
> **Mnemonic for Simple Prepositions:** "**I Often Ask Big Friendly Dogs For Warm Treats**"
> → In, On, At, By, From, During, For, With, To

## 10.2 IN / ON / AT — Time (The Golden Triangle)

This is the **single most tested** preposition topic in placement exams. Think of it as a zoom lens — AT is most specific, ON is medium, IN is most general.

```
TIME ZOOM LENS:
IN  →  Longest periods (years, months, seasons, centuries)
ON  →  Specific days and dates
AT  →  Exact clock times and precise moments
```

### AT for Time

```
Use AT for:
• Clock times:        at 5:00, at noon, at midnight, at 3:30 PM
• Meal times:         at breakfast, at lunch, at dinner
• Specific moments:   at the moment, at the time, at that instant
• Fixed expressions:  at night, at sunrise, at sunset, at dawn, at dusk
• Festivals:          at Christmas, at Diwali, at Eid, at Easter
• Ages:               at the age of 25
```

**Examples:**
1. The meeting starts **at** 9 AM. ✓
2. She studied **at** midnight. ✓
3. He retired **at** the age of 60. ✓
4. The accident happened **at** dawn. ✓
5. I will call you **at** noon. ✓

> [!WARNING]
> **Common Error:** "He comes home **in** night." ❌ → Correct: "He comes home **at** night." ✓
> "Night" as a fixed expression uses AT, not IN.

### ON for Time

```
Use ON for:
• Specific days:      on Monday, on Tuesday, on weekdays, on weekends
• Specific dates:     on 15th August, on March 3rd
• Special days:       on Independence Day, on my birthday, on New Year's Day
• Days + part:        on Monday morning, on Friday evening, on Christmas Day
```

**Examples:**
1. The exam is **on** Monday. ✓
2. She was born **on** 12th January. ✓
3. The party is **on** New Year's Eve. ✓
4. I will meet you **on** Friday afternoon. ✓
5. The office is closed **on** Sundays. ✓

> [!WARNING]
> **Common Error:** "The exam is **in** Monday." ❌ → Correct: "The exam is **on** Monday." ✓

### IN for Time

```
Use IN for:
• Years:              in 2024, in 1947, in the 21st century
• Months:             in January, in March, in December
• Seasons:            in summer, in winter, in spring, in autumn/fall
• Parts of day:       in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening
• Long periods:       in the past, in the future, in the Middle Ages
• Duration (future):  in five minutes, in two weeks, in a moment
• Decades/centuries:  in the 1990s, in the 19th century
```

**Examples:**
1. India became independent **in** 1947. ✓
2. She was promoted **in** January. ✓
3. Birds migrate **in** autumn. ✓
4. I will call you **in** the morning. ✓
5. The project will be completed **in** three weeks. ✓

### The Complete IN/ON/AT Time Table

| Time Reference | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clock times | AT | at 7:30, at noon |
| Night (fixed) | AT | at night |
| Sunrise/Sunset | AT | at dawn, at dusk |
| Christmas/Diwali | AT | at Christmas |
| Days of the week | ON | on Monday |
| Dates | ON | on 26th January |
| Weekends/Weekdays | ON | on weekends |
| Birthdays | ON | on my birthday |
| Years | IN | in 2024 |
| Months | IN | in March |
| Seasons | IN | in summer |
| Parts of day | IN | in the morning |
| Decades/Centuries | IN | in the 1990s |

> [!TIP]
> **Magic Trick:** Ask yourself — How big is the time reference?
> - Tiny (seconds, exact moment) → **AT**
> - Medium (a day) → **ON**
> - Large (month, year, season) → **IN**

## 10.3 IN / ON / AT — Place (The Space Triangle)

```
PLACE ZOOM LENS:
IN  →  Enclosed spaces, inside something (cities, countries, rooms)
ON  →  Surfaces (floor, table, wall, road)
AT  →  Specific points/locations (address, building, event location)
```

### AT for Place

```
Use AT for:
• Specific points:    at the door, at the corner, at the top
• Addresses:          at 14 Baker Street (exact address)
• Events/venues:      at the concert, at the party, at school
• Institutions:       at work, at home, at university
• Direction:          at the end of the street
```

**Examples:**
1. She is waiting **at** the bus stop. ✓
2. He works **at** IBM. ✓
3. Meet me **at** the entrance. ✓
4. I was **at** the meeting all morning. ✓
5. The dog is sitting **at** the gate. ✓

### ON for Place

```
Use ON for:
• Surfaces:           on the table, on the floor, on the wall, on the ceiling
• Lines:              on the road, on the river, on the coast, on the border
• Transport (open):   on the bus, on the train, on the plane, on the ship
• Levels:             on the second floor, on the ground floor
• Body parts:         on his face, on her hand, on the left
• Media:              on TV, on the radio, on the internet, on Facebook
```

**Examples:**
1. The book is **on** the table. ✓
2. I saw it **on** TV. ✓
3. She lives **on** the third floor. ✓
4. The cat sat **on** the mat. ✓
5. We traveled **on** the train. ✓

### IN for Place

```
Use IN for:
• Enclosed spaces:    in the room, in the box, in the building
• Geographic areas:   in India, in Mumbai, in Asia, in the world
• Liquids:            in water, in the ocean, in the lake
• Lines/queues:       in a queue, in a row, in a line
• Containers:         in a bottle, in a jar, in a bag
• Areas:              in the park, in the garden, in the field
```

**Examples:**
1. She lives **in** Mumbai. ✓
2. The key is **in** the drawer. ✓
3. The fish swim **in** the river. ✓
4. He is standing **in** the queue. ✓
5. India is located **in** South Asia. ✓

### AT vs IN for Locations (Tricky!)

| Situation | AT | IN |
|---|---|---|
| Small village (you're there) | at Lonavala | — |
| City you're living in | — | in Mumbai |
| Specific building | at the hospital | — |
| Inside building | — | in the hospital |
| Event | at the wedding | — |
| During trip abroad | in England | — |
| Exact street address | at 10 Downing Street | — |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Distinction:**
> - "I am **at** the hospital" = I am at the hospital (visiting, could be outside)
> - "I am **in** the hospital" = I am admitted as a patient
> This distinction appears in TCS and Infosys error detection!

## 10.4 Prepositions of Movement

```
Prepositions of Movement show DIRECTION or JOURNEY:
TO, INTO, ONTO, THROUGH, ACROSS, ALONG, AROUND, OVER,
UNDER, PAST, TOWARD(S), AWAY FROM, UP, DOWN, OUT OF
```

### Key Movement Prepositions

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to** | Destination | He walked to the store. |
| **into** | Entering enclosed space | She walked into the room. |
| **onto** | Moving onto a surface | He jumped onto the stage. |
| **through** | Moving from one side to other | Drive through the tunnel. |
| **across** | Moving from one side to other (surface) | Swim across the river. |
| **along** | Moving parallel to something | Walk along the road. |
| **around** | Moving in a circle/curve | Run around the track. |
| **over** | Moving above | Jump over the fence. |
| **under** | Moving below | Crawl under the table. |
| **past** | Moving beyond | Walk past the shop. |
| **toward(s)** | Moving in direction of | He ran toward the exit. |
| **up** | Moving higher | Climb up the mountain. |
| **down** | Moving lower | Roll down the hill. |
| **out of** | Exiting enclosed space | She walked out of the room. |
| **away from** | Increasing distance | She moved away from danger. |

### IN vs INTO / ON vs ONTO

```
IN    = Position (static):   He is in the car.
INTO  = Movement (dynamic):  He got into the car.

ON    = Position (static):   The book is on the shelf.
ONTO  = Movement (dynamic):  She put the book onto the shelf.
```

**Examples:**
1. The cat jumped **onto** the sofa. (movement) ✓
2. The cat is **on** the sofa. (position) ✓
3. Pour the water **into** the glass. (movement) ✓
4. The water is **in** the glass. (position) ✓

## 10.8 Common Preposition Errors — The Placement Trap List

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These errors appear in EVERY placement exam. Learn each one cold.

### Error Type 1: ARRIVE AT vs ARRIVE IN

```
ARRIVE AT = small/specific places (station, airport, office, door)
ARRIVE IN = large/geographic areas (cities, countries, continents)
ARRIVE HOME, ARRIVE THERE = no preposition needed
```

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He arrived in the station. | He arrived **at** the station. |
| She arrived at Mumbai. | She arrived **in** Mumbai. |
| I arrived to school late. | I arrived **at** school late. |
| We arrived to Delhi yesterday. | We arrived **in** Delhi yesterday. |
| He arrived to home. | He arrived **home**. (no preposition) |

### Error Type 2: DISCUSS (no "about")

```
DISCUSS = transitive verb → takes direct object WITHOUT preposition
WRONG: "discuss about"
RIGHT: "discuss"
```

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| We discussed about the project. | We **discussed** the project. |
| Let us discuss about this issue. | Let us **discuss** this issue. |

> [!WARNING]
> **Other verbs with the same trap:**
> - ORDER (not "order for"): "He ordered a pizza." ✓
> - ENTER (not "enter into a room"): "She entered the room." ✓
> - REACH (not "reach at/to"): "We reached Delhi." ✓
> - ATTACK (not "attack on"): "They attacked the enemy." ✓
> - RESEMBLE (not "resemble with"): "She resembles her mother." ✓

### Error Type 3: MARRIED TO vs MARRIED WITH

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She is married with a doctor. | She is married **to** a doctor. |
| He got married with Priya. | He got married **to** Priya. |

### Error Type 4: SUPERIOR/INFERIOR/SENIOR/JUNIOR + TO

```
Superior TO, Inferior TO, Senior TO, Junior TO, Prior TO
(NEVER use "than" with these)
```

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He is senior than me. | He is senior **to** me. |
| This is superior than that. | This is superior **to** that. |
| Quality is preferable than speed. | Quality is preferable **to** speed. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Memory Trick:** These words end in "-or" (superiOR, inferiOR, seniOR). They need TO because they already CARRY a comparison meaning — no need for "than."

### Error Type 5: COMPRISE — No "of"

```
COMPRISE = consist of, made up of
"Comprise of" is WRONG → Just use "comprise"
OR rephrase: "is composed of" / "consists of"
```

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| The team comprises of 10 members. | The team **comprises** 10 members. |
| India comprises of 28 states. | India **comprises** 28 states. |

### Error Type 6: COPE WITH (not cope up with)

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She cannot cope up with stress. | She cannot **cope with** stress. |
| He is unable to cope up with the workload. | He is unable to **cope with** the workload. |

### Error Type 7: LOOK FORWARD TO + Gerund

```
"Look forward to" — TO is a PREPOSITION here
Must be followed by a NOUN or GERUND (not base verb)
```

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| I look forward to meet you. | I look forward to **meeting** you. |
| She looks forward to hear from you. | She looks forward to **hearing** from you. |

### Error Type 8: ENTER (not enter into)

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She entered into the room. | She **entered** the room. |
| He entered into the hall. | He **entered** the hall. |
| They entered into an agreement. | ✅ Acceptable (abstract noun) |

### Error Type 9: PREFER ... TO (not prefer ... than)

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| I prefer coffee than tea. | I prefer coffee **to** tea. |
| She prefers reading than watching TV. | She prefers reading **to** watching TV. |

### Error Type 10: REASON FOR (not reason of/behind)

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| The reason of the delay is unknown. | The reason **for** the delay is unknown. |
| What is the reason of his failure? | What is the reason **for** his failure? |

### Error Type 11: DIFFERENT FROM (not different than/to)

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| This is different than expected. | This is **different from** what was expected. |
| His approach is different to mine. | His approach is **different from** mine. |

### Quick-Fire Error Corrections Table

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| He is envious on her. | He is envious **of** her. | Envious OF |
| She is desirous for success. | She is desirous **of** success. | Desirous OF |
| I am confident on winning. | I am confident **of** winning. | Confident OF |
| She died with an accident. | She died **in** an accident. | Die IN an accident |
| He is addicted of drugs. | He is addicted **to** drugs. | Addicted TO |
| She is devoted for her work. | She is devoted **to** her work. | Devoted TO |
| He is allergic with dust. | He is allergic **to** dust. | Allergic TO |
| I am bored of this work. | I am bored **with** this work. | Bored WITH |
| She married with a lawyer. | She married **a** lawyer. | Married = transitive |

## 10.10 Practice Questions — 25 Questions with Full Solutions

**Q1. She arrived _____ the airport two hours before the flight.**

(A) in
(B) on
(C) at
(D) to

**✅ Answer: (C) at**

**📖 Explanation:** Airport is a specific, defined location (not a city or country). Use AT for specific points/buildings/venues.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** IN is used for cities, countries, enclosed spaces. Airport is a specific point.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** ON is used for surfaces, specific days — not for specific locations.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** ARRIVE TO is never correct. Arrive is always followed by AT or IN.

**📏 Rule:** ARRIVE AT (small/specific places), ARRIVE IN (cities/countries)
**⚡ Shortcut:** If it is a geographic area → IN; if it is a specific building/point → AT.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys

---

**Q2. The students discussed _____ the upcoming exam for hours.**

(A) about
(B) on
(C) regarding
(D) — (no preposition)

**✅ Answer: (D) — (no preposition)**

**📖 Explanation:** DISCUSS is a transitive verb that directly takes an object. "Discuss about" is incorrect.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Discuss about" is a common Indian English error — DISCUSS already means "talk about."
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Discuss on" is equally wrong.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** REGARDING would change the sentence structure.

**📏 Rule:** DISCUSS = transitive verb; no preposition required
**⚡ Shortcut:** Replace DISCUSS with "talk about" — you'd say "talk about the exam." DISCUSS drops the "about."
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture, IBM, Infosys

---

**Q3. He is married _____ a prominent cardiologist in the city.**

(A) with
(B) to
(C) by
(D) from

**✅ Answer: (B) to**

**📖 Explanation:** "Married to" is the correct collocation. "Married with" is a very common Indian English error.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** WITH shows accompaniment, not the marital bond.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** BY indicates the officiating person ("married by a priest"), not the spouse.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Married from" is grammatically incorrect.

**📏 Rule:** MARRIED TO (not married with)
**⚡ Shortcut:** "Committed TO a person" = "married TO a person."
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** TCS, Cognizant, Wipro

---

**Q4. This product is superior _____ all competing brands in the market.**

(A) than
(B) over
(C) to
(D) above

**✅ Answer: (C) to**

**📖 Explanation:** Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior — all take TO, never THAN. They already carry comparison within them.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** THAN is used with comparative adjectives (bigger than, better than) but not with Latin comparatives like superior.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** OVER is not the correct preposition for superior.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** ABOVE is a preposition of position.

**📏 Rule:** Superior/Inferior/Senior/Junior/Prior all take TO
**⚡ Shortcut:** Mnemonic: SISJ+P words (Superior, Inferior, Senior, Junior, Prior) → TO
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** IBM, Accenture, TCS

---

**Q5. I am looking forward to _____ you at the annual conference.**

(A) meet
(B) met
(C) having met
(D) meeting

**✅ Answer: (D) meeting**

**📖 Explanation:** "Look forward to" — TO is a PREPOSITION, not part of an infinitive. Prepositions must be followed by a noun or gerund.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** Base verb cannot follow a preposition — TO here is not an infinitive marker.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** Past tense cannot follow a preposition.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Having met" implies the meeting already occurred.

**📏 Rule:** LOOK FORWARD TO + Gerund (TO is a preposition here)
**⚡ Shortcut:** Test by replacing "to meeting" with "to the meeting" — if natural, TO is a preposition → use gerund.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, Infosys, Capgemini

---

**Q6. The team was unable to cope _____ the pressure of the deadline.**

(A) up
(B) up with
(C) with
(D) against

**✅ Answer: (C) with**

**📖 Explanation:** The correct idiom is "cope WITH." "Cope up with" is an Indian English error not found in standard English.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Cope up" is incomplete and incorrect.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Cope up with" is wrong — there is no "up" in the standard idiom.
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Cope against" is not a standard expression.

**📏 Rule:** COPE WITH (not cope up with)
**⚡ Shortcut:** "I deal WITH, I cope WITH." Both use WITH.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture

---

**Q7. She is angry _____ her team for missing the deadline.**

(A) at
(B) about
(C) with
(D) on

**✅ Answer: (C) with**

**📖 Explanation:** "Angry WITH" is used when angry at a PERSON. "Angry AT" is for situations/things.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** AT is for situations: "angry AT the situation." For persons, use WITH.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Angry about" works for topics/situations, not directly for a person (team).
**❌ Why (D) is wrong:** "Angry on" is not standard English.

**📏 Rule:** Angry WITH (person) / Angry AT (situation or thing)
**⚡ Shortcut:** Person = WITH. Thing/Situation = AT.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Infosys, TCS NQT

**Q8. The children entered _____ the classroom noisily.**

(A) into
(B) in
(C) to
(D) — (no preposition)

**✅ Answer: (D) — (no preposition)**

**📖 Explanation:** ENTER is a transitive verb meaning "to go into." Adding INTO is redundant for physical spaces.

**❌ Why (A) is wrong:** "Enter into the classroom" is redundant. "Enter into" is acceptable only with abstract nouns like agreements.
**❌ Why (B) is wrong:** "Enter in" is incorrect.
**❌ Why (C) is wrong:** "Enter to" is incorrect.

**📏 Rule:** ENTER + direct object (no preposition for physical spaces)
**⚡ Shortcut:** Other verbs: Reach, Discuss, Order, Attack, Resemble — all take direct objects without prepositions.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** TCS NQT, IBM
