## 18.1 The "God Mode" 6-Step Scanning System

When you look at an Error Detection question, do not just read it and rely on "what sounds right." Spoken English is full of errors. You must scan the sentence like a compiler debugging code.

Follow this strict 6-step checklist for EVERY sentence:

### 🔍 Step 1: Check Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA)
*   Find the main verb. Ask: "Who or what is doing this action?"
*   Match the subject to the verb. 
*   **Trap:** Ignore prepositional phrases in the middle!
*   *Scan:* The quality [of the apples] **were** poor. -> *Error found: quality is singular, 'were' is plural. Fix: was.*

### 🔍 Step 2: Check Tense Consistency
*   Did the sentence start in the past and suddenly jump to the present without a logical reason?
*   *Scan:* He **went** to the store and **buys** milk. -> *Error found: went (past) vs buys (present). Fix: bought.*

### 🔍 Step 3: Check Pronouns
*   Does every pronoun (it, they, his, her, their, which, who) have a clear, matching noun it refers to?
*   *Scan:* The company updated **their** policy. -> *Error found: 'company' is an 'it', not a 'they'. Fix: its.*

### 🔍 Step 4: Check Prepositions and Phrasal Verbs
*   Are fixed prepositions used correctly? 
*   *Scan:* He is senior **than** me. -> *Error found: senior takes 'to'. Fix: senior to.*

### 🔍 Step 5: Check Parallelism
*   Are items in a list grammatically identical?
*   *Scan:* I like hiking, swimming, and **to run**. -> *Error found: breaking the -ing pattern. Fix: running.*

### 🔍 Step 6: Check Articles and Modifiers
*   Is 'a/an/the' used correctly? Are modifiers dangling?
*   *Scan:* Walking in the park, **a snake** bit him. -> *Error found: the snake wasn't walking. Fix: While he was walking...*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Placement Strategy:** **TCS, Infosys, and Cognizant** use Step 1 (SVA) and Step 4 (Prepositions) in 60% of their error detection questions. Always check those first to save time!

## 18.2 Rapid-Fire Error Patterns (Memorize These)

| The Trap (Incorrect) | The Fix (Correct) | Why? (The Rule) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| discuss about | discuss | 'Discuss' is a transitive verb; no preposition. |
| return back | return | Redundancy (return implies back). |
| comprise of | comprise | 'Comprise' = 'consist of'. Do not add 'of' to comprise in active voice. |
| despite of | despite | Use 'despite' OR 'in spite of'. Never mix them. |
| cope up with | cope with | 'up' is slang. It is always 'cope with'. |
| order for | order | Transitive verb. I ordered a pizza. |
| angry on | angry with | Angry with a person. Angry at a situation. |
| prefer...than | prefer...to | 'Prefer' always takes 'to'. |
| one of the boy | one of the boys | 'One of the' must be followed by a plural noun. |
| did not went | did not go | 'did' is already past tense. V1 must follow. |
| informations, furnitures | information, furniture | Uncountable nouns have no plural form. |
| cousin brother | cousin | Redundancy. Just use 'cousin'. |

## 18.3 Master Question Bank: Deep Error Detection

**Q1. (SVA Trap)**
The manager, as well as the lead developers and the QA team, are responsible for the recent production bug.
(A) The manager, as well as
(B) the lead developers and the QA team,
(C) are responsible for
(D) the recent production bug.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 1 Scan! The subject is "The manager". The phrase "as well as the lead developers..." is an additive phrase, not a compound subject. The verb must agree with the first subject ("manager", singular). "are" should be "is".
**Rule:** Nouns joined by *as well as, along with, together with* agree with the FIRST subject.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Accenture, TCS

**Q2. (Tense Sequence Trap)**
The CEO stated that the company will launch its new product in the European market next quarter.
(A) The CEO stated
(B) that the company
(C) will launch its new product
(D) in the European market next quarter.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 2 Scan! The reporting verb "stated" is in the past tense. In indirect speech, the tense of the reported clause must backshift. "will" must change to its past equivalent "would".
**Rule:** Past tense reporting verbs require past tense in the dependent clause.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** IBM

**Q3. (Pronoun / Redundancy Trap)**
When the new intern joins the team, he should quickly familiarize himself with the codebase.
(A) When the new intern joins
(B) the team, he should quickly
(C) familiarize himself with
(D) the codebase.

**✅ Answer: (D) NO ERROR**
**📖 Explanation:** Let's scan. SVA: intern joins (Correct). Tense: Present/Future (Correct). Pronouns: intern -> he/himself (Correct). Prepositions: familiarize with (Correct). Many students assume 'himself' is wrong here, but 'familiarize' requires a reflexive pronoun when the subject and object are the same.
**Rule:** Reflexive pronouns.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Cognizant

**Q4. (Article / Countable Trap)**
The IT department has requested for an equipment to handle the increased server load.
(A) The IT department has
(B) requested for
(C) an equipment to handle
(D) the increased server load.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Equipment" is an uncountable noun in English. You cannot use the indefinite article "a/an" with uncountable nouns, nor can you make them plural (equipments). It should be "requested equipment" or "a piece of equipment". (Note: 'requested for' is also often considered wordy, 'requested' is transitive, so B is also slightly faulty, but C is a fatal grammatical error).
**Rule:** Articles with uncountable nouns.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Wipro

**Q5. (Inversion Trap)**
Hardly I had sat down at my desk to code when the fire alarm started blaring.
(A) Hardly I had sat down
(B) at my desk to code
(C) when the fire alarm
(D) started blaring.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** When a sentence begins with a negative adverb (Hardly, Scarcely, No sooner, Never), you MUST use the Rule of Inversion (Verb before Subject). It should be: "Hardly **had I** sat down...".
**Rule:** Law of Inversion.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Infosys

**Q6. (Preposition Trap)**
The new junior developer was accused for leaking confidential company data to a competitor.
(A) The new junior developer
(B) was accused for
(C) leaking confidential company data
(D) to a competitor.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 4 Scan! The verb "accused" is always followed by the preposition "of", never "for". (Accused of, charged with, blamed for).
**Rule:** Fixed Prepositions.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** TCS

**Q7. (Parallelism Trap)**
To deploy the application, you must configure the servers, setting up the database, and run the automated tests.
(A) To deploy the application,
(B) you must configure the servers,
(C) setting up the database,
(D) and run the automated tests.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 5 Scan! Look at the list of verbs following "must": configure (V1), setting (V-ing), run (V1). "setting up" breaks the parallelism. It should be "set up".
**Rule:** Parallel structure in lists.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Capgemini

**Q8. (Modifier Trap)**
Being a rainy day, the project manager decided to cancel the outdoor team-building event.
(A) Being a rainy day,
(B) the project manager decided
(C) to cancel the outdoor
(D) team-building event.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 6 Scan! Dangling modifier. The phrase "Being a rainy day" modifies the noun immediately following the comma ("the project manager"). Was the project manager a rainy day? No. It should be "It being a rainy day, the project manager...".
**Rule:** Dangling Participles.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Accenture

**Q9. (Conditional Trap)**
If I would have known about the security vulnerability, I would have patched the software immediately.
(A) If I would have known
(B) about the security vulnerability,
(C) I would have patched
(D) the software immediately.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a Type 3 Conditional (Past unfulfilled). The structure is: `If + Past Perfect, Subject + would have + V3`. You cannot use "would have" in the "if" clause. It should be "If I had known...".
**Rule:** Third Conditional structure.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** IBM

**Q10. (Subject-Verb Agreement Trap 2)**
Either the front-end developers or the lead designer are responsible for the terrible UI on the new application.
(A) Either the front-end developers
(B) or the lead designer
(C) are responsible for
(D) the terrible UI on the new application.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Step 1 Scan! With correlative conjunctions like "Either...or" and "Neither...nor", the verb agrees with the subject CLOSEST to it. The closest subject to the verb is "the lead designer" (singular). Therefore, "are" must become "is".
**Rule:** Proximity Rule for SVA.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** TCS NQT

**Q11. (Superfluous/Redundancy Trap)**
Despite of the heavy workload, the team managed to deliver the project ahead of schedule.
(A) Despite of the
(B) heavy workload, the team
(C) managed to deliver
(D) the project ahead of schedule.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Despite" means "in spite of". Using "despite of" is a massive grammatical redundancy. Remove "of".
**Rule:** Redundant Prepositions.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Company:** Cognizant

**Q12. (Pronoun Antecedent Trap)**
Every programmer must ensure that they comment their code properly before pushing it to the main branch.
(A) Every programmer must
(B) ensure that they comment
(C) their code properly
(D) before pushing it to the main branch.

**✅ Answer: (B) (Technically C as well)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Every" makes a noun singular (Every programmer). Therefore, the pronoun referring back to it must be singular (he/she, his/her). Formal grammar requires "ensure that he or she comments his or her code". (Note: In casual modern English, singular 'they' is accepted, but strictly penalized in traditional placement exams).
**Rule:** Indefinite Pronouns are Singular.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** Wipro

**Q13. (Comparative Trap)**
The salary offered by TCS is comparatively lower than what Infosys offered for the same role.
(A) The salary offered by TCS
(B) is comparatively lower than
(C) what Infosys offered
(D) for the same role.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The word "comparatively" already implies comparison. Using it with a comparative degree adjective ("lower") is redundant. You should say either "is comparatively low" or "is lower than".
**Rule:** Double Comparatives / Redundancy.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Capgemini

**Q14. (Fixed Preposition / Infinitive Trap)**
She prevented him to access the server logs by changing all the administrative passwords.
(A) She prevented him
(B) to access the server logs
(C) by changing all the
(D) administrative passwords.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The verb "prevent" is followed by the preposition "from" and a gerund. It should be "prevented him **from accessing**".
**Rule:** Prevent from + V-ing.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Company:** IBM

**Q15. (SVA / 'Number of' Trap)**
A number of severe bugs was found during the final phase of user acceptance testing.
(A) A number of
(B) severe bugs was
(C) found during the final
(D) phase of user acceptance testing.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** 
*   "A number of" = Plural verb ("were found").
*   "The number of" = Singular verb ("was found").
**Rule:** A number vs The number.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Company:** Infosys
