**Q1. Identify the grammatical error in the following sentence:**
(A) The CEO, along with his board of directors,
(B) are planning to launch
(C) a new subsidiary company
(D) in the upcoming financial year.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** According to Subject-Verb Agreement rules, when two subjects are joined by "along with", "as well as", or "together with", the verb must agree with the FIRST subject. The first subject is "The CEO" (singular). Therefore, the verb should be "is planning", not "are planning".
**⚡ Shortcut:** "along with" -> look at first word -> CEO -> singular -> is.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q2. Choose the correct option to fill in the blank:**
The manager was absolutely ________ when he found out that the confidential data had been leaked to the competitors.
(A) annoyed
(B) irate
(C) furious
(D) irritated

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence uses the adverb "absolutely". We use "absolutely" with extreme adjectives (non-gradable), not regular adjectives. You can be "very annoyed" or "very irritated", but you are "absolutely furious". "Furious" is an extreme adjective meaning extremely angry.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Absolutely + Extreme Adjective.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q3. Improve the bracketed part of the sentence:**
I would have bought a new car if I [would have had] enough money.
(A) have had
(B) had
(C) had had
(D) No improvement required

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** This is a Type 3 Conditional sentence dealing with an unreal past situation. The structure is: `If + Past Perfect (had + V3), Subject + would have + V3`. The past perfect of "have" is "had had" (auxiliary 'had' + main verb V3 'had'). So, "if I had had enough money".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Never use "would have" inside the IF clause. Cross out (A) and (D). Past perfect requires two "hads".
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q4. Fill in the blank with the appropriate preposition:**
Despite his vast wealth, he is notoriously stingy and often haggles ________ the street vendors over a few rupees.
(A) to
(B) with
(C) for
(D) against

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** The verb "haggle" (to argue about a price) is followed by the preposition "with" when referring to the person you are arguing with, and "over/about" for the thing being argued about. Here, it refers to the vendors (people), so "haggle with".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Argue/Haggle + with + person.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q5. Identify the error in the sentence:**
(A) Scarcely had the minister
(B) started his speech
(C) then the crowd began
(D) to chant slogans against him.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The correlative conjunction pair is "Scarcely...when" or "Hardly...when". It is never "Scarcely...then". The error is in part C; "then" should be replaced by "when".
**⚡ Shortcut:** Scarcely/Hardly -> WHEN. No sooner -> THAN.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q7. Rearrange the following parts (P, Q, R, S) to form a meaningful sentence:**
P: to ensure that the project is
Q: the team has been working relentlessly
R: completed within the tight deadline
S: despite the numerous technical challenges

(A) SQPR
(B) QPRS
(C) QSPR
(D) SPRQ

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** The sentence must start with a subject. Q starts with "the team". So Q is first. Working relentlessly... why? "to ensure that the project is" (P). Is what? "completed within the tight deadline" (R). Despite what? "despite the numerous technical challenges" (S). Sequence: Q-P-R-S.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Q is the only independent clause opening. P ends with "is", needing an adjective/V3, which is "completed" in R. So PR is a pair. Only B and C have Q first. C has PR together (actually B has PR too), but QSPR makes sense logically.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

---

**Q8. Choose the most appropriate synonym for the word: MITIGATE**
(A) Aggravate
(B) Alleviate
(C) Instigate
(D) Replicate

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Mitigate" means to make something less severe, harmful, or painful. "Alleviate" is its exact synonym. "Aggravate" is an antonym.
**⚡ Shortcut:** Miti (mild) -> make mild. Alleviate -> relieve.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q9. Improve the bracketed part:**
No sooner did the bell ring [when the students rushed] out of the class.
(A) than the students rushed
(B) then the students rushed
(C) but the students rushed
(D) No improvement

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "No sooner" must be followed by "than", not "when" or "then".
**⚡ Shortcut:** No sooner ends in 'er' (comparative form) -> always takes 'than'.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q10. Identify the error:**
(A) One of the biggest problem
(B) faced by the IT industry today
(C) is the high rate of
(D) employee attrition.

**✅ Answer: (A)**
**📖 Explanation:** The phrase "One of the..." is always followed by a PLURAL noun. It should be "One of the biggest problems", because you are choosing one out of many problems.
**⚡ Shortcut:** One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium
**🏢 Company:** Accenture

**Q12. Fill in the blank:**
By the time you reach the station, the train ________.
(A) will leave
(B) left
(C) will have left
(D) is leaving

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** "By the time" indicates a deadline in the future. The action (leaving) will be completed before that deadline. Thus, Future Perfect Tense (will have + V3) is required.
**📊 Difficulty:** Medium

**Q13. Identify the correct passive voice:**
They are painting the walls.
(A) The walls are painted by them.
(B) The walls have been painted by them.
(C) The walls are being painted by them.
(D) The walls were being painted by them.

**✅ Answer: (C)**
**📖 Explanation:** Present continuous tense (are painting) changes to `is/are + being + V3` in passive voice.
**📊 Difficulty:** Easy

**Q15. Error Detection:**
(A) The sceneries of

(D) visit it again.

**✅ Answer: (A)**

**Q19. Error Detection:**
(A) Neither of the two brothers
(B) have brought
(C) their documents
(D) for verification.

**✅ Answer: (B)**
**📖 Explanation:** "Neither of" takes a plural noun but a singular verb. It should be "has brought". Also, "their" in part C should technically be "his". But B is the primary subject-verb error.
**📊 Difficulty:** Hard
