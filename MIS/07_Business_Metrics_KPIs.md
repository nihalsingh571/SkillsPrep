# Business Metrics & KPIs — 30 Interview Questions

> For MIS Executive interviews — core knowledge expected at every company

---

**1. What is a KPI?**

A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a measurable value that demonstrates how effectively a company or individual is achieving key business objectives. KPIs are selected based on strategic goals — for example, a sales team may use "Monthly Revenue" as a KPI. Unlike general metrics, KPIs are tied to specific targets (e.g., achieve ₹50L revenue in Q1). They help management take corrective action when performance deviates from the goal.

---

**2. What is the difference between a KPI and a Metric?**

A metric is any quantifiable measurement (e.g., number of website visits, number of calls made). A KPI is a subset of metrics — it is a metric that is directly tied to a strategic business goal. All KPIs are metrics, but not all metrics are KPIs. For example, "total emails sent" is a metric, but "email conversion rate vs. 5% target" is a KPI.

---

**3. What is a Dashboard KPI?**

A dashboard KPI is a key metric displayed visually on a management dashboard so stakeholders can monitor performance at a glance. It typically shows the current value, target, and status (on track / at risk / behind). Dashboard KPIs are designed for quick decision-making without reading full reports. Examples include a speedometer chart for CSAT score or a progress bar for monthly revenue achievement.

---

**4. What are common Sales KPIs?**

Common sales KPIs include: Revenue (total income generated), Conversion Rate (% of leads that become customers), Average Order Value (AOV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Win Rate (deals won vs. total deals), Sales Cycle Length (average days to close a deal), and Revenue per Sales Rep. These KPIs help sales managers identify top performers, forecast revenue, and optimize the sales process.

---

**5. What is Revenue as a KPI and how is it tracked?**

Revenue is the total income generated from sales of goods or services in a given period. As a KPI, it is tracked monthly, quarterly, and annually against a set target. It can be broken down by product line, region, or sales rep for deeper analysis. Formula: Revenue = Units Sold × Selling Price.

---

**6. What is Conversion Rate and how is it calculated?**

Conversion Rate measures the percentage of prospects or leads that complete a desired action (e.g., make a purchase, sign a contract). Formula: Conversion Rate = (Number of Conversions / Total Leads) × 100. For example, if 200 leads result in 40 sales, the conversion rate is 20%. A higher conversion rate indicates an efficient sales or marketing process.

---

**7. What is Average Order Value (AOV)?**

Average Order Value (AOV) is the average amount spent by a customer per transaction. Formula: AOV = Total Revenue / Number of Orders. For example, if total revenue is ₹5,00,000 from 500 orders, AOV = ₹1,000. Increasing AOV through upselling or bundling directly boosts revenue without increasing customer count.

---

**8. What is Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)?**

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is the total cost spent to acquire one new customer, including marketing, sales, and onboarding expenses. Formula: CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend / Number of New Customers Acquired. For example, if ₹10,00,000 is spent and 500 customers are acquired, CAC = ₹2,000. A lower CAC with maintained revenue indicates improved efficiency.

---

**9. What is Win Rate in sales?**

Win Rate is the percentage of sales opportunities that result in a closed deal. Formula: Win Rate = (Deals Won / Total Deals Pursued) × 100. For example, if 30 out of 100 proposals convert to sales, the win rate is 30%. It helps assess the effectiveness of the sales team and quality of leads.

---

**10. What is Sales Cycle Length?**

Sales Cycle Length is the average number of days it takes to close a deal, from first contact to signed contract. Formula: Sales Cycle Length = Total Days to Close All Deals / Number of Deals Closed. A shorter sales cycle means faster revenue realization. Tracking this KPI helps identify bottlenecks in the sales process.

---

**11. What are common Operations KPIs?**

Common operations KPIs include: TAT (Turnaround Time), SLA Compliance %, First Call Resolution (FCR), Average Handle Time (AHT), CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score), NPS (Net Promoter Score), Defect Rate, and Throughput. These KPIs measure the efficiency, quality, and speed of operational processes. They are critical in BPO, customer service, manufacturing, and logistics sectors.

---

**12. What is TAT (Turnaround Time)?**

TAT (Turnaround Time) is the total time taken to complete a specific task or process from the moment it is received to the moment it is delivered or resolved. For example, in a service center, TAT might be the time from complaint registration to resolution. Formula: TAT = Time of Completion − Time of Receipt. Monitoring TAT ensures operational efficiency and helps meet SLA commitments.

---

**13. What is SLA Compliance % and how is it calculated?**

SLA Compliance % measures the percentage of tasks or service requests resolved within the agreed Service Level Agreement timeframe. Formula: SLA Compliance % = (Tasks Resolved Within SLA / Total Tasks) × 100. For example, if 950 out of 1,000 tickets are resolved on time, SLA Compliance = 95%. It is a critical KPI in IT, BPO, and customer service operations.

---

**14. What is First Call Resolution (FCR)?**

First Call Resolution (FCR) is the percentage of customer issues resolved on the very first interaction without any follow-up required. Formula: FCR = (Issues Resolved on First Contact / Total Issues) × 100. A high FCR (above 70–80%) indicates effective customer service and reduces repeat contacts. It directly impacts CSAT and operational costs.

---

**15. What is Average Handle Time (AHT)?**

Average Handle Time (AHT) is the average time an agent spends on a customer interaction, including talk time, hold time, and after-call work. Formula: AHT = (Talk Time + Hold Time + After-Call Work) / Number of Calls Handled. Lower AHT is generally better, but not at the expense of resolution quality. AHT is a standard KPI in call centers and BPO operations.

---

**16. What is CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score)?**

CSAT measures how satisfied customers are with a product, service, or specific interaction. It is typically collected via a post-interaction survey asking customers to rate their experience (e.g., 1–5 scale). Formula: CSAT % = (Number of Satisfied Responses / Total Responses) × 100. A CSAT above 80% is generally considered good across industries.

---

**17. What is NPS (Net Promoter Score) and how is it calculated?**

NPS measures customer loyalty by asking: "On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend?" Respondents are classified as Promoters (9–10), Passives (7–8), and Detractors (0–6). Formula: NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors. NPS ranges from −100 to +100; a score above 50 is considered excellent. It predicts business growth through customer advocacy.

---

**18. What are common HR KPIs?**

Common HR KPIs include: Attrition Rate, Absenteeism Rate, Time to Hire, Cost per Hire, and Employee Satisfaction Score. These metrics help HR managers monitor workforce stability, recruitment efficiency, and employee engagement. They are frequently reported in monthly MIS decks submitted to HR heads and business leadership.

---

**19. What is Attrition Rate and how is it calculated?**

Attrition Rate measures the percentage of employees who leave an organization over a given period. Formula: Attrition Rate = (Number of Employees Who Left / Average Number of Employees) × 100. Average Employees = (Opening Headcount + Closing Headcount) / 2. For example, if 20 employees left and the average headcount is 200, Attrition Rate = 10%. High attrition signals HR and management issues.

---

**20. What is Absenteeism Rate?**

Absenteeism Rate tracks the percentage of workdays lost due to unplanned employee absence. Formula: Absenteeism Rate = (Number of Absent Days / Total Available Working Days) × 100. High absenteeism impacts productivity and is a leading indicator of employee disengagement. HR uses this KPI to identify departments or teams with attendance concerns.

---

**21. What are financial KPIs?**

Common financial KPIs include: Gross Margin (revenue minus cost of goods sold as a %), Net Margin (net profit as a % of revenue), EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortization), ROI (Return on Investment), and Working Capital (current assets minus current liabilities). These KPIs are used by finance teams and reported in monthly P&L and MIS decks to senior management.

---

**22. What is ROI (Return on Investment)?**

ROI measures the profitability or efficiency of an investment relative to its cost. Formula: ROI = [(Net Profit from Investment − Cost of Investment) / Cost of Investment] × 100. For example, if a campaign costing ₹1,00,000 generates ₹1,50,000 in revenue (net gain ₹50,000), ROI = 50%. It is used to justify business spending and compare projects.

---

**23. What is ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)?**

ARPU measures the average revenue generated per user or customer in a specific period. Formula: ARPU = Total Revenue / Total Number of Active Users. It is widely used in telecom, SaaS, and subscription-based businesses. Tracking ARPU helps companies understand revenue efficiency and identify upsell opportunities.

---

**24. What is Churn Rate?**

Churn Rate is the percentage of customers who stop using a product or service within a given period. Formula: Churn Rate = (Customers Lost During Period / Customers at Start of Period) × 100. A high churn rate indicates customer dissatisfaction or strong competition. Reducing churn is often more cost-effective than acquiring new customers.

---

**25. What is Burn Rate?**

Burn Rate is the rate at which a company spends its cash reserves, typically measured monthly. Formula: Monthly Burn Rate = Cash Spent in a Month. It is critical for startups to monitor because it determines the "runway" — how long the company can operate before running out of funds. Gross Burn = total monthly expenses; Net Burn = expenses minus revenue.

---

**26. What is the MoM Growth formula?**

MoM (Month-over-Month) Growth measures the percentage change in a metric from one month to the next. Formula: MoM Growth = [(Current Month Value − Previous Month Value) / Previous Month Value] × 100. For example, if revenue was ₹10L in June and ₹12L in July, MoM growth = 20%. It is useful for identifying short-term trends and sudden changes.

---

**27. What is YoY Growth and how is it calculated?**

YoY (Year-over-Year) Growth compares performance in the current period against the same period in the previous year, eliminating seasonal effects. Formula: YoY Growth = [(Current Year Value − Previous Year Value) / Previous Year Value] × 100. For example, if Q2 revenue this year is ₹50L vs. ₹40L last year, YoY growth = 25%. It is the most commonly used growth metric in annual reports.

---

**28. What is a Rolling Average?**

A Rolling (or Moving) Average smooths out short-term fluctuations to reveal underlying trends in a dataset. For example, a 3-month rolling average of sales sums the last 3 months' values and divides by 3, recalculated each month. It is used in MIS to make trend lines cleaner and reduce noise from one-off spikes. Commonly used in sales, operations, and financial reporting.

---

**29. What is the difference between Leading and Lagging Indicators?**

Leading indicators are forward-looking metrics that predict future performance (e.g., number of proposals submitted predicts future revenue). Lagging indicators are backward-looking metrics that confirm past performance (e.g., revenue earned last month). In KPI frameworks, a good balance of both is recommended. For example, "number of demos booked" (leading) vs. "revenue closed" (lagging).

---

**30. What are common mistakes when tracking KPIs?**

Common KPI tracking mistakes include: tracking too many KPIs (leading to analysis paralysis), not setting clear targets, using vanity metrics that look good but drive no decisions, not updating KPIs when business goals change, and presenting KPIs without context or benchmarks. Best practice is to track 5–10 meaningful KPIs per function, always with a target, trend, and commentary. KPIs should drive action, not just fill a report.
